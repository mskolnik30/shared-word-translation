# The Shared Word Translation Family

## One project, two reading postures

The Shared Word Project offers two complementary English translations of the Christian Scriptures. Each is complete. Each is translated from verified Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek sources. Neither is a revision of the other.

They differ in the kind of attention they invite.

| Translation | Short label | Best suited for | Character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Shared Word Translation (TSW) | **Close Reading** | Close reading, preaching, study, and shared discernment | Preserves literary shape, tension, repetition, and meaningful ambiguity when English can bear them |
| Fluent Translation | **Natural Reading** | Daily reading, devotion, teaching, and growing biblical fluency | Carries the sense and movement of the text in direct, natural contemporary English |

The two short descriptions should be used consistently in the Bible Reader, landing pages, teaching tools, downloads, and promotional language:

> **TSW — Close Reading:** for close reading, preaching, study, and shared discernment.

> **Fluent — Natural Reading:** for daily reading, devotion, teaching, and growing biblical fluency.

## Choosing a translation

Choose **TSW** when a community wants to slow down, notice how the text works, remain with tensions, and discuss interpretive possibilities together.

Choose **Fluent** when a reader or community wants the passage to move clearly in contemporary English across sustained reading, prayer, teaching, or formation.

Readers do not have to choose one translation permanently. Moving between them can be fruitful: read Fluent for the whole movement of a passage, then turn to TSW to notice details, tensions, and structure.

## What the distinction does not mean

- TSW is not only for scholars, and Fluent is not only for beginners.
- TSW is not the “literal” text while Fluent is an interpretation. All translation requires interpretation.
- Fluent is not a simplified paraphrase or a devotional commentary.
- The translations are not coded as progressive and conservative options.
- Neither translation should be presented as more faithful, more spiritual, or more authoritative than the other.

## Editorial relationship

TSW and Fluent are translated independently from the source texts. TSW may serve as a comparator during Fluent review, but Fluent does not begin with TSW and rewrite it sentence by sentence. Differences between them should therefore be treated as useful evidence of translation posture, not as mistakes by default.

Both translations remain provisional and may receive corrections or careful revisions as they are tested in worship, teaching, personal reading, and communal study. Material changes should be documented through versioned releases and public release notes.

## Public naming

Use these names consistently:

- **The Shared Word Project** — the umbrella translation project
- **The Shared Word Translation (TSW)** — the close-reading translation
- **Fluent Translation** — the natural-reading translation
- **Church Commons Bible Reader** — the shared reading interface
- **Fluent Companion** — the future formational layer of introductions, context, reflection, prayer, and practices

“The Shared Word Translation” should no longer be used as the umbrella name when both translations are meant.
