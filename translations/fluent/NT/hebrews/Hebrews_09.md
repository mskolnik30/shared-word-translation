## The Earthly Holy Place

<p>
v01: Now the first covenant also had regulations for worship and an earthly sanctuary.
v02: A tent was prepared. In its first section were the lampstand, the table and the presented bread; this section is called the Holy Place.
v03: Behind the second curtain was a section of the tent called the Most Holy Place.
v04: It had the golden incense altar and the ark of the covenant covered completely with gold. In the ark were a gold jar holding the manna, Aaron's staff that had budded and the tablets of the covenant.
v05: Above it were the cherubim of glory overshadowing the atonement cover. We cannot now discuss these things in detail.
v06: With these things arranged this way, the priests continually enter the first section of the tent to perform their services.
v07: But only the high priest enters the second section, and only once a year, never without blood, which he offers for himself and for the people's sins committed in ignorance.
v08: By this the Holy Spirit indicates that the way into the holy place has not yet been disclosed while the first tent is still standing.
v09: This is an illustration for the present time, in which gifts and sacrifices are offered that cannot perfect the worshiper's conscience.
v10: They concern only food, drink and various washings—physical regulations imposed until a time of reformation.
</p>

## Redemption Through the Blood of Christ

<p>
v11: But when Christ came as high priest of the good things that have come, he passed through the greater and more perfect tent not made by hands, that is, not of this creation.
v12: He entered the holy place once for all, not by the blood of goats and calves but by his own blood, obtaining eternal redemption.
v13: For if the blood of goats and bulls and the ashes of a heifer sprinkled on those who are defiled make them holy for outward cleansing,
v14: how much more will Christ's blood, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without blemish to God, cleanse our conscience from dead works so that we may serve the living God?
v15: For this reason Christ is mediator of a new covenant, so that, since a death has occurred to redeem transgressions under the first covenant, those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance.
v16: For where there is a will, the death of the one who made it must be established.
v17: A will is valid only after death; it never takes effect while the one who made it is living.
v18: Therefore, not even the first covenant was inaugurated without blood.
v19: After Moses had spoken every command of the law to all the people, he took the blood of calves with water, scarlet wool and hyssop and sprinkled the scroll itself and all the people,
v20: saying, “This is the blood of the covenant that God commanded for you.”
v21: In the same way, he sprinkled the tent and all the vessels used in worship with blood.
v22: Indeed, under the law almost everything is cleansed with blood, and without blood being poured out there is no forgiveness.
</p>

## Christ’s Sacrifice Once for All

<p>
v23: Therefore, it was necessary for the copies of heavenly things to be cleansed with these sacrifices, but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these.
v24: For Christ did not enter a holy place made by hands, a copy of the true one, but heaven itself, now to appear before God's face on our behalf.
v25: Nor did he enter to offer himself again and again, as the high priest enters the holy place every year with blood not his own.
v26: Otherwise, he would have had to suffer repeatedly since the world's foundation. But now he has appeared once at the culmination of the ages to abolish sin through his sacrifice.
v27: And just as human beings are appointed to die once, and after that comes judgment,
v28: so Christ too, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time—not to deal with sin, but to bring salvation to those eagerly waiting for him.
</p>
