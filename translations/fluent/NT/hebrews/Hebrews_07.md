## The Priestly Order of Melchizedek

<p>
v01: This Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, met Abraham as he returned from defeating the kings and blessed him.
v02: Abraham gave him a tenth of everything. His name first means “king of righteousness,” and then “king of Salem,” that is, “king of peace.”
v03: Without father, mother or genealogy, with neither beginning of days nor end of life, resembling God's Son, he remains a priest continually.
v04: Consider how great he was: even the patriarch Abraham gave him a tenth of the spoils.
v05: Now those descendants of Levi who receive the priesthood have a command in the law to collect a tenth from the people—that is, from their own brothers and sisters—even though they too come from Abraham's body.
v06: But Melchizedek, whose genealogy is not traced from them, collected a tenth from Abraham and blessed the one who had the promises.
v07: Without any dispute, the lesser is blessed by the greater.
v08: In the one case, mortal people receive tithes; in the other, one receives them who is testified to be living.
v09: One might even say that Levi, who receives tithes, paid a tithe through Abraham,
v10: for he was still in his ancestor's body when Melchizedek met Abraham.
</p>

## Jesus Compared to Melchizedek

<p>
v11: Now if perfection had come through the Levitical priesthood—for on its basis the people received the law—what further need was there for another priest to arise according to Melchizedek's order rather than Aaron's?
v12: For when the priesthood is changed, a change of law necessarily follows.
v13: The one about whom these things are said belonged to a different tribe, from which no one served at the altar.
v14: For it is evident that our Lord arose from Judah, a tribe about which Moses said nothing concerning priests.
v15: This becomes even clearer when another priest arises in the likeness of Melchizedek,
v16: one who became priest not according to a law based on physical descent but according to the power of an indestructible life.
v17: For it is testified, “You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.”
v18: On the one hand, a former command is set aside because it was weak and ineffective,
v19: for the law perfected nothing. On the other hand, a better hope is introduced, through which we draw near to God.
v20: And this was not done without an oath. Others became priests without an oath,
v21: but he became priest with an oath through the one who said to him, “The Lord has sworn and will not change his mind: ‘You are a priest forever.’”
v22: Accordingly, Jesus has become the guarantor of a better covenant.
v23: There have been many priests because death prevented them from continuing in office,
v24: but because Jesus remains forever, he holds a permanent priesthood.
v25: Therefore, he is able to save completely those who approach God through him, since he always lives to intercede for them.
v26: For this is the kind of high priest we needed: holy, innocent, undefiled, separated from sinners and exalted above the heavens.
v27: He does not need to offer sacrifices every day, like the high priests—first for his own sins and then for the people's—because he did this once for all when he offered himself.
v28: For the law appoints as high priests people who have weakness, but the word of the oath, which came after the law, appoints a Son who has been perfected forever.
</p>
