## Prophecy and Tongues

<p>
v01: Pursue love and eagerly desire spiritual gifts, especially that you may prophesy.
v02: For the one who speaks in a tongue speaks not to people but to God, since no one understands; by the Spirit that person speaks mysteries.
</p>

<p>
v03: But the one who prophesies speaks to people for building up, encouragement, and comfort.
v04: The one who speaks in a tongue builds up oneself, but the one who prophesies builds up the church.
</p>

<p>
v05: Now I want all of you to speak in tongues, but even more to prophesy. The one who prophesies is greater than the one who speaks in tongues, unless someone interprets so that the church may be built up.
</p>

<p>
v06: Now, brothers and sisters, if I come to you speaking in tongues, how will I benefit you unless I speak to you with a revelation, knowledge, prophecy, or teaching?
</p>

<p>
v07: Even lifeless instruments that produce sound, whether flute or harp, if they make no distinction between notes, how will anyone know what is played?
v08: And if a trumpet gives an unclear sound, who will prepare for battle?
</p>

<p>
v09: So also with you: unless you give intelligible speech with your tongue, how will anyone know what is being said? You will simply be speaking into the air.
</p>

<p>
v10: There are, perhaps, many kinds of languages in the world, and none is without meaning.
v11: So if I do not know the meaning of the language, I will be a foreigner to the speaker, and the speaker a foreigner to me.
</p>

<p>
v12: So also with you: since you are eager for spiritual manifestations, seek to excel in building up the church.
</p>

<p>
v13: Therefore, the one who speaks in a tongue should pray to interpret.
v14: For if I pray in a tongue, my spirit prays, but my mind is unfruitful.
</p>

<p>
v15: What then? I will pray with the spirit, but I will also pray with the mind. I will sing praise with the spirit, but I will also sing praise with the mind.
v16: Otherwise, if you offer praise by the spirit, how can someone in the place of an outsider say “Amen” to your thanksgiving, since that person does not know what you are saying?
</p>

<p>
v17: You may be giving thanks well enough, but the other person is not built up.
v18: I thank God that I speak in tongues more than all of you.
v19: But in church I would rather speak five intelligible words to instruct others than ten thousand words in a tongue.
</p>

## Orderly Worship

<p>
v20: Brothers and sisters, do not be children in your thinking. Be infants in evil, but mature in your thinking.
</p>

<p>
v21: In the law it is written: “By people of other tongues and by the lips of foreigners I will speak to this people, yet even then they will not listen to me,” says the Lord.
</p>

<p>
v22: So tongues are a sign not for believers but for unbelievers, while prophecy is not for unbelievers but for believers.
</p>

<p>
v23: Therefore, if the whole church gathers together and everyone speaks in tongues, and outsiders or unbelievers enter, will they not say you are out of your minds?
v24: But if everyone prophesies and an unbeliever or outsider enters, that person is convicted by everyone and called to account by everyone.
v25: The secrets of that person's heart become evident, and so, falling facedown, the person will worship God and declare, “God is truly among you.”
</p>

<p>
v26: What then, brothers and sisters? When you gather, each one has a psalm, a teaching, a revelation, a tongue, or an interpretation. Let everything be done for building up.
</p>

<p>
v27: If anyone speaks in a tongue, let there be two, or at most three, each in turn, and let one person interpret.
v28: But if there is no interpreter, the speaker should remain silent in church and speak to oneself and to God.
</p>

<p>
v29: Let two or three prophets speak, and let the others discern what is said.
v30: But if a revelation comes to another person who is seated, the first speaker should be silent.
v31: For you can all prophesy one by one, so that everyone may learn and everyone may be encouraged.
v32: And prophets' spirits are subject to prophets,
</p>

<p>
v33: for God is not a God of disorder but of peace, as in all the churches of the saints.
</p>

<p>
As in all the churches of the holy ones,

v34: Women should remain silent in the churches, for they are not permitted to speak but should be in submission, as the law also says.
v35: If they want to learn something, they should ask their own husbands at home, for it is shameful for a woman to speak in church.
</p>

<p>
v36: Or did God's word originate with you? Or did it reach only you?
v37: If anyone thinks they are a prophet or spiritual, that person should recognize that what I write to you is the Lord's command.
v38: But if anyone refuses to recognize this, that person is not recognized.
</p>

<p>
v39: So then, my brothers and sisters, eagerly desire to prophesy, and do not forbid speaking in tongues.
v40: But let everything be done properly and in an orderly way.
</p>

## Notes

v02–19: Uninterpreted tongues address God but do not build listeners who cannot understand. Paul values both spirit and mind and prefers five intelligible words in church to ten thousand unintelligible ones.

v21–25: Paul's sign statement and the visitor scenarios create a difficult sequence. The translation preserves the tension rather than forcing one simple rule.

v26–33: Tongues and prophecy are limited, interpreted, weighed, and ordered; spiritual speech is not presented as uncontrollable.

v34–35: These verses command women to be silent and ask husbands at home, while 11:5 assumes women pray and prophesy. Their textual placement and application are disputed; the pinned main text includes them here.

v38: The pinned Greek reads the passive ‘is not recognized’; some witnesses read the imperative ‘let that person remain ignorant.’

## Vocabulary

v03: **οἰκοδομή (oikodomē)**: Building up or strengthening.

v11: **βάρβαρος (barbaros)**: A foreign-language speaker, heard as unintelligible.

v29: **διακρίνω (diakrinō)**: Evaluate, discern, or distinguish.
