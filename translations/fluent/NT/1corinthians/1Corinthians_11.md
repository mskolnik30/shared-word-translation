## Imitate Christ
<p>
v01: Imitate me, just as I imitate Christ.
v02: Now I praise you because you remember me in everything and hold firmly to the traditions just as I delivered them to you.
</p>

## Headship and Worship
<p>
v03: But I want you to know that Christ is the head of every man, the man is the head of a woman, and God is the head of Christ.
v04: Every man who prays or prophesies with something hanging down from his head dishonors his head.
v05: But every woman who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered dishonors her head, for it is one and the same as having her head shaved.
v06: For if a woman does not cover her head, she should also have her hair cut off. But if it is shameful for a woman to have her hair cut or shaved, she should cover her head.
</p>

<p>
v07: For a man ought not cover his head, since he is God's image and glory, but woman is man's glory.
v08: For man did not come from woman, but woman from man;
v09: and man was not created for woman, but woman for man.
v10: For this reason, and because of the angels, a woman ought to have authority over her head.
</p>

<p>
v11: Nevertheless, in the Lord woman is not independent of man, nor man independent of woman.
v12: For just as woman came from man, so man is born through woman, and all things come from God.
v13: Judge for yourselves: is it fitting for a woman to pray to God with her head uncovered?
</p>

<p>
v14: Does not nature itself teach you that if a man wears long hair, it is a dishonor to him,
v15: but if a woman wears long hair, it is her glory? For her hair is given to her as a covering.
v16: But if anyone is inclined to be contentious, we have no other practice, nor do God's churches.
</p>

## Abuses at the Lord’s Table
<p>
v17: Now in giving this instruction, I do not praise you, because when you gather it is not for the better but for the worse.
v18: For first, when you gather as a church, I hear there are divisions among you, and to some extent I believe it.
v19: Indeed, factions must also arise among you so that those who are approved may become evident among you.
</p>

<p>
v20: So when you gather in the same place, it is not really the Lord's supper you eat.
v21: For in eating, each person goes ahead with their own supper; one goes hungry while another gets drunk.
v22: Do you not have houses for eating and drinking? Or do you despise God's church and humiliate those who have nothing? What shall I say to you? Shall I praise you? In this I do not praise you.
</p>

## The Tradition of the Supper
<p>
v23: For I received from the Lord what I also handed on to you: the Lord Jesus, on the night when he was handed over, took bread,
v24: and after giving thanks, he broke it and said, “This is my body, which is for you. Do this in remembrance of me.”
v25: In the same way, after supper he took the cup, saying, “This cup is the new covenant in my blood. Whenever you drink it, do this in remembrance of me.”
</p>

<p>
v26: For whenever you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes.
</p>

## Discern the Body
<p>
v27: Therefore, whoever eats the bread or drinks the Lord's cup in an unworthy manner will be answerable for the Lord's body and blood.
v28: A person should examine oneself, and in this way eat from the bread and drink from the cup.
v29: For the one who eats and drinks without discerning the body eats and drinks judgment on oneself.
</p>

<p>
v30: That is why many among you are weak and sick, and quite a few have fallen asleep in death.
v31: But if we discerned ourselves, we would not be judged.
v32: Yet when we are judged by the Lord, we are disciplined so that we will not be condemned with the world.
</p>

## Practical Correction
<p>
v33: Therefore, my brothers and sisters, when you gather to eat, wait for one another.
v34: If anyone is hungry, that person should eat at home, so that your gathering does not result in judgment. I will arrange the remaining matters when I come.
</p>

## Notes

v03: ‘Head’ can carry senses of authority, prominence, or source. The main text retains the metaphor and gendered sequence.

v04–16: The precise head covering and the appeal to nature are culturally and grammatically disputed. Women are explicitly praying and prophesying; verse 10 says a woman has authority over her head.

v21–22: The meal's abuse includes hunger among those who have nothing and drunkenness among others. Paul's rebuke names class-based humiliation.

v24: The pinned Greek says ‘my body, which is for you’; it does not contain ‘broken for you.’

v27–32: ‘Unworthy’ modifies the manner of eating and drinking. The passage links failure to discern the body with communal judgment and discipline, not with a requirement that participants be morally flawless.

## Vocabulary

v03: **κεφαλή (kephalē)**: Head, used literally and as a relational metaphor.

v25: **διαθήκη (diathēkē)**: Covenant or binding arrangement.

v29: **διακρίνω (diakrinō)**: Discern, distinguish, or judge.
