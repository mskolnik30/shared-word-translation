## Ready for Every Good Work

<p>
v01: Remind them to be subject to rulers and authorities, to obey, to be ready for every good work,
v02: to slander no one, to avoid fighting, to be gentle, showing complete courtesy toward all people.
v03: For we ourselves were once foolish, disobedient, deceived, enslaved to various desires and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hated and hating one another.
</p>

## Saved by Mercy, Renewed by the Spirit

<p>
v04: But when the kindness and love for humanity of God our Savior appeared,
v05: he saved us—not because of works we had done in righteousness, but according to his mercy—through the washing of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit,
v06: whom he poured out on us richly through Jesus Christ our Savior,
v07: so that, having been set right by his grace, we might become heirs according to the hope of eternal life.
v08: This saying is trustworthy, and I want you to insist on these things so that those who have trusted God may be careful to devote themselves to good works. These things are good and beneficial for people.
</p>

## Avoid Foolish Controversies

<p>
v09: But avoid foolish controversies, genealogies, quarrels, and fights about the Law, because they are unprofitable and worthless.
v10: After a first and second warning, have nothing more to do with a divisive person,
v11: knowing that such a person has become twisted and is sinning, being self-condemned.
</p>

## Final Instructions

<p>
v12: When I send Artemas or Tychicus to you, make every effort to come to me at Nicopolis, because I have decided to spend the winter there.
v13: Diligently help Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their way so that they lack nothing.
v14: Our people must also learn to devote themselves to good works for urgent needs, so that they will not be unfruitful.
v15: Everyone with me greets you. Greet those who love us in the trust. Grace be with all of you.
</p>

## Notes

v01–02: Submission to governing authorities is paired with public gentleness and readiness for good works; it is not framed as worship or absolute obedience to every command.

v03: Paul places himself and his coworkers inside the same former human condition he critiques, preventing moral superiority.

v04: `philanthrōpia` literally means love for humanity and is applied to God’s saving kindness.

v05: Salvation is explicitly “not because of works we had done in righteousness” but according to mercy.

v05–06: Washing, rebirth, renewal, Holy Spirit, and pouring-out imagery converge without the text specifying one sacramental mechanism.

v07: `dikaioō` is rendered “set right,” consistent with the project’s Romans/Galatians lexical policy.

v08,14: Good works are strongly commanded precisely after works are denied as the basis of salvation. Grace produces useful practice.

v09: The problem is not Torah itself but unprofitable fights “about the Law.”

v10: `hairetikos` here means divisive or faction-producing person, not merely someone holding a later-defined doctrinal “heresy.”

v13: Zenas is called `nomikos`, which can mean lawyer or expert in law; in this travel context “lawyer” is natural, though the exact profession is uncertain.
