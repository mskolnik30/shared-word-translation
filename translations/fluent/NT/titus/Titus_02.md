## Teach What Fits Sound Teaching

<p>
v01: But you, speak what fits sound teaching.
v02: Older men are to be sober, dignified, self-controlled, sound in trust, love, and endurance.
v03: Older women likewise are to be reverent in behavior, not slanderers or enslaved to much wine, but teachers of what is good,
v04: so that they may train the younger women to love their husbands and children,
v05: to be self-controlled, pure, workers at home, kind, and responsive to their own husbands, so that God’s word is not slandered.
v06: Likewise urge younger men to be self-controlled.
v07: In everything present yourself as an example of good works, showing integrity and dignity in your teaching,
v08: and sound speech that cannot be condemned, so that an opponent may be put to shame, having nothing bad to say about us.
</p>

## Slaves Adorn the Teaching of God Our Savior

<p>
v09: Slaves are to be responsive to their own masters in everything, seeking to please them, not talking back,
v10: not stealing, but showing complete good faith, so that in everything they may adorn the teaching of God our Savior.
</p>

## Grace Trains Us

<p>
v11: For God’s grace has appeared, bringing salvation for all people,
v12: training us to deny ungodliness and worldly desires and to live self-controlled, righteous, and godly lives in the present age,
v13: while we wait for the blessed hope and the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ,
v14: who gave himself for us to redeem us from all lawlessness and cleanse for himself a people as his own possession, zealous for good works.
v15: Speak these things, encourage, and correct with all authority. Let no one disregard you.
</p>

## Notes

v01: “Sound teaching” is healthy teaching fitted to healthy communal life; doctrine and practice are inseparable.

v02–06: The instructions are age- and household-specific but repeatedly center self-control, love, integrity, and the public credibility of God’s word.

v03: Older women are explicitly assigned a teaching role toward younger women.

v05: Household instructions reflect ancient social structures. Fluent retains the text without expanding it into a universal claim that every woman must work only inside a home.

v09–10: The letter speaks within slavery rather than abolishing it, but enslaved believers are moral agents whose faithfulness can “adorn” teaching. Fluent does not replace slaves with employees.

v11: Grace does not merely pardon; it “trains” people toward a transformed life.

v11: “Salvation for all people” is universal in scope. Fluent does not narrow “all” by translation.

v13: The grammar strongly supports identifying Jesus Christ as “our great God and Savior.” Fluent preserves this direct christological wording.

v14: Redemption creates a people “for his own possession,” echoing Israel’s covenant language and directing them toward good works.
