## Greeting

<p>
v01: Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus, and Timothy our brother, to Philemon our beloved fellow worker,
v02: to Apphia our sister, Archippus our fellow soldier, and the church in your house:
v03: Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.
</p>

## Thanksgiving for Philemon’s Love and Trust

<p>
v04: I always thank my God when I mention you in my prayers,
v05: because I hear about your love and trust toward the Lord Jesus and for all the holy ones.
v06: I pray that the sharing of your trust may become effective through the knowledge of every good thing among us for Christ.
v07: For I have received much joy and encouragement from your love, because the hearts of the holy ones have been refreshed through you, brother.
</p>

## An Appeal for Onesimus

<p>
v08: Therefore, although I have great boldness in Christ to command you to do what is fitting,
v09: because of love I prefer to appeal to you—being such a person as Paul, an old man, and now also a prisoner of Christ Jesus.
v10: I appeal to you for my child Onesimus, whose father I became in my chains.
v11: Formerly he was useless to you, but now he is useful both to you and to me.
v12: I am sending him back to you—him, that is, my own heart.
v13: I wanted to keep him with me so that he might serve me on your behalf in my chains for the good news.
v14: But I did not want to do anything without your consent, so that your good deed might not be by compulsion but willingly.
</p>

## No Longer as a Slave but More than a Slave

<p>
v15: Perhaps this is why he was separated from you for a while: so that you might have him back forever,
v16: no longer as a slave but more than a slave—a beloved brother, especially to me, but how much more to you, both in the flesh and in the Lord.
v17: Therefore, if you consider me a partner, welcome him as you would me.
v18: If he has wronged you in any way or owes you anything, charge that to me.
v19: I, Paul, write this with my own hand: I will repay it—not to mention that you owe me even your own self.
v20: Yes, brother, let me have this benefit from you in the Lord. Refresh my heart in Christ.
v21: Confident of your obedience, I write to you, knowing that you will do even more than I say.
</p>

## Final Requests and Greetings

<p>
v22: At the same time, prepare a guest room for me, because I hope that through your prayers I will be graciously given back to you.
v23: Epaphras, my fellow prisoner in Christ Jesus, greets you,
v24: as do Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke, my fellow workers.
v25: The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit.
</p>

## Notes

v01–02: The letter is addressed not only to Philemon but also Apphia, Archippus, and the house church, making the Onesimus question at least partly communal rather than purely private.

v06: `koinōnia` is sharing or participation. Paul prays that Philemon’s trust becomes effective through practiced recognition of the good found in the Christ-community.

v07,12,20: “Heart” translates `splanchna`, literally inward organs, and forms a repeated emotional-relational thread through the letter.

v08–09: Paul explicitly contrasts his authority to command with his decision to appeal “because of love.”

v10: Paul calls Onesimus “my child,” using family language to transform how Philemon is to perceive him.

v11: Paul makes a wordplay on the name Onesimus, which is related to usefulness: once “useless,” now “useful.”

v13–14: Paul refuses to keep Onesimus without Philemon’s consent, because coerced goodness would violate the voluntary love he seeks.

v15: “Perhaps” is important. Paul offers providential interpretation without claiming certainty about why the separation occurred.

v16: The letter does not explicitly command legal manumission, but “no longer as a slave but more than a slave—a beloved brother” radically reframes the relationship. Fluent does not soften `doulos` into servant or employee.

v17: Paul asks Philemon to receive Onesimus as though receiving Paul himself, making partnership socially concrete.

v18–19: Paul offers to absorb Onesimus’ debt, while gently reminding Philemon of his own indebtedness to Paul.

v21: “Even more than I say” is deliberately open. Fluent does not insert emancipation into the main text, though manumission is a serious historical and interpretive possibility.

v22: Paul expects prayer to have practical effect on his release and travel.
