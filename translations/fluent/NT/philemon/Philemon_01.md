## Greeting

<p>
v01: Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus, and Timothy our brother, to Philemon our beloved coworker,
v02: to Apphia our sister, Archippus our fellow soldier and the church that meets in your house:
v03: Grace and peace to you from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.
</p>

## Thanksgiving for Philemon’s Love and Trust

<p>
v04: I always thank my God when I remember you in my prayers,
v05: because I hear of your love and faith toward the Lord Jesus and for all the saints.
v06: I pray that the sharing of your faith may become effective through recognizing every good thing among us for Christ.
v07: For I have received great joy and encouragement from your love, brother, because through you the hearts of the saints have been refreshed.
</p>

## An Appeal for Onesimus

<p>
v08: Therefore, though in Christ I have great boldness to command you to do what is fitting,
v09: I prefer to appeal to you on the basis of love—being who I am, Paul, an old man and now also a prisoner of Christ Jesus.
v10: I appeal to you for my child Onesimus, whose father I became while in chains.
v11: Formerly he was useless to you, but now he is useful both to you and to me.
v12: I am sending him back to you—him, that is, my own heart.
v13: I wanted to keep him with me, so that he might serve me on your behalf while I am in chains for the good news.
v14: But I did not want to do anything without your consent, so that your good deed would not be forced but voluntary.
</p>

## No Longer as a Slave but More than a Slave

<p>
v15: Perhaps this is why he was separated from you for a while, so that you might have him back forever,
v16: no longer merely as a slave but more than a slave—as a beloved brother, especially to me, and how much more to you, both in the flesh and in the Lord.
v17: So if you consider me a partner, welcome him as you would welcome me.
v18: If he has wronged you in any way or owes you anything, charge it to me.
v19: I, Paul, write this with my own hand: I will repay it—not to mention that you owe me your very self.
v20: Yes, brother, let me benefit from you in the Lord; refresh my heart in Christ.
v21: Confident of your obedience, I write to you, knowing that you will do even more than I say.
</p>

## Final Requests and Greetings

<p>
v22: At the same time, prepare a guest room for me, for I hope that through your prayers I will be restored to you.
v23: Epaphras, my fellow prisoner in Christ Jesus, greets you,
v24: as do Mark, Aristarchus, Demas and Luke, my coworkers.
v25: The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit.
</p>

## Notes

v10–16: Onesimus is treated within an enslaving relationship. Paul’s appeal changes the relationship’s moral terms but does not explicitly order legal manumission.

v18–19: The debt language carries real economic and relational pressure; Paul also invokes Philemon’s debt of self to him.

## Vocabulary

v16: **δοῦλος (doulos)**: Slave; the social status is stated directly before the appeal to receive Onesimus as a beloved brother.
