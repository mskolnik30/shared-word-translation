## The Vineyard and the Beloved Son

<p>
v01: Jesus began speaking to them in parables. “A man planted a vineyard, put a fence around it, dug a pit for the winepress, and built a watchtower. He leased it to tenant farmers and went away.
v02: At harvest time he sent a slave to the tenants to collect his share of the vineyard's fruit.
v03: They seized the slave, beat him, and sent him away empty-handed.
v04: He sent another slave to them. They struck this one on the head and treated him shamefully.
v05: He sent another, and they killed him. He sent many others: some they beat, and others they killed.
v06: He still had one left to send, a beloved son. He sent him to them last of all, saying, ‘They will respect my son.’
v07: But the tenants said to one another, ‘This is the heir. Come, let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours.’
v08: They seized him, killed him, and threw him out of the vineyard.
v09: What will the owner of the vineyard do? He will come and destroy the tenants and give the vineyard to others.
v10: Have you not read this scripture?
‘The stone the builders rejected
has become the cornerstone.
v11: This has come from the Lord,
and it is wonderful in our eyes.’”
v12: The leaders wanted to arrest Jesus, for they knew he had told the parable against them. But they feared the crowd, so they left him and went away.
</p>

## Caesar and God

<p>
v13: They sent some Pharisees and Herodians to trap him in something he said.
v14: They came and said, “Teacher, we know you are truthful and are not swayed by anyone, because you do not favor people according to their standing. You teach God's way truthfully. Is it lawful to pay the tax to Caesar or not? Should we pay it or should we not?”
v15: Jesus knew their hypocrisy. “Why are you testing me?” he asked. “Bring me a denarius so I can look at it.”
v16: They brought one. He asked, “Whose image and inscription are these?” “Caesar's,” they answered.
v17: Jesus told them, “Give back to Caesar what belongs to Caesar, and to God what belongs to God.” They were amazed at him.
</p>

## Resurrection and the God of the Living

<p>
v18: Some Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, came to him with a question:
v19: “Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if a man's brother dies, leaving a wife but no child, the man must marry his brother's widow and raise up offspring for his brother.
v20: There were seven brothers. The first married a woman and died without leaving any offspring.
v21: The second married her and also died without offspring. The third did the same.
v22: None of the seven left offspring. Last of all, the woman died too.
v23: In the resurrection, when they rise, whose wife will she be? All seven had married her.”
v24: Jesus told them, “Is this not why you are mistaken—you know neither the Scriptures nor the power of God?
v25: When people rise from the dead, they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but are like angels in heaven.
v26: As for the dead being raised, have you not read in the book of Moses, in the passage about the bush, how God said to him, ‘I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob’?
v27: He is God of the living, not of the dead. You are badly mistaken.”
</p>

## The Greatest Commandments

<p>
v28: One of the scribes came up and heard their discussion. Seeing that Jesus had answered them well, he asked, “Which commandment comes first of all?”
v29: Jesus answered, “The first is, ‘Hear, Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one.
v30: Love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, with all your mind, and with all your strength.’
v31: The second is this: ‘Love your neighbor as yourself.’ No other commandment is greater than these.”
v32: The scribe said, “Well said, Teacher. You have spoken truly: he is one, and there is no other besides him.
v33: To love him with all your heart, all your understanding, and all your strength, and to love your neighbor as yourself, matters more than all burnt offerings and sacrifices.”
v34: When Jesus saw how thoughtfully he had answered, he said, “You are not far from the kingdom of God.” After that, no one dared question him further.
</p>

## David’s Son and David’s Lord

<p>
v35: While teaching in the temple, Jesus asked, “How can the scribes say that the Messiah is David's son?
v36: David himself said by the Holy Spirit:
‘The Lord said to my lord,
“Sit at my right
until I put your enemies beneath your feet.”’
v37: David himself calls him ‘lord.’ How, then, is he his son?” The large crowd listened to him gladly.
</p>

## Scribes and a Poor Widow

<p>
v38: As he taught, he said, “Beware of the scribes who like to walk about in long robes and be greeted in the marketplaces,
v39: who want the best seats in the synagogues and places of honor at banquets.
v40: They devour widows' houses and make long prayers for show. They will receive a harsher judgment.”
v41: Jesus sat opposite the treasury and watched how the crowd put money into it. Many rich people put in large amounts.
v42: Then a poor widow came and put in two small copper coins, together worth a quadrans.
v43: Jesus called his disciples over and said, “Truly I tell you, this poor widow has put in more than everyone else who gave to the treasury.
v44: They all gave out of their surplus, but she, out of her poverty, put in everything she had—her whole livelihood.”
</p>

## Notes

v02: The Greek calls the person sent a slave, the same social term used in 10:44.

v08: In Mark's telling, the tenants kill the son before throwing him out of the vineyard.

v10: ‘Cornerstone’ renders ‘head of the corner,’ an image of a stone given a decisive place in a building.

v14–15: The repeated question ‘Should we pay it or should we not?’ belongs to verse 14 in the Greek text followed here.

v25: The comparison says people are ‘like angels,’ not that they become angels.

v29: The confession may also be rendered ‘The Lord is our God, the Lord alone.’ The Greek can be construed in more than one way.

v33: The scribe names heart, understanding, and strength. His reply is not expanded to repeat all four terms of verse 30.

v36: ‘The Lord’ and ‘my lord’ are distinct referents within the quotation. The text here says ‘beneath your feet,’ not ‘a footstool.’

v41–44: The widow's gift follows the warning about devouring widows' houses. The translation preserves Jesus' comparison without adding a command to imitate her or a single explanation of the scene.

## Vocabulary

v06: **ἀγαπητός (agapētos)**: Beloved; the same description used of the Son in 9:7.

v13: **Ἡρῳδιανοί (Hērōdianoi)**: Herodians, people associated with Herod's cause or rule.

v15: **δηνάριον (dēnarion)**: Denarius, a Roman coin.

v17: **ἀποδίδωμι (apodidōmi)**: Give back, repay, or render what is due.

v42: **λεπτόν / κοδράντης (lepton / kodrantēs)**: Lepton / quadrans, names of small coins. Mark states that the widow's two lepta together equal one quadrans.

v44: **βίος (bios)**: Life or means of living; here ‘livelihood.’
