## The Vineyard and the Beloved Son

<p>
v01: Jesus began to speak to them in parables: “A man planted a vineyard, put a fence around it, dug a winepress, and built a tower. Then he leased it to tenant farmers and went away.
v02: At harvest time he sent a servant to the tenants to receive some of the vineyard’s fruit from them.
v03: But they seized the servant, beat him, and sent him away empty-handed.
v04: Again the owner sent another servant. They struck this one on the head and treated him shamefully.
v05: He sent another, and they killed him. He sent many others; some they beat and some they killed.
v06: He still had one person left: a beloved son. He sent him to them last, saying, ‘They will respect my son.’
v07: But those tenants said to one another, ‘This is the heir. Come, let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours.’
v08: So they seized him, killed him, and threw him out of the vineyard.
v09: What will the owner of the vineyard do? He will come, destroy the tenants, and give the vineyard to others.
v10: Have you not read this Scripture:
‘The stone the builders rejected
has become the cornerstone;
v11: this came from the Lord,
and it is marvelous in our eyes’?”
v12: They wanted to arrest him because they knew he had spoken the parable against them, but they were afraid of the crowd. So they left him and went away.
</p>

## Caesar and God

<p>
v13: They sent some Pharisees and Herodians to Jesus to trap him in his words.
v14: They came and said, “Teacher, we know that you are truthful and do not show favoritism. You do not judge by outward status but teach the way of God in truth. Is it lawful to pay tribute to Caesar or not?
v15: Should we pay, or should we not?” Knowing their hypocrisy, Jesus said, “Why are you testing me? Bring me a denarius so I can see it.”
v16: They brought one. He asked, “Whose image and inscription is this?” They said, “Caesar’s.”
v17: Jesus told them, “Give back to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s.” And they were amazed at him.
</p>

## Resurrection and the God of the Living

<p>
v18: Some Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, came to Jesus and asked him,
v19: “Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if a man’s brother dies and leaves a wife but no child, his brother should take the widow and raise up offspring for his brother.
v20: There were seven brothers. The first married a woman and died without leaving offspring.
v21: The second married her and died without leaving offspring, and the third did the same.
v22: None of the seven left offspring. Last of all, the woman also died.
v23: In the resurrection, when they rise, whose wife will she be? All seven had married her.”
v24: Jesus said to them, “Is this not why you are mistaken: you know neither the Scriptures nor the power of God?
v25: When people rise from the dead, they neither marry nor are given in marriage but are like angels in heaven.
v26: And concerning the dead being raised, have you not read in the book of Moses, in the passage about the bush, how God said to him, ‘I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob’?
v27: He is not God of the dead but of the living. You are greatly mistaken.”
</p>

## The Greatest Commandments

<p>
v28: One of the scribes came near and heard them debating. Seeing that Jesus had answered them well, he asked, “Which commandment is first of all?”
v29: Jesus answered, “The first is: ‘Hear, Israel: The Lord our God, the Lord is one.
v30: Love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, with all your mind, and with all your strength.’
v31: The second is this: ‘Love your neighbor as yourself.’ There is no other commandment greater than these.”
v32: The scribe said, “Well said, Teacher. You have spoken truly: he is one, and there is no other besides him.
v33: And to love him with all the heart, all the understanding, and all the strength, and to love one’s neighbor as oneself, is more than all burnt offerings and sacrifices.”
v34: When Jesus saw that he answered thoughtfully, he said to him, “You are not far from the kingdom of God.” After that, no one dared to ask him any more questions.
</p>

## David’s Son and David’s Lord

<p>
v35: While Jesus was teaching in the temple, he asked, “How can the scribes say that the Christ is the son of David?
v36: David himself said by the Holy Spirit,
‘The Lord said to my Lord,
Sit at my right hand
until I put your enemies under your feet.’
v37: David himself calls him Lord. So how is he his son?” The large crowd listened to him gladly.
</p>

## Scribes and a Poor Widow

<p>
v38: In his teaching Jesus said, “Beware of the scribes who like to walk around in long robes, receive greetings in the marketplaces,
v39: have the best seats in synagogues and places of honor at banquets,
v40: devour widows’ houses, and make long prayers for appearance. They will receive a greater judgment.”
v41: Jesus sat down opposite the treasury and watched how the crowd put money into it. Many rich people put in large amounts.
v42: Then a poor widow came and put in two small copper coins, worth very little.
v43: Jesus called his disciples and said, “Truly I tell you, this poor widow has put in more than all the others putting money into the treasury.
v44: They all gave out of their abundance, but she, out of her poverty, put in everything she had—all she had to live on.”
</p>

## Notes

v01–09: The vineyard parable draws on scriptural vineyard imagery, but Fluent lets Mark’s hearers make the connections rather than identifying every character inside the main text.

v06: “Beloved son” echoes the heavenly declarations about Jesus while remaining fully part of the parable’s own story.

v10: “Cornerstone” represents a stone at a decisive building position. The rejection/reversal image matters more than specifying one ancient construction technique.

v14: The opponents flatter Jesus by saying he does not “look at the face” of people. Fluent renders the idiom as not showing favoritism while preserving the status issue.

v17: The verb carries the sense “give back/render.” Jesus’ saying does not spell out a complete theory of church and state, taxation, or political allegiance.

v16–17: Caesar’s coin bears Caesar’s image. The saying does not explicitly state what bears God’s image, though that scriptural resonance is available to hearers.

v24: Jesus locates the Sadducees’ error in both Scripture and divine power. The translation does not make resurrection simply an extension of present social arrangements.

v29–31: The Shema and love-of-neighbor command are distinct quotations joined into one answer about what is greatest.

v34: “Not far from the kingdom” is genuine affirmation but stops short of declaring that the scribe has entered it.

v35–37: Jesus complicates the category “son of David” rather than denying Davidic messianic hope.

v38–44: The poor widow scene directly follows Jesus’ condemnation of scribes who “devour widows’ houses.” The heading is kept neutral; Fluent does not force the episode to be only praise of sacrificial giving or only condemnation of an exploitative system.

v44: `bios` can mean life or livelihood. “Everything she had—all she had to live on” makes the economic force natural while retaining the totality of the phrase.
