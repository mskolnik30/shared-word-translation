## A Man Among the Tombs

<p>
v01: They reached the other side of the sea, in the region of the Gerasenes.
v02: As soon as Jesus stepped out of the boat, a man with an unclean spirit came from the tombs to meet him.
v03: The man lived among the tombs. No one could restrain him anymore, not even with a chain.
v04: He had often been bound with shackles and chains, but he had torn the chains apart and smashed the shackles. No one was strong enough to subdue him.
v05: Night and day, among the tombs and in the hills, he kept crying out and cutting himself with stones.
</p>

<p>
v06: When he saw Jesus from a distance, he ran and bowed down before him.
v07: He shouted at the top of his voice, “What do you want with me, Jesus, Son of the Most High God? I put you under oath by God: do not torment me!”
v08: For Jesus had been saying to him, “Come out of this man, you unclean spirit!”
v09: Jesus asked him, “What is your name?” He answered, “My name is Legion, because we are many.”
v10: He begged Jesus insistently not to send them out of the region.
</p>

<p>
v11: A large herd of pigs was feeding there on the hillside.
v12: The spirits begged him, “Send us into the pigs. Let us enter them.”
v13: He allowed them. The unclean spirits came out and entered the pigs, and the herd—about two thousand—rushed down the steep bank into the sea and drowned.
v14: The herders ran away and reported what had happened in the town and countryside. People came to see.
v15: They came to Jesus and saw the man who had been possessed by demons sitting there, clothed and in his right mind—the man who had had the legion. And they were afraid.
v16: Those who had seen it told them what had happened to the man possessed by demons and to the pigs.
v17: Then the people began begging Jesus to leave their territory.
</p>

<p>
v18: As Jesus was getting into the boat, the man who had been possessed by demons begged to be with him.
v19: Jesus did not let him. Instead, he said, “Go home to your own people and tell them how much the Lord has done for you and how he has shown you mercy.”
v20: The man went away and began proclaiming throughout the Decapolis how much Jesus had done for him. Everyone was amazed.
</p>

## A Woman Healed and a Girl Raised

<p>
v21: Jesus crossed back to the other side in the boat. A large crowd gathered around him, and he stayed beside the sea.
v22: One of the synagogue leaders, named Jairus, came. When he saw Jesus, he fell at his feet
v23: and begged him urgently, “My little daughter is near death. Please come and lay your hands on her, so that she may be healed and live.”
v24: Jesus went with him, and a large crowd followed, pressing in on him.
</p>

<p>
v25: A woman was there who had been bleeding for twelve years.
v26: She had suffered a great deal under many doctors and had spent everything she had. Yet she was no better; instead, she had grown worse.
v27: She had heard about Jesus, and she came up behind him in the crowd and touched his cloak.
v28: For she had been saying, “If I can touch even his clothes, I will be healed.”
v29: At once her bleeding stopped, and she felt in her body that she was healed of her affliction.
v30: At once Jesus knew within himself that power had gone out from him. He turned around in the crowd and asked, “Who touched my clothes?”
v31: His disciples said to him, “You can see the crowd pressing in on you, and you ask, ‘Who touched me?’”
v32: But he kept looking around to see who had done it.
v33: The woman knew what had happened to her. Afraid and trembling, she came, fell before him, and told him the whole truth.
v34: He said to her, “Daughter, your faith has made you well. Go in peace, and be free of your affliction.”
</p>

<p>
v35: While he was still speaking, people came from the synagogue leader’s house and said, “Your daughter has died. Why trouble the teacher any longer?”
v36: Jesus overheard what they were saying and told the synagogue leader, “Do not be afraid. Only believe.”
v37: He let no one follow him except Peter, James, and John, James’s brother.
v38: They came to the synagogue leader’s house, and Jesus saw the commotion: people weeping and wailing loudly.
v39: He went inside and said to them, “Why all this commotion and weeping? The child is not dead but sleeping.”
v40: They laughed at him. But he put them all outside, took the child’s father and mother and those with him, and went in where the child was.
v41: Taking the child by the hand, he said to her, “Talitha koum,” which means, “Little girl, I say to you, get up!”
v42: At once the girl got up and began walking—she was twelve years old. They were immediately overwhelmed with amazement.
v43: He gave them strict instructions that no one should know about this, and told them to give her something to eat.
</p>

## Notes

v09–10: The reply shifts from ‘my’ to ‘we,’ and the next sentence has one speaker pleading for ‘them.’ These shifts are retained.

v15: ‘Possessed by demons’ translates the narrator’s description. It is not a modern medical diagnosis.

v23, 28, 34: The same Greek verb can mean ‘save’ or ‘heal.’ The immediate setting concerns bodily healing; the wider sense of rescue is not excluded.

v36: The verb rendered ‘overheard’ can also mean ‘disregarded.’ Either reading has Jesus responding to the report of the girl’s death.

v41: ‘Talitha koum’ is an Aramaic expression retained in the Greek narrative, which then gives its meaning.

## Vocabulary

v02: **πνεῦμα ἀκάθαρτον (pneuma akatharton)**: Unclean spirit.

v09: **λεγιών (legiōn)**: Legion, a large Roman military unit; the name here is explained by ‘we are many.’

v20: **Δεκάπολις (Dekapolis)**: Decapolis, literally ‘Ten Cities,’ the name of a region.

v34: **σῴζω (sōzō)**: Save, rescue, or heal; here ‘has made you well.’

v36: **πιστεύω (pisteuō)**: Believe or trust; related to ‘faith’ in verse 34.
