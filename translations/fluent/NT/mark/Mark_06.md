## Jesus Is Rejected in His Hometown

<p>
v01: Jesus left there and came to his hometown, with his disciples following him.
v02: On the Sabbath he began teaching in the synagogue. Many who heard him were astonished. “Where did he get all this?” they asked. “What is this wisdom he has been given? How are miracles like these being done through his hands?
v03: Isn’t this the carpenter, Mary’s son, and the brother of James, Joses, Judas, and Simon? Aren’t his sisters here with us?” And they took offense at him.
v04: Jesus told them, “A prophet is not without honor except in his hometown, among his relatives, and in his own household.”
v05: He could do no miracle there, except that he laid his hands on a few sick people and healed them.
v06: He was amazed at their unbelief. Then he went around the surrounding villages, teaching.
</p>

## The Twelve Are Sent Out

<p>
v07: He called the Twelve to him and began sending them out two by two, giving them authority over unclean spirits.
v08: He told them to take nothing for the journey except a staff—no bread, no bag, no money in their belts.
v09: They were to wear sandals. “But do not wear two tunics,” he said.
v10: He told them, “Whenever you enter a house, stay there until you leave that place.
v11: If any place will not welcome you and its people will not listen to you, leave it and shake the dust off your feet as a testimony to them.”
v12: They went out and proclaimed that people should repent.
v13: They drove out many demons and anointed many sick people with oil and healed them.
</p>

## Herod and John the Baptist

<p>
v14: King Herod heard about Jesus, whose name had become well known. People were saying, “John the Baptist has been raised from the dead. That is why miraculous powers are at work in him.”
v15: Others said, “He is Elijah.” Still others said, “He is a prophet, like one of the prophets.”
v16: But when Herod heard this, he said, “John, whom I beheaded, has been raised!”
v17: For Herod himself had sent men to arrest John and bind him in prison because of Herodias, his brother Philip’s wife, whom Herod had married.
v18: John had been telling Herod, “It is not lawful for you to have your brother’s wife.”
v19: Herodias held a grudge against John and wanted to kill him, but she could not.
v20: For Herod feared John, knowing that he was a righteous and holy man, and he kept him safe. When he listened to John, he was deeply puzzled, yet he liked hearing him.
</p>

<p>
v21: Then an opportunity came. On his birthday, Herod gave a banquet for his high officials, military commanders, and the leading men of Galilee.
v22: Herodias’s own daughter came in and danced, pleasing Herod and his dinner guests. The king said to the girl, “Ask me for whatever you want, and I will give it to you.”
v23: He swore to her, “Whatever you ask me for, I will give you—even half my kingdom.”
v24: She went out and asked her mother, “What should I ask for?” Her mother said, “The head of John the Baptist.”
v25: At once she hurried back to the king and made her request: “I want you to give me the head of John the Baptist on a platter, right now.”
v26: The king was deeply grieved. But because of his oaths and his dinner guests, he did not want to refuse her.
v27: At once the king sent a guard with orders to bring John’s head. The guard went and beheaded him in prison.
v28: He brought the head on a platter and gave it to the girl, and the girl gave it to her mother.
v29: When John’s disciples heard, they came, took his body, and laid it in a tomb.
</p>

## The Apostles Return

<p>
v30: The apostles gathered around Jesus and told him everything they had done and taught.
v31: He said to them, “Come away by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a little.” So many people were coming and going that they had no chance even to eat.
v32: So they went away by themselves in the boat to a deserted place.
v33: But many saw them leaving and recognized them. People ran there on foot from all the towns and arrived ahead of them.
v34: When Jesus stepped ashore, he saw a large crowd and felt compassion for them, because they were like sheep without a shepherd. He began teaching them many things.
</p>

## Jesus Feeds Five Thousand

<p>
v35: By then it was late. His disciples came to him and said, “This is a deserted place, and it is already late.
v36: Send them away so they can go to the surrounding farms and villages and buy themselves something to eat.”
v37: But he answered, “You give them something to eat.” They said to him, “Are we to go and buy two hundred denarii worth of bread and give them something to eat?”
v38: He asked them, “How many loaves do you have? Go and see.” When they found out, they said, “Five, and two fish.”
v39: He told the disciples to have everyone sit down in groups on the green grass.
v40: They sat down in groups of a hundred and of fifty.
v41: Jesus took the five loaves and the two fish, looked up to heaven, and spoke a blessing. He broke the loaves and kept giving them to the disciples to set before the people. He also divided the two fish among them all.
v42: They all ate and had enough.
v43: They gathered twelve basketfuls of broken pieces, as well as what was left of the fish.
v44: The men who ate the bread numbered five thousand.
</p>

## Jesus Walks on the Sea

<p>
v45: At once Jesus made his disciples get into the boat and go ahead of him to the other side, toward Bethsaida, while he dismissed the crowd.
v46: After saying goodbye to them, he went up the mountain to pray.
v47: When evening came, the boat was out in the middle of the sea, and he was alone on land.
v48: He saw them struggling at the oars, because the wind was against them. Around the fourth watch of the night, he came toward them, walking on the sea. He intended to pass by them.
v49: When they saw him walking on the sea, they thought he was a ghost and cried out,
v50: because they all saw him and were terrified. At once he spoke to them: “Take courage! It is I. Do not be afraid.”
v51: He got into the boat with them, and the wind died down. They were utterly astonished,
v52: for they had not understood about the loaves; their hearts were hardened.
</p>

## Healing at Gennesaret

<p>
v53: After crossing over, they reached land at Gennesaret and moored the boat.
v54: As soon as they got out of the boat, people recognized Jesus.
v55: They ran all around that region and began carrying the sick on mats to wherever they heard he was.
v56: Wherever he went—villages, towns, or farms—they laid the sick in the marketplaces and begged him to let them touch even the fringe of his cloak. Everyone who touched him was healed.
</p>

## Notes

v05: The Greek says that he ‘could not,’ not merely that he chose not to. It then names the healings that did occur.

v08–09: Mark explicitly permits a staff and sandals. These instructions are not adjusted to match the other Gospel accounts.

v11: ‘A testimony to them’ can carry the force of testimony against them in this setting.

v22: The Greek wording followed here identifies the girl as Herodias’s own daughter; it does not give the girl a personal name.

v48: The fourth watch was the last part of the night, roughly three to six in the morning. ‘He intended to pass by them’ is retained without explaining his intention.

v50: ‘It is I’ translates egō eimi, literally ‘I am.’ It can serve as ordinary self-identification; possible scriptural echoes are left open.

v56: The final pronoun may refer to Jesus or to his garment or its fringe. The preceding request specifically concerns touching the fringe.

## Vocabulary

v03: **τέκτων (tektōn)**: Carpenter, builder, or skilled craftworker.

v09: **χιτών (chitōn)**: Tunic, a basic garment worn beneath an outer cloak.

v30: **ἀπόστολος (apostolos)**: Apostle or one sent out; here the returning Twelve.

v37: **δηνάριον (dēnarion)**: Denarius, a Roman coin; two hundred are named here.

v52: **πωρόω (pōroō)**: Harden or make unresponsive; the Greek describes their heart as hardened.
