## Jesus Is Rejected in His Hometown

<p>
v01: Jesus left there and came to his hometown, and his disciples followed him.
v02: When the Sabbath came, he began to teach in the synagogue. Many who heard him were astonished and said, “Where did this man get these things? What is this wisdom that has been given to him? What are these works of power being done through his hands?
v03: Is this not the carpenter, the son of Mary and the brother of James, Joses, Judas, and Simon? And are not his sisters here with us?” And they took offense at him.
v04: Jesus said to them, “A prophet is not without honor except in his hometown, among his relatives, and in his own house.”
v05: He could do no work of power there except to lay his hands on a few sick people and heal them.
v06: And he was amazed at their unbelief. Then he went around the surrounding villages teaching.
</p>

## The Twelve Are Sent Out

<p>
v07: Jesus called the twelve and began sending them out two by two, giving them authority over unclean spirits.
v08: He instructed them to take nothing for the journey except a staff—no bread, no bag, no money in their belts—
v09: but to wear sandals and not put on two tunics.
v10: He said to them, “Whenever you enter a house, stay there until you leave that place.
v11: If any place will not welcome you or listen to you, leave and shake the dust off your feet as a testimony against them.”
v12: They went out and proclaimed that people should repent.
v13: They cast out many demons and anointed many sick people with oil and healed them.
</p>

## Herod and John the Baptist

<p>
v14: King Herod heard about Jesus, because his name had become well known. Some were saying, “John the Baptist has been raised from the dead, and that is why these powers are at work in him.”
v15: Others said, “He is Elijah.” Still others said, “He is a prophet, like one of the prophets of old.”
v16: But when Herod heard, he said, “John, whom I beheaded, has been raised.”
v17: Herod himself had sent men to arrest John and bind him in prison because of Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip, whom Herod had married.
v18: John had been telling Herod, “It is not lawful for you to have your brother’s wife.”
v19: So Herodias held a grudge against John and wanted to kill him, but she could not,
v20: because Herod feared John, knowing he was a righteous and holy man, and protected him. When Herod listened to him, he was greatly perplexed, yet he still liked to listen.
</p>

<p>
v21: An opportunity came on Herod’s birthday, when he gave a banquet for his officials, military commanders, and the leading people of Galilee.
v22: Herodias’s own daughter came in and danced, and she pleased Herod and his guests. The king said to the girl, “Ask me for whatever you want, and I will give it to you.”
v23: He swore to her, “Whatever you ask me, I will give you, up to half my kingdom.”
v24: She went out and asked her mother, “What should I ask for?” Her mother said, “The head of John the Baptist.”
v25: The girl hurried back at once to the king and said, “I want you to give me the head of John the Baptist on a platter right now.”
v26: The king became deeply grieved, but because of his oaths and his dinner guests, he did not want to refuse her.
v27: Immediately the king sent an executioner with orders to bring John’s head. The man went and beheaded John in the prison,
v28: brought his head on a platter, and gave it to the girl. The girl gave it to her mother.
v29: When John’s disciples heard, they came, took his body, and laid it in a tomb.
</p>

## The Apostles Return

<p>
v30: The apostles gathered around Jesus and reported everything they had done and taught.
v31: He said to them, “Come away by yourselves to a deserted place and rest for a while.” So many people were coming and going that they did not even have time to eat.
v32: They went away in the boat to a deserted place by themselves.
v33: But many people saw them leaving and recognized them. They ran there on foot from all the towns and arrived ahead of them.
v34: When Jesus came ashore and saw a large crowd, he had compassion on them because they were like sheep without a shepherd. So he began teaching them many things.
</p>

## Jesus Feeds Five Thousand

<p>
v35: When it was already late, his disciples came to him and said, “This is a deserted place, and it is already very late.
v36: Send the people away so they can go into the surrounding countryside and villages and buy themselves something to eat.”
v37: Jesus answered, “You give them something to eat.” They said, “Are we supposed to go and buy two hundred denarii worth of bread and give it to them?”
v38: He asked, “How many loaves do you have? Go and see.” When they found out, they said, “Five—and two fish.”
v39: He told them to have everyone sit down in groups on the green grass.
v40: They sat down in groups of hundreds and fifties.
v41: Jesus took the five loaves and two fish, looked up to heaven, blessed them, broke the loaves, and kept giving them to the disciples to set before the people. He also divided the two fish among everyone.
v42: They all ate and were satisfied.
v43: The disciples picked up twelve baskets full of broken pieces and fish.
v44: Those who ate the loaves numbered five thousand men.
</p>

## Jesus Walks on the Sea

<p>
v45: Immediately Jesus made his disciples get into the boat and go ahead of him toward Bethsaida on the other side while he dismissed the crowd.
v46: After saying goodbye to them, he went up on the mountain to pray.
v47: When evening came, the boat was in the middle of the sea, and he was alone on land.
v48: He saw them struggling at the oars because the wind was against them. Around the fourth watch of the night, he came toward them walking on the sea, and he intended to pass by them.
v49: When they saw him walking on the sea, they thought he was a ghost and cried out,
v50: because they all saw him and were terrified. Immediately he spoke to them: “Take courage. I am. Do not be afraid.”
v51: He got into the boat with them, and the wind stopped. They were utterly astonished,
v52: because they had not understood about the loaves; instead, their hearts were hardened.
</p>

## Healing at Gennesaret

<p>
v53: After crossing over, they came to land at Gennesaret and moored the boat.
v54: As soon as they got out of the boat, people recognized Jesus.
v55: They ran throughout that whole region and began carrying the sick on mats to wherever they heard he was.
v56: Wherever he entered—villages, cities, or the countryside—they laid the sick in the marketplaces and begged him to let them touch even the fringe of his cloak. Everyone who touched it was made well.
</p>

## Notes

v03: “The carpenter” keeps Jesus within an ordinary craft and village identity. “Son of Mary” is striking but is not expanded into speculation about why maternal identification is used.

v05–06: Mark places Jesus’ inability to do many works of power beside the hometown’s unbelief without giving a philosophical explanation of their relation.

v06: The second sentence is part of public verse 6. Fluent keeps it under `v06` rather than leaving an unnumbered line between verses.

v07–13: The mission of the twelve joins proclamation, repentance, authority over unclean spirits, healing, and material dependence.

v20: Herod’s response to John is internally divided: fear, protection, perplexity, and willing listening coexist.

v22: SBLGNT’s wording supports “Herodias’s own daughter.” Textual history around this phrase is complicated; Fluent follows the pinned source rather than the current TSW wording.

v30: Mark calls the returning twelve “apostles,” meaning those who have been sent. The title is tied directly to their mission and report.

v31–34: Jesus’ invitation to rest is interrupted by the crowd. Mark preserves both human need for rest and compassion that responds to need.

v41: The sequence take–bless–break–give is preserved without importing later sacramental interpretation into the narrative itself.

v48: “He intended to pass by them” is deliberately retained. The phrase may echo scriptural scenes of divine self-disclosure, but the narrative does not explain it.

v50: `egō eimi` can function as ordinary reassurance (“It is I”) while also carrying deeper scriptural resonance. Fluent retains the compact “I am” and leaves both levels available.

v52: The failure to understand the loaves is explicitly connected to hardened hearts. This bread/perception theme continues into chapter 8.

v56: The Greek uses `sōzō`, the same broad save/heal/restore verb heard elsewhere in Mark. “Made well” is natural in this plural healing summary while the lexical link is recorded here.
