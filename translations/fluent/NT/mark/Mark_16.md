## The Empty Tomb

<p>
v01: When the Sabbath was over, Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James, and Salome bought spices so they could go and anoint Jesus.
v02: Very early on the first day of the week, just after sunrise, they came to the tomb.
v03: They were saying to one another, “Who will roll the stone away from the entrance of the tomb for us?”
v04: But when they looked up, they saw that the stone had been rolled away, even though it was extremely large.
v05: They entered the tomb and saw a young man sitting on the right, dressed in a white robe, and they were alarmed.
v06: He said to them, “Do not be alarmed. You are looking for Jesus the Nazarene, who was crucified. He has been raised; he is not here. Look at the place where they laid him.
v07: But go, tell his disciples and Peter: ‘He is going ahead of you into Galilee. You will see him there, just as he told you.’”
v08: They went out and fled from the tomb, for trembling and astonishment had seized them. They said nothing to anyone, because they were afraid.
</p>

## The Longer Ending of Mark — Ancient Textual Tradition

<p>
v09: After Jesus rose early on the first day of the week, he appeared first to Mary Magdalene, from whom he had driven out seven demons.
v10: She went and reported it to those who had been with him, while they were mourning and weeping.
v11: When they heard that Jesus was alive and had been seen by her, they did not believe.
v12: After this, he appeared in a different form to two of them as they walked on their way into the countryside.
v13: They went back and reported it to the rest, but the others did not believe them either.
v14: Later he appeared to the Eleven as they reclined at the table. He rebuked their unbelief and hardness of heart because they had not believed those who saw him after he was raised.
v15: He said to them, “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to all creation.
v16: Whoever believes and is baptized will be saved, but whoever does not believe will be condemned.
v17: These signs will accompany those who believe: in my name they will drive out demons; they will speak in new tongues;
v18: they will pick up snakes, and if they drink anything deadly, it will certainly not harm them; they will lay hands on the sick, and the sick will become well.”
v19: After the Lord Jesus had spoken to them, he was taken up into heaven and sat at God’s right hand.
v20: They went out and proclaimed everywhere, while the Lord worked with them and confirmed the word through the signs that accompanied it.
</p>

## Notes

v08: The continuous main text followed here ends with trembling, astonishment, fear, and silence. The exact source record also preserves a bracketed shorter ending: ‘They briefly reported everything they had been told to those around Peter. Afterward Jesus himself sent out through them, from east to west, the sacred and imperishable proclamation of eternal salvation. Amen.’ This shorter ending is an alternative ancient textual tradition, not a continuation treated as identical to verses 1–8.

v09–20: These verses are preserved in the source inside brackets as the longer ending. They are absent from important early witnesses and differ in style from 1:1–16:8, yet they are ancient and influential. The separate heading distinguishes their textual status without suppressing them.

v16: Belief and baptism appear together in the positive statement; the negative statement names unbelief as the ground of condemnation.

v17–18: The signs belong to the longer ending’s textual tradition. These statements are not commands to handle snakes, ingest poison, or test God.

v19–20: The longer ending closes with Jesus’ exaltation, worldwide proclamation, the Lord’s continuing work, and confirming signs.

## Vocabulary

v05: **νεανίσκος (neaniskos)**: Young man; Mark does not call the figure an angel here.

v06: **ἐγείρω (egeirō)**: Raise; here passive, ‘he has been raised.’

v08: **ἔκστασις (ekstasis)**: Astonishment, amazement, or displacement from ordinary composure.

v14: **σκληροκαρδία (sklērokardia)**: Hardness of heart.

v15: **κτίσις (ktisis)**: Creation or created order.

v19: **ἀναλαμβάνω (analambanō)**: Take up; here used of Jesus being taken into heaven.
