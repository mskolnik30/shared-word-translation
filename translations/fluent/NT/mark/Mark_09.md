## Jesus Is Transfigured

<p>
v01: He went on to say, “Truly I tell you, some standing here will not taste death before they see that the kingdom of God has come in power.”
v02: Six days later Jesus took Peter, James, and John up a high mountain, where they were alone. There, before their eyes, he was transformed.
v03: His clothes shone an intense white, whiter than any launderer on earth could make them.
v04: Elijah appeared to them with Moses, and the two were talking with Jesus.
v05: Peter said to Jesus, “Rabbi, it is good for us to be here. Let us put up three shelters: one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah.”
v06: He did not know what to say, for they were terrified.
v07: A cloud came over them, and a voice spoke from the cloud: “This is my beloved Son. Listen to him!”
v08: Suddenly they looked around and saw no one with them but Jesus.
</p>

## Elijah and the Son of Man

<p>
v09: As they came down the mountain, Jesus ordered them to tell no one what they had seen until the Son of Man had risen from the dead.
v10: They kept the matter to themselves, discussing what “rising from the dead” meant.
v11: They asked him, “Why do the scribes say that Elijah must come first?”
v12: He replied, “Elijah does come first to restore everything. Yet how is it written about the Son of Man that he must suffer greatly and be treated with contempt?
v13: But I tell you, Elijah has already come, and they did whatever they wanted to him, just as it is written about him.”
</p>

## “I Believe! Help My Unbelief!”

<p>
v14: When they reached the other disciples, they saw a large crowd around them and scribes arguing with them.
v15: As soon as the crowd saw Jesus, everyone was astonished and ran to greet him.
v16: He asked them, “What are you arguing with them about?”
v17: Someone in the crowd answered, “Teacher, I brought you my son. He has a spirit that keeps him from speaking.
v18: Whenever it seizes him, it throws him down. He foams at the mouth, grinds his teeth, and becomes rigid. I asked your disciples to drive it out, but they could not.”
v19: Jesus answered them, “You faithless generation! How long must I stay with you? How long must I put up with you? Bring him to me.”
v20: They brought the boy to him. When the spirit saw Jesus, it immediately threw the boy into convulsions. He fell to the ground and rolled about, foaming at the mouth.
v21: Jesus asked his father, “How long has this been happening to him?” “Since childhood,” he answered.
v22: “Often it has thrown him into fire and water to destroy him. But if you can do anything, have compassion on us and help us!”
v23: Jesus said to him, “‘If you can’? Everything is possible for the one who believes.”
v24: At once the boy's father cried out, “I believe! Help my unbelief!”
v25: When Jesus saw a crowd rushing toward them, he rebuked the unclean spirit: “Spirit that keeps him from speaking and hearing, I command you: come out of him and never enter him again!”
v26: The spirit cried out, convulsed the boy violently, and came out. The boy lay like a corpse, so that most of the people said, “He is dead.”
v27: But Jesus took him by the hand and raised him up, and he stood.
v28: After Jesus had gone indoors, his disciples asked him privately, “Why could we not drive it out?”
v29: He told them, “This kind can come out only through prayer.”
</p>

## The Son of Man Will Be Handed Over

<p>
v30: They left that place and traveled through Galilee. Jesus did not want anyone to know,
v31: for he was teaching his disciples. He told them, “The Son of Man is being handed over to human hands. They will kill him, and after he is killed, he will rise three days later.”
v32: They did not understand what he meant and were afraid to ask him.
</p>

## Who Is Greatest?

<p>
v33: They came to Capernaum. Once inside the house, Jesus asked them, “What were you discussing on the road?”
v34: They said nothing, because on the road they had been arguing about which of them was greatest.
v35: Jesus sat down, called the Twelve, and said, “Anyone who wants to be first must be last of all and servant of all.”
v36: He took a child and stood the child among them. Then, holding the child in his arms, he said,
v37: “Whoever welcomes one child like this in my name welcomes me. And whoever welcomes me welcomes not only me but the one who sent me.”
</p>

## Belonging and Stumbling

<p>
v38: John said to him, “Teacher, we saw someone driving out demons in your name. We tried to stop him because he was not following us.”
v39: “Do not stop him,” Jesus said. “No one who does a deed of power in my name can turn around and speak evil of me.
v40: For whoever is not against us is for us.
v41: Truly I tell you, anyone who gives you a cup of water because you belong to the Messiah will certainly not lose their reward.”
v42: “If anyone causes one of these little ones who believe in me to stumble, it would be better for that person to have a great millstone hung around their neck and be thrown into the sea.
v43: If your hand causes you to stumble, cut it off. It is better to enter life with one hand than to have both hands and go into Gehenna, into the fire that cannot be put out.
v45: If your foot causes you to stumble, cut it off. It is better to enter life lame than to have both feet and be thrown into Gehenna.
v47: If your eye causes you to stumble, throw it away. It is better to enter the kingdom of God with one eye than to have two eyes and be thrown into Gehenna,
v48: where their worm does not die and the fire is not put out.
v49: For everyone will be salted with fire.
v50: Salt is good, but if salt loses its saltiness, how will you season it? Have salt in yourselves, and live at peace with one another.”
</p>

## Notes

v01: ‘Has come in power’ describes the kingdom as having arrived when it is seen. The saying does not identify a single event as its fulfillment.

v12–13: The question about the Son of Man's suffering stands alongside Elijah's restoration. The text does not explicitly name Elijah's counterpart here.

v23: The compact Greek is punctuated here as Jesus echoing ‘if you can.’ Its wording connects the father's question about ability with the possibility of believing.

v29: The Greek text followed here has ‘prayer,’ without the addition ‘and fasting’ found in another reading.

v42: The millstone is literally one turned by a donkey, emphasizing its size.

v43–48: These severe bodily images warn against causing people to stumble. Verses 44 and 46 are absent from the Greek text followed here; the numbering gaps are retained.

v49–50: ‘Salted with fire’ and ‘have salt in yourselves’ preserve difficult images. Their meaning is not reduced to a single explanation in the translation.

## Vocabulary

v02: **μεταμορφόω (metamorphoō)**: Transform; here the change witnessed on the mountain, often called the transfiguration.

v05: **σκηνή (skēnē)**: Tent or shelter.

v23–24: **πιστεύω / ἀπιστία (pisteuō / apistia)**: Believe or trust / unbelief. The father's reply holds these related words together.

v31: **παραδίδωμι (paradidōmi)**: Hand over; the passive leaves the agent unstated here.

v42: **σκανδαλίζω (skandalizō)**: Cause to stumble or fall; repeated in the warnings that follow.

v43: **γέεννα (Gehenna)**: Gehenna, a name used here for the place of fiery judgment.
