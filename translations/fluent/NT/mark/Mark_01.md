## The Beginning of the Good News

<p>
v01: The beginning of the good news of Jesus Christ.
v02: As it is written in Isaiah the prophet:
“Look, I am sending my messenger ahead of you,
who will prepare your way.”
v03: “A voice cries out in the wilderness:
‘Prepare the Lord’s way;
make his paths straight.’”
</p>

## John in the Wilderness

<p>
v04: John the baptizer appeared in the wilderness, proclaiming a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins.
v05: People from all Judea and all Jerusalem went out to him. Confessing their sins, they were baptized by him in the Jordan River.
v06: John wore camel-hair clothing with a leather belt around his waist, and he ate locusts and wild honey.
v07: He proclaimed, “Someone stronger than I am is coming after me. I am not worthy to stoop down and untie his sandal straps.
v08: I have baptized you with water, but he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit.”
</p>

## Jesus Is Baptized and Tested

<p>
v09: In those days Jesus came from Nazareth in Galilee and was baptized by John in the Jordan.
v10: As he came up out of the water, he immediately saw the heavens being torn open and the Spirit descending on him like a dove.
v11: A voice came from heaven: “You are my beloved Son; I am well pleased with you.”
v12: At once the Spirit drove him into the wilderness.
v13: He was in the wilderness forty days, tempted by Satan. He was with the wild animals, and the angels served him.
</p>

## Jesus Begins to Proclaim

<p>
v14: After John was handed over, Jesus came into Galilee proclaiming God’s good news.
v15: “The time is fulfilled,” he said. “The kingdom of God has drawn near. Repent and believe the good news.”
</p>

## Jesus Calls His First Disciples

<p>
v16: As he walked beside the Sea of Galilee, he saw Simon and Simon’s brother Andrew casting a net into the sea; they were fishermen.
v17: Jesus said to them, “Come, follow me, and I will make you fishers of people.”
v18: At once they left their nets and followed him.
v19: A little farther on, he saw James son of Zebedee and his brother John in their boat, putting their nets in order.
v20: He called them at once. They left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired workers and went after him.
</p>

## Authority in Capernaum

<p>
v21: They went into Capernaum. On the Sabbath he immediately began teaching in the synagogue.
v22: The people were astonished at his teaching, for he taught them with authority, unlike the scribes.
v23: Just then a man with an unclean spirit was in their synagogue. He cried out,
v24: “What have you to do with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are—the Holy One of God!”
v25: Jesus rebuked the spirit: “Be silent and come out of him!”
v26: The unclean spirit convulsed the man and came out of him with a loud cry.
v27: Everyone was amazed. They asked one another, “What is this? A new teaching with authority! He even commands unclean spirits, and they obey him.”
v28: News of him immediately spread everywhere throughout the surrounding region of Galilee.
</p>

## Healing and Prayer

<p>
v29: As soon as they left the synagogue, they went to Simon and Andrew’s house with James and John.
v30: Simon’s mother-in-law was lying down with a fever. They told Jesus about her at once.
v31: He went to her, took her hand, and raised her up. The fever left her, and she began serving them.
</p>

<p>
v32: That evening, after sunset, they brought him everyone who was ill or afflicted by demons.
v33: The whole town gathered at the door.
v34: He healed many with various diseases and drove out many demons. He would not let the demons speak, because they knew him.
</p>

<p>
v35: Very early, while it was still dark, he got up and went out to a deserted place. There he prayed.
v36: Simon and his companions went looking for him.
v37: When they found him, they said, “Everyone is looking for you.”
v38: He answered, “Let us go elsewhere, to the nearby towns, so I can proclaim the message there too. That is why I came out.”
v39: He went throughout Galilee, proclaiming the message in their synagogues and driving out demons.
</p>

## A Man Is Made Clean

<p>
v40: A man with leprosy came to him, knelt, and pleaded, “If you are willing, you can make me clean.”
v41: Moved to anger, Jesus reached out his hand and touched him. “I am willing,” he said. “Be clean.”
v42: At once the leprosy left him, and he was made clean.
v43: Jesus sternly warned him and immediately sent him away.
v44: “See that you tell no one anything,” he said. “Instead, go, show yourself to the priest, and offer what Moses commanded for your cleansing, as a testimony to them.”
v45: But the man went out and began telling the news widely. As a result, Jesus could no longer enter a town openly. He stayed outside in deserted places, but people kept coming to him from everywhere.
</p>

## Notes

v01: The pinned Greek does not include “Son of God” here. Many manuscripts include that phrase; the title appears explicitly elsewhere in Mark.

v02–03: The quotation combines language associated with Exodus 23:20, Malachi 3:1, and Isaiah 40:3. Mark introduces it under Isaiah’s name.

v10: “Torn open” uses a verb also used for the sanctuary curtain in 15:38.

v21: The pinned wording says Jesus taught in the synagogue. Some manuscripts also state explicitly that he entered it.

v38: “I came out” can refer to leaving a place or, more broadly, to the purpose of Jesus’ mission. The expression is left open.

v41: The pinned Greek reads “moved to anger.” Many manuscripts read “moved with compassion.” The text does not explicitly identify the object of his anger.

v44: “As a testimony to them” may carry a confirming or challenging force. The recipients are not more precisely identified here.

## Vocabulary

v01: **Good news**: Greek euangelion: a message of good news.

v04: **Repentance**: Greek metanoia: a change of mind or direction, here in response to God.

v12: **Drove**: The forceful verb ekballō is also used in this chapter for driving out demons.

v13: **Tempted**: The Greek can also mean tested or put to the test.

v14: **Handed over**: Greek paradidōmi: delivered into another’s custody; the wording recurs in Jesus’ arrest and death.

v15: **Kingdom**: God’s kingdom, reign, or rule.

v22: **Scribes**: Specialists in written texts and their interpretation, especially the law.

v40: **Leprosy**: An ancient category of serious skin conditions, not necessarily modern Hansen’s disease.

v40: **Clean**: Cleansing language involves ritual status as well as the bodily condition in this scene.
