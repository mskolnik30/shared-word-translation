## The Beginning of the Good News

<p>
v01: The beginning of the good news of Jesus Christ.
v02: As it is written in Isaiah the prophet:
“Look, I am sending my messenger ahead of you,
who will prepare your way;
v03: a voice of one crying in the wilderness,
‘Prepare the way of the Lord;
make his paths straight.’”
</p>

## John in the Wilderness

<p>
v04: John appeared in the wilderness, baptizing and proclaiming a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins.
v05: People from all across Judea and all the people of Jerusalem were going out to him. They were baptized by him in the Jordan River, confessing their sins.
v06: John wore clothing made of camel’s hair with a leather belt around his waist, and he ate locusts and wild honey.
v07: He proclaimed, “After me comes one stronger than I am. I am not worthy to stoop down and untie the strap of his sandals.
v08: I baptized you with water, but he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit.”
</p>

## Jesus Is Baptized and Tested

<p>
v09: In those days Jesus came from Nazareth in Galilee and was baptized by John in the Jordan.
v10: As soon as he came up out of the water, he saw the heavens being torn open and the Spirit descending on him like a dove.
v11: And a voice came from heaven: “You are my beloved Son; with you I am well pleased.”
v12: Immediately the Spirit drove him out into the wilderness.
v13: He was in the wilderness forty days, being tested by Satan. He was with the wild animals, and the angels were serving him.
</p>

## Jesus Begins to Proclaim

<p>
v14: After John was handed over, Jesus came into Galilee proclaiming the good news of God.
v15: He said, “The time has been fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has drawn near. Repent and trust the good news.”
</p>

## Jesus Calls His First Disciples

<p>
v16: As Jesus passed along the Sea of Galilee, he saw Simon and Simon’s brother Andrew casting a net into the sea, because they were fishermen.
v17: Jesus said to them, “Come after me, and I will make you become fishers of people.”
v18: Immediately they left their nets and followed him.
v19: Going a little farther, he saw James son of Zebedee and his brother John in their boat, mending the nets.
v20: Immediately he called them. They left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired workers and went after him.
</p>

## Authority in Capernaum

<p>
v21: They went into Capernaum, and on the Sabbath Jesus entered the synagogue and began to teach.
v22: The people were astonished at his teaching, because he taught them as one who had authority, and not as the scribes.
v23: Just then, a man in their synagogue who had an unclean spirit cried out,
v24: “What do you want with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are—the Holy One of God!”
v25: Jesus rebuked the spirit: “Be silent and come out of him!”
v26: The unclean spirit convulsed the man, cried out with a loud voice, and came out of him.
v27: Everyone was amazed, and they began asking one another, “What is this? A new teaching—with authority! He even commands the unclean spirits, and they obey him.”
v28: News about him spread at once throughout the surrounding region of Galilee.
</p>

## Healing and Prayer

<p>
v29: As soon as they left the synagogue, they went with James and John to the home of Simon and Andrew.
v30: Simon’s mother-in-law was in bed with a fever, and they immediately told Jesus about her.
v31: He went to her, took her by the hand, and raised her up. The fever left her, and she began to serve them.
</p>

<p>
v32: That evening, after the sun had set, people brought to him everyone who was sick or oppressed by demons.
v33: The whole city gathered at the door.
v34: He healed many who were sick with various diseases and cast out many demons. He would not allow the demons to speak, because they knew him.
</p>

<p>
v35: Very early in the morning, while it was still dark, Jesus got up, went out to a deserted place, and prayed there.
v36: Simon and those with him went looking for him.
v37: When they found him, they said, “Everyone is looking for you.”
v38: He said to them, “Let us go somewhere else, to the nearby towns, so that I may proclaim there too. This is why I came out.”
v39: So he went throughout Galilee, proclaiming in their synagogues and casting out demons.
</p>

## A Man Is Made Clean

<p>
v40: A man with a skin disease came to him, pleading with him and kneeling. He said, “If you are willing, you can make me clean.”
v41: Moved with anger, Jesus reached out his hand, touched him, and said, “I am willing. Be made clean.”
v42: Immediately the disease left him, and he was made clean.
v43: Jesus sternly warned him and sent him away at once.
v44: He said, “See that you say nothing to anyone. Go, show yourself to the priest and offer for your cleansing what Moses commanded, as a testimony to them.”
v45: But the man went out and began proclaiming the matter freely and spreading the news. As a result, Jesus could no longer enter a town openly but stayed outside in deserted places. Yet people kept coming to him from everywhere.
</p>

## Notes

v01: SBLGNT does not include “Son of God” in its main text at Mark 1:1. The phrase is an important and ancient textual variant and is recorded in the source apparatus. Fluent follows the pinned SBLGNT main text here rather than silently inheriting the TSW wording.

v02–03: The citation combines scriptural language associated with Exodus/Malachi and Isaiah while introducing it under the name of Isaiah. The translation retains Mark’s wording rather than harmonizing the sources.

v10: “Torn open” preserves the force of `schizō`, an image that will echo when the temple curtain is torn at Jesus’ death.

v12: The Spirit “drives” Jesus into the wilderness. The strong verb is retained rather than softened to “led.”

v15: “Kingdom of God” retains the familiar biblical phrase while the note recognizes the sense of God’s reign or rule drawing near.

v18–20: Mark’s repeated “immediately” contributes to narrative urgency. Fluent preserves the word at structurally significant moments without reproducing every connective mechanically.

v22–27: Authority is not explained abstractly; it becomes visible in teaching and command over an unclean spirit.

v31: “Raised her up” uses a verb that can also be used for resurrection. The ordinary healing sense belongs fully to this scene while the lexical resonance remains available in Mark’s larger narrative.

v38: “This is why I came out” can be heard in relation to leaving the place of prayer or more broadly to Jesus’ mission of proclamation. Fluent leaves the compact wording open.

v40: The traditional English “leprosy” can suggest modern Hansen’s disease too narrowly. The Greek `lepra` covers a broader category of serious skin conditions associated with ritual and social exclusion.

v41: SBLGNT reads `orgistheis`, “moved with anger.” A well-known textual variant reads “moved with compassion,” which is the reading currently reflected in TSW and many translations. Fluent follows the pinned SBLGNT text and records the alternative explicitly rather than concealing the textual decision.

v44: Cleansing includes a public, Torah-shaped return to communal life; the translation does not turn the verse into a rejection of the law.

## Vocabulary

v01: εὐαγγέλιον (euangelion)
“Good news,” public announcement of God’s saving action and reign.

v04: μετάνοια (metanoia)
“Repentance,” turning or reorientation.

v04: ἄφεσις (aphesis)
“Forgiveness, release.”

v10: σχίζω (schizō)
“To tear open, split.”

v12: ἐκβάλλω (ekballō)
“To drive out, cast out.”

v14: παραδίδωμι (paradidōmi)
“To hand over.” A major Markan verb that becomes increasingly important in the passion narrative.

v15: βασιλεία (basileia)
“Kingdom, reign, rule.”

v15: πιστεύω (pisteuō)
“To trust, believe.”

v18: εὐθύς (euthys)
“Immediately, at once.” A characteristic Markan narrative word.

v22: ἐξουσία (exousia)
“Authority, right or power to act.”

v23: πνεῦμα ἀκάθαρτον (pneuma akatharton)
“Unclean spirit.”

v35: ἔρημος (erēmos)
“Wilderness, deserted place.”

v40–42: καθαρίζω (katharizō)
“To cleanse, make clean.”

v41: ὀργίζομαι (orgizomai)
“To be angry.” SBLGNT’s reading in this verse; “moved with compassion” reflects an important textual variant.
