## Jesus Before Pilate

<p>
v01: Very early in the morning, the chief priests, elders, and scribes, together with the whole council, reached a decision. They bound Jesus, led him away, and handed him over to Pilate.
v02: Pilate asked him, “Are you the King of the Jews?” Jesus answered him, “You say so.”
v03: The chief priests brought many accusations against him.
v04: Pilate questioned him again: “Do you have no answer? Look how many accusations they are bringing against you.”
v05: But Jesus gave no further answer, so Pilate was amazed.
</p>

## Jesus Is Sentenced to Crucifixion

<p>
v06: At each festival, Pilate used to release for them one prisoner they requested.
v07: A man called Barabbas was imprisoned with rebels who had committed murder during the uprising.
v08: The crowd came up and began asking Pilate to do for them what he usually did.
v09: Pilate answered them, “Do you want me to release the King of the Jews for you?”
v10: For he knew that the chief priests had handed Jesus over because of envy.
v11: But the chief priests stirred up the crowd to have him release Barabbas for them instead.
v12: Pilate asked them again, “Then what do you want me to do with the one you call the King of the Jews?”
v13: They shouted back, “Crucify him!”
v14: Pilate asked them, “Why? What evil has he done?” But they shouted all the louder, “Crucify him!”
v15: Wanting to satisfy the crowd, Pilate released Barabbas for them. He had Jesus flogged and handed him over to be crucified.
</p>

## The Soldiers Mock Jesus

<p>
v16: The soldiers led Jesus inside the courtyard—that is, the governor’s headquarters—and called together the whole cohort.
v17: They dressed him in purple and twisted together a crown of thorns, which they put on him.
v18: Then they began to salute him: “Hail, King of the Jews!”
v19: They kept striking his head with a reed and spitting on him. They knelt down and paid him mocking homage.
v20: When they had finished mocking him, they stripped off the purple, put his own clothes on him, and led him out to crucify him.
</p>

## Jesus Is Crucified

<p>
v21: They forced a passerby to carry Jesus’ cross: Simon of Cyrene, who was coming in from the countryside, the father of Alexander and Rufus.
v22: They brought Jesus to the place called Golgotha, which means Place of a Skull.
v23: They offered him wine mixed with myrrh, but he did not take it.
v24: They crucified him and divided his clothes among themselves, casting lots to determine what each person would take.
v25: It was the third hour when they crucified him.
v26: The inscription stating the charge against him read, “The King of the Jews.”
v27: They crucified two bandits with him, one on his right and one on his left.
</p>

<p>
v29: Those passing by reviled him, shaking their heads and saying, “Ha! You who would tear down the temple and build it in three days,
v30: save yourself and come down from the cross!”
v31: In the same way, the chief priests and scribes mocked him among themselves: “He saved others; he cannot save himself!
v32: Let the Messiah, the King of Israel, come down now from the cross, so that we may see and believe.” Those crucified with him also insulted him.
</p>

## Jesus Dies

<p>
v33: When the sixth hour came, darkness covered the whole land until the ninth hour.
v34: At the ninth hour Jesus cried out in a loud voice, “Eloi, Eloi, lema sabachthani?” which means, “My God, my God, why have you abandoned me?”
v35: When some of those standing nearby heard it, they said, “Listen, he is calling Elijah.”
v36: Someone ran, filled a sponge with sour wine, put it on a reed, and gave it to Jesus to drink, saying, “Wait! Let us see whether Elijah comes to take him down.”
v37: Jesus let out a loud cry and breathed his last.
v38: The curtain of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom.
v39: When the centurion standing opposite Jesus saw that he breathed his last in this way, he said, “Truly this man was God’s Son.”
</p>

<p>
v40: Some women were also watching from a distance. Among them were Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James the younger and Joses, and Salome.
v41: When Jesus was in Galilee, these women followed him and served him. Many other women who had come up with him to Jerusalem were also there.
</p>

## Jesus Is Buried

<p>
v42: Evening had already come. Since it was Preparation Day—that is, the day before the Sabbath—
v43: Joseph of Arimathea, a respected council member who was himself waiting for God’s kingdom, came and boldly went in to Pilate to ask for Jesus’ body.
v44: Pilate was surprised that Jesus was already dead. He summoned the centurion and asked whether he had been dead for some time.
v45: After learning from the centurion that Jesus was dead, Pilate granted the corpse to Joseph.
v46: Joseph bought a linen cloth, took Jesus down, wrapped him in the cloth, and laid him in a tomb cut out of rock. Then he rolled a stone against the tomb’s entrance.
v47: Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses were watching where Jesus was laid.
</p>

## Notes

v02: ‘You say so’ is an indirect reply. It is not expanded into either a straightforward denial or a simple affirmative.

v15: Flogging was severe Roman violence preceding crucifixion. The concise narrative wording does not lessen the harm.

v25, 33–34: The third, sixth, and ninth hours correspond roughly to nine in the morning, noon, and three in the afternoon.

v28: Verse 28 is absent from the Greek text followed here. The numbering gap is retained.

v34: Mark preserves Jesus’ Aramaic cry and then translates it. Both are retained.

v39: The centurion’s confession lacks the Greek article before ‘God’s Son.’ The main text uses natural English without deciding whether his words express a full later Christian confession.

v40–41: The women are named as witnesses and described with the same actions of following and serving that characterize discipleship.

v42: Mark explains Preparation Day as the day before the Sabbath.

v45: The Greek shifts from ‘body’ in Joseph’s request to the stark word ‘corpse’ when Pilate grants it.

## Vocabulary

v07: **στάσις (stasis)**: Uprising, revolt, or insurrection.

v15: **φραγελλόω (phragelloō)**: Flog or scourge.

v21: **ἀγγαρεύω (angareuō)**: Compel someone into service.

v29: **βλασφημέω (blasphēmeō)**: Revile, slander, or blaspheme.

v37: **ἐκπνέω (ekpneō)**: Breathe out or expire.

v39: **κεντυρίων (kentyriōn)**: Centurion, a Roman military officer.
