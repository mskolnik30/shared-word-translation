## The Parable of the Sower

<p>
v01: Jesus began teaching beside the sea again. Such a large crowd gathered around him that he got into a boat and sat in it out on the water, while the whole crowd stayed on the shore.
v02: He taught them many things in parables. As he taught, he said,
v03: “Listen! Look, a farmer went out to sow.
v04: As he scattered the seed, some fell beside the path, and the birds came and ate it.
v05: Other seed fell on rocky ground, where there was little soil. It sprang up at once because the soil was shallow.
v06: When the sun rose, it was scorched. It had no root, so it withered.
v07: Other seed fell among thorns. The thorns grew up and choked it, and it produced no crop.
v08: Still other seed fell on good soil. It grew and flourished, producing a crop—thirty, sixty, and a hundred times what was sown.”
v09: Then he said, “Anyone with ears to hear should listen!”
</p>

## Hearing and the Mystery of the Kingdom

<p>
v10: When he was alone, those around him, along with the Twelve, asked him about the parables.
v11: He told them, “The mystery of God’s kingdom has been given to you. But for those outside, everything comes in parables,
v12: so that
‘they may look and look but not see,
and listen and listen but not understand,
lest they turn back and be forgiven.’”
v13: Then he said to them, “Don’t you understand this parable? How, then, will you understand all the parables?
</p>

## The Sower Explained

<p>
v14: The farmer sows the word.
v15: Some are like the seed beside the path, where the word is sown. As soon as they hear it, Satan comes and takes away the word sown in them.
v16: Others are like the seed sown on rocky ground. When they hear the word, they receive it with joy at once.
v17: But they have no root in themselves and last only a little while. When trouble or persecution comes because of the word, they fall away at once.
v18: Still others are like the seed sown among thorns. They hear the word,
v19: but the worries of this age, the deceitfulness of wealth, and desires for other things come in and choke the word, and it bears no fruit.
v20: But those like the seed sown on good soil hear the word, accept it, and bear fruit—thirty, sixty, and a hundred times what was sown.”
</p>

## A Lamp and a Measure

<p>
v21: He also said to them, “Is a lamp brought in to be put under a measuring basket or under a bed? Isn’t it brought in to be put on a lampstand?
v22: For nothing is hidden except to be revealed, and nothing is kept secret except to come into the open.
v23: Anyone with ears to hear should listen!”
v24: He went on, “Pay attention to what you hear. The measure you use will be used for you, and more will be given to you.
v25: For whoever has will be given more; whoever does not have will lose even what they have.”
</p>

## The Seed Growing by Itself

<p>
v26: He also said, “God’s kingdom is like someone scattering seed on the ground.
v27: Night and day, the person sleeps and gets up, while the seed sprouts and grows—though the person does not know how.
v28: The ground produces a crop on its own: first the shoot, then the head, then the full grain in the head.
v29: As soon as the crop is ready, the farmer puts in the sickle, because harvest time has come.”
</p>

## The Mustard Seed

<p>
v30: He also said, “What can we compare God’s kingdom to? What parable can we use for it?
v31: It is like a mustard seed. When sown in the ground, it is the smallest of all the seeds on earth.
v32: Yet once sown, it grows and becomes larger than all the garden plants. It puts out great branches, so that the birds of the sky can nest in its shade.”
v33: With many parables like these, he spoke the word to them, as much as they were able to hear.
v34: He did not speak to them without a parable, but privately he explained everything to his own disciples.
</p>

## Jesus Calms the Storm

<p>
v35: That same day, when evening came, he said to them, “Let’s cross to the other side.”
v36: Leaving the crowd behind, they took him along in the boat, just as he was. Other boats were with him.
v37: A fierce windstorm arose, and waves broke into the boat until it was already filling with water.
v38: Jesus was in the stern, asleep on the cushion. They woke him and said, “Teacher, don’t you care that we’re perishing?”
v39: He woke up, rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, “Be quiet! Be still!” The wind died down, and there was a great calm.
v40: He said to them, “Why are you so afraid? Do you still have no faith?”
v41: They were overcome with fear and said to one another, “Who is this, then? Even the wind and the sea obey him!”
</p>

## Notes

v11–12: The quotation echoes Isaiah 6:9–10. The Greek connects the parables with not perceiving and not turning back. ‘So that’ and ‘lest’ retain the difficult wording; the relation of purpose and result remains a matter for interpretation.

v31: The Greek says ‘smallest of all the seeds on earth.’ This translation retains the saying’s superlative rather than adding ‘among’ or limiting it to seeds a particular farmer used.

## Vocabulary

v11: **μυστήριον (mystērion)**: Mystery or secret; here something given to the hearers.

v14: **λόγος (logos)**: Word or message; repeated throughout the explanation of the sower.

v21: **μόδιος (modios)**: A container used for measuring grain, here placed over a lamp.

v28: **αὐτομάτη (automatē)**: On its own, without the sower directing the growth.

v40: **πίστις (pistis)**: Faith or trust.
