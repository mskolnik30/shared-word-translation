## Marriage and Hardness of Heart

<p>
v01: Jesus left there and went to the region of Judea and beyond the Jordan. Crowds gathered around him again, and again he taught them, as he usually did.
v02: They asked him, “Is a man allowed to divorce his wife?” They were putting him to the test.
v03: He answered, “What did Moses command you?”
v04: They said, “Moses allowed a man to write a certificate of divorce and send his wife away.”
v05: Jesus told them, “He wrote you this command because your hearts were hard.
v06: But from the beginning of creation, ‘he made them male and female.’
v07: ‘For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife,
v08: and the two will become one flesh.’ So they are no longer two, but one flesh.
v09: Therefore, what God has joined together, no human being should separate.”
v10: Back in the house, the disciples asked him about this again.
v11: He told them, “Anyone who divorces his wife and marries another woman commits adultery against her.
v12: And if a woman divorces her husband and marries another man, she commits adultery.”
</p>

## Jesus Welcomes Children

<p>
v13: People were bringing children to Jesus for him to touch, but the disciples rebuked them.
v14: When Jesus saw this, he was indignant. “Let the children come to me,” he said. “Do not stop them, for the kingdom of God belongs to such as these.
v15: Truly I tell you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God like a child will never enter it.”
v16: He took the children in his arms and blessed them, laying his hands on them.
</p>

## A Rich Man and the Kingdom

<p>
v17: As Jesus set out on the road, a man ran up and knelt before him. “Good Teacher,” he asked, “what must I do to inherit eternal life?”
v18: Jesus said, “Why do you call me good? No one is good except God alone.
v19: You know the commandments: Do not murder; do not commit adultery; do not steal; do not give false testimony; do not defraud; honor your father and mother.”
v20: “Teacher,” he answered, “I have kept all these since my youth.”
v21: Jesus looked at him and loved him. “You lack one thing,” he said. “Go, sell everything you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. Then come and follow me.”
v22: His face fell at these words, and he went away grieving, for he owned much property.
</p>

<p>
v23: Jesus looked around and said to his disciples, “How hard it will be for those who have wealth to enter the kingdom of God!”
v24: The disciples were astonished at his words. Jesus spoke again: “Children, how hard it is to enter the kingdom of God!
v25: It is easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle than for someone rich to enter the kingdom of God.”
v26: They were even more astounded and said to one another, “Then who can be saved?”
v27: Jesus looked at them and said, “For human beings it is impossible, but not for God. Everything is possible for God.”
v28: Peter began to say to him, “Look, we have left everything and followed you.”
v29: Jesus said, “Truly I tell you, everyone who has left a house, brothers, sisters, mother, father, children, or fields for my sake and for the good news
v30: will receive a hundred times as much now in this present time—houses, brothers, sisters, mothers, children, and fields, along with persecutions—and in the age to come, eternal life.
v31: But many who are first will be last, and the last will be first.”
</p>

## On the Way to Jerusalem

<p>
v32: They were on the road going up to Jerusalem, with Jesus walking ahead of them. They were astonished, and those who followed were afraid. Once more he took the Twelve aside and began to tell them what was going to happen to him.
v33: “Look, we are going up to Jerusalem. The Son of Man will be handed over to the chief priests and scribes. They will condemn him to death and hand him over to the Gentiles.
v34: They will mock him, spit on him, flog him, and kill him. Three days later he will rise.”
</p>

## Greatness Through Service

<p>
v35: James and John, the sons of Zebedee, came to him. “Teacher,” they said, “we want you to do whatever we ask of you.”
v36: He asked, “What do you want me to do for you?”
v37: They answered, “Let one of us sit at your right and the other at your left in your glory.”
v38: Jesus said, “You do not know what you are asking. Can you drink the cup I drink, or undergo the baptism I undergo?”
v39: “We can,” they answered. Jesus told them, “You will drink the cup I drink and undergo the baptism I undergo.
v40: But to sit at my right or left is not mine to grant. Those places belong to the ones for whom they have been prepared.”
v41: When the other ten heard this, they grew indignant with James and John.
v42: Jesus called them together and said, “You know how those regarded as rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, and their powerful people exercise authority over them.
v43: That is not how it is among you. Whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant,
v44: and whoever wants to be first among you must be everyone's slave.
v45: For even the Son of Man came to serve, not to be served, and to give his life as a ransom for many.”
</p>

## Bartimaeus Sees and Follows

<p>
v46: They came to Jericho. As Jesus left the city with his disciples and a large crowd, Bartimaeus—the son of Timaeus, a blind beggar—was sitting beside the road.
v47: When he heard that it was Jesus of Nazareth, he began to shout, “Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!”
v48: Many people rebuked him and told him to be quiet. But he shouted all the louder, “Son of David, have mercy on me!”
v49: Jesus stopped. “Call him,” he said. They called the blind man: “Take courage! Get up! He is calling you.”
v50: He threw off his cloak, sprang up, and came to Jesus.
v51: Jesus asked him, “What do you want me to do for you?” The blind man answered, “Rabbouni, let me see again.”
v52: “Go,” Jesus told him. “Your faith has made you well.” At once he could see again, and he followed Jesus on the road.
</p>

## Notes

v02: The Greek text followed here says ‘they asked’ without naming the questioners. Another reading explicitly names Pharisees.

v07: The Greek text followed here includes ‘and be joined to his wife’; variation around this clause does not make it absent from this edition.

v11–12: ‘Against her’ is retained in the first saying. The next saying explicitly describes a woman divorcing her husband; neither saying is expanded with an exception from another Gospel.

v15: ‘Like a child’ is left as a comparison. The saying does not specify a single quality such as innocence or dependence.

v24: The Greek text followed here does not add ‘for those who trust in riches.’ The preceding verse speaks specifically about wealth.

v30: The return list includes mothers but not fathers, and explicitly includes persecutions.

v38–40: Cup and baptism remain images in Jesus' reply. The passive ‘have been prepared’ does not name who prepared the places.

v45: ‘Ransom for many’ retains the compact release image. The saying does not name a recipient of the ransom.

v52: ‘Has made you well’ renders a verb that can also mean ‘has saved you.’ Here it accompanies the recovery of sight and following Jesus.

## Vocabulary

v05: **σκληροκαρδία (sklērokardia)**: Hardness of heart.

v17, 32, 52: **ὁδός (hodos)**: Road or way; the same word links the encounters and journey toward Jerusalem.

v43–44: **διάκονος / δοῦλος (diakonos / doulos)**: Servant / slave; the stronger social term in verse 44 is retained.

v45: **λύτρον (lytron)**: Ransom, a price or means of release.

v51: **Ραββουνι (Rabbouni)**: An address meaning ‘my master’ or ‘my teacher.’
