## Marriage and Hardness of Heart

<p>
v01: Jesus left there and went to the region of Judea and beyond the Jordan. Crowds gathered around him again, and as was his custom, he taught them.
v02: Some Pharisees came to test him and asked, “Is it lawful for a man to divorce his wife?”
v03: He answered them, “What did Moses command you?”
v04: They said, “Moses permitted a man to write a certificate of divorce and send her away.”
v05: Jesus said, “Because of your hardness of heart he wrote this command for you.
v06: But from the beginning of creation, ‘Male and female he made them.’
v07: ‘For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife,
v08: and the two will become one flesh.’ So they are no longer two but one flesh.
v09: Therefore, what God has joined together, let no human being separate.”
v10: In the house, the disciples asked him about this again.
v11: He said to them, “Whoever divorces his wife and marries another commits adultery against her,
v12: and if she divorces her husband and marries another, she commits adultery.”
</p>

## Jesus Welcomes Children

<p>
v13: People were bringing children to Jesus so that he might touch them, but the disciples rebuked them.
v14: When Jesus saw this, he was indignant and said, “Let the children come to me. Do not stop them, because the kingdom of God belongs to such as these.
v15: Truly I tell you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God like a child will certainly not enter it.”
v16: Then he took the children in his arms and blessed them, laying his hands on them.
</p>

## A Rich Man and the Kingdom

<p>
v17: As Jesus was setting out on the way, a man ran up, knelt before him, and asked, “Good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?”
v18: Jesus said to him, “Why do you call me good? No one is good except God alone.
v19: You know the commandments: Do not murder; do not commit adultery; do not steal; do not bear false witness; do not defraud; honor your father and mother.”
v20: The man said, “Teacher, I have kept all these from my youth.”
v21: Jesus looked at him and loved him. He said, “You lack one thing. Go, sell what you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. Then come, follow me.”
v22: But he was shocked by this word and went away grieving, because he had many possessions.
</p>

<p>
v23: Jesus looked around and said to his disciples, “How difficult it will be for those who have wealth to enter the kingdom of God.”
v24: The disciples were amazed at his words. Jesus said to them again, “Children, how difficult it is to enter the kingdom of God!
v25: It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich person to enter the kingdom of God.”
v26: They were even more astonished and said to one another, “Then who can be saved?”
v27: Jesus looked at them and said, “For human beings it is impossible, but not for God. For everything is possible with God.”
v28: Peter began to say to him, “Look, we have left everything and followed you.”
v29: Jesus said, “Truly I tell you, no one has left house, brothers, sisters, mother, father, children, or fields for my sake and for the sake of the good news
v30: who will not receive a hundred times as much now in this age—houses, brothers, sisters, mothers, children, and fields, with persecutions—and in the age to come eternal life.
v31: But many who are first will be last, and the last first.”
</p>

## On the Way to Jerusalem

<p>
v32: They were on the way going up to Jerusalem, and Jesus was walking ahead of them. The disciples were amazed, and those following were afraid. Taking the twelve aside again, he began telling them what was about to happen to him:
v33: “Look, we are going up to Jerusalem. The Son of Man will be handed over to the chief priests and scribes. They will condemn him to death and hand him over to the Gentiles.
v34: They will mock him, spit on him, flog him, and kill him. After three days he will rise.”
</p>

## Greatness Through Service

<p>
v35: James and John, the sons of Zebedee, came to Jesus and said, “Teacher, we want you to do for us whatever we ask.”
v36: He asked them, “What do you want me to do for you?”
v37: They said, “Grant that we may sit, one at your right and one at your left, in your glory.”
v38: Jesus said, “You do not know what you are asking. Are you able to drink the cup I drink or be baptized with the baptism I am baptized with?”
v39: They said, “We are able.” Jesus told them, “You will drink the cup I drink, and you will be baptized with the baptism I am baptized with.
v40: But to sit at my right or left is not mine to grant. Those places are for those for whom they have been prepared.”
v41: When the other ten heard this, they began to be indignant with James and John.
v42: Jesus called them together and said, “You know that those regarded as rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, and their great ones exercise authority over them.
v43: But it must not be this way among you. Whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant,
v44: and whoever wants to be first among you must be slave of all.
v45: For even the Son of Man did not come to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many.”
</p>

## Bartimaeus Sees and Follows

<p>
v46: They came to Jericho. As Jesus was leaving Jericho with his disciples and a large crowd, Bartimaeus son of Timaeus, a blind beggar, was sitting beside the way.
v47: When he heard that it was Jesus of Nazareth, he began to cry out, “Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!”
v48: Many people rebuked him and told him to be quiet, but he cried out even louder, “Son of David, have mercy on me!”
v49: Jesus stopped and said, “Call him.” They called the blind man and said, “Take courage! Get up; he is calling you.”
v50: Throwing off his cloak, he jumped up and came to Jesus.
v51: Jesus asked him, “What do you want me to do for you?” The blind man said, “Rabbouni, I want to see again.”
v52: Jesus said to him, “Go. Your faith has saved you.” Immediately he could see again, and he followed Jesus on the way.
</p>

## Notes

v02: The question is presented as a test. Jesus answers by moving from legal permission to hardness of heart and then to creation.

v05: “Hardness of heart” connects this debate with Mark’s wider concern for resistant perception and will.

v06–09: Jesus quotes creation language and speaks of God joining the two. Fluent preserves the argument without adding later legal exceptions or applications that Mark does not state here.

v12: The reciprocal formulation involving a woman divorcing her husband fits the legal-social setting Mark addresses. The translation does not erase the asymmetry of ancient marriage practice or turn the saying into a complete modern legal code.

v15: “Like a child” is tied grammatically to receiving the kingdom, not explicitly to innocence, humility, dependence, or another single childlike trait. Fluent leaves the comparison open.

v17: “On the way” marks both literal travel and the narrative path toward Jerusalem, suffering, and discipleship.

v21: Jesus’ demanding command arises after Mark explicitly says that Jesus loved the man. The translation keeps love and costly summons together.

v24: SBLGNT does not include the later expansion “for those who trust in riches.” Fluent follows the critical text: the difficulty is stated as difficulty entering the kingdom, while v23 has already focused specifically on wealth.

v26–27: The disciples’ question about salvation is answered by divine possibility, not by a new human technique.

v30: Promise is explicitly joined “with persecutions.” Discipleship is not narrated as a prosperity formula.

v32: Jesus walks ahead of those who are amazed and afraid. Leadership and the “way” converge as he moves toward Jerusalem.

v33: `paradidōmi`, “hand over,” repeats twice in the prediction and links religious and Gentile authorities within the passion sequence.

v38–40: Cup and baptism become images of participation in Jesus’ coming suffering. The translation does not collapse them into later sacramental explanations.

v42–45: Jesus does not merely recommend kinder leadership; he contrasts domination with a community ordered by serving and becoming slave of all.

v45: `lytron`, “ransom,” refers to a price or means of release. Fluent preserves the compact saying “ransom for many” without inserting a developed atonement theory into the verse.

v52: Bartimaeus receives sight and then follows Jesus “on the way.” The sequence brings the Gospel’s seeing and discipleship themes together immediately before Jerusalem.
