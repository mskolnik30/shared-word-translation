## Jesus Feeds Four Thousand

<p>
v01: In those days another large crowd gathered and had nothing to eat. Jesus called the disciples to him and said,
v02: “I feel compassion for the crowd. They have already stayed with me for three days and have nothing to eat.
v03: If I send them home hungry, they will collapse on the way. Some of them have come from far away.”
v04: His disciples answered, “Where could anyone find enough bread to feed these people here in this deserted place?”
v05: He asked them, “How many loaves do you have?” They said, “Seven.”
v06: He told the crowd to sit on the ground. Then he took the seven loaves, gave thanks, broke them, and kept giving them to his disciples to serve. They served them to the crowd.
v07: They also had a few small fish. He blessed them and told the disciples to serve these as well.
v08: They ate and had enough, and they gathered seven large baskets of leftover pieces.
v09: There were about four thousand people. Then he sent them away.
</p>

## A Sign Is Demanded

<p>
v10: At once he got into the boat with his disciples and went to the district of Dalmanutha.
v11: The Pharisees came out and began arguing with him. To test him, they demanded a sign from heaven.
v12: He sighed deeply in his spirit and said, “Why does this generation demand a sign? Truly I tell you, no sign will be given to this generation.”
v13: He left them, got back into the boat, and went to the other side.
</p>

## Bread and Hardened Hearts

<p>
v14: The disciples had forgotten to bring bread. They had only one loaf with them in the boat.
v15: He warned them, “Watch out! Beware of the yeast of the Pharisees and the yeast of Herod.”
v16: They began talking with one another about having no bread.
v17: Jesus knew this and said to them, “Why are you talking about having no bread? Do you still not see or understand? Are your hearts hardened?
v18: You have eyes—do you not see? You have ears—do you not hear? And do you not remember?
v19: When I broke the five loaves for the five thousand, how many baskets full of pieces did you gather?” They said to him, “Twelve.”
v20: “And when I broke the seven loaves for the four thousand, how many large baskets full of pieces did you gather?” They said, “Seven.”
v21: He said to them, “Do you still not understand?”
</p>

## A Blind Man Begins to See

<p>
v22: They came to Bethsaida. People brought a blind man to Jesus and begged him to touch him.
v23: Jesus took the blind man by the hand and led him outside the village. He spat on the man’s eyes, laid his hands on him, and asked, “Can you see anything?”
v24: The man looked up and said, “I can see people. They look like trees walking around.”
v25: Then Jesus laid his hands on the man’s eyes again. The man saw clearly; his sight was restored, and he could see everything distinctly.
v26: Jesus sent him home, saying, “Do not even go into the village.”
</p>

## Peter Confesses Jesus as the Messiah

<p>
v27: Jesus and his disciples went on to the villages around Caesarea Philippi. On the way he asked his disciples, “Who do people say I am?”
v28: They answered, “John the Baptist; others say Elijah; and others, one of the prophets.”
v29: He asked them, “But you—who do you say I am?” Peter answered, “You are the Messiah.”
v30: He sternly warned them not to tell anyone about him.
</p>

## The Son of Man Must Suffer

<p>
v31: Then he began teaching them that the Son of Man must suffer greatly, be rejected by the elders, chief priests, and scribes, be killed, and rise after three days.
v32: He spoke about this openly. Peter took him aside and began to rebuke him.
v33: But Jesus turned, saw his disciples, and rebuked Peter. “Get behind me, Satan!” he said. “You are not thinking about God’s concerns, but human concerns.”
</p>

## The Way of the Cross

<p>
v34: He called the crowd to him along with his disciples and said, “If anyone wants to come after me, they must deny themselves, take up their cross, and follow me.
v35: For whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for me and for the good news will save it.
v36: What does it profit a person to gain the whole world and forfeit their life?
v37: What could a person give in exchange for their life?
v38: For whoever is ashamed of me and my words in this adulterous and sinful generation, the Son of Man will also be ashamed of them when he comes in his Father’s glory with the holy angels.”
</p>

## Notes

v08, 19–20: Mark uses two words for baskets. The larger baskets in this feeding and verse 20 translate spyris; the baskets in verse 19 and the earlier feeding translate kophinos.

v12: The Greek uses a shortened oath formula, literally ‘if a sign will be given.’ Here it expresses a firm refusal, not uncertainty about whether a sign will come.

v23–25: The man’s sight returns in two stages. His first report and Jesus’ second touch are both retained.

v31: ‘Must’ preserves the stated necessity without explaining its basis. ‘After three days’ follows Mark’s wording here.

v35–37: The same Greek word, psychē, is rendered ‘life’ throughout these sayings. It can also refer to the self or soul; the translation does not impose a separation of body and soul on the passage.

## Vocabulary

v15: **ζύμη (zymē)**: Yeast or leaven; retained as the warning’s image without supplying its interpretation.

v29: **χριστός (christos)**: Messiah or Christ, meaning ‘anointed one.’

v31: **υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου (huios tou anthrōpou)**: Son of Man, Jesus’ recurring self-designation.

v34: **σταυρός (stauros)**: Cross, an instrument of execution.

v35: **ψυχή (psychē)**: Life, self, or soul; here repeated in the save–lose and gain–forfeit sayings.
