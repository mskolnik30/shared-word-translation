## Jesus Feeds Four Thousand

<p>
v01: In those days another large crowd gathered and had nothing to eat. Jesus called his disciples and said,
v02: “I have compassion on the crowd because they have stayed with me for three days now and have nothing to eat.
v03: If I send them home hungry, they will collapse on the way, and some of them have come from far away.”
v04: His disciples answered, “Where could anyone get enough bread here in this deserted place to feed these people?”
v05: He asked them, “How many loaves do you have?” They said, “Seven.”
v06: He told the crowd to sit down on the ground. Then he took the seven loaves, gave thanks, broke them, and gave them to his disciples to set before the people.
v07: They also had a few small fish. After blessing them, Jesus said to set these before the people too.
v08: They ate and were satisfied, and the disciples picked up seven baskets of leftover pieces.
v09: There were about four thousand people. Then Jesus sent them away.
</p>

## A Sign Is Demanded

<p>
v10: Immediately Jesus got into the boat with his disciples and went to the region of Dalmanutha.
v11: The Pharisees came and began arguing with him, testing him by asking for a sign from heaven.
v12: Sighing deeply in his spirit, he said, “Why does this generation seek a sign? Truly I tell you, no sign will be given to this generation.”
v13: Then he left them, got back into the boat, and went to the other side.
</p>

## Bread and Hardened Hearts

<p>
v14: The disciples had forgotten to bring bread and had only one loaf with them in the boat.
v15: Jesus warned them, “Watch out! Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and the leaven of Herod.”
v16: They began discussing with one another that they had no bread.
v17: Jesus knew what they were saying and asked, “Why are you discussing the fact that you have no bread? Do you still not perceive or understand? Are your hearts hardened?
v18: Having eyes, do you not see? Having ears, do you not hear? And do you not remember?
v19: When I broke the five loaves for the five thousand, how many baskets full of pieces did you pick up?” They said, “Twelve.”
v20: “And when I broke the seven loaves for the four thousand, how many baskets full of pieces did you pick up?” They said, “Seven.”
v21: He said to them, “Do you still not understand?”
</p>

## A Blind Man Begins to See

<p>
v22: They came to Bethsaida. People brought a blind man to Jesus and begged him to touch him.
v23: Jesus took the blind man by the hand and led him outside the village. After spitting on his eyes and laying his hands on him, he asked, “Do you see anything?”
v24: The man looked up and said, “I see people; they look like trees walking around.”
v25: Then Jesus placed his hands on the man’s eyes again. The man looked intently, his sight was restored, and he saw everything clearly.
v26: Jesus sent him home and said, “Do not even go into the village.”
</p>

## Peter Confesses Jesus as the Christ

<p>
v27: Jesus and his disciples went on to the villages around Caesarea Philippi. On the way he asked his disciples, “Who do people say that I am?”
v28: They told him, “John the Baptist; others say Elijah; still others, one of the prophets.”
v29: He asked them, “But you—who do you say that I am?” Peter answered, “You are the Christ.”
v30: Jesus strictly ordered them not to tell anyone about him.
</p>

## The Son of Man Must Suffer

<p>
v31: Then he began teaching them that the Son of Man must suffer many things, be rejected by the elders, chief priests, and scribes, be killed, and after three days rise.
v32: He spoke this word openly. Peter took him aside and began to rebuke him.
v33: But Jesus turned, looked at his disciples, rebuked Peter, and said, “Get behind me, Satan! You are not setting your mind on the things of God but on human things.”
</p>

## The Way of the Cross

<p>
v34: Jesus called the crowd together with his disciples and said, “If anyone wants to come after me, let that person deny themselves, take up their cross, and follow me.
v35: For whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for my sake and for the good news will save it.
v36: What does it profit a person to gain the whole world and forfeit their life?
v37: What can a person give in exchange for their life?
v38: Whoever is ashamed of me and my words in this adulterous and sinful generation, the Son of Man will also be ashamed of that person when he comes in his Father’s glory with the holy angels.”
</p>

## Notes

v01–09: The second bread feeding should be heard alongside chapter 6. Mark repeats provision before narrating the disciples’ continuing failure to understand the significance of the bread.

v12: Jesus’ deep sigh is retained. The refusal of a demanded sign does not mean Mark lacks signs; it rejects the demand that Jesus submit his identity to the opponents’ terms of proof.

v15: “Leaven” functions as a metaphor for a corrupting or spreading influence. The verse does not define the metaphor more precisely.

v17–21: Seeing, hearing, remembering, understanding, bread, and hardened hearts form one connected perception sequence.

v22–25: The two-stage healing is unusual in Mark and sits immediately before the disciples’ partial recognition of Jesus. The translation preserves the narrative placement without turning the healing into an explicit allegory.

v27: “On the way” becomes increasingly important as Jesus moves toward Jerusalem and teaches what following him means.

v29: Peter’s confession is correct but incomplete within Mark’s narrative. Jesus immediately begins to define Messiahship through the suffering Son of Man.

v31: “Must” expresses necessity without explaining whether that necessity is scriptural, divine, narrative, or all of these at once.

v32: Jesus speaks “openly/plainly,” contrasting with earlier restrained disclosure. Peter’s rebuke shows that clearer speech does not automatically produce understanding.

v33: “Get behind me” also echoes discipleship language: Peter belongs behind Jesus as follower, not in front of him redefining the path.

v34–37: `psychē` can mean life or self. “Life” preserves the repeated save/lose/gain/exchange logic without turning the passage into a contrast between body and an immaterial soul.

v35: The same `sōzō` word-family used for healing elsewhere now appears in a discipleship saying about losing and saving one’s life.
