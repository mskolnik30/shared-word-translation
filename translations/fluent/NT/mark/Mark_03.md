## A Man with a Withered Hand

<p>
v01: Jesus entered a synagogue again. A man with a withered hand was there.
v02: They watched Jesus closely to see whether he would heal the man on the Sabbath, so they could accuse him.
v03: He told the man with the withered hand, “Stand up here in the middle.”
v04: Then he asked them, “Is it lawful on the Sabbath to do good or to do harm, to save a life or to kill?” But they remained silent.
v05: He looked around at them in anger, grieved by their hardened hearts. He told the man, “Stretch out your hand.” He stretched it out, and his hand was restored.
v06: The Pharisees went out and immediately began plotting with the Herodians how to destroy him.
</p>

## Crowds and Unclean Spirits

<p>
v07: Jesus withdrew to the sea with his disciples, and a large crowd from Galilee followed. People also came from Judea,
v08: Jerusalem, Idumea, beyond the Jordan, and the area around Tyre and Sidon. Hearing all he was doing, a great crowd came to him.
v09: Because of the crowd, he told his disciples to keep a small boat ready for him so that people would not crush him.
v10: He had healed many, so everyone with an affliction pressed toward him to touch him.
v11: Whenever the unclean spirits saw him, they fell before him and cried out, “You are the Son of God!”
v12: He sternly ordered them not to make him known.
</p>

## Jesus Appoints the Twelve

<p>
v13: He went up the mountain and called those he wanted, and they came to him.
v14: He appointed twelve to be with him and to be sent out to proclaim the message,
v15: with authority to drive out demons.
v16: These are the twelve he appointed: Simon, whom he named Peter;
v17: James son of Zebedee and John, James’s brother, whom he named Boanerges, meaning “Sons of Thunder”;
v18: Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Cananaean,
v19: and Judas Iscariot, who handed him over.
</p>

## A House Divided

<p>
v20: Then he went home. Again a crowd gathered, so that he and his disciples could not even eat.
v21: When his family heard, they went out to take charge of him, for they were saying, “He is out of his mind.”
v22: The scribes who had come down from Jerusalem said, “He has Beelzebul,” and, “He drives out demons by the ruler of demons.”
v23: Jesus called them over and spoke to them in parables: “How can Satan drive out Satan?
v24: If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand.
v25: If a household is divided against itself, that household cannot stand.
v26: And if Satan has risen against himself and is divided, he cannot stand; his end has come.
v27: But no one can enter a strong man’s house and plunder his possessions without first tying up the strong man. Only then can his house be plundered.
</p>

<p>
v28: I tell you the truth: people will be forgiven all their sins and whatever blasphemies they utter.
v29: But whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit will never have forgiveness and is guilty of an eternal sin.”
v30: He said this because they were saying, “He has an unclean spirit.”
</p>

## Jesus’ True Family

<p>
v31: His mother and his brothers arrived. Standing outside, they sent word to him and called for him.
v32: A crowd was sitting around him. They told him, “Your mother and your brothers are outside looking for you.”
v33: He answered, “Who are my mother and my brothers?”
v34: Looking around at those seated in a circle around him, he said, “Here are my mother and my brothers!
v35: Whoever does God’s will is my brother and sister and mother.”
</p>

## Notes

v05: The text presents Jesus’ anger and grief together.

v14: The pinned Greek does not include “whom he also named apostles.” Some manuscripts include it.

v18: “Cananaean” represents a term associated with zeal. It does not identify Simon as a Canaanite or establish membership in a later political organization.

v21: The expression rendered “his family” can also mean his associates or those close to him. The subject of “they were saying” is also open to more than one reading.

v29–30: The warning is explicitly connected to the claim that Jesus has an unclean spirit. The pinned Greek says “eternal sin”; some manuscripts read “eternal judgment.”

v32: Some manuscripts add “and your sisters.” The pinned Greek names his mother and brothers here; verse 35 explicitly includes “sister.”

## Vocabulary

v06: **Herodians**: People associated with or supportive of Herod’s rule.

v17: **Boanerges**: A name Mark explains as “Sons of Thunder.”

v22: **Beelzebul**: A name used in the accusation about the ruler of demons.

v23: **Parables**: Comparisons or figurative sayings, not only extended stories.

v29: **Blaspheme**: To speak abusively or irreverently against; here directed against the Holy Spirit.
