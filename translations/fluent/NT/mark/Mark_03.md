## A Man with a Withered Hand

<p>
v01: Jesus entered the synagogue again, and a man with a withered hand was there.
v02: They were watching Jesus closely to see whether he would heal the man on the Sabbath, so that they could accuse him.
v03: Jesus said to the man with the withered hand, “Stand up here in front of everyone.”
v04: Then he asked them, “Is it lawful on the Sabbath to do good or to do harm, to save a life or to kill?” But they were silent.
v05: Looking around at them with anger and deeply grieved at the hardness of their hearts, Jesus said to the man, “Stretch out your hand.” He stretched it out, and his hand was restored.
v06: The Pharisees went out and immediately began plotting with the Herodians against Jesus, discussing how they might destroy him.
</p>

## Crowds and Unclean Spirits

<p>
v07: Jesus withdrew with his disciples to the sea, and a great crowd followed from Galilee and Judea,
v08: from Jerusalem, Idumea, beyond the Jordan, and the regions around Tyre and Sidon. A great crowd came to him because they had heard what he was doing.
v09: Because of the crowd, he told his disciples to have a small boat ready for him so that the people would not crush him.
v10: He had healed many, so everyone who had an affliction pressed toward him to touch him.
v11: Whenever the unclean spirits saw him, they fell down before him and cried out, “You are the Son of God!”
v12: But he repeatedly ordered them not to make him known.
</p>

## Jesus Appoints the Twelve

<p>
v13: Jesus went up on the mountain and called those he wanted, and they came to him.
v14: He appointed twelve, whom he also named apostles, so that they might be with him and so that he might send them out to proclaim,
v15: and to have authority to cast out demons.
v16: He appointed the twelve: Simon, to whom he gave the name Peter;
v17: James son of Zebedee and John the brother of James, to whom he gave the name Boanerges, that is, Sons of Thunder;
v18: Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Zealot,
v19: and Judas Iscariot, who later handed him over.
</p>

## A House Divided

<p>
v20: Then Jesus went into a house, and the crowd gathered again, so that they could not even eat.
v21: When those close to him heard about it, they went out to restrain him, because they were saying, “He is out of his mind.”
v22: The scribes who came down from Jerusalem were saying, “He has Beelzebul,” and, “He casts out demons by the ruler of the demons.”
v23: Jesus called them to himself and spoke to them in parables: “How can Satan cast out Satan?
v24: If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand.
v25: If a house is divided against itself, that house will not be able to stand.
v26: And if Satan has risen up against himself and is divided, he cannot stand; his end has come.
v27: But no one can enter a strong man’s house and plunder his possessions unless he first binds the strong man. Then he can plunder the house.
</p>

<p>
v28: Truly I tell you, all sins will be forgiven the children of humanity, and whatever blasphemies they speak;
v29: but whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit never has forgiveness and is guilty of an eternal sin.”
v30: He said this because they were saying, “He has an unclean spirit.”
</p>

## Jesus’ True Family

<p>
v31: His mother and brothers came, and standing outside they sent word to him and called for him.
v32: A crowd was sitting around him, and they said, “Look, your mother and your brothers are outside looking for you.”
v33: He answered them, “Who are my mother and my brothers?”
v34: Looking around at those sitting in a circle around him, he said, “Here are my mother and my brothers!
v35: Whoever does the will of God is my brother and sister and mother.”
</p>

## Notes

v04–05: Jesus frames the Sabbath dispute in terms of doing good, saving life, and the moral danger of hardened hearts. His anger and grief are held together.

v06: Pharisees and Herodians represent groups with different interests; their cooperation signals the seriousness of the emerging threat.

v11–12: Unclean spirits identify Jesus correctly but are silenced. In Mark, correct words do not by themselves constitute faithful recognition.

v14: “Whom he also named apostles” is included in SBLGNT. The title means those sent on commission; Mark also emphasizes that they are first called “to be with him.”

v19: `paradidōmi`, “hand over,” is used for Judas’s future action. Fluent keeps that wording so the betrayal vocabulary remains connected through the passion narrative.

v21: `hoi par’ autou` can mean those close to Jesus, his associates, or his family. The subject of “they were saying” can also be heard in more than one way. Fluent uses “those close to him” and leaves the precise group open rather than naming “his family” as certain.

v23–27: The strong-man image presents Jesus’ exorcistic work as an invasion of hostile power and liberation of what has been held.

v28: “Children of humanity” preserves the inclusive sense of the Semitic expression rather than reading the phrase as only male.

v29–30: The warning is immediately tied to calling the work of God’s Spirit unclean. Fluent does not reduce the verse to a checklist for anxious readers or detach it from the narrative accusation.

v31–35: Jesus does not deny biological family; he expands family identity around doing the will of God.

## Vocabulary

v05: πώρωσις (pōrōsis)
“Hardness, dullness,” especially of perception and heart.

v14: ἀπόστολος (apostolos)
“One sent, an authorized messenger.”

v19: παραδίδωμι (paradidōmi)
“To hand over, deliver over.”

v23: παραβολή (parabolē)
“Parable, comparison, figurative saying.”

v27: ἰσχυρός (ischyros)
“Strong, powerful.”

v29: βλασφημέω (blasphēmeō)
“To blaspheme, slander, speak against.”
