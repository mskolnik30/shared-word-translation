## A Paralytic Is Forgiven and Healed

<p>
v01: A few days later, Jesus returned to Capernaum, and word spread that he was at home.
v02: So many gathered that there was no room left, not even at the door. He was speaking the message to them.
v03: Some people came bringing a paralyzed man, carried by four of them.
v04: Unable to bring him to Jesus because of the crowd, they opened the roof above him. They dug through it and lowered the mat on which the paralyzed man lay.
v05: Seeing their faith, Jesus said to the paralyzed man, “Child, your sins are forgiven.”
</p>

<p>
v06: Some scribes were sitting there, reasoning within themselves,
v07: “Why does this man speak like this? He is blaspheming! Who can forgive sins but God alone?”
v08: Jesus immediately recognized in his spirit what they were thinking. He asked them, “Why are you reasoning like this in your hearts?
v09: Which is easier: to tell the paralyzed man, ‘Your sins are forgiven,’ or to say, ‘Get up, take your mat, and walk’?
v10: But so that you may know the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins”—he said to the paralyzed man,
v11: “I tell you, get up, take your mat, and go home.”
v12: He got up, immediately picked up his mat, and walked out in front of everyone. They were all amazed and glorified God, saying, “We have never seen anything like this!”
</p>

## Jesus Calls Levi

<p>
v13: Jesus went out beside the sea again. The whole crowd came to him, and he taught them.
v14: As he passed by, he saw Levi son of Alphaeus sitting at the tax booth. “Follow me,” he said. Levi got up and followed him.
v15: As Jesus reclined at the table in Levi’s house, many tax collectors and sinners ate with him and his disciples, for many were following him.
v16: When the scribes of the Pharisees saw him eating with sinners and tax collectors, they asked his disciples, “Why does he eat with tax collectors and sinners?”
v17: Jesus heard this and said, “Healthy people do not need a doctor; sick people do. I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners.”
</p>

## Questions About Fasting

<p>
v18: John’s disciples and the Pharisees were fasting. People came and asked Jesus, “Why do John’s disciples and the Pharisees’ disciples fast, but yours do not?”
v19: Jesus answered, “Can the wedding guests fast while the bridegroom is with them? As long as they have the bridegroom with them, they cannot fast.
v20: But the days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them. Then, on that day, they will fast.
v21: “No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth onto an old garment. Otherwise the new patch pulls away from the old cloth, and the tear becomes worse.
v22: And no one puts new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise the wine bursts the skins, and both the wine and the skins are ruined. New wine belongs in fresh wineskins.”
</p>

## Lord of the Sabbath

<p>
v23: One Sabbath Jesus was passing through grainfields. As his disciples made their way along, they began plucking heads of grain.
v24: The Pharisees said to him, “Look! Why are they doing what is not lawful on the Sabbath?”
v25: He answered, “Have you never read what David did when he and his companions were hungry and in need?
v26: He entered God’s house in the time of Abiathar the high priest and ate the bread of the Presence, which only priests may lawfully eat. He also gave some to those with him.”
v27: Then he told them, “The Sabbath was made for humanity, not humanity for the Sabbath.
v28: So the Son of Man is lord even of the Sabbath.”
</p>

## Notes

v01: “At home” can also be rendered “in a house.” The Greek does not identify its owner.

v05: “Their faith” is plural; the text does not single out only the man being carried.

v15: The Greek says “in his house.” Levi is a natural referent in context, but the pronoun can also refer to Jesus.

v17: The pinned Greek ends with “sinners,” without the words “to repentance” found in some manuscripts.

v19: “Wedding guests” renders the expression “sons of the wedding chamber.”

v23: The wording describes making their way while plucking grain. It does not explicitly say they were eating it here.

v26: Mark names Abiathar. The corresponding account in 1 Samuel 21 names Ahimelech; the translation preserves Mark’s wording.

## Vocabulary

v04: **Mat**: A portable bed or sleeping pallet.

v10: **Son of Man**: Jesus’ recurring self-designation, retained without substituting a single interpretation.

v14: **Tax booth**: A place where taxes or tolls were collected.

v22: **Wineskins**: Animal-skin containers used for liquids, including wine.

v26: **Bread of the Presence**: Bread set before God in the sanctuary.

v27: **Humanity**: Greek anthrōpos here refers to human beings generally.
