## Tradition and Defilement

<p>
v01: The Pharisees and some scribes who had come from Jerusalem gathered around Jesus.
v02: They saw some of his disciples eating bread with defiled—that is, unwashed—hands.
v03: For the Pharisees and all the Jews do not eat unless they wash their hands carefully, holding to the tradition of the elders.
v04: And when they come from the marketplace, they do not eat unless they wash. There are many other traditions they have received and hold, including washings of cups, pitchers, and copper vessels.
v05: The Pharisees and scribes asked Jesus, “Why do your disciples not walk according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with defiled hands?”
v06: He said to them, “Isaiah prophesied well about you hypocrites, as it is written:
‘This people honors me with their lips,
but their heart is far from me;
v07: they worship me in vain,
teaching human commands as doctrines.’
v08: You leave the commandment of God and hold on to human tradition.”
</p>

<p>
v09: He also said, “You have a fine way of setting aside God’s commandment in order to establish your tradition.
v10: Moses said, ‘Honor your father and your mother,’ and, ‘Whoever speaks evil of father or mother must surely die.’
v11: But you say that if someone tells father or mother, ‘Whatever benefit you might have received from me is Corban’—that is, a gift devoted to God—
v12: then you no longer allow that person to do anything for father or mother.
v13: In this way you nullify the word of God by the tradition you have handed down. And you do many things like this.”
</p>

## What Defiles a Person

<p>
v14: Jesus called the crowd again and said, “Listen to me, all of you, and understand:
v15: nothing outside a person can defile that person by going in. Rather, the things that come out of a person are what defile.”
</p>

<p>
v17: When he entered the house away from the crowd, his disciples asked him about the parable.
v18: He said to them, “Are you also without understanding? Do you not understand that nothing entering a person from outside can defile them,
v19: because it does not enter the heart but the stomach and then goes out into the latrine?” Thus he made all foods clean.
v20: And he said, “What comes out of a person is what defiles the person.
v21: For from within, out of the human heart, come evil thoughts: sexual immoralities, thefts, murders, adulteries,
v22: greed, wickedness, deceit, sensuality, an evil eye, slander, pride, and foolishness.
v23: All these evil things come from within and defile a person.”
</p>

## The Syrophoenician Woman

<p>
v24: Jesus got up and went away to the region of Tyre. He entered a house and did not want anyone to know he was there, but he could not remain hidden.
v25: Immediately a woman whose little daughter had an unclean spirit heard about him, came, and fell at his feet.
v26: The woman was Greek, Syrophoenician by birth. She kept asking him to cast the demon out of her daughter.
v27: Jesus said to her, “Let the children be fed first, because it is not right to take the children’s bread and throw it to the dogs.”
v28: She answered him, “Lord, even the dogs under the table eat the children’s crumbs.”
v29: He said to her, “Because of this word, go. The demon has gone out of your daughter.”
v30: She went home and found the child lying on the bed and the demon gone.
</p>

## A Deaf Man Is Opened

<p>
v31: Jesus left the region of Tyre again and went through Sidon to the Sea of Galilee, through the region of the Decapolis.
v32: People brought him a man who was deaf and had difficulty speaking, and they begged Jesus to lay his hand on him.
v33: Jesus took him away from the crowd privately, put his fingers into the man’s ears, spat, and touched his tongue.
v34: Looking up to heaven, Jesus sighed and said to him, “Ephphatha,” which means, “Be opened.”
v35: Immediately his ears were opened, the bond of his tongue was released, and he began to speak clearly.
v36: Jesus ordered them to tell no one. But the more he ordered them, the more they proclaimed it.
v37: They were overwhelmed with amazement and said, “He has done everything well. He even makes the deaf hear and those unable to speak talk.”
</p>

## Notes

v02–05: “Defiled” translates `koinos`, literally “common,” used here in a purity context. The dispute is about received practices and purity, not ordinary hygiene alone.

v03: Mark’s broad explanatory statement about “all the Jews” is part of the narrator’s own framing. Fluent retains it without turning it into a comprehensive sociological claim about every Jewish practice in every place.

v08–13: Jesus’ criticism is not “tradition versus Scripture” in the abstract; the concrete charge is that a tradition can be used to set aside a divine command to honor parents.

v15: Public verse 16 is absent from SBLGNT and the earliest/strongest witnesses. Later manuscripts add a familiar hearing saying. Fluent retains public numbering and records the absence rather than inventing a verse.

v19: The final participle `katharizōn` is grammatically compact. “Thus he made all foods clean” follows the common interpretation that the narrator draws a purity conclusion from Jesus’ saying; the grammatical construction remains worth noting.

v21–23: Defilement is relocated to what emerges from the human heart. Fluent retains the list rather than compressing it into a generalized moral category.

v27: The Greek uses the diminutive `kynaria`, often “little dogs” or household dogs. The image remains socially and rhetorically difficult even with the diminutive; Fluent does not use a softening paraphrase to remove the tension.

v28–29: The woman engages Jesus’ own image and her “word” becomes the stated turning point. The translation does not add an explanation of Jesus’ motive or emotional state.

v34: Mark preserves the Aramaic `Ephphatha` and translates it. Fluent follows the same pattern.

v36: The command to silence is again ineffective; the more Jesus orders silence, the more the people proclaim. This contributes to Mark’s restrained-disclosure pattern.

## Vocabulary

v02: κοινός (koinos)
“Common; defiled” in a purity context.

v03: παράδοσις (paradosis)
“Tradition, something handed down.”

v11: κορβᾶν (korban)
“Corban,” a gift devoted to God.

v15: κοινόω (koinoō)
“To defile, make common.”

v21: καρδία (kardia)
“Heart,” the center of desire, thought, and will.

v27: κυνάριον (kynarion)
“Little dog, household dog.”

v29: λόγος (logos)
“Word, saying, speech.”

v34: ἐφφαθά (ephphatha)
Aramaic, “Be opened.”
