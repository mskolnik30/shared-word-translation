## Tradition and Defilement

<p>
v01: The Pharisees and some scribes who had come from Jerusalem gathered around Jesus.
v02: They saw some of his disciples eating bread with defiled hands—that is, unwashed hands.
v03: For the Pharisees, and all the Jews, do not eat unless they wash their hands in the prescribed way, holding to the tradition of the elders.
v04: When they come from the marketplace, they do not eat without washing. They also observe many other traditions they have received, such as washing cups, pitchers, bronze vessels, and dining couches.
v05: So the Pharisees and scribes asked him, “Why don’t your disciples live by the tradition of the elders? Why do they eat bread with defiled hands?”
v06: He said to them, “Isaiah was right to prophesy about you hypocrites, as it is written:
‘This people honors me with their lips,
but their heart is far from me.
v07: Their worship of me is in vain,
for they teach human commands as doctrines.’
v08: You let go of God’s command and hold on to human tradition.”
</p>

<p>
v09: He went on, “You have a fine way of setting aside God’s command so that you can keep your own tradition!
v10: For Moses said, ‘Honor your father and your mother,’ and, ‘Whoever speaks evil of father or mother must be put to death.’
v11: But you say that if someone tells their father or mother, ‘Whatever you might have received from me is Corban’—that is, a gift dedicated to God—
v12: you no longer let that person do anything for their father or mother.
v13: You make God’s word ineffective by the tradition you have handed down. And you do many things like this.”
</p>

## What Defiles a Person

<p>
v14: He called the crowd to him again and said, “Listen to me, all of you, and understand.
v15: Nothing outside a person can defile them by going into them. It is what comes out of a person that defiles them.”
</p>

<p>
v17: After he left the crowd and went into a house, his disciples asked him about the parable.
v18: He said to them, “Are you too without understanding? Don’t you see that nothing entering a person from outside can defile them,
v19: because it does not enter the heart but the stomach, and then goes out into the latrine?” In saying this, he made all foods clean.
v20: He went on, “What comes out of a person is what defiles them.
v21: For from within, from the human heart, come evil thoughts, sexual immorality, theft, murder,
v22: adultery, greed, wickedness, deceit, shameless conduct, envy, slander, arrogance, and foolishness.
v23: All these evil things come from within and defile a person.”
</p>

## The Syrophoenician Woman

<p>
v24: Jesus left there and went to the region of Tyre. He entered a house and wanted no one to know, but he could not go unnoticed.
v25: At once a woman whose little daughter had an unclean spirit heard about him. She came and fell at his feet.
v26: The woman was a Gentile, Syrophoenician by birth. She begged him to drive the demon out of her daughter.
v27: He said to her, “Let the children eat their fill first. It is not right to take the children’s bread and throw it to the dogs.”
v28: She answered, “Lord, even the dogs under the table eat the children’s crumbs.”
v29: He said to her, “Because of what you have said, go. The demon has left your daughter.”
v30: She went home and found the child lying on the bed, and the demon gone.
</p>

## A Deaf Man Hears and Speaks Clearly

<p>
v31: Jesus left the region of Tyre again and went through Sidon to the Sea of Galilee, through the region of the Decapolis.
v32: People brought him a man who was deaf and had difficulty speaking. They begged Jesus to lay his hand on him.
v33: Jesus took him aside, away from the crowd, and put his fingers into the man’s ears. Then he spat and touched the man’s tongue.
v34: He looked up to heaven, sighed, and said to him, “Ephphatha,” which means, “Be opened!”
v35: His ears were opened, the bond on his tongue was released, and he spoke clearly.
v36: Jesus told them to tell no one. But the more he told them not to, the more widely they proclaimed it.
v37: They were utterly astonished and said, “He has done everything well. He even makes the deaf hear and those who cannot speak talk!”
</p>

## Notes

v03: ‘In the prescribed way’ renders an uncertain expression, literally ‘with the fist.’ It may describe how the hands were washed; ‘carefully’ is another interpretation. The exact gesture is uncertain.

v03–04: These verses are the narrator’s explanation of the customs at issue. The broad phrase ‘all the Jews’ is retained as part of that description, not expanded into a claim about every Jewish community in every period.

v16: Verse 16 is absent from the Greek text followed here. The numbering gap is retained.

v19: The closing words are literally ‘cleansing all foods.’ The masculine participle is taken here as a comment about Jesus and his teaching. Another interpretation connects it with the process of digestion and elimination.

v22: ‘Envy’ renders the expression ‘evil eye,’ which can suggest a begrudging or ungenerous attitude.

v26: ‘Gentile’ is literally ‘Greek’ here, in contrast to Jewish identity. ‘Syrophoenician by birth’ gives the woman’s more specific background.

v27–28: The word for ‘dogs’ is a diminutive that can refer to small or household dogs. This does not remove the contrast between children and dogs in the exchange. ‘Lord’ can also be a respectful address, ‘Sir.’

## Vocabulary

v02: **κοινός (koinos)**: Common or defiled; here contrasted with hands washed according to the tradition.

v03: **παράδοσις (paradosis)**: Tradition, something handed down.

v11: **Κορβᾶν (Korban)**: Corban, a gift dedicated to God; the designation here affects what can be given to a parent.

v32: **μογιλάλος (mogilalos)**: Having difficulty speaking.

v34: **Εφφαθα (Ephphatha)**: An Aramaic command, explained in the narrative as ‘Be opened.’
