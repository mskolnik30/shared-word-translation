## Jesus Enters Jerusalem

<p>
v01: As they neared Jerusalem, at Bethphage and Bethany by the Mount of Olives, Jesus sent two of his disciples ahead.
v02: He told them, “Go into the village opposite you. As soon as you enter it, you will find a colt tied there that no one has ever ridden. Untie it and bring it here.
v03: If anyone asks you why you are doing this, say, ‘The Lord needs it and will send it back here at once.’”
v04: They went and found a colt tied outside a doorway in the street, and they untied it.
v05: Some people standing there asked, “What are you doing, untying that colt?”
v06: They answered just as Jesus had told them, and the people let them go.
v07: They brought the colt to Jesus and put their cloaks on it. Then he sat on it.
v08: Many spread their cloaks on the road, while others spread leafy branches they had cut from the fields.
v09: Those walking ahead and those following were shouting:
“Hosanna!
Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord!
v10: Blessed is the coming kingdom of our father David!
Hosanna in the highest!”
v11: Jesus entered Jerusalem and went into the temple. He looked around at everything, but it was already late, so he went out to Bethany with the Twelve.
</p>

## The Fig Tree and the Temple

<p>
v12: The next day, as they were leaving Bethany, Jesus became hungry.
v13: He saw a leafy fig tree in the distance and went to see whether he might find anything on it. When he reached it, he found nothing but leaves, for it was not the season for figs.
v14: He said to the tree, “May no one ever eat fruit from you again.” His disciples heard him.
</p>

<p>
v15: They came to Jerusalem, and Jesus entered the temple. He began driving out those buying and selling there. He overturned the money changers' tables and the seats of those selling doves,
v16: and he would not let anyone carry goods through the temple.
v17: As he taught them, he said, “Is it not written, ‘My house will be called a house of prayer for all nations’? But you have made it a den of robbers.”
v18: The chief priests and scribes heard this and began looking for a way to destroy him. They were afraid of him, because the whole crowd was astonished at his teaching.
v19: When evening came, Jesus and his disciples would leave the city.
</p>

## Faith, Prayer, and Forgiveness

<p>
v20: As they passed by early in the morning, they saw the fig tree withered from its roots.
v21: Peter remembered and said to Jesus, “Rabbi, look! The fig tree you cursed has withered.”
v22: Jesus answered them, “Have faith in God.
v23: Truly I tell you, whoever says to this mountain, ‘Be lifted up and thrown into the sea,’ and does not doubt in their heart but believes that what they say is happening, will have it happen for them.
v24: That is why I tell you: whatever you pray and ask for, believe that you have received it, and it will be yours.
v25: And whenever you stand praying, forgive anyone you have anything against, so that your Father in heaven may also forgive your offenses.”
</p>

## Jesus’ Authority Is Questioned

<p>
v27: They came to Jerusalem again. As Jesus walked in the temple, the chief priests, scribes, and elders came to him.
v28: “By what authority are you doing these things?” they asked. “Who gave you this authority to do them?”
v29: Jesus told them, “I will ask you one question. Answer me, and I will tell you by what authority I do these things.
v30: Was John's baptism from heaven or from human beings? Answer me.”
v31: They discussed it among themselves: “What should we say? If we say, ‘From heaven,’ he will ask, ‘Then why did you not believe him?’
v32: But can we say, ‘From human beings’?” They were afraid of the crowd, for everyone regarded John as a true prophet.
v33: So they answered Jesus, “We do not know.” Jesus told them, “Then neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things.”
</p>

## Notes

v03: The promise to send the colt back is read here as part of the reply the disciples are to give. The clause can also be read as Jesus describing what the person questioning them will do.

v13: The narrator explicitly says it was not the season for figs. That tension is retained rather than explained away.

v19: The Greek construction can describe a customary departure: ‘whenever evening came, they would leave.’

v23–24: ‘This mountain’ is not identified further. Verse 24 says ‘you have received,’ followed by a future promise; the difference in tense is retained.

v26: Verse 26 is absent from the Greek text followed here. The numbering gap is retained.

v31: The Greek text followed here includes the leaders' question, ‘What should we say?’

## Vocabulary

v02: **πῶλος (pōlos)**: Colt or young animal; Mark does not add a second animal.

v09: **ὡσαννά (hōsanna)**: Hosanna, a cry meaning ‘save, please,’ used here in acclamation.

v15: **ἱερόν (hieron)**: Temple or temple precincts, the wider sacred complex.

v16: **σκεῦος (skeuos)**: An object, vessel, or item of equipment; here rendered collectively as ‘goods.’

v22: **πίστις (pistis)**: Faith or trust; here directed toward God.

v28: **ἐξουσία (exousia)**: Authority; the repeated subject of the challenge and reply.
