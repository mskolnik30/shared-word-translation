## A Plot and an Anointing

<p>
v01: It was two days before Passover and the Festival of Unleavened Bread. The chief priests and scribes were looking for a cunning way to arrest Jesus and kill him,
v02: for they were saying, “Not during the festival, or there may be an uproar among the people.”
</p>

<p>
v03: While Jesus was in Bethany, reclining at the table in the house of Simon the leper, a woman came with an alabaster jar of very expensive perfume made from pure nard. She broke the jar and poured the perfume over his head.
v04: Some there were indignant and said to one another, “Why has this perfume been wasted?
v05: It could have been sold for more than three hundred denarii and the money given to the poor.” And they scolded her harshly.
v06: But Jesus said, “Leave her alone. Why are you causing her trouble? She has done a beautiful work for me.
v07: You always have the poor with you, and whenever you want, you can do good for them. But you will not always have me.
v08: She did what she could. She anointed my body beforehand for burial.
v09: Truly I tell you, wherever the good news is proclaimed in the whole world, what she has done will also be told in memory of her.”
</p>

## Judas Agrees to Hand Jesus Over

<p>
v10: Then Judas Iscariot, one of the Twelve, went to the chief priests in order to hand Jesus over to them.
v11: When they heard this, they were glad and promised to give him money. So he began looking for an opportunity to hand Jesus over.
</p>

## Passover and the Supper

<p>
v12: On the first day of Unleavened Bread, when the Passover lambs were sacrificed, Jesus’ disciples asked him, “Where do you want us to go and prepare for you to eat the Passover?”
v13: He sent two of his disciples and told them, “Go into the city. A man carrying a jar of water will meet you. Follow him.
v14: Wherever he enters, say to the owner of the house, ‘The Teacher says, “Where is my guest room, where I may eat the Passover with my disciples?”’
v15: He will show you a large upstairs room, furnished and ready. Prepare the meal for us there.”
v16: The disciples left, went into the city, and found everything just as he had told them. They prepared the Passover.
v17: When evening came, Jesus arrived with the Twelve.
v18: As they reclined at the table and ate, Jesus said, “Truly I tell you, one of you will hand me over—one who is eating with me.”
v19: They began to grieve and ask him one after another, “Surely not I?”
v20: He said to them, “It is one of the Twelve—one who dips bread into the bowl with me.
v21: The Son of Man goes just as it is written about him, but woe to that person through whom the Son of Man is handed over! It would have been better for that person if he had not been born.”
</p>

<p>
v22: While they were eating, Jesus took bread, spoke a blessing, broke it, gave it to them, and said, “Take it; this is my body.”
v23: Then he took a cup, gave thanks, and gave it to them, and they all drank from it.
v24: He said to them, “This is my blood of the covenant, poured out for many.
v25: Truly I tell you, I will certainly not drink again from the fruit of the vine until that day when I drink it new in God’s kingdom.”
v26: After singing a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives.
</p>

## Peter’s Denial Foretold

<p>
v27: Jesus said to them, “All of you will fall away, because it is written,
‘I will strike the shepherd,
and the sheep will be scattered.’
v28: But after I am raised, I will go ahead of you into Galilee.”
v29: Peter said to him, “Even if everyone else falls away, I will not.”
v30: Jesus said to him, “Truly I tell you, today—this very night—before a rooster crows twice, you will deny me three times.”
v31: But Peter kept insisting, “Even if I must die with you, I will never deny you.” And all the others said the same.
</p>

## Gethsemane

<p>
v32: They came to a place called Gethsemane, and Jesus told his disciples, “Sit here while I pray.”
v33: He took Peter, James, and John with him and began to be overwhelmed and deeply troubled.
v34: He said to them, “My soul is overwhelmed with sorrow to the point of death. Stay here and stay awake.”
v35: Going a little farther, he fell to the ground and prayed that, if possible, the hour might pass from him.
v36: He said, “Abba, Father, everything is possible for you. Take this cup away from me. Yet not what I want, but what you want.”
v37: He returned and found them sleeping. He said to Peter, “Simon, are you sleeping? Could you not stay awake for one hour?
v38: Stay awake and pray so that you do not enter into testing. The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak.”
v39: He went away again and prayed, saying the same words.
v40: When he returned again, he found them sleeping, for their eyes were weighed down. They did not know what to answer him.
v41: He came a third time and said to them, “Are you still sleeping and resting? Enough! The hour has come. Look, the Son of Man is being handed over into the hands of sinners.
v42: Get up; let us go. Look, the one handing me over has come near.”
</p>

## Jesus Is Arrested

<p>
v43: Immediately, while Jesus was still speaking, Judas arrived—one of the Twelve. With him was a crowd armed with swords and clubs, sent by the chief priests, scribes, and elders.
v44: The one handing Jesus over had given them a signal: “The one I kiss is the man. Arrest him and lead him away securely.”
v45: As soon as Judas arrived, he went straight to Jesus, said, “Rabbi!” and kissed him.
v46: They laid their hands on Jesus and arrested him.
v47: One of those standing nearby drew his sword, struck the high priest’s slave, and cut off his ear.
v48: Jesus said to them, “Have you come out with swords and clubs to arrest me as though I were a bandit?
v49: Day after day I was with you, teaching in the temple, and you did not arrest me. But let the Scriptures be fulfilled.”
v50: Then everyone abandoned him and fled.
v51: A young man was following him, wearing nothing but a linen cloth over his naked body. They seized him,
v52: but he left the linen cloth behind and fled naked.
</p>

## Jesus Before the Council

<p>
v53: They led Jesus away to the high priest, and all the chief priests, elders, and scribes assembled.
v54: Peter followed him at a distance, right into the high priest’s courtyard. He sat with the attendants, warming himself by the fire.
v55: The chief priests and the whole council were looking for testimony against Jesus so they could put him to death, but they could find none.
v56: Many gave false testimony against him, but their testimonies did not agree.
v57: Then some stood up and gave false testimony against him, saying,
v58: “We heard him say, ‘I will tear down this temple made by hands and in three days build another not made by hands.’”
v59: Yet even on this point their testimony did not agree.
v60: The high priest stood up in their midst and questioned Jesus: “Do you have no answer? What is this testimony these people are giving against you?”
v61: But Jesus remained silent and gave no answer. Again the high priest questioned him: “Are you the Messiah, the Son of the Blessed One?”
v62: Jesus said, “I am. And you will see the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of Power and coming with the clouds of heaven.”
v63: The high priest tore his garments and said, “Why do we need any more witnesses?
v64: You heard the blasphemy. What is your verdict?” They all condemned him as deserving death.
v65: Some began to spit on him, cover his face, strike him with their fists, and say, “Prophesy!” The attendants also struck him with slaps.
</p>

## Peter Denies Jesus

<p>
v66: While Peter was below in the courtyard, one of the high priest’s servant girls came by.
v67: When she saw Peter warming himself, she looked closely at him and said, “You also were with Jesus the Nazarene.”
v68: But Peter denied it: “I neither know nor understand what you are talking about.” He went out into the forecourt, and a rooster crowed.
v69: The servant girl saw him and began again to tell those standing there, “This man is one of them.”
v70: But Peter denied it again. After a little while, those standing there said to him again, “Surely you are one of them, for you are a Galilean, and your speech sounds like it.”
v71: But he began to call down curses and swear, “I do not know this man you are talking about!”
v72: Immediately a rooster crowed a second time. Peter remembered what Jesus had said to him: “Before a rooster crows twice, you will deny me three times.” And he broke down and wept.
</p>

## Notes

v03: The adjective describing the nard can mean ‘pure’ or ‘genuine’; its exact nuance is uncertain. The perfume is explicitly very expensive.

v07: The sentence says that the poor remain present and can be helped whenever the hearers choose. Its immediate contrast concerns Jesus’ approaching death.

v24: The Greek text followed here says ‘my blood of the covenant,’ without the expanded word ‘new.’

v30, 68, 72: The Greek text followed here includes two rooster crowings. Other manuscript traditions vary in these details.

v36: ‘Abba’ is an Aramaic address retained alongside its Greek equivalent, ‘Father.’ Jesus’ desire and his submission are both stated.

v41: The word rendered ‘Enough!’ is difficult and has been understood in several ways. Here it marks the transition to the arrived hour and betrayer.

v47: The assailant is unnamed, the injured person is the high priest’s slave, and the object cut off is his ear. Details from other Gospel accounts are not added.

v62: ‘Power’ is a reverential way of referring to God. The answer joins the Son of Man’s enthronement with coming in heaven’s clouds.

v72: The final verb is difficult. ‘He broke down and wept’ represents Peter’s collapse into weeping without claiming certainty about the verb’s every nuance.
