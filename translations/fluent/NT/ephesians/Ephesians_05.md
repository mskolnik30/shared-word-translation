## Walk in Love and Light

<p>
v01: Therefore imitate God as beloved children,
v02: and walk in love, just as Christ loved us and gave himself for us, an offering and sacrifice to God as a fragrant aroma.
v03: But sexual immorality and every kind of impurity or greed must not even be named among you, as is fitting for holy people,
v04: nor obscenity, foolish talk, or crude joking, which are out of place, but rather thanksgiving.
v05: For know this clearly: no sexually immoral, impure, or greedy person—who is an idolater—has an inheritance in Christ’s and God’s kingdom.
v06: Let no one deceive you with empty words, because because of these things God’s wrath comes on the children of disobedience.
v07: Therefore do not become partners with them.
v08: For you were once darkness, but now you are light in the Lord. Walk as children of light—
v09: for the fruit of the light consists in every kind of goodness, righteousness, and truth—
v10: testing what is pleasing to the Lord.
v11: Do not participate in the fruitless works of darkness; instead, expose them.
v12: For it is shameful even to speak about the things they do in secret.
v13: But everything exposed by the light becomes visible,
v14: for everything made visible is light. Therefore it says,
“Wake up, sleeper,
and rise from the dead,
and Christ will shine on you.”
</p>

## Walk Wisely and Be Filled with the Spirit

<p>
v15: Therefore watch carefully how you walk—not as unwise people but as wise,
v16: making the most of the time, because the days are evil.
v17: For this reason do not be foolish, but understand what the Lord’s will is.
v18: Do not get drunk with wine, which leads to reckless living, but be filled with the Spirit,
v19: speaking to one another with psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, singing and making music in your heart to the Lord,
v20: giving thanks always for everything to God the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ,
v21: submitting to one another in reverence for Christ.
</p>

## Marriage and the Mystery of Christ and the Church

<p>
v22: Wives, submit to your own husbands as to the Lord,
v23: because the husband is head of the wife as Christ also is head of the church—he himself being Savior of the body.
v24: But as the church submits to Christ, so wives should submit to their husbands in everything.
v25: Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself for her,
v26: so that he might make her holy, cleansing her with the washing of water by the word,
v27: so that he might present the church to himself in splendor, without spot or wrinkle or anything like that, but holy and blameless.
v28: In the same way husbands ought to love their wives as their own bodies. The one who loves his wife loves himself.
v29: For no one ever hated their own flesh, but nourishes and cares for it, just as Christ does the church,
v30: because we are members of his body.
v31: “For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two will become one flesh.”
v32: This mystery is great, but I am speaking about Christ and the church.
v33: Nevertheless, each one of you must love his own wife as himself, and the wife must respect her husband.
</p>

## Notes

v01–02: Imitating God is immediately defined through Christ-shaped self-giving love.

v05: Greed is explicitly called idolatry, turning acquisitiveness into a worship issue rather than merely poor financial discipline.

v08: Paul says believers were “darkness,” not merely “in darkness,” and now are “light in the Lord.”

v14: The source of this poetic line is uncertain; it may preserve an early Christian hymn or liturgical adaptation rather than a direct Old Testament quotation.

v16: `exagorazō ton kairon` is literally to buy up the opportunity or time. “Making the most of the time” conveys purposeful use of a dangerous moment.

v18–21: Being filled with the Spirit is followed grammatically by communal singing, heart worship, thanksgiving, and mutual submission.

v21: Mutual submission forms the grammatical bridge into the household instructions that follow.

v22: The oldest Greek form depends on the verb “submit” from v21; Fluent supplies it in English because the sentence requires it.

v23: “Head” is a live metaphor whose nuances are debated. Fluent retains it rather than translating it as “boss,” “source,” or another interpretive substitute.

v25: The command to husbands is not “rule” but love patterned on Christ’s self-giving for the church.

v31–32: Genesis’ one-flesh marriage text is read as a “great mystery” concerning Christ and the church without erasing its marital sense.
