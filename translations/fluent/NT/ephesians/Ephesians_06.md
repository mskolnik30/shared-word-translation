## Children, Parents, Slaves, and Masters

<p>
v01: Children, obey your parents in the Lord, because this is right.
v02: “Honor your father and mother”—which is the first commandment with a promise—
v03: “so that it may go well with you and you may live long on the earth.”
v04: Fathers, do not provoke your children to anger, but bring them up in the discipline and instruction of the Lord.
v05: Slaves, obey your earthly masters with fear and trembling, in sincerity of heart, as you would Christ,
v06: not with eye-service as people-pleasers, but as Christ’s slaves doing God’s will from the heart.
v07: Serve with goodwill as serving the Lord and not people,
v08: knowing that whatever good each person does, they will receive back from the Lord, whether slave or free.
v09: Masters, do the same things toward them, giving up threats, knowing that both their Master and yours is in heaven and that there is no partiality with him.
</p>

## The Armor of God

<p>
v10: Finally, be strengthened in the Lord and in the power of his might.
v11: Put on the full armor of God so that you may be able to stand against the devil’s schemes.
v12: For our struggle is not against flesh and blood but against rulers, authorities, the cosmic powers of this darkness, and spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms.
v13: Therefore take up the full armor of God so that you may be able to resist in the evil day and, after accomplishing everything, to stand.
v14: Stand, therefore, with truth fastened around your waist, wearing the breastplate of righteousness,
v15: and with your feet prepared with the readiness of the good news of peace.
v16: In every situation take up the shield of trust, with which you will be able to extinguish all the flaming arrows of the evil one.
v17: Take the helmet of salvation and the sword of the Spirit, which is God’s word.
v18: Pray at every time in the Spirit with every kind of prayer and request. To this end stay alert with all perseverance and prayer for all the holy ones,
v19: and also for me, that a word may be given to me when I open my mouth to make known the mystery of the good news with boldness,
v20: for which I am an ambassador in chains. Pray that I may speak boldly in it as I ought to speak.
</p>

## Final Greetings

<p>
v21: So that you also may know about my circumstances and what I am doing, Tychicus, the beloved brother and faithful servant in the Lord, will tell you everything.
v22: I am sending him to you for this very reason, so that you may know about us and he may encourage your hearts.
v23: Peace to the brothers and sisters, and love with trust, from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.
v24: Grace be with all who love our Lord Jesus Christ with undying love.
</p>

## Notes

v01–04: Children are addressed directly as moral members of the worshiping community. Fathers are also constrained: parental authority must not become provocation.

v05–09: The household code speaks inside the first-century institution of slavery rather than directly abolishing it. Yet both enslaved people and masters are placed under the same heavenly Lord, and masters are forbidden threats and reminded that God shows no partiality. Fluent does not sanitize the word `doulos` into an employee relationship.

v05: “Fear and trembling” is conventional language of serious responsibility; the object is not necessarily terror before abusive masters.

v09: “Do the same things” places masters under the same Lord-oriented ethic just applied to enslaved believers.

v10–17: The armor imagery draws heavily on Isaiah’s depiction of God or God’s agent wearing righteousness and salvation. The community is equipped with God’s own armor.

v12: The struggle is explicitly not against “flesh and blood,” resisting the temptation to demonize human opponents.

v16: `pistis` is “trust” here as a protective posture toward God, though “faith” remains the traditional term.

v17: `rhēma theou` is “God’s word/saying,” not necessarily a reference to a bound Bible as a physical object.

v18–20: Prayer is not another detached piece of armor; it saturates the entire posture of standing and includes intercession for Paul’s bold speech in chains.

v24: `aphtharsia` can mean immortality, incorruptibility, or undying quality. “Undying love” preserves the relational force.
