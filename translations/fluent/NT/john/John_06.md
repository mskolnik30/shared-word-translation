## Jesus Feeds Five Thousand

<p>
v01: After this Jesus went to the other side of the Sea of Galilee, also called the Sea of Tiberias.
v02: A large crowd followed him because they were seeing the signs he was doing for the sick.
v03: Jesus went up the mountain and sat down there with his disciples.
v04: The Jewish Passover festival was near.
v05: When Jesus looked up and saw a large crowd coming toward him, he said to Philip, “Where can we buy bread so these people may eat?”
v06: He said this to test him, because he himself knew what he was about to do.
v07: Philip answered, “Two hundred denarii worth of bread would not be enough for each person to receive even a little.”
v08: One of his disciples, Andrew, Simon Peter’s brother, said,
v09: “There is a boy here who has five barley loaves and two fish, but what are these for so many people?”
v10: Jesus said, “Have the people sit down.” There was plenty of grass in that place, so they sat down, about five thousand men.
v11: Jesus took the loaves, gave thanks, and distributed them to those sitting there. He did the same with the fish, as much as they wanted.
v12: When they had eaten their fill, he told his disciples, “Gather the pieces that remain so that nothing is lost.”
v13: So they gathered them and filled twelve baskets with pieces from the five barley loaves left over by those who had eaten.
v14: When the people saw the sign he had done, they said, “This truly is the Prophet who is coming into the world.”
v15: Jesus knew they were about to come and seize him to make him king, so he withdrew again to the mountain by himself.
</p>

## Jesus Walks on the Sea

<p>
v16: When evening came, his disciples went down to the sea,
v17: got into a boat, and started across the sea toward Capernaum. It had already become dark, and Jesus had not yet come to them.
v18: A strong wind was blowing, and the sea was becoming rough.
v19: After rowing about twenty-five or thirty stadia, they saw Jesus walking on the sea and coming near the boat, and they were afraid.
v20: But he said to them, “I am. Do not be afraid.”
v21: Then they wanted to take him into the boat, and immediately the boat reached the land toward which they were going.
</p>

## The Crowd Seeks Jesus

<p>
v22: The next day the crowd that had stayed on the other side of the sea saw that only one boat had been there. They knew Jesus had not entered the boat with his disciples but that the disciples had gone away alone.
v23: Other boats from Tiberias came near the place where they had eaten the bread after the Lord gave thanks.
v24: When the crowd saw that neither Jesus nor his disciples were there, they got into the boats and went to Capernaum looking for Jesus.
v25: When they found him on the other side of the sea, they asked, “Rabbi, when did you get here?”
v26: Jesus answered, “Truly, truly, I tell you, you are looking for me not because you saw signs but because you ate the loaves and were filled.
v27: Do not work for food that perishes, but for food that remains to eternal life, which the Son of Man will give you. For God the Father has set his seal on him.”
v28: They asked, “What must we do to perform the works of God?”
v29: Jesus answered, “This is the work of God: that you trust in the one he sent.”
v30: So they said, “What sign then will you do so we may see and trust you? What work will you perform?
v31: Our ancestors ate the manna in the wilderness, as it is written, ‘He gave them bread from heaven to eat.’”
v32: Jesus said, “Truly, truly, I tell you, Moses did not give you the bread from heaven. My Father gives you the true bread from heaven.
v33: For God’s bread is the one who comes down from heaven and gives life to the world.”
v34: They said, “Lord, give us this bread always.”
</p>

## The Bread of Life

<p>
v35: Jesus said, “I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to me will never be hungry, and whoever trusts in me will never be thirsty.
v36: But I told you: you have seen me and still do not trust.
v37: Everything the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will certainly never cast out,
v38: because I have come down from heaven not to do my own will but the will of the one who sent me.
v39: This is the will of the one who sent me: that I lose nothing of everything he has given me but raise it up on the last day.
v40: For this is my Father’s will: everyone who sees the Son and trusts in him has eternal life, and I will raise that person up on the last day.”
v41: Then some Jewish people began grumbling about him because he said, “I am the bread that came down from heaven.”
v42: They said, “Is this not Jesus son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know? How can he now say, ‘I have come down from heaven’?”
v43: Jesus answered, “Stop grumbling among yourselves.
v44: No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws that person, and I will raise them up on the last day.
v45: It is written in the Prophets, ‘They will all be taught by God.’ Everyone who has heard and learned from the Father comes to me.
v46: Not that anyone has seen the Father except the one who is from God; he has seen the Father.
v47: Truly, truly, I tell you, the one who trusts has eternal life.
v48: I am the bread of life.
v49: Your ancestors ate the manna in the wilderness and died.
v50: This is the bread that comes down from heaven so that anyone may eat from it and not die.
v51: I am the living bread that came down from heaven. If anyone eats from this bread, they will live forever. And the bread I will give for the life of the world is my flesh.”
v52: Then the Jewish people began arguing with one another: “How can this man give us his flesh to eat?”
v53: Jesus said, “Truly, truly, I tell you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you do not have life in yourselves.
v54: Whoever eats my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life, and I will raise that person up on the last day.
v55: For my flesh is true food, and my blood is true drink.
v56: Whoever eats my flesh and drinks my blood remains in me, and I in them.
v57: Just as the living Father sent me and I live because of the Father, so the one who eats me will live because of me.
v58: This is the bread that came down from heaven, not like what the ancestors ate and died. Whoever eats this bread will live forever.”
v59: He said these things while teaching in the synagogue in Capernaum.
</p>

## Words of Eternal Life

<p>
v60: When many of his disciples heard this, they said, “This teaching is hard. Who can listen to it?”
v61: Jesus knew within himself that his disciples were grumbling about this, so he said, “Does this cause you to stumble?
v62: Then what if you see the Son of Man ascending to where he was before?
v63: The Spirit is the one who gives life; the flesh is of no help. The words I have spoken to you are Spirit and life.
v64: But there are some among you who do not trust.” For Jesus knew from the beginning who did not trust and who would hand him over.
v65: He said, “This is why I told you that no one can come to me unless it has been granted by the Father.”
v66: Because of this, many of his disciples turned back and no longer walked with him.
v67: Jesus said to the twelve, “You do not want to go away too, do you?”
v68: Simon Peter answered, “Lord, to whom will we go? You have words of eternal life.
v69: We have trusted and have come to know that you are the Holy One of God.”
v70: Jesus answered them, “Did I not choose you, the twelve? Yet one of you is a devil.”
v71: He was speaking about Judas son of Simon Iscariot, because he, one of the twelve, was going to hand Jesus over.
</p>

## Notes

v04: John again identifies Passover as a Jewish festival while narrating Jesus and his disciples fully within that festival world.

v14–15: The crowd correctly recognizes prophetic significance but moves toward a political kingship Jesus refuses.

v20: `egō eimi` can be ordinary reassurance, “It is I,” while also resonating with Johannine divine self-identification. Fluent retains “I am.”

v27–29: The crowd asks for “works”; Jesus singularly names trust in the one God sent as “the work of God.”

v35: Bread, hunger, and thirst are interpreted relationally in terms of coming to and trusting Jesus.

v41–52: Here `Ioudaioi` appears as a broader group engaged in synagogue dispute, not merely an official leadership body; Fluent therefore uses “Jewish people.”

v44: `helkyō`, “draw,” is strong attraction language without describing coercion or the mechanism of coming to Jesus.

v51–58: Eating flesh and drinking blood are deliberately shocking participation language. Fluent does not reduce the passage to either a purely sacramental or purely metaphorical explanation.

v55: Some manuscripts vary between “true” and “real” forms; Fluent retains “true,” consistent with John’s truth network.

v63: “Flesh” here is contrasted with Spirit as source of life. It cannot simply mean Jesus’ flesh is useless, since the preceding discourse gives his flesh life-giving significance.

v69: SBLGNT reads “the Holy One of God,” not later expanded christological readings. Fluent follows the pinned text.
