## So That You Do Not Stumble

<p>
v01: “I have told you these things so that you do not stumble.
v02: They will put you out of the synagogues. In fact, an hour is coming when everyone who kills you will think they are offering service to God.
v03: They will do these things because they have not known the Father or me.
v04: But I have told you these things so that when their hour comes, you may remember that I told you. I did not tell you these things from the beginning because I was with you.
</p>

## The Advocate and the World

<p>
v05: “But now I am going to the one who sent me, and none of you asks me, ‘Where are you going?’
v06: Instead, because I have told you these things, sorrow has filled your hearts.
v07: But I tell you the truth: it is better for you that I go away. For if I do not go away, the Advocate will not come to you. But if I go, I will send him to you.
v08: When he comes, he will expose the world concerning sin, righteousness, and judgment:
v09: concerning sin, because they do not trust in me;
v10: concerning righteousness, because I am going to the Father and you will no longer see me;
v11: concerning judgment, because the ruler of this world has been judged.
</p>

## The Spirit of Truth

<p>
v12: “I still have many things to tell you, but you cannot bear them now.
v13: When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth. For he will not speak from himself, but whatever he hears he will speak, and he will announce to you the things that are coming.
v14: He will glorify me because he will take from what is mine and announce it to you.
v15: Everything the Father has is mine. This is why I said that he takes from what is mine and will announce it to you.
</p>

## Sorrow Turned to Joy

<p>
v16: “A little while, and you will no longer see me; and again a little while, and you will see me.”
v17: Some of his disciples said to one another, “What does he mean by saying, ‘A little while, and you will not see me, and again a little while, and you will see me,’ and, ‘because I am going to the Father’?”
v18: They kept saying, “What is this ‘little while’ he speaks about? We do not know what he means.”
v19: Jesus knew they wanted to ask him, so he said, “Are you discussing with one another what I meant when I said, ‘A little while, and you will not see me, and again a little while, and you will see me’?
v20: Truly, truly, I tell you, you will weep and mourn, but the world will rejoice. You will be sorrowful, but your sorrow will become joy.
v21: When a woman gives birth, she has sorrow because her hour has come. But when she has given birth to the child, she no longer remembers the distress because of the joy that a human being has been born into the world.
v22: So you also have sorrow now, but I will see you again, and your hearts will rejoice, and no one will take your joy from you.
</p>

## Ask the Father in My Name

<p>
v23: “On that day you will ask me nothing. Truly, truly, I tell you, whatever you ask the Father in my name, he will give you.
v24: Until now you have asked nothing in my name. Ask, and you will receive, so that your joy may be complete.
v25: I have spoken these things to you in figures of speech. An hour is coming when I will no longer speak to you in figures but will tell you plainly about the Father.
v26: On that day you will ask in my name. I am not telling you that I will ask the Father for you,
v27: because the Father himself loves you because you have loved me and trusted that I came from God.
v28: I came from the Father and have come into the world. Again, I am leaving the world and going to the Father.”
</p>

## You Will Be Scattered

<p>
v29: His disciples said, “Look, now you are speaking plainly and not using a figure of speech.
v30: Now we know that you know everything and do not need anyone to question you. Because of this, we trust that you came from God.”
v31: Jesus answered them, “Do you trust now?
v32: Look—an hour is coming, and has come, when you will be scattered, each to your own place, and you will leave me alone. Yet I am not alone, because the Father is with me.
v33: I have told you these things so that in me you may have peace. In the world you have distress. But take courage: I have overcome the world.”
</p>

## Notes

v01: `skandalizō` means stumble or fall away. Jesus’ warnings are meant to keep persecution from becoming a crisis of trust.

v02: The violence described is religiously rationalized by its perpetrators as service to God. The verse describes persecutors’ perception, not genuine divine worship.

v08: `elenchō` can mean expose, convict, or demonstrate wrong. Fluent uses “expose” because the following clauses explain what the world’s misunderstanding is.

v10: “Righteousness” is connected with Jesus’ going to the Father, making his vindication central to the clause.

v11: Judgment is announced first as judgment already rendered on “the ruler of this world.”

v13: “All the truth” belongs to the Spirit’s continuing witness to Jesus; the verse is not a blank authorization for claims disconnected from Jesus’ teaching.

v16–22: The repeated “little while” remains intentionally frustrating until Jesus interprets it through sorrow becoming joy.

v21: Birth imagery frames the disciples’ suffering as real distress that gives way to new-life joy.

v23: The Greek verb for “ask” can shift between questioning Jesus and petitioning the Father. Fluent uses the contextual senses.

v26–27: Jesus does not say he ceases intercession elsewhere; the point here is direct access grounded in the Father’s own love.

v32: The disciples’ confident confession is immediately qualified by their coming desertion.

v33: “I have overcome the world” closes the discourse section with completed victory spoken before the arrest.
