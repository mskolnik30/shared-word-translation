## The Wedding at Cana

<p>
v01: On the third day there was a wedding in Cana of Galilee, and Jesus’ mother was there.
v02: Jesus and his disciples were also invited to the wedding.
v03: When the wine ran out, Jesus’ mother said to him, “They have no wine.”
v04: Jesus said to her, “Woman, what does this have to do with you and me? My hour has not yet come.”
v05: His mother told the servants, “Do whatever he tells you.”
v06: Six stone water jars were standing there for Jewish purification, each holding two or three measures.
v07: Jesus told the servants, “Fill the jars with water.” They filled them to the brim.
v08: Then he said, “Now draw some out and take it to the master of the banquet.” They took it.
v09: When the master of the banquet tasted the water that had become wine, he did not know where it came from, though the servants who drew the water knew. He called the bridegroom
v10: and said, “Everyone serves the good wine first, and when people have drunk freely, the inferior wine. You have kept the good wine until now.”
v11: Jesus did this beginning of signs in Cana of Galilee and revealed his glory, and his disciples trusted in him.
v12: After this he went down to Capernaum with his mother, brothers, and disciples, and they stayed there a few days.
</p>

## Jesus in the Temple

<p>
v13: The Jewish Passover was near, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.
v14: In the temple he found people selling cattle, sheep, and doves, and money changers sitting at their tables.
v15: He made a whip from cords and drove them all out of the temple, along with the sheep and cattle. He poured out the money changers’ coins and overturned their tables.
v16: He told those selling doves, “Take these things out of here! Stop making my Father’s house a house of trade.”
v17: His disciples remembered that it is written, “Zeal for your house will consume me.”
v18: The Jewish leaders responded, “What sign can you show us to justify doing these things?”
v19: Jesus answered, “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it.”
v20: The leaders said, “This temple has been under construction for forty-six years, and you will raise it in three days?”
v21: But he was speaking about the temple of his body.
v22: So when he was raised from the dead, his disciples remembered that he had said this, and they trusted the Scripture and the word Jesus had spoken.
</p>

## Jesus Knows What Is in Humanity

<p>
v23: While Jesus was in Jerusalem during the Passover festival, many trusted in his name when they saw the signs he was doing.
v24: But Jesus himself did not entrust himself to them, because he knew everyone
v25: and did not need anyone to testify about a person, because he knew what was in a person.
</p>

## Notes

v04: The idiom literally asks, “What to me and to you?” It creates distance without requiring hostility. “My hour” introduces a major Johannine timeline of revelation, death, and glorification.

v06: The jars are described as serving Jewish purification. The narrative does not say purification itself is false or abolished.

v11: John calls the event the “beginning of signs.” Signs disclose glory but do not guarantee mature trust.

v13: “Jewish Passover” preserves John’s cultural clarification without implying Jesus stands outside Judaism.

v15: The grammar can associate “all” with people and animals in more than one way. Fluent keeps the action concrete without adding violence toward persons beyond what is stated.

v18: In this temple setting, `Ioudaioi` refers to the Jewish authorities challenging Jesus’ right to act.

v19–22: The narrator explicitly identifies Jesus’ body as the temple referent and tells the story through post-resurrection remembrance.

v23–24: John makes a wordplay: many “trusted” in Jesus, but Jesus did not “entrust” himself to them.
