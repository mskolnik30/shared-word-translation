## The Wedding at Cana

<p>
v01: On the third day there was a wedding in Cana of Galilee, and Jesus’ mother was there.
v02: Jesus and his disciples were also invited to the wedding.
v03: When the wine ran out, Jesus’ mother told him, “They have no wine.”
v04: Jesus said to her, “Woman, what does this have to do with you and me? My hour has not yet come.”
v05: His mother told the servants, “Do whatever he tells you.”
v06: Six stone water jars were standing there for Jewish purification, each holding two or three measures.
v07: Jesus told the servants, “Fill the jars with water.” They filled them to the brim.
v08: Then he said, “Now draw some out and take it to the banquet master.” So they took it.
v09: When the banquet master tasted the water that had become wine, he did not know where it came from, though the servants who had drawn the water knew. He called the bridegroom
v10: and said, “Everyone serves the good wine first and then the inferior wine after people have drunk freely. But you have kept the good wine until now.”
v11: Jesus did this in Cana of Galilee as the beginning of his signs. He revealed his glory, and his disciples believed in him.
v12: After this, he went down to Capernaum with his mother, brothers, and disciples, and they stayed there a few days.
</p>

## Jesus in the Temple

<p>
v13: The Jewish Passover was near, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.
v14: In the temple courts he found people selling cattle, sheep, and doves, and the money changers seated at their tables.
v15: He made a whip from cords and drove them all out of the temple courts, including the sheep and cattle. He poured out the money changers’ coins and overturned their tables.
v16: He told those selling doves, “Take these things away from here! Stop making my Father’s house a marketplace!”
v17: His disciples remembered that it is written, “Zeal for your house will consume me.”
v18: The Jewish authorities responded, “What sign can you show us to authorize your doing these things?”
v19: Jesus answered them, “Destroy this sanctuary, and in three days I will raise it.”
v20: The authorities replied, “This sanctuary has been under construction for forty-six years, and you will raise it in three days?”
v21: But he was speaking about the sanctuary of his body.
v22: So when he was raised from the dead, his disciples remembered that he had said this, and they believed the Scripture and the word Jesus had spoken.
</p>

## Jesus Knows What Is in Humanity

<p>
v23: While Jesus was in Jerusalem during the Passover Festival, many believed in his name when they saw the signs he was doing.
v24: But Jesus did not entrust himself to them, because he knew everyone
v25: and did not need anyone to testify about a person, for he knew what was in each person.
</p>

## Notes

v04: ‘What does this have to do with you and me?’ is a distancing idiom, not an insult. ‘Woman’ was a respectful form of address in context.

v06: Each stone jar held two or three ancient measures, probably a very large quantity. The narrative stresses abundant provision rather than requiring a precise modern conversion.

v15: The sentence says Jesus drove everyone out, along with the sheep and cattle; it does not explicitly say the cord whip struck people.

v19–22: Jesus’ hearers understand the sanctuary as the Jerusalem structure; the narrator identifies the sanctuary as Jesus’ body and connects the saying with resurrection.

## Vocabulary

v11: **σημεῖον (sēmeion)**: Sign, an act that reveals Jesus’ identity and glory.

v13: **πάσχα (pascha)**: Passover.

v19: **ναός (naos)**: Sanctuary, especially the temple’s sacred inner structure.

v23: **πιστεύω (pisteuō)**: Believe, trust, or entrust oneself.
