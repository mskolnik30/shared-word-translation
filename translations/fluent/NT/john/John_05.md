## A Man Is Made Well at the Pool

<p>
v01: After this there was a Jewish festival, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.
v02: Now in Jerusalem by the Sheep Gate there is a pool called Bethesda in Hebrew, with five covered walkways.
v03: A multitude of sick people lay in them—people who were blind, lame, or paralyzed.
v05: One man there had lived with his illness for thirty-eight years.
v06: When Jesus saw him lying there and knew he had already been in that condition a long time, he asked, “Do you want to become well?”
v07: The sick man answered, “Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool when the water is stirred. While I am going, someone else goes down ahead of me.”
v08: Jesus told him, “Get up, pick up your mat, and walk.”
v09: Immediately the man became well, picked up his mat, and began walking. That day was a Sabbath.
v10: So the Jewish authorities told the man who had been healed, “It is the Sabbath, and you are not permitted to carry your mat.”
v11: He answered them, “The one who made me well told me, ‘Pick up your mat and walk.’”
v12: They asked him, “Who is the man who told you, ‘Pick it up and walk’?”
v13: But the man who had been healed did not know who it was, because Jesus had slipped away into the crowd that was there.
v14: Afterward, Jesus found him in the temple courts and said, “Look, you have become well. Do not keep sinning, so that nothing worse happens to you.”
v15: The man went away and told the Jewish authorities that Jesus was the one who had made him well.
v16: Because Jesus was doing these things on the Sabbath, the authorities began persecuting him.
v17: But Jesus answered them, “My Father is still working, and I too am working.”
v18: For this reason the authorities sought all the more to kill him, because he was not only loosening the Sabbath but also calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God.
</p>

## The Son Gives Life and Judges

<p>
v19: So Jesus answered them, “Truly, truly, I tell you, the Son can do nothing on his own, but only what he sees the Father doing. Whatever the Father does, the Son does likewise.
v20: For the Father loves the Son and shows him everything he is doing. He will show him greater works than these, so that you will be amazed.
v21: Just as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so the Son gives life to whomever he wishes.
v22: The Father does not judge anyone but has entrusted all judgment to the Son,
v23: so that everyone may honor the Son just as they honor the Father. Whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him.
v24: Truly, truly, I tell you, whoever hears my word and believes the one who sent me has eternal life and does not come into judgment, but has crossed over from death into life.
v25: Truly, truly, I tell you, an hour is coming—and is now here—when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live.
v26: For just as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself.
v27: And he has given the Son authority to execute judgment because he is the Son of Man.
v28: Do not be amazed at this, because an hour is coming when everyone in the tombs will hear his voice
v29: and come out: those who have done good to a resurrection of life, and those who have practiced evil to a resurrection of judgment.
v30: I can do nothing on my own. I judge according to what I hear, and my judgment is just, because I do not seek my own will but the will of the one who sent me.
</p>

## Witnesses to Jesus

<p>
v31: If I testify about myself, my testimony is not valid by itself.
v32: There is another who testifies about me, and I know that the testimony he gives about me is true.
v33: You sent messengers to John, and he testified to the truth.
v34: I do not depend on testimony from a human being, but I say these things so that you may be saved.
v35: John was the lamp that burned and shone, and for a time you were willing to rejoice in his light.
v36: But I have testimony greater than John’s: the works the Father has given me to complete—the very works I am doing—testify about me that the Father has sent me.
v37: And the Father who sent me has himself testified about me. You have never heard his voice or seen his form,
v38: and you do not have his word remaining in you, because you do not believe the one he sent.
v39: You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; yet they are the very writings that testify about me,
v40: but you are unwilling to come to me so that you may have life.
v41: I do not accept glory from people.
v42: But I know you: you do not have the love of God in yourselves.
v43: I have come in my Father’s name, and you do not receive me. If someone else comes in his own name, you will receive him.
v44: How can you believe when you accept glory from one another but do not seek the glory that comes from the only God?
v45: Do not think that I will accuse you before the Father. Your accuser is Moses, in whom you have placed your hope.
v46: For if you believed Moses, you would believe me, because he wrote about me.
v47: But if you do not believe his writings, how will you believe my words?”
</p>

## Notes

v04: Public verse 4 is absent from the pinned Greek text. The later explanation about an angel stirring the water is therefore not included.

v07: The man reports that he has no one to put him into the pool and that another person always reaches it first. The narrative reports this belief without confirming the pool’s healing mechanism.

v10–18: ‘The Jewish authorities’ identifies the local leaders enforcing the Sabbath interpretation and persecuting Jesus. It does not refer to Jewish people as a whole; Jesus and the healed man are themselves Jewish.

v14: Jesus warns the healed man not to continue sinning so that nothing worse happens. The verse does not say that his thirty-eight-year illness was caused by a particular sin.

v31: In the legal argument, self-testimony alone is not treated as sufficient verification. Jesus does not deny that his own words are true.

v39: The verb may be read as ‘you search’ or as a command, ‘search.’ The surrounding address most naturally describes the hearers’ practice and exposes its failure to come to Jesus.

## Vocabulary

v02: **Βηθεσδά (Bēthesda)**: Bethesda, the pinned Greek form of the pool’s name.

v21: **ζῳοποιέω (zōopoieō)**: Give life or make alive.

v29: **ἀνάστασις (anastasis)**: Resurrection, rising from the dead.

v36: **μαρτυρία (martyria)**: Testimony or witness.
