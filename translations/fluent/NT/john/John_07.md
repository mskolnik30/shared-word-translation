## Jesus and the Festival of Booths

<p>
v01: After this, Jesus traveled around Galilee. He did not want to travel in Judea because the Jewish authorities there were seeking to kill him.
v02: The Jewish Festival of Shelters was near.
v03: So Jesus’ brothers told him, “Leave here and go to Judea so that your disciples there may also see the works you are doing.
v04: For no one does something in secret while seeking to be known publicly. If you are doing these things, show yourself to the world.”
v05: For not even his brothers believed in him.
v06: Jesus told them, “My time has not yet come, but your time is always ready.
v07: The world cannot hate you, but it hates me because I testify that its works are evil.
v08: You go up to the festival. I am not going up to this festival now, because my time has not yet been fulfilled.”
v09: After saying these things, he remained in Galilee.
v10: But after his brothers went up to the festival, Jesus also went up, not publicly but in secret.
</p>

## Debate at the Festival

<p>
v11: The Jewish authorities were looking for him at the festival and asking, “Where is that man?”
v12: There was much quiet discussion about him among the crowds. Some said, “He is a good man.” Others said, “No, he is misleading the crowd.”
v13: Yet no one spoke openly about him because they were afraid of the Jewish authorities.
v14: When the festival was already half over, Jesus went up to the temple courts and began teaching.
v15: The Jewish leaders were astonished and asked, “How does this man know the Scriptures without having been formally taught?”
v16: Jesus answered them, “My teaching is not mine; it belongs to the one who sent me.
v17: Anyone who wants to do his will shall know whether my teaching comes from God or whether I speak on my own.
v18: Whoever speaks on his own seeks his own glory, but the one who seeks the glory of the one who sent him is true, and there is no injustice in him.
v19: Did not Moses give you the Law? Yet none of you carries out the Law. Why are you trying to kill me?”
v20: The crowd answered, “You have a demon! Who is trying to kill you?”
v21: Jesus replied, “I did one work, and all of you are astonished.
v22: Moses gave you circumcision—though it comes not from Moses but from the ancestors—and you circumcise a male on the Sabbath.
v23: If a male receives circumcision on the Sabbath so that Moses’ Law is not broken, why are you angry with me because I made a whole person well on the Sabbath?
v24: Do not judge by outward appearance, but judge with just judgment.”
</p>

## Where Is Jesus From?

<p>
v25: Then some people from Jerusalem said, “Is this not the man they are trying to kill?
v26: Yet look, he is speaking openly, and they say nothing to him. Can the rulers have actually concluded that he is the Messiah?
v27: But we know where this man comes from. When the Messiah comes, no one knows where he is from.”
v28: As Jesus taught in the temple courts, he cried out, “Yes, you know me, and you know where I am from! Yet I have not come on my own. The one who sent me is true, and you do not know him.
v29: I know him because I am from him, and he sent me.”
v30: Then they sought to seize him, but no one laid a hand on him because his hour had not yet come.
v31: Yet many from the crowd believed in him and said, “When the Messiah comes, will he perform more signs than this man has?”
v32: The Pharisees heard the crowd whispering these things about him, so the chief priests and Pharisees sent temple officers to arrest him.
v33: Then Jesus said, “I am with you only a little longer, and then I am going to the one who sent me.
v34: You will look for me but will not find me, and where I am, you cannot come.”
v35: The Jewish leaders said to one another, “Where does this man intend to go that we will not find him? Does he intend to go to the Dispersion among the Greeks and teach the Greeks?
v36: What does he mean by saying, ‘You will look for me but will not find me,’ and, ‘Where I am, you cannot come’?”
</p>

## Rivers of Living Water

<p>
v37: On the last and greatest day of the festival, Jesus stood and cried out, “If anyone is thirsty, let them come to me and drink.
v38: Whoever believes in me—as Scripture has said—rivers of living water will flow from within that person.”
v39: He said this about the Spirit, whom those who believed in him were going to receive. The Spirit had not yet been given, because Jesus had not yet been glorified.
</p>

<p>

v41: Others said, “This is the Messiah.” But some said, “Surely the Messiah does not come from Galilee, does he?
v42: Does not Scripture say that the Messiah comes from David’s offspring and from Bethlehem, the village where David lived?”
v43: So the crowd was divided because of him.
v44: Some of them wanted to seize him, but no one laid hands on him.
v45: Then the temple officers returned to the chief priests and Pharisees, who asked them, “Why did you not bring him?”
v46: The officers answered, “No one has ever spoken like this man.”
v47: The Pharisees replied, “Have you also been deceived?
v48: Has any one of the rulers or Pharisees believed in him?
v49: But this crowd, which does not know the Law, is cursed.”
v50: Nicodemus, the one who had gone to Jesus earlier and who was one of them, said,
v51: “Does our Law judge a person without first hearing from him and learning what he is doing?”
v52: They answered him, “You are not from Galilee too, are you? Search and see that no prophet arises from Galilee.”
</p>

## Notes

v08: The pinned text has a variant-marked ‘not yet’: Jesus is not yet going up to the festival. Some witnesses read simply ‘not.’

v37–39: The punctuation and the Scripture allusion allow more than one connection for ‘from within him.’ The main text links the living-water saying with the believer and the narrator identifies the water with the Spirit.

v52: The leaders’ claim that no prophet arises from Galilee is part of their argument, not the narrator’s endorsement.

v53: John 7:53–8:11 is enclosed in double brackets in the pinned source because its earliest textual attestation is uncertain. It is translated here as a clearly marked textual tradition, not silently presented as secure main text.
