## Jesus and Nicodemus

<p>
v01: There was a Pharisee named Nicodemus, a Jewish leader.
v02: He came to Jesus at night and said, “Rabbi, we know you are a teacher who has come from God, because no one can do these signs you do unless God is with him.”
v03: Jesus answered him, “Truly, truly, I tell you, unless someone is born from above, they cannot see God’s kingdom.”
v04: Nicodemus said, “How can someone be born when they are old? Can they enter their mother’s womb a second time and be born?”
v05: Jesus answered, “Truly, truly, I tell you, unless someone is born of water and Spirit, they cannot enter God’s kingdom.
v06: What is born of flesh is flesh, and what is born of the Spirit is spirit.
v07: Do not be amazed that I told you, ‘You must be born from above.’
v08: The wind blows where it wants. You hear its sound, but you do not know where it comes from or where it goes. So it is with everyone born of the Spirit.”
v09: Nicodemus asked him, “How can these things happen?”
v10: Jesus answered, “You are Israel’s teacher, and you do not understand these things?
v11: Truly, truly, I tell you, we speak about what we know and testify to what we have seen, yet you do not receive our testimony.
v12: If I told you earthly things and you do not trust, how will you trust if I tell you heavenly things?
v13: No one has ascended into heaven except the one who descended from heaven—the Son of Man.
v14: Just as Moses lifted up the snake in the wilderness, so the Son of Man must be lifted up,
v15: so that everyone who trusts in him may have eternal life.
v16: For God loved the world in this way: he gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who trusts in him will not perish but have eternal life.
v17: God did not send the Son into the world to condemn the world, but so that the world might be saved through him.
v18: The one who trusts in him is not condemned, but the one who does not trust has already been condemned because they have not trusted in the name of God’s one and only Son.
v19: This is the judgment: the light has come into the world, and people loved the darkness rather than the light because their works were evil.
v20: Everyone who practices evil hates the light and does not come to the light, so that their works will not be exposed.
v21: But the one who does what is true comes to the light, so that it may become clear that their works have been done in God.
</p>

## John Testifies Again

<p>
v22: After this, Jesus and his disciples went into the Judean countryside. He spent time there with them and was baptizing.
v23: John was also baptizing at Aenon near Salim because there was plenty of water there. People kept coming and being baptized.
v24: John had not yet been thrown into prison.
v25: A discussion about purification arose between John’s disciples and a Judean.
v26: They came to John and said, “Rabbi, the one who was with you across the Jordan, the one you testified about—look, he is baptizing, and everyone is going to him.”
v27: John answered, “A person cannot receive anything unless it has been given from heaven.
v28: You yourselves are my witnesses that I said, ‘I am not the Christ, but I have been sent ahead of him.’
v29: The one who has the bride is the bridegroom. The bridegroom’s friend, who stands and listens for him, rejoices greatly at the bridegroom’s voice. So this joy of mine has been made complete.
v30: He must increase, but I must decrease.
v31: The one who comes from above is above all. The one who is from the earth belongs to the earth and speaks from the earth. The one who comes from heaven is above all.
v32: He testifies to what he has seen and heard, yet no one receives his testimony.
v33: Whoever receives his testimony confirms that God is true.
v34: For the one God sent speaks God’s words, because God gives the Spirit without measure.
v35: The Father loves the Son and has given everything into his hand.
v36: The one who trusts in the Son has eternal life. The one who refuses the Son will not see life; instead, God’s wrath remains on that person.”
</p>

## Notes

v03: `anōthen` deliberately means both “from above” and “again.” Fluent uses “from above,” while Nicodemus’ response preserves how the second sense drives the conversation.

v05: “Water and Spirit” is not explained by the passage. Fluent does not decide among baptismal, birth, Ezekiel-new-creation, or other proposed readings in the main text.

v08: `pneuma` means both wind and Spirit; the wordplay cannot be reproduced with one English word, so the two contextual renderings remain visible.

v13: SBLGNT does not include the later expansion “who is in heaven” after “Son of Man.”

v14: “Lifted up” will carry both crucifixion and exaltation through the Gospel.

v16: “God loved the world in this way” lets `houtōs` describe the manner demonstrated by giving the Son rather than only intensity.

v16: `monogenēs` is again “one and only,” consistent with John 1 and 1 John.

v16–21: Greek manuscripts do not mark quotation boundaries. These verses may continue Jesus’ speech or shift into the evangelist’s exposition. Fluent keeps the traditional continuous speech layout but records the uncertainty here.

v25: The singular counterpart in the dispute is textually read as “a Judean/Jew” in SBLGNT. The geographical setting supports “a Judean.”

v29–30: John interprets decrease as completed joy, not competitive loss.

v31–36: The boundary between John the Baptist’s speech and the evangelist’s voice is also debated. Fluent leaves the traditional speech continuity in place.
