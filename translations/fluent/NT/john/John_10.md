## The Shepherd and the Sheep

<p>
v01: “Truly, truly, I tell you, anyone who does not enter the sheepfold through the gate but climbs in some other way is a thief and a robber.
v02: But the one who enters through the gate is the shepherd of the sheep.
v03: The gatekeeper opens for him, and the sheep hear his voice. He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out.
v04: When he has brought out all his own, he goes ahead of them, and the sheep follow him because they know his voice.
v05: They will certainly not follow a stranger but will run away, because they do not know strangers’ voices.”
v06: Jesus used this figure of speech with them, but they did not understand what he was telling them.
</p>

## I Am the Door

<p>
v07: So Jesus told them again, “Truly, truly, I tell you, I am the gate for the sheep.
v08: All who came before me were thieves and robbers, but the sheep did not listen to them.
v09: I am the gate. Whoever enters through me will be saved, and will come in and go out and find pasture.
v10: The thief comes only to steal, kill, and destroy. I came so that they may have life and have it abundantly.
</p>

## I Am the Good Shepherd

<p>
v11: I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.
v12: The hired worker, who is not the shepherd and does not own the sheep, sees the wolf coming, abandons the sheep, and runs away. Then the wolf snatches and scatters them.
v13: He runs because he is a hired worker and does not care about the sheep.
v14: I am the good shepherd. I know my own, and my own know me,
v15: just as the Father knows me and I know the Father. And I lay down my life for the sheep.
v16: I have other sheep that are not from this fold. I must bring them too, and they will listen to my voice. Then there will be one flock, one shepherd.
v17: This is why the Father loves me: because I lay down my life so that I may take it up again.
v18: No one takes it from me; I lay it down of my own accord. I have authority to lay it down and authority to take it up again. I received this command from my Father.”
</p>

## Division over Jesus

<p>
v19: The Jewish hearers were divided again because of these words.
v20: Many of them said, “He has a demon and is out of his mind. Why listen to him?”
v21: Others said, “These are not the words of someone demon-possessed. Can a demon open blind people’s eyes?”
</p>

## At the Festival of Dedication

<p>
v22: Then the Festival of Dedication took place in Jerusalem. It was winter,
v23: and Jesus was walking in the temple courts in Solomon’s Colonnade.
v24: The Jewish leaders gathered around him and said, “How long will you keep us in suspense? If you are the Messiah, tell us plainly.”
v25: Jesus answered them, “I told you, but you do not believe. The works I do in my Father’s name testify about me.
v26: But you do not believe because you are not among my sheep.
v27: My sheep listen to my voice. I know them, and they follow me.
v28: I give them eternal life. They will certainly never perish, and no one will snatch them out of my hand.
v29: What my Father has given me is greater than everything, and no one can snatch it out of the Father’s hand.
v30: I and the Father are one.”
</p>

## Stones and Scripture

<p>
v31: The leaders picked up stones again to stone him.
v32: Jesus responded, “I showed you many good works from the Father. For which of these works are you stoning me?”
v33: They answered, “We are not stoning you for a good work, but for blasphemy—because you, a human being, make yourself God.”
v34: Jesus replied, “Is it not written in your Law, ‘I said, you are gods’?
v35: If Scripture called those to whom God’s word came ‘gods’—and Scripture cannot be undone—
v36: then why do you say, ‘You are blaspheming,’ when the one the Father set apart and sent into the world says, ‘I am God’s Son’?
v37: If I am not doing my Father’s works, do not believe me.
v38: But if I am doing them, even if you do not believe me, believe the works, so that you may know and continue knowing that the Father is in me and I am in the Father.”
v39: Again they tried to arrest him, but he escaped from their hands.
v40: He went back across the Jordan to the place where John had first been baptizing, and he stayed there.
v41: Many people came to him and said, “John did not perform any sign, but everything John said about this man was true.”
v42: And many believed in Jesus there.
</p>

## Notes

v01–10: The same speech moves between the images of shepherd, sheepfold, gatekeeper, and gate. The translation does not force every detail into a separate allegorical identity.

v11–18: The good shepherd willingly lays down his life for the sheep and has authority both to lay it down and take it up again.

v16: The ‘other sheep’ become one flock with one shepherd; the verse does not say they become one physical enclosure.

v30–36: Jesus’ statement ‘I and the Father are one’ prompts an accusation of blasphemy. His appeal to Psalm 82 argues from the scriptural use of ‘gods’ without withdrawing his claim to be the consecrated Son sent by the Father.

## Vocabulary

v01: **αὐλή (aulē)**: Courtyard or sheepfold.

v10: **περισσός (perissos)**: Abundant, overflowing, or beyond the usual measure.

v11: **ποιμὴν ὁ καλός (poimēn ho kalos)**: The good or noble shepherd.
