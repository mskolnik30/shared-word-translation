## Jesus Washes the Disciples’ Feet

<p>
v01: Before the Passover festival, Jesus knew his hour had come to leave this world and go to the Father. Having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end.
v02: During supper, the devil had already put into the heart of Judas son of Simon Iscariot to hand Jesus over.
v03: Jesus knew the Father had given everything into his hands, that he had come from God, and that he was going to God.
v04: So he got up from supper, laid aside his outer clothing, took a towel, and tied it around himself.
v05: Then he poured water into a basin and began washing the disciples’ feet and wiping them with the towel tied around him.
v06: He came to Simon Peter, who said, “Lord, are you going to wash my feet?”
v07: Jesus answered, “You do not understand what I am doing now, but afterward you will understand.”
v08: Peter said, “You will certainly never wash my feet!” Jesus answered, “Unless I wash you, you have no share with me.”
v09: Simon Peter said, “Lord, not my feet only, but also my hands and head!”
v10: Jesus said, “The one who has bathed does not need to wash except the feet but is completely clean. And you are clean—but not all of you.”
v11: For he knew who was handing him over. This is why he said, “Not all of you are clean.”
</p>

## An Example of Service

<p>
v12: After he had washed their feet, put on his outer clothing, and reclined at the table again, he said, “Do you understand what I have done for you?
v13: You call me Teacher and Lord, and you speak correctly, because that is what I am.
v14: So if I, your Lord and Teacher, washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another’s feet.
v15: For I have given you an example so that just as I have done for you, you also should do.
v16: Truly, truly, I tell you, a slave is not greater than the master, nor is a messenger greater than the one who sent him.
v17: If you know these things, you are blessed if you do them.
</p>

## The Betrayer Identified

<p>
v18: “I am not speaking about all of you. I know those I have chosen. But Scripture must be fulfilled: ‘The one eating my bread has lifted his heel against me.’
v19: I tell you now before it happens so that when it happens, you may trust that I am.
v20: Truly, truly, I tell you, whoever receives anyone I send receives me, and whoever receives me receives the one who sent me.”
v21: After saying these things, Jesus was troubled in spirit and testified, “Truly, truly, I tell you, one of you will hand me over.”
v22: The disciples looked at one another, uncertain about whom he was speaking.
v23: One of his disciples, the one Jesus loved, was reclining close beside Jesus.
v24: Simon Peter motioned to him to ask Jesus whom he meant.
v25: Leaning back against Jesus, he asked, “Lord, who is it?”
v26: Jesus answered, “It is the one for whom I dip the piece of bread and give it to him.” He dipped the piece and gave it to Judas son of Simon Iscariot.
v27: After Judas took the piece, Satan entered him. Jesus said to him, “What you are doing, do quickly.”
v28: No one reclining at the table knew why Jesus said this to him.
v29: Because Judas kept the moneybag, some thought Jesus was telling him, “Buy what we need for the festival,” or that he should give something to the poor.
v30: After receiving the piece of bread, Judas immediately went out. And it was night.
</p>

## The New Commandment

<p>
v31: When Judas had gone out, Jesus said, “Now the Son of Man has been glorified, and God has been glorified in him.
v32: If God has been glorified in him, God will also glorify him in himself and will glorify him immediately.
v33: Little children, I am with you only a little longer. You will look for me, and just as I told the Jewish leaders, ‘Where I am going you cannot come,’ now I also tell you.
v34: I give you a new commandment: love one another. Just as I have loved you, you also must love one another.
v35: By this everyone will know that you are my disciples: if you have love for one another.”
</p>

## Peter’s Denial Foretold

<p>
v36: Simon Peter asked, “Lord, where are you going?” Jesus answered, “Where I am going you cannot follow me now, but you will follow later.”
v37: Peter said, “Lord, why can I not follow you now? I will lay down my life for you.”
v38: Jesus answered, “Will you lay down your life for me? Truly, truly, I tell you, the rooster will certainly not crow until you have denied me three times.”
</p>

## Notes

v01: `eis telos` can mean “to the end,” “completely,” or “to the uttermost.” Fluent uses “to the end,” allowing both temporal and qualitative resonance.

v02: The textual syntax around the devil’s action and Judas’ heart has variants. Fluent follows the pinned SBLGNT sense that the betrayal intention had already been placed in Judas’ heart.

v03–05: Jesus’ knowledge of origin, destination, and authority leads directly into servant action rather than visible status assertion.

v08: “Share” translates `meros`, participation or belonging with Jesus, not a detachable reward.

v10: The wording about washing only the feet has textual variation; Fluent follows SBLGNT.

v14–15: Footwashing is both a concrete act and an enacted pattern of status-reversing service.

v19: “You may trust that I am” retains the absolute `egō eimi` form.

v23: “The disciple Jesus loved” is introduced without naming him.

v27: Satan’s entry does not erase Judas’ ongoing agency in the narrative.

v30: “It was night” is chronological and also resonates with John’s light and darkness symbolism.

v31: Glorification language begins precisely after Judas leaves to carry out the hand-over.

v34: The command is “new” not because love had never been commanded, but because Jesus’ own love becomes the defining pattern: “just as I have loved you.”

v37–38: Peter promises to lay down his life for Jesus, echoing the good shepherd language, but Jesus predicts Peter’s failure before later restoration.
