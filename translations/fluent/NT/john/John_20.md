## Mary Magdalene Finds the Tomb Open

<p>
v01: On the first day of the week, Mary Magdalene came to the tomb early, while it was still dark, and saw the stone removed from the tomb.
v02: So she ran to Simon Peter and the other disciple, the one Jesus loved, and told them, “They have taken the Lord out of the tomb, and we do not know where they put him!”
v03: Peter and the other disciple went out toward the tomb.
v04: The two were running together, but the other disciple ran faster than Peter and reached the tomb first.
v05: He bent down and saw the linen cloths lying there, but he did not enter.
v06: Then Simon Peter came behind him and entered the tomb. He saw the linen cloths lying there,
v07: and the cloth that had been on Jesus’ head, not lying with the linen cloths but folded separately in one place.
v08: Then the other disciple, who had reached the tomb first, also went in. He saw and trusted.
v09: For they did not yet understand the Scripture that Jesus had to rise from the dead.
v10: Then the disciples returned to their homes.
</p>

## Jesus Appears to Mary Magdalene

<p>
v11: But Mary stood outside near the tomb, weeping. As she wept, she bent down to look into the tomb
v12: and saw two angels in white sitting where Jesus’ body had been, one at the head and one at the feet.
v13: They asked her, “Woman, why are you weeping?” She told them, “They have taken my Lord away, and I do not know where they put him.”
v14: After saying this, she turned around and saw Jesus standing there, but she did not know it was Jesus.
v15: Jesus asked, “Woman, why are you weeping? Who are you looking for?” Thinking he was the gardener, she said, “Sir, if you carried him away, tell me where you put him, and I will take him.”
v16: Jesus said to her, “Mary.” She turned and said to him in Hebrew, “Rabbouni!”—which means Teacher.
v17: Jesus said, “Do not keep holding on to me, because I have not yet ascended to the Father. But go to my brothers and tell them, ‘I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God.’”
v18: Mary Magdalene went and announced to the disciples, “I have seen the Lord!” and told them what he had said to her.
</p>

## Jesus Appears to the Disciples

<p>
v19: When it was evening on that first day of the week, and the doors were locked where the disciples were because of fear of the Jewish authorities, Jesus came and stood among them. He said, “Peace to you.”
v20: After saying this, he showed them his hands and his side. The disciples rejoiced when they saw the Lord.
v21: Jesus said to them again, “Peace to you. Just as the Father has sent me, I also send you.”
v22: After saying this, he breathed on them and said, “Receive the Holy Spirit.
v23: If you forgive anyone’s sins, they are forgiven them; if you hold any sins, they are held.”
</p>

## Thomas Sees and Trusts

<p>
v24: Thomas, one of the twelve, called Twin, was not with them when Jesus came.
v25: So the other disciples told him, “We have seen the Lord!” But he said, “Unless I see the nail mark in his hands, put my finger into the nail mark, and put my hand into his side, I will certainly not trust.”
v26: Eight days later his disciples were inside again, and Thomas was with them. Though the doors were locked, Jesus came and stood among them. He said, “Peace to you.”
v27: Then he said to Thomas, “Put your finger here and see my hands. Bring your hand and put it into my side. Do not become unbelieving, but trusting.”
v28: Thomas answered him, “My Lord and my God!”
v29: Jesus said, “Because you have seen me, you have trusted. Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have trusted.”
</p>

## Why This Gospel Was Written

<p>
v30: Jesus did many other signs in the presence of his disciples that are not written in this book.
v31: But these have been written so that you may trust that Jesus is the Christ, God’s Son, and so that by trusting you may have life in his name.
</p>

## Notes

v01: John begins the resurrection scene “while it was still dark,” allowing both chronological and light and darkness resonance.

v02: Mary says “we do not know,” implying others may be associated with her even though John’s scene names her alone at this point.

v08–09: The beloved disciple “saw and trusted,” yet the narrator says the disciples still did not understand the Scripture about resurrection. Trust and full understanding are not identical.

v16: Recognition comes when Jesus speaks Mary’s name, recalling the shepherd who calls his own sheep by name.

v17: `mou haptou` is better understood as “do not keep holding on to me” than as a prohibition of all physical touch.

v17: “My Father and your Father, my God and your God” joins disciples to Jesus’ relationship while preserving distinction in wording.

v18: Mary Magdalene becomes the first named person in John to announce the risen Lord to the disciples.

v19: “Fear of the Jewish authorities” identifies the feared group in context; it must not be generalized to Jewish people.

v21: The sending of disciples is patterned directly on the Father’s sending of Jesus.

v22: Jesus’ breathing evokes creation, breath, and Spirit imagery. John narrates this gift in his own theological chronology rather than being harmonized to Acts 2.

v23: The forgive and retain saying gives the Spirit-empowered community a serious role in dealing with sin; Fluent leaves the exact ecclesial mechanism unstated.

v28: Thomas’ confession, “My Lord and my God,” is the Gospel’s climactic direct address to Jesus.

v29: The final beatitude explicitly extends trust beyond eyewitness sight.

v31: Manuscripts can support present “may trust” or aorist “may come to trust.” Fluent uses purpose language broad enough for both evangelistic and sustaining functions.
