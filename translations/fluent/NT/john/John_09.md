## A Man Born Blind Receives Sight

<p>
v01: As Jesus passed by, he saw a man who had been blind from birth.
v02: His disciples asked him, “Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, so that he was born blind?”
v03: Jesus answered, “Neither this man nor his parents sinned. Rather, this happened so that God’s works might be revealed in him.
v04: We must do the works of the one who sent me while it is day. Night is coming when no one can work.
v05: As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world.”
v06: After saying this, he spat on the ground, made mud with the saliva, and spread the mud on the man’s eyes.
v07: He told him, “Go wash in the pool of Siloam” (which means Sent). So the man went, washed, and came back seeing.
v08: His neighbors and those who had seen him before as a beggar asked, “Is this not the man who used to sit and beg?”
v09: Some said, “It is he.” Others said, “No, but he looks like him.” The man kept saying, “I am the one.”
v10: So they asked him, “How were your eyes opened?”
v11: He answered, “The man called Jesus made mud, spread it on my eyes, and told me, ‘Go to Siloam and wash.’ So I went, washed, and received my sight.”
v12: They asked him, “Where is this man?” He said, “I do not know.”
</p>

## The Pharisees Investigate

<p>
v13: They brought the man who had formerly been blind to the Pharisees.
v14: The day when Jesus made the mud and opened the man’s eyes was a Sabbath.
v15: So the Pharisees also asked him again how he had received his sight. He told them, “He put mud on my eyes, I washed, and now I see.”
v16: Some Pharisees said, “This man is not from God, because he does not keep the Sabbath.” But others said, “How can a sinful man perform such signs?” So they were divided.
v17: They asked the man who had been blind again, “What do you say about him, since he opened your eyes?” He answered, “He is a prophet.”
v18: The Jewish authorities did not believe that he had been blind and received his sight until they summoned the parents of the man who now saw.
v19: They asked them, “Is this your son, whom you say was born blind? How, then, does he now see?”
v20: His parents answered, “We know that this is our son and that he was born blind.
v21: But we do not know how he now sees, or who opened his eyes. Ask him. He is of age and can speak for himself.”
v22: His parents said this because they were afraid of the Jewish authorities, who had already agreed that anyone who confessed Jesus as Messiah would be put out of the synagogue.
v23: That is why his parents said, “He is of age; ask him.”
</p>

## The Healed Man Testifies

<p>
v24: So for a second time they summoned the man who had been blind and told him, “Give glory to God! We know that this man is a sinner.”
v25: He answered, “Whether he is a sinner, I do not know. One thing I know: I was blind, and now I see.”
v26: They asked him, “What did he do to you? How did he open your eyes?”
v27: He answered them, “I already told you, and you did not listen. Why do you want to hear it again? You do not want to become his disciples too, do you?”
v28: They insulted him and said, “You are that man’s disciple, but we are Moses’ disciples.
v29: We know that God spoke to Moses, but as for this man, we do not know where he comes from.”
v30: The man answered them, “This is astonishing! You do not know where he comes from, yet he opened my eyes.
v31: We know that God does not listen to sinners, but he does listen to anyone who worships God and does his will.
v32: Never since the world began has anyone heard of someone opening the eyes of a person born blind.
v33: If this man were not from God, he could do nothing.”
v34: They answered him, “You were born entirely in sins, and you are teaching us?” Then they threw him out.
</p>

## Seeing and Blindness

<p>
v35: Jesus heard that they had thrown the man out. When he found him, he asked, “Do you believe in the Son of Man?”
v36: He answered, “Who is he, sir, so that I may believe in him?”
v37: Jesus told him, “You have seen him; in fact, he is the one speaking with you.”
v38: He said, “Lord, I believe,” and he worshiped Jesus.
v39: Jesus said, “I came into this world for judgment, so that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind.”
v40: Some Pharisees who were with him heard this and asked, “We are not blind too, are we?”
v41: Jesus told them, “If you were blind, you would not have sin. But now that you say, ‘We see,’ your sin remains.”
</p>

## Notes

v03: Jesus explicitly rejects the proposed explanation that the man’s blindness resulted from either his own sin or his parents’ sin.

v04: The pinned text reads ‘we must do the works’ rather than ‘I must do.’

v22: ‘Put out of the synagogue’ describes exclusion from the community’s assembly; the precise later institutional procedure should not be read back into the narrative.

v24: ‘Give glory to God’ is a solemn demand to tell the truth, not a neutral invitation to worship.

v39–41: The sight-blindness contrast becomes a judgment on claimed spiritual perception. It does not make physical blindness a moral defect.

## Vocabulary

v07: **Σιλωάμ (Silōam)**: Siloam; John explains the name as Sent.

v22: **ἀποσυνάγωγος (aposynagōgos)**: Excluded from the synagogue or assembly.

v38: **προσκυνέω (proskyneō)**: Bow down, worship, or show reverence.
