## Mary Anoints Jesus

<p>
v01: Six days before Passover, Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus lived—the man Jesus had raised from the dead.
v02: They gave a dinner for Jesus there. Martha was serving, and Lazarus was among those reclining at the table with him.
v03: Then Mary took a litra of expensive perfume made from pure nard, anointed Jesus’ feet, and wiped his feet with her hair. The house was filled with the perfume’s fragrance.
v04: But Judas Iscariot, one of Jesus’ disciples—the one who was going to betray him—said,
v05: “Why was this perfume not sold for three hundred denarii and the money given to the poor?”
v06: He said this not because he cared about the poor, but because he was a thief. He kept the common money box and stole what was put into it.
v07: Jesus said, “Leave her alone, so that she may keep it for the day of my burial.
v08: For you always have the poor with you, but you do not always have me.”
v09: A large crowd of Jewish people learned that Jesus was there and came not only because of him but also to see Lazarus, whom he had raised from the dead.
v10: So the chief priests planned to kill Lazarus too,
v11: because on account of him many Jewish people were leaving them and believing in Jesus.
</p>

## Jesus Enters Jerusalem

<p>
v12: The next day, the large crowd that had come to the festival heard that Jesus was coming to Jerusalem.
v13: They took palm branches and went out to meet him, shouting, “Hosanna! Blessed is the one who comes in the Lord’s name—the King of Israel!”
v14: Jesus found a young donkey and sat on it, as it is written:
v15: “Do not be afraid, daughter Zion. Look, your king is coming, seated on a donkey’s colt.”
v16: His disciples did not understand these things at first. But when Jesus was glorified, they remembered that these things had been written about him and had been done to him.
v17: The crowd that had been with Jesus when he called Lazarus from the tomb and raised him from the dead continued to testify.
v18: That is also why the crowd went to meet him: they heard he had performed this sign.
v19: So the Pharisees said to one another, “You see that you are accomplishing nothing. Look, the world has gone after him!”
</p>

## Greeks Seek Jesus

<p>
v20: Some Greeks were among those going up to worship at the festival.
v21: They came to Philip, who was from Bethsaida in Galilee, and asked him, “Sir, we want to see Jesus.”
v22: Philip went and told Andrew; then Andrew and Philip went and told Jesus.
v23: Jesus answered them, “The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified.
v24: Truly, truly, I tell you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the ground and dies, it remains alone. But if it dies, it bears much fruit.
v25: Whoever loves their life loses it, but whoever hates their life in this world will keep it for eternal life.
v26: If anyone serves me, that person must follow me; where I am, my servant will also be. If anyone serves me, the Father will honor that person.
</p>

## The Son of Man Must Be Lifted Up

<p>
v27: “Now my soul is troubled. What should I say—‘Father, save me from this hour’? But this is the very reason I came to this hour.
v28: Father, glorify your name.” Then a voice came from heaven: “I have glorified it, and I will glorify it again.”
v29: The crowd standing there heard it and said it had thundered. Others said, “An angel has spoken to him.”
v30: Jesus answered, “This voice came not for my sake but for yours.
v31: Now is the judgment of this world; now the ruler of this world will be driven out.
v32: And when I am lifted up from the earth, I will draw everyone to myself.”
v33: He said this to indicate what kind of death he was about to die.
v34: The crowd answered him, “We have heard from the Law that the Messiah remains forever. So how can you say that the Son of Man must be lifted up? Who is this Son of Man?”
v35: Jesus told them, “The light is among you a little while longer. Walk while you have the light, so that darkness does not overtake you. Whoever walks in darkness does not know where they are going.
v36: While you have the light, believe in the light, so that you may become children of light.” After Jesus said these things, he left and hid from them.
</p>

## Unbelief and Isaiah

<p>
v37: Although he had performed so many signs in their presence, they did not believe in him.
v38: This fulfilled the word of the prophet Isaiah: “Lord, who has believed our report? And to whom has the Lord’s arm been revealed?”
v39: For this reason they were unable to believe, because Isaiah also said,
v40: “He has blinded their eyes and hardened their heart, so that they might not see with their eyes, understand with their heart, turn, and I would heal them.”
v41: Isaiah said these things because he saw Jesus’ glory and spoke about him.
v42: Nevertheless, even many of the rulers believed in him. But because of the Pharisees they did not confess it, so that they would not be put out of the synagogue.
v43: For they loved human glory more than God’s glory.
</p>

## Jesus’ Final Public Appeal

<p>
v44: Jesus cried out, “Whoever believes in me believes not only in me but in the one who sent me,
v45: and whoever sees me sees the one who sent me.
v46: I have come into the world as light so that everyone who believes in me will not remain in darkness.
v47: If anyone hears my words and does not keep them, I do not judge that person. For I did not come to judge the world but to save the world.
v48: Whoever rejects me and does not receive my words has a judge: the word I spoke will judge that person on the last day.
v49: For I did not speak on my own; the Father who sent me has himself commanded me what to say and how to speak.
v50: And I know that his command is eternal life. So whatever I speak, I speak just as the Father has told me.”
</p>

## Notes

v03–6: Mary’s costly act fills the house with fragrance. Judas’s objection is narrated as theft from the shared money box, not as evidence that concern for poor people is itself suspect.

v24–25: The grain’s death and fruit interpret Jesus’ approaching death and shape discipleship. ‘Hates life’ is a sharp comparative allegiance idiom, not permission for self-harm.

v27: Jesus’ troubled prayer can be punctuated as a question answered by his commitment to the hour; the translation retains that movement.

v32–33: Being lifted up joins crucifixion and exaltation. The narrator explicitly says the phrase indicates the kind of death Jesus would die.

v38–41: John quotes Isaiah’s report of unbelief and then says Isaiah saw Jesus’ glory. The difficult hardening language is retained rather than softened into mere lack of information.

## Vocabulary

v03: **νάρδος (nardos)**: Nard, an aromatic oil used as costly perfume.

v24: **κόκκος τοῦ σίτου (kokkos tou sitou)**: A grain of wheat.

v31: **ἄρχων τοῦ κόσμου (archōn tou kosmou)**: Ruler of the world.
