## Jesus Is Mocked as King

<p>
v01: Then Pilate took Jesus and had him flogged.
v02: The soldiers twisted together a crown of thorns, put it on his head, and dressed him in a purple robe.
v03: They kept coming up to him and saying, “Hail, king of the Jews!” and slapping him.
v04: Pilate went outside again and said to them, “Look, I am bringing him out to you so that you may know I find no basis for a charge against him.”
v05: Jesus came out wearing the crown of thorns and purple robe. Pilate said to them, “Look—the man!”
v06: When the chief priests and officers saw him, they shouted, “Crucify! Crucify!” Pilate said, “Take him yourselves and crucify him, because I find no basis for a charge against him.”
v07: The Jewish leaders answered, “We have a law, and according to that law he ought to die because he made himself God’s Son.”
v08: When Pilate heard this statement, he became even more afraid.
v09: He went back into the headquarters and asked Jesus, “Where are you from?” But Jesus gave him no answer.
v10: Pilate said, “You will not speak to me? Do you not know that I have authority to release you and authority to crucify you?”
v11: Jesus answered, “You would have no authority over me at all unless it had been given you from above. Therefore the one who handed me over to you has greater sin.”
v12: From then on Pilate tried to release him, but the Jewish leaders shouted, “If you release this man, you are not Caesar’s friend. Everyone who makes himself a king opposes Caesar.”
v13: When Pilate heard these words, he brought Jesus outside and sat down on the judge’s seat at a place called Stone Pavement, and in Hebrew Gabbatha.
v14: It was the day of Preparation for Passover, about the sixth hour. Pilate said to the Jewish leaders, “Look—your king!”
v15: They shouted, “Take him away! Take him away! Crucify him!” Pilate asked, “Shall I crucify your king?” The chief priests answered, “We have no king but Caesar.”
v16: Then Pilate handed Jesus over to them to be crucified.
</p>

## Jesus Is Crucified

<p>
They took Jesus,
v17: and carrying the cross by himself, he went out to the place called Place of the Skull, which is called Golgotha in Hebrew.
v18: There they crucified him, and with him two others, one on each side, with Jesus between them.
v19: Pilate also wrote a notice and put it on the cross. It read, “Jesus the Nazarene, the King of the Jews.”
v20: Many Jewish people read this notice because the place where Jesus was crucified was near the city, and it was written in Hebrew, Latin, and Greek.
v21: The chief priests of the Jewish people said to Pilate, “Do not write, ‘The King of the Jews,’ but, ‘This man said, I am King of the Jews.’”
v22: Pilate answered, “What I have written, I have written.”
</p>

## The Soldiers Divide Jesus’ Clothing

<p>
v23: When the soldiers crucified Jesus, they took his clothing and divided it into four parts, one for each soldier. They also took his tunic. The tunic was seamless, woven in one piece from top to bottom.
v24: So they said to one another, “Let us not tear it, but cast lots for it to see whose it will be.” This happened so that Scripture might be fulfilled:
“They divided my clothes among themselves,
and for my clothing they cast lots.”
So the soldiers did these things.
</p>

## Jesus’ Mother and the Beloved Disciple

<p>
v25: Standing near Jesus’ cross were his mother, his mother’s sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene.
v26: When Jesus saw his mother and the disciple he loved standing nearby, he said to his mother, “Woman, look—your son.”
v27: Then he said to the disciple, “Look—your mother.” From that hour the disciple took her into his own home.
</p>

## Jesus Dies

<p>
v28: After this, Jesus knew that everything had now been completed. So that Scripture might be fulfilled, he said, “I am thirsty.”
v29: A jar full of sour wine was standing there. They put a sponge full of sour wine on a hyssop branch and held it to his mouth.
v30: When Jesus received the sour wine, he said, “It is completed.” Then he bowed his head and gave up his spirit.
</p>

## Jesus’ Side Is Pierced

<p>
v31: Because it was Preparation Day, the Jewish leaders did not want the bodies to remain on the crosses during the Sabbath—because that Sabbath was a great day—so they asked Pilate to have the legs broken and the bodies taken away.
v32: The soldiers came and broke the legs of the first man and then the other who had been crucified with Jesus.
v33: But when they came to Jesus and saw he was already dead, they did not break his legs.
v34: Instead, one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and immediately blood and water came out.
v35: The one who saw this has testified, and his testimony is true. He knows he is telling the truth so that you also may trust.
v36: These things happened so that Scripture might be fulfilled: “Not one of his bones will be broken.”
v37: And again another Scripture says, “They will look on the one they pierced.”
</p>

## Jesus Is Buried

<p>
v38: After these things Joseph of Arimathea, who was a disciple of Jesus secretly because of fear of the Jewish authorities, asked Pilate for permission to take Jesus’ body. Pilate gave permission, so he came and took the body.
v39: Nicodemus, the man who had first come to Jesus at night, also came, bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes weighing about a hundred pounds.
v40: They took Jesus’ body and wrapped it with the spices in linen cloths, according to Jewish burial custom.
v41: There was a garden at the place where Jesus was crucified, and in the garden was a new tomb in which no one had yet been laid.
v42: Because it was the Jewish day of Preparation and the tomb was nearby, they laid Jesus there.
</p>

## Notes

v01: John states Pilate ordered Jesus flogged while continuing to declare him legally innocent.

v05: “Look—the man” may be mockery, pity, or a deeper Johannine irony. Fluent does not decide Pilate’s full intention.

v07: The leadership group frames the charge in terms of Jesus making himself God’s Son.

v11: Jesus relativizes Pilate’s political authority as something “given from above” while still assigning moral responsibility in degrees.

v14: John places the sentencing at Preparation for Passover, preserving his chronology rather than harmonizing it to the Synoptic meal sequence.

v15: The chief priests’ “We have no king but Caesar” is presented as a devastating narrative irony within a particular leadership confrontation. It must not be generalized to Jewish people.

v17: John says Jesus carries his own cross. Simon of Cyrene does not appear in John’s account.

v20: The notice is publicly multilingual and read by many Jewish people; here `Ioudaioi` is broad rather than an official body.

v25: The exact number and relationships among the women can be parsed in more than one way. Fluent follows the common four-woman reading.

v28–30: `teleō` and `teleioō` completion language reaches its climax in “It is completed.”

v29: Hyssop is retained despite questions about the physical size of the plant; John may intentionally evoke Passover and scriptural imagery.

v34–35: Blood and water are eyewitness testimony in the narrative. Fluent does not force one exclusive sacramental, physiological, or symbolic interpretation.

v35: The identity of “the one who saw” is not explicitly named.

v36–37: John interprets details of Jesus’ death through Scripture without presenting the death as accidental.

v38: Joseph’s secrecy is explicitly connected to fear of the Jewish authorities, not fear of Jewish identity or community as a whole.

v39: The enormous quantity of burial spices gives the burial royal and extravagant scale.
