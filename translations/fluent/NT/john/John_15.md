## I Am the True Vine

<p>
v01: “I am the true vine, and my Father is the gardener.
v02: Every branch in me that does not bear fruit he removes, and every branch that bears fruit he prunes so that it may bear more fruit.
v03: You are already clean because of the word I have spoken to you.
v04: Remain in me, and I in you. Just as a branch cannot bear fruit from itself unless it remains in the vine, neither can you unless you remain in me.
v05: I am the vine; you are the branches. Whoever remains in me and I in them bears much fruit, because apart from me you can do nothing.
v06: If anyone does not remain in me, they are thrown out like a branch and wither. Such branches are gathered, thrown into the fire, and burned.
v07: If you remain in me and my words remain in you, ask whatever you want, and it will happen for you.
v08: My Father is glorified in this: that you bear much fruit and become my disciples.
</p>

## Remain in My Love

<p>
v09: “Just as the Father has loved me, I also have loved you. Remain in my love.
v10: If you keep my commandments, you will remain in my love, just as I have kept my Father’s commandments and remain in his love.
v11: I have spoken these things to you so that my joy may be in you and your joy may be complete.
v12: This is my commandment: love one another just as I have loved you.
v13: No one has greater love than this: to lay down one’s life for one’s friends.
v14: You are my friends if you do what I command you.
v15: I no longer call you slaves, because a slave does not know what the master is doing. I have called you friends because I have made known to you everything I heard from my Father.
v16: You did not choose me, but I chose you and appointed you to go and bear fruit—fruit that remains—so that whatever you ask the Father in my name, he may give you.
v17: These things I command you so that you love one another.
</p>

## The World’s Hatred

<p>
v18: “If the world hates you, know that it hated me before it hated you.
v19: If you were from the world, the world would love its own. But because you are not from the world—rather, I chose you out of the world—the world hates you.
v20: Remember the word I told you: ‘A slave is not greater than the master.’ If they persecuted me, they will also persecute you. If they kept my word, they will also keep yours.
v21: But they will do all these things to you because of my name, because they do not know the one who sent me.
v22: If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not have sin; but now they have no excuse for their sin.
v23: Whoever hates me hates my Father also.
v24: If I had not done among them the works no one else did, they would not have sin. But now they have seen and hated both me and my Father.
v25: But this happened so that the word written in their Law might be fulfilled: ‘They hated me without cause.’
v26: “When the Advocate comes, whom I will send to you from the Father—the Spirit of truth who goes out from the Father—he will testify about me.
v27: And you also testify, because you have been with me from the beginning.”
</p>

## Notes

v01: “True vine” draws biblical vine and Israel imagery into Jesus without requiring the translation to state that Israel has simply been discarded or replaced.

v02: `airō` can mean remove or take away and has sometimes been interpreted as “lift up.” In this pruning and fire context, “removes” is the more natural main reading.

v02: `kathairō`, prune or clean, forms a wordplay with `katharos`, “clean,” in v03.

v04–10: `menō`, remain or abide, is intentionally repeated rather than varied away. The repetition is theological architecture.

v07: Prayer remains inside the mutual-indwelling and word-remains frame; the verse is not a detached promise of unrestricted request fulfillment.

v12: “Just as I have loved you” defines the form of the command.

v13: Jesus’ self-giving death stands immediately behind the language of laying down life for friends.

v15: The move from slave to friend concerns disclosed purpose and relationship, not denial that other New Testament texts can call disciples servants of God.

v16: Choosing, sending, fruit-bearing, prayer, and love are woven together rather than separate discipleship programs.

v18–25: “World” here names the human order resistant to Jesus, not every human being, since John also says God loves the world.

v22–24: Increased revelation produces increased responsibility; the language does not mean people without Jesus’ historical ministry were literally sinless.

v25: Jesus calls the quoted Psalm “their Law,” using “Law” broadly for Scripture.

v26: John holds together the Son sending the Advocate “from the Father” and the Spirit “going out from the Father” without translation resolving later doctrinal disputes.
