## Jesus and a Samaritan Woman

<p>
v01: When Jesus learned that the Pharisees had heard he was making and baptizing more disciples than John—
v02: although Jesus himself was not baptizing, but his disciples were—
v03: he left Judea and went back to Galilee.
v04: But he had to pass through Samaria.
v05: So he came to a Samaritan town called Sychar, near the plot of land Jacob had given to his son Joseph.
v06: Jacob’s spring was there. Jesus, tired from the journey, sat down beside the spring. It was about noon.
v07: A Samaritan woman came to draw water. Jesus said to her, “Give me a drink.”
v08: His disciples had gone into the town to buy food.
v09: The Samaritan woman asked him, “How can you, a Jewish man, ask me, a Samaritan woman, for a drink?” For Jewish people do not share utensils with Samaritans.
v10: Jesus answered her, “If you knew God’s gift and who it is saying to you, ‘Give me a drink,’ you would have asked him, and he would have given you living water.”
v11: The woman said, “Sir, you do not even have a bucket, and the well is deep. Where, then, do you get this living water?
v12: Are you greater than our father Jacob, who gave us the well and drank from it himself, along with his sons and livestock?”
v13: Jesus answered, “Everyone who drinks this water will become thirsty again,
v14: but whoever drinks the water I will give will certainly never be thirsty. The water I give will become in that person a spring of water welling up to eternal life.”
v15: The woman said, “Sir, give me this water so I will not be thirsty or have to keep coming here to draw water.”
v16: Jesus told her, “Go call your husband and come back here.”
v17: The woman answered, “I do not have a husband.” Jesus said, “You are right to say, ‘I do not have a husband,’
v18: because you have had five husbands, and the man you now have is not your husband. What you have said is true.”
v19: The woman said, “Sir, I see that you are a prophet.
v20: Our ancestors worshiped on this mountain, but you Jewish people say that Jerusalem is the place where people must worship.”
v21: Jesus told her, “Believe me, woman, an hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem.
v22: You Samaritans worship what you do not know; we worship what we know, because salvation comes from the Jewish people.
v23: But an hour is coming—and is now here—when true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth, for the Father seeks people like these to worship him.
v24: God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.”
v25: The woman said, “I know that Messiah is coming” (the one called Christ). “When he comes, he will explain everything to us.”
v26: Jesus told her, “I am he—the one speaking with you.”
</p>

## The Harvest and Samaritan Trust

<p>
v27: Just then his disciples returned. They were astonished that he was speaking with a woman, yet no one asked, “What do you want?” or “Why are you speaking with her?”
v28: Then the woman left her water jar, went into the town, and said to the people,
v29: “Come see a man who told me everything I have done. Could this be the Messiah?”
v30: They left the town and began coming toward him.
v31: Meanwhile, the disciples urged him, “Rabbi, eat something.”
v32: But he told them, “I have food to eat that you do not know about.”
v33: So the disciples began asking one another, “Could someone have brought him food?”
v34: Jesus told them, “My food is to do the will of the one who sent me and to complete his work.
v35: Do you not say, ‘Four months remain, and then comes the harvest’? Look, I tell you: lift up your eyes and see that the fields are already white for harvest.
v36: The reaper is already receiving wages and gathering fruit for eternal life, so that sower and reaper may rejoice together.
v37: For in this case the saying is true: ‘One sows and another reaps.’
v38: I sent you to reap what you did not labor for. Others have labored, and you have entered into their labor.”
v39: Many Samaritans from that town believed in Jesus because of the woman’s testimony: “He told me everything I have done.”
v40: So when the Samaritans came to him, they asked him to stay with them, and he stayed there two days.
v41: Many more believed because of his own word.
v42: They told the woman, “We no longer believe only because of what you said; we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this man truly is the Savior of the world.”
</p>

## An Official Trusts Jesus’ Word

<p>
v43: After the two days, Jesus left there for Galilee.
v44: For Jesus himself had testified that a prophet has no honor in his own homeland.
v45: When he arrived in Galilee, the Galileans welcomed him because they had seen everything he did in Jerusalem during the festival; they too had gone to the festival.
v46: So Jesus came again to Cana of Galilee, where he had made the water wine. There was a royal official in Capernaum whose son was sick.
v47: When he heard that Jesus had come from Judea to Galilee, he went to him and begged him to come down and heal his son, who was about to die.
v48: Jesus said to him, “Unless all of you see signs and wonders, you will certainly not believe.”
v49: The royal official said, “Lord, come down before my child dies.”
v50: Jesus told him, “Go; your son lives.” The man believed the word Jesus spoke to him and went on his way.
v51: While he was still going down, his servants met him and said that his child was alive.
v52: He asked them at what hour his son began to get better. They told him, “The fever left him yesterday at one in the afternoon.”
v53: Then the father realized that this was the very hour when Jesus had told him, “Your son lives.” So he believed, along with his entire household.
v54: This was the second sign Jesus performed after coming from Judea to Galilee.
</p>

## Notes

v09: The final verb can mean associate with, use together, or share vessels. The immediate request for a drink makes shared utensils a likely part of the boundary being named.

v18: The verse states the woman’s marital history and present relationship but does not state how the relationships ended or assign her blame for them.

v22: ‘Salvation comes from the Jewish people’ affirms the Jewish origin of the saving story and of Jesus; it must not be detached from the Gospel’s Jewish setting.

v23–24: ‘Spirit and truth’ identifies the sphere and character of worship centered on God’s Spirit and reality, not merely inward sincerity contrasted with embodied practice.

v39–42: The Samaritan woman’s testimony brings her town to Jesus. Their direct hearing expands rather than dismisses the importance of her witness.

## Vocabulary

v10: **ὕδωρ ζῶν (hydōr zōn)**: Living water; naturally, flowing fresh water, and in the dialogue God-given life.

v20: **προσκυνέω (proskyneō)**: Bow down, worship, or show reverence.

v25: **Μεσσίας (Messias)**: Messiah, the anointed one; translated into Greek as Christ.

v42: **σωτὴρ τοῦ κόσμου (sōtēr tou kosmou)**: Savior of the world.
