## Jesus and a Samaritan Woman

<p>
v01: When Jesus learned that the Pharisees had heard he was making and baptizing more disciples than John—
v02: although Jesus himself was not baptizing, but his disciples were—
v03: he left Judea and went back to Galilee.
v04: He had to pass through Samaria.
v05: So he came to a Samaritan town called Sychar, near the field Jacob had given to his son Joseph.
v06: Jacob’s well was there. Jesus, tired from the journey, sat down by the well. It was about the sixth hour.
v07: A Samaritan woman came to draw water. Jesus said to her, “Give me a drink.”
v08: His disciples had gone into town to buy food.
v09: The Samaritan woman said, “How can you, a Jewish man, ask me, a Samaritan woman, for a drink?” For Jewish people do not share such things with Samaritans.
v10: Jesus answered, “If you knew God’s gift and who it is saying to you, ‘Give me a drink,’ you would have asked him, and he would have given you living water.”
v11: She said, “Sir, you have no bucket, and the well is deep. Where then do you get this living water?
v12: Are you greater than our ancestor Jacob, who gave us the well and drank from it himself, along with his sons and livestock?”
v13: Jesus answered, “Everyone who drinks this water will be thirsty again.
v14: But whoever drinks the water I will give will never be thirsty. The water I will give will become in that person a spring of water welling up to eternal life.”
v15: The woman said, “Sir, give me this water so that I will not be thirsty or have to keep coming here to draw water.”
v16: Jesus said to her, “Go, call your husband, and come back.”
v17: The woman answered, “I have no husband.” Jesus said, “You are right to say, ‘I have no husband,’
v18: because you have had five husbands, and the man you now have is not your husband. What you said is true.”
v19: The woman said, “Sir, I see that you are a prophet.
v20: Our ancestors worshiped on this mountain, but you Jewish people say the place where people must worship is in Jerusalem.”
v21: Jesus said, “Woman, trust me, an hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem.
v22: You worship what you do not know; we worship what we know, because salvation is from the Jews.
v23: But an hour is coming—and is now here—when true worshipers will worship the Father in Spirit and truth. Indeed, the Father is seeking such people to worship him.
v24: God is Spirit, and those who worship him must worship in Spirit and truth.”
v25: The woman said, “I know that Messiah is coming”—the one called Christ. “When he comes, he will tell us everything.”
v26: Jesus said to her, “I am—he is the one speaking to you.”
</p>

## The Harvest and Samaritan Trust

<p>
v27: Just then his disciples returned. They were amazed that he was speaking with a woman, yet no one asked, “What do you want?” or, “Why are you talking with her?”
v28: The woman left her water jar, went into the town, and told the people,
v29: “Come and see a man who told me everything I ever did! Could this be the Christ?”
v30: They left the town and began coming to him.
v31: Meanwhile the disciples kept urging him, “Rabbi, eat.”
v32: But he said, “I have food to eat that you do not know about.”
v33: So the disciples asked one another, “Did someone bring him something to eat?”
v34: Jesus said, “My food is to do the will of the one who sent me and complete his work.
v35: Do you not say, ‘Four months more, and then comes the harvest’? Look, I tell you, lift up your eyes and see that the fields are already white for harvest.
v36: The one who reaps is receiving wages and gathering fruit for eternal life, so that the sower and reaper may rejoice together.
v37: In this the saying is true: ‘One sows and another reaps.’
v38: I sent you to reap what you did not labor for. Others have labored, and you have entered into their labor.”
v39: Many Samaritans from that town trusted in Jesus because of the woman’s testimony: “He told me everything I ever did.”
v40: So when the Samaritans came to him, they asked him to remain with them, and he remained there two days.
v41: Many more trusted because of his word.
v42: They told the woman, “We no longer trust only because of what you said. We have heard for ourselves, and we know that this man truly is the Savior of the world.”
</p>

## An Official Trusts Jesus’ Word

<p>
v43: After the two days Jesus left there for Galilee.
v44: For Jesus himself had testified that a prophet has no honor in his own country.
v45: When he came to Galilee, the Galileans welcomed him because they had seen everything he did in Jerusalem during the festival, since they too had gone to the festival.
v46: Jesus came again to Cana in Galilee, where he had made the water wine. A royal official was there whose son was sick in Capernaum.
v47: When he heard Jesus had come from Judea into Galilee, he went to him and begged him to come down and heal his son, who was about to die.
v48: Jesus said to him, “Unless you people see signs and wonders, you will certainly not trust.”
v49: The official said, “Sir, come down before my child dies.”
v50: Jesus told him, “Go. Your son lives.” The man trusted the word Jesus spoke and went on his way.
v51: While he was going down, his servants met him and said his child was alive.
v52: He asked them when the boy began to get better. They told him, “Yesterday at the seventh hour the fever left him.”
v53: The father knew this was the hour when Jesus told him, “Your son lives.” He and his whole household trusted.
v54: This was the second sign Jesus did after coming from Judea to Galilee.
</p>

## Notes

v04: `dei`, “had to,” can express geographical necessity but often carries theological or narrative necessity in John. Fluent preserves the strong wording without specifying which sense dominates.

v09: The parenthetical comment about Jewish and Samaritan interaction is difficult in detail. Fluent communicates the social boundary without implying absolute absence of all contact.

v10: “Living water” means fresh or flowing water in ordinary speech and life-giving water in Jesus’ metaphor.

v18: Jesus states the woman’s relational history without adding moral accusations or sexual motives the text does not supply.

v22: “Salvation is from the Jews” is retained without qualification. Jesus speaks as a Jew inside Israel’s story, not against Judaism as an outsider.

v23–24: “Spirit and truth” does not simply mean sincerity or inwardness. The Gospel connects Spirit and truth with God’s own life and revelation.

v26: `egō eimi` is an “I am” self-identification. The following participial phrase makes the identification especially direct.

v34: Sending and completing-work language becomes a major way John describes Jesus’ relationship to the Father.

v39–42: The woman’s testimony leads others to hear for themselves; Samaritans voice the expansive confession “Savior of the world.”

v48–50: The official is challenged about sign-dependent trust and then trusts Jesus’ word before seeing the result.
