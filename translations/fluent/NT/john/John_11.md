## Lazarus Dies

<p>
v01: A man named Lazarus was sick. He was from Bethany, the village of Mary and her sister Martha.
v02: This Mary was the one who anointed the Lord with perfume and wiped his feet with her hair; her brother Lazarus was sick.
v03: So the sisters sent word to Jesus: “Lord, look—the one you love is sick.”
v04: When Jesus heard this, he said, “This sickness will not end in death, but is for God’s glory, so that God’s Son may be glorified through it.”
v05: Jesus loved Martha, her sister, and Lazarus.
v06: Yet when he heard Lazarus was sick, he stayed two more days in the place where he was.
v07: Then after this he said to the disciples, “Let us go to Judea again.”
v08: The disciples said, “Rabbi, the Jewish leaders were just now trying to stone you, and you are going there again?”
v09: Jesus answered, “Are there not twelve hours in the day? If anyone walks during the day, they do not stumble because they see this world’s light.
v10: But if anyone walks at night, they stumble because the light is not in them.”
v11: After saying this, he told them, “Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep, but I am going to awaken him.”
v12: The disciples said, “Lord, if he has fallen asleep, he will recover.”
v13: Jesus had spoken about his death, but they thought he meant ordinary sleep.
v14: Then Jesus told them plainly, “Lazarus has died.
v15: For your sake I am glad I was not there, so that you may trust. But let us go to him.”
v16: Thomas, called Twin, said to his fellow disciples, “Let us also go so that we may die with him.”
</p>

## I Am the Resurrection and the Life

<p>
v17: When Jesus arrived, he found that Lazarus had already been in the tomb four days.
v18: Bethany was near Jerusalem, about fifteen stadia away.
v19: Many Jewish people had come to Martha and Mary to comfort them concerning their brother.
v20: When Martha heard Jesus was coming, she went to meet him, but Mary stayed in the house.
v21: Martha said to Jesus, “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.
v22: Even now I know that whatever you ask God, God will give you.”
v23: Jesus told her, “Your brother will rise.”
v24: Martha said, “I know he will rise in the resurrection on the last day.”
v25: Jesus said to her, “I am the resurrection and the life. Whoever trusts in me will live even if they die,
v26: and everyone who lives and trusts in me will never die. Do you trust this?”
v27: She said, “Yes, Lord. I have trusted that you are the Christ, God’s Son, the one coming into the world.”
</p>

## Jesus Weeps

<p>
v28: After saying this, Martha went and called her sister Mary privately: “The Teacher is here and is calling for you.”
v29: When Mary heard this, she got up quickly and went to him.
v30: Jesus had not yet entered the village but was still in the place where Martha had met him.
v31: The Jewish people who were with Mary in the house comforting her saw her get up quickly and go out, and they followed her, thinking she was going to the tomb to weep there.
v32: When Mary came to where Jesus was and saw him, she fell at his feet and said, “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.”
v33: When Jesus saw her weeping, and the Jewish people who came with her also weeping, he was deeply moved in spirit and troubled.
v34: He asked, “Where have you laid him?” They said, “Lord, come and see.”
v35: Jesus wept.
v36: So the Jewish people said, “See how he loved him!”
v37: But some of them said, “Could not the one who opened the blind man’s eyes have kept this man from dying?”
</p>

## Lazarus Is Raised

<p>
v38: Jesus, deeply moved again, came to the tomb. It was a cave, and a stone lay against it.
v39: Jesus said, “Take away the stone.” Martha, the dead man’s sister, said, “Lord, by now there will be an odor, because it has been four days.”
v40: Jesus told her, “Did I not tell you that if you trust, you will see God’s glory?”
v41: So they took away the stone. Jesus lifted his eyes and said, “Father, I thank you that you heard me.
v42: I knew you always hear me, but I said this because of the crowd standing here, so that they may trust that you sent me.”
v43: After saying this, he cried out with a loud voice, “Lazarus, come out!”
v44: The dead man came out, his feet and hands bound with strips of cloth and his face wrapped with a cloth. Jesus told them, “Unbind him and let him go.”
</p>

## The Plot to Kill Jesus

<p>
v45: Many of the Jewish people who had come to Mary and saw what Jesus did trusted in him.
v46: But some of them went to the Pharisees and told them what Jesus had done.
v47: So the chief priests and Pharisees gathered the council and said, “What are we doing? This man performs many signs.
v48: If we let him continue like this, everyone will trust in him, and the Romans will come and take away both our place and our nation.”
v49: But one of them, Caiaphas, who was high priest that year, said, “You know nothing at all.
v50: You do not consider that it is better for you that one man die for the people rather than the whole nation perish.”
v51: He did not say this from himself. As high priest that year, he prophesied that Jesus was about to die for the nation,
v52: and not for the nation alone, but also to gather into one the scattered children of God.
v53: So from that day they planned together to kill him.
v54: Therefore Jesus no longer walked openly among the Jewish people, but went from there to the region near the wilderness, to a town called Ephraim, and stayed there with the disciples.
v55: The Jewish Passover was near. Many people from the countryside went up to Jerusalem before Passover to purify themselves.
v56: They were looking for Jesus and saying to one another while standing in the temple, “What do you think? Surely he will not come to the festival, will he?”
v57: The chief priests and Pharisees had given orders that if anyone knew where Jesus was, they should report it so they could arrest him.
</p>

## Notes

v04–06: John explicitly says Jesus loves Lazarus and his sisters, then narrates a two-day delay. The delay cannot be explained by indifference.

v11–14: Jesus uses sleep as a death metaphor; the disciples misunderstand it literally, continuing John’s pattern of misunderstanding language with deeper meaning.

v25: “I am the resurrection and the life” makes resurrection not only a future event but a reality centered in Jesus.

v26: “Will never die” stands beside v25’s acknowledgment that believers physically die. John’s life/death contrast is larger than biological survival.

v33: `embrimaomai` can express deep agitation, indignation, or groaning. Fluent uses “deeply moved” without eliminating the stronger emotional range.

v35: The shortest verse in many English traditions remains direct: “Jesus wept.”

v40: Trust and seeing glory are linked, but the sign itself also generates new trust in vv45–46.

v44: “Unbind him and let him go” restores Lazarus to ordinary communal life after the extraordinary sign.

v48: “Our place and our nation” expresses leadership fear of Roman intervention; “place” may especially refer to the temple.

v50–52: Caiaphas speaks politically, while the narrator interprets his words prophetically and expands their scope beyond the nation.

v54: “Jewish people” here refers to the wider public setting in which Jesus no longer walks openly, while the death plot is specifically attributed to leadership bodies.
