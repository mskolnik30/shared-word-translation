## Lazarus Dies

<p>
v01: A man named Lazarus was sick. He was from Bethany, the village of Mary and her sister Martha.
v02: This was the Mary who anointed the Lord with perfumed oil and wiped his feet with her hair; it was her brother Lazarus who was sick.
v03: So the sisters sent word to Jesus: “Lord, look, the one you love is sick.”
v04: When Jesus heard this, he said, “This illness will not end in death but is for God’s glory, so that God’s Son may be glorified through it.”
v05: Jesus loved Martha, her sister, and Lazarus.
v06: Yet when he heard that Lazarus was sick, he stayed where he was two more days.
v07: Then after this he told the disciples, “Let us go back to Judea.”
v08: The disciples said, “Rabbi, the Jewish authorities were just now trying to stone you, and you are going there again?”
v09: Jesus answered, “Are there not twelve hours of daylight? If anyone walks during the day, they do not stumble, because they see by this world’s light.
v10: But if anyone walks at night, they stumble because the light is not in them.”
v11: After saying this, he told them, “Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep, but I am going to wake him.”
v12: The disciples said, “Lord, if he has fallen asleep, he will recover.”
v13: Jesus had spoken about Lazarus’s death, but they thought he meant natural sleep.
v14: Then Jesus told them plainly, “Lazarus has died.
v15: For your sake I am glad I was not there, so that you may believe. But let us go to him.”
v16: Then Thomas, who was called Twin, said to his fellow disciples, “Let us go too, so that we may die with him.”
</p>

## I Am the Resurrection and the Life

<p>
v17: When Jesus arrived, he found that Lazarus had already been in the tomb four days.
v18: Bethany was near Jerusalem, about fifteen stadia away,
v19: and many Jewish people had come to Martha and Mary to comfort them about their brother.
v20: When Martha heard that Jesus was coming, she went to meet him, while Mary remained seated in the house.
v21: Martha told Jesus, “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.
v22: But even now I know that God will give you whatever you ask him.”
v23: Jesus told her, “Your brother will rise.”
v24: Martha replied, “I know that he will rise in the resurrection on the last day.”
v25: Jesus told her, “I am the resurrection and the life. Whoever believes in me will live even if they die,
v26: and everyone who lives and believes in me will certainly never die. Do you believe this?”
v27: She told him, “Yes, Lord. I have come to believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, the one coming into the world.”
</p>

## Jesus Weeps

<p>
v28: After saying this, she went and called her sister Mary privately, saying, “The Teacher is here and is calling for you.”
v29: When Mary heard this, she got up quickly and went to him.
v30: Jesus had not yet entered the village but was still at the place where Martha had met him.
v31: The Jewish mourners who were with Mary in the house comforting her saw her get up quickly and go out. They followed her, thinking she was going to the tomb to weep there.
v32: When Mary reached the place where Jesus was and saw him, she fell at his feet and said, “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.”
v33: When Jesus saw her weeping, and the Jewish mourners who came with her also weeping, he was deeply moved in spirit and troubled himself.
v34: He asked, “Where have you laid him?” They said, “Lord, come and see.”
v35: Jesus wept.
v36: So the mourners said, “See how he loved him!”
v37: But some of them said, “Could not the one who opened the blind man’s eyes also have kept this man from dying?”
</p>

## Lazarus Is Raised

<p>
v38: Deeply moved again within himself, Jesus came to the tomb. It was a cave with a stone lying against it.
v39: Jesus said, “Remove the stone.” Martha, the dead man’s sister, told him, “Lord, by now there will be a smell, because it has been four days.”
v40: Jesus said to her, “Did I not tell you that if you believed, you would see God’s glory?”
v41: So they removed the stone. Jesus looked upward and said, “Father, I thank you that you have heard me.
v42: I knew that you always hear me, but I said this because of the crowd standing around, so that they may believe that you sent me.”
v43: After saying this, he cried out in a loud voice, “Lazarus, come out!”
v44: The man who had died came out, his feet and hands bound with strips of cloth and his face wrapped in a cloth. Jesus told them, “Unbind him and let him go.”
</p>

## The Plot to Kill Jesus

<p>
v45: Therefore, many of the Jewish people who had come to Mary and saw what Jesus did believed in him.
v46: But some of them went to the Pharisees and told them what Jesus had done.
v47: So the chief priests and Pharisees convened the council and said, “What are we doing? This man is performing many signs.
v48: If we let him continue like this, everyone will believe in him, and the Romans will come and take away both our place and our nation.”
v49: But one of them, Caiaphas, who was high priest that year, told them, “You know nothing at all.
v50: You do not consider that it is better for you that one man die for the people than that the whole nation perish.”
v51: He did not say this on his own; as high priest that year, he prophesied that Jesus was going to die for the nation,
v52: and not for the nation alone, but also to gather into one God’s scattered children.
v53: So from that day on, they planned to kill him.
v54: Therefore Jesus no longer moved about publicly among the Judeans. Instead, he left there for the region near the wilderness, to a town called Ephraim, and stayed there with his disciples.
v55: The Jewish Passover was near, and many people from the countryside went up to Jerusalem before Passover to purify themselves.
v56: They kept looking for Jesus and, while standing in the temple courts, asked one another, “What do you think? Surely he will not come to the festival, will he?”
v57: The chief priests and Pharisees had given orders that anyone who knew where Jesus was should report it so they could arrest him.
</p>

## Notes

v04: The illness is ‘for God’s glory’ in the narrative’s purpose statement. The wording should not be turned into a general claim that sufferers’ illnesses are divinely inflicted for display.

v16: Thomas’s courageous or resigned proposal to die with Jesus should be heard before his later doubt is imported into the scene.

v25–26: Jesus identifies himself as resurrection and life and then asks Martha for trust, keeping resurrection hope personal and relational.

v33, 38: The verbs describe profound agitation as well as emotion. Jesus’ tears do not remove his anger or disturbance at death.

v49–52: Caiaphas’s political calculation accepts one man’s death for the people. The narrator gives his words a further prophetic meaning about Jesus gathering God’s scattered children.

## Vocabulary

v16: **Δίδυμος (Didymos)**: Twin, the Greek explanation of Thomas’s name.

v25: **ἀνάστασις (anastasis)**: Resurrection, rising from the dead.

v35: **δακρύω (dakryō)**: Shed tears or weep.
