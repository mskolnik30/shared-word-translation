## The Word

<p>
v01: In the beginning was the Word,
and the Word was with God,
and the Word was God.
v02: He was in the beginning with God.
v03: All things came into being through him,
and apart from him not one thing came into being.
v04: In him was life,
and the life was the light of humanity.
v05: The light shines in the darkness,
and the darkness has not overcome it.
</p>

<p>
v06: There was a man sent from God whose name was John.
v07: He came as a witness, to testify about the light, so that all might trust through him.
v08: He was not the light, but came to testify about the light.
v09: The true light, which gives light to everyone, was coming into the world.
v10: He was in the world,
and the world came into being through him,
yet the world did not know him.
v11: He came to his own,
and his own did not receive him.
v12: But to all who received him,
who trusted in his name,
he gave authority to become children of God—
v13: those who were born,
not from blood,
nor from the will of flesh,
nor from the will of a man,
but from God.
</p>

<p>
v14: The Word became flesh
and made his dwelling among us.
We saw his glory,
glory as of the Father’s one and only Son,
full of grace and truth.
v15: John testified about him and cried out, “This is the one I spoke about: ‘The one coming after me has come before me, because he existed before me.’”
v16: From his fullness we have all received,
grace upon grace.
v17: For the Law was given through Moses;
grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.
v18: No one has ever seen God.
The one and only God,
who is close to the Father’s heart,
has made him known.
</p>

## John’s Witness

<p>
v19: This is John’s testimony when the Jewish leaders sent priests and Levites from Jerusalem to ask him, “Who are you?”
v20: He confessed and did not deny it. He confessed, “I am not the Christ.”
v21: They asked him, “What then? Are you Elijah?” He said, “I am not.” “Are you the Prophet?” He answered, “No.”
v22: They said to him, “Who are you? We need an answer for those who sent us. What do you say about yourself?”
v23: He said, “I am
‘a voice crying in the wilderness:
Make straight the way of the Lord,’
as Isaiah the prophet said.”
v24: Those who had been sent were from the Pharisees.
v25: They asked him, “Why then are you baptizing if you are not the Christ, Elijah, or the Prophet?”
v26: John answered, “I baptize with water. Among you stands one you do not know,
v27: the one coming after me. I am not worthy to untie the strap of his sandal.”
v28: These things happened in Bethany across the Jordan, where John was baptizing.
</p>

## The Lamb of God

<p>
v29: The next day John saw Jesus coming toward him and said, “Look—the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!
v30: This is the one I spoke about: ‘After me comes a man who has come before me, because he existed before me.’
v31: I myself did not know him, but I came baptizing with water so that he might be revealed to Israel.”
v32: John testified, “I saw the Spirit descending from heaven like a dove, and it remained on him.
v33: I myself did not know him, but the one who sent me to baptize with water told me, ‘The one on whom you see the Spirit descend and remain is the one who baptizes with the Holy Spirit.’
v34: I have seen and testified that this is God’s Chosen One.”
</p>

## The First Disciples

<p>
v35: The next day John was standing there again with two of his disciples.
v36: Watching Jesus walk by, he said, “Look—the Lamb of God!”
v37: The two disciples heard him say this and followed Jesus.
v38: Jesus turned, saw them following, and asked, “What are you looking for?” They said, “Rabbi”—which means Teacher—“where are you staying?”
v39: He said, “Come and see.” So they went and saw where he was staying, and they remained with him that day. It was about the tenth hour.
v40: Andrew, Simon Peter’s brother, was one of the two who heard John and followed Jesus.
v41: He first found his own brother Simon and told him, “We have found the Messiah”—which means Christ.
v42: He brought Simon to Jesus. Jesus looked at him and said, “You are Simon son of John. You will be called Cephas”—which is translated Peter.
v43: The next day Jesus decided to go to Galilee. He found Philip and said to him, “Follow me.”
v44: Philip was from Bethsaida, the town of Andrew and Peter.
v45: Philip found Nathanael and told him, “We have found the one Moses wrote about in the Law, and the Prophets too: Jesus son of Joseph, from Nazareth.”
v46: Nathanael said, “Can anything good come from Nazareth?” Philip said, “Come and see.”
v47: Jesus saw Nathanael coming toward him and said, “Look—truly an Israelite in whom there is no deceit.”
v48: Nathanael asked, “How do you know me?” Jesus answered, “Before Philip called you, while you were under the fig tree, I saw you.”
v49: Nathanael answered, “Rabbi, you are God’s Son! You are the King of Israel!”
v50: Jesus answered, “Do you trust because I told you I saw you under the fig tree? You will see greater things than these.”
v51: Then he said, “Truly, truly, I tell you: you will see heaven opened and God’s angels ascending and descending on the Son of Man.”
</p>

## Notes

v01: “The Word was God” preserves the Greek predication without weakening it to divine quality alone or collapsing the Word into the Father.

v03–04: Ancient punctuation traditions differ over where “what has come into being” belongs. Fluent follows the common critical-text division reflected here.

v05: `katalambanō` can mean grasp or comprehend as well as seize or overcome. Fluent uses “overcome” while preserving the other range here.

v09: The syntax can attach “coming into the world” either to the light or to every person. Fluent follows the reading in which the true light is coming into the world.

v14: `eskēnōsen` literally evokes pitching a tent or tabernacling. “Made his dwelling among us” preserves natural English while retaining the resonance here.

v14: `monogenēs` means one-and-only or unique. Fluent does not use “only begotten” as a lexical claim.

v16: “Grace upon grace” preserves the compact phrase without resolving whether it means grace replacing grace or grace piled upon grace.

v18: SBLGNT reads `monogenēs theos`, “the one and only God,” rather than the “only Son” reading in current TSW. Fluent follows the pinned source and records the important variant.

v19: “Jewish leaders” is contextual: priests and Levites are sent from Jerusalem by an identifiable authority group. It is not a replacement for every occurrence of `Ioudaioi` in John.

v29: “Lamb of God” is retained without choosing one exclusive Passover, sacrificial, Servant, or apocalyptic background.

v32–33: “Remain” begins a major Johannine word-family that will become central later in the Gospel and in 1 John.

v34: SBLGNT reads “God’s Chosen One.” “Son of God” is an important textual variant and the reading in current TSW.

v51: John’s doubled “Truly, truly” formula is retained throughout the Gospel.

“Word, speech, message.”

v05: καταλαμβάνω (katalambanō)
“To grasp, comprehend, seize, overcome.”

“True, genuine.”

v14: σκηνόω (skēnoō)
“To dwell in a tent, tabernacle.”

v14: μονογενής (monogenēs)
“One and only, unique.”

v14: χάρις (charis)
“Grace, favor, gift.”

v32: μένω (menō)
“To remain, abide, stay.”

v34: ἐκλεκτός (eklektos)
“Chosen.”

v36: ἀμνός (amnos)
“Lamb.”

v51: ἀμήν ἀμήν (amēn amēn)
“Truly, truly.”
