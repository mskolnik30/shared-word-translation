## Jesus Is Arrested

<p>
v01: After saying these things, Jesus went out with his disciples across the Kidron ravine, where there was a garden. He and his disciples entered it.
v02: Judas, who was betraying him, also knew the place, because Jesus had often gathered there with his disciples.
v03: So Judas brought a cohort of soldiers and officers from the chief priests and Pharisees, and came there with lanterns, torches, and weapons.
v04: Jesus, knowing everything that was about to happen to him, stepped forward and asked them, “Whom are you looking for?”
v05: They answered him, “Jesus the Nazarene.” He told them, “I am he.” Judas, who was betraying him, was standing with them.
v06: When Jesus told them, “I am he,” they drew back and fell to the ground.
v07: So he asked them again, “Whom are you looking for?” They answered, “Jesus the Nazarene.”
v08: Jesus replied, “I told you that I am he. So if you are looking for me, let these men go.”
v09: This happened so that the word he had spoken would be fulfilled: “I did not lose one of those you gave me.”
v10: Then Simon Peter, who had a sword, drew it and struck the high priest’s slave, cutting off his right ear. The slave’s name was Malchus.
v11: Jesus told Peter, “Put the sword into its sheath. Should I not drink the cup the Father has given me?”
</p>

## Jesus Before Annas and Caiaphas

<p>
v12: Then the cohort, its commander, and the Judean leaders’ officers arrested Jesus and bound him.
v13: They took him first to Annas, because he was the father-in-law of Caiaphas, who was high priest that year.
v14: Caiaphas was the one who had advised the Judean leaders that it was better for one man to die for the people.
v15: Simon Peter and another disciple followed Jesus. That disciple was known to the high priest, so he went with Jesus into the high priest’s courtyard.
v16: Peter remained outside by the gate. So the other disciple, who was known to the high priest, went out, spoke to the gatekeeper, and brought Peter in.
v17: The servant girl who kept the gate asked Peter, “You are not one of this man’s disciples too, are you?” He answered, “I am not.”
v18: The slaves and officers had made a charcoal fire because it was cold, and they were standing around warming themselves. Peter was also standing with them, warming himself.
v19: Meanwhile, the high priest questioned Jesus about his disciples and his teaching.
v20: Jesus answered him, “I have spoken openly to the world. I always taught in a synagogue and in the temple courts, where all the Jewish people gather. I said nothing in secret.
v21: Why question me? Question those who heard what I told them. Look, they know what I said.”
v22: When Jesus said this, one of the officers standing nearby slapped him and said, “Is that how you answer the high priest?”
v23: Jesus answered him, “If I spoke wrongly, testify to what was wrong. But if I spoke rightly, why do you hit me?”
v24: Then Annas sent him, still bound, to Caiaphas the high priest.
</p>

## Peter Denies Jesus

<p>
v25: Simon Peter was still standing and warming himself. So they asked him, “You are not one of his disciples too, are you?” He denied it and said, “I am not.”
v26: One of the high priest’s slaves—a relative of the man whose ear Peter had cut off—asked, “Didn’t I see you in the garden with him?”
v27: Peter denied it again, and immediately a rooster crowed.
</p>

## Jesus Before Pilate

<p>
v28: Then they led Jesus from Caiaphas to the governor’s headquarters. It was early morning. They did not enter the headquarters themselves, so that they would not become ceremonially unclean but could eat the Passover meal.
v29: So Pilate came outside to them and asked, “What accusation do you bring against this man?”
v30: They answered him, “If this man were not doing evil, we would not have handed him over to you.”
v31: Pilate told them, “Take him yourselves and judge him according to your law.” The Judean leaders replied, “We are not permitted to put anyone to death.”
v32: This happened so that Jesus’ word would be fulfilled, indicating what kind of death he was about to die.
v33: Pilate went back into the headquarters, summoned Jesus, and asked him, “Are you the king of the Jews?”
v34: Jesus answered, “Are you saying this on your own, or did others tell you about me?”
v35: Pilate replied, “I am not Jewish, am I? Your own nation and the chief priests handed you over to me. What have you done?”
v36: Jesus answered, “My kingdom does not come from this world. If my kingdom came from this world, my servants would be fighting to keep me from being handed over to the Judean leaders. But as it is, my kingdom does not come from here.”
v37: Pilate asked him, “So you are a king?” Jesus answered, “You say that I am a king. For this I was born, and for this I came into the world: to testify to the truth. Everyone who belongs to the truth listens to my voice.”
v38: Pilate asked him, “What is truth?” After saying this, he went outside again to the Judean leaders and told them, “I find no basis for a charge against him.
v39: But you have a custom that I release one prisoner to you at Passover. So do you want me to release the king of the Jews?”
v40: They shouted again, “Not this man, but Barabbas!” Now Barabbas was a violent rebel.
</p>

## Notes

v05–06: ‘I am he’ is ordinary self-identification in the arrest exchange and also echoes John’s absolute ‘I am’ language. The wording leaves that resonance visible.

v12, 31, 36: ‘The Judean leaders’ identifies the authorities and officers acting in the Jerusalem proceedings. It does not refer to Jewish people as a whole; Jesus, his disciples, and the first witnesses are Jewish.

v17: The gatekeeper is a young female slave. Her gender and social status are retained.

v22–23: An officer slaps Jesus during questioning. The translation names the blow plainly rather than turning it into a verbal insult.

v28: The leaders remain outside the Roman headquarters to avoid ceremonial defilement and to eat the Passover meal, creating a deliberate irony in the narrative.

v36: Jesus says his kingdom is not ‘from’ this world, describing its source. He does not say it has no claim upon or concern for the world.

v40: The noun can mean robber, bandit, or insurrectionist. ‘Violent rebel’ fits the political contrast with Jesus’ kingship without denying criminal violence.

## Vocabulary

v03: **σπεῖρα (speira)**: A Roman military cohort or a detachment from one.

v28: **πραιτώριον (praitōrion)**: The Roman governor’s headquarters.

v36: **βασιλεία (basileia)**: Kingdom, reign, or royal rule.
