## Jesus Appears by the Sea

<p>
v01: After these things Jesus revealed himself again to the disciples at the Sea of Tiberias. He revealed himself in this way.
v02: Simon Peter, Thomas called Twin, Nathanael from Cana in Galilee, the sons of Zebedee, and two other disciples were together.
v03: Simon Peter said, “I am going fishing.” They told him, “We are coming with you.” They went out and got into the boat, but that night they caught nothing.
v04: Just as day was breaking, Jesus stood on the shore, but the disciples did not know it was Jesus.
v05: Jesus said, “Children, you do not have any fish, do you?” They answered, “No.”
v06: He said, “Cast the net on the right side of the boat, and you will find some.” So they cast it, and now they could not haul it in because of the great number of fish.
v07: The disciple Jesus loved said to Peter, “It is the Lord!” When Simon Peter heard it was the Lord, he put on his outer garment, because he was stripped for work, and threw himself into the sea.
v08: The other disciples came in the little boat, dragging the net full of fish, because they were not far from land, only about two hundred cubits away.
v09: When they came ashore, they saw a charcoal fire there with fish lying on it and bread.
v10: Jesus told them, “Bring some of the fish you just caught.”
v11: Simon Peter went aboard and hauled the net ashore, full of large fish—one hundred fifty-three of them. Even though there were so many, the net was not torn.
v12: Jesus said, “Come and have breakfast.” None of the disciples dared ask him, “Who are you?” because they knew it was the Lord.
v13: Jesus came, took the bread, and gave it to them, and did the same with the fish.
v14: This was now the third time Jesus was revealed to the disciples after being raised from the dead.
</p>
## Jesus Restores Peter

<p>
v15: When they had eaten breakfast, Jesus said to Simon Peter, “Simon son of John, do you love me more than these?” Peter said, “Yes, Lord. You know that I love you.” Jesus told him, “Feed my lambs.”
v16: He asked him a second time, “Simon son of John, do you love me?” Peter said, “Yes, Lord. You know that I love you.” Jesus told him, “Shepherd my sheep.”
v17: He asked him the third time, “Simon son of John, do you love me?” Peter was grieved because Jesus asked him the third time, “Do you love me?” He said, “Lord, you know everything. You know that I love you.” Jesus told him, “Feed my sheep.”
v18: “Truly, truly, I tell you, when you were younger, you tied your own belt and walked where you wanted. But when you grow old, you will stretch out your hands, and someone else will tie you and carry you where you do not want to go.”
v19: He said this to indicate by what kind of death Peter would glorify God. After saying this, he told him, “Follow me.”
</p>
## What About This Disciple?

<p>
v20: Peter turned and saw the disciple Jesus loved following them—the one who had leaned back against Jesus at the supper and asked, “Lord, who is the one handing you over?”
v21: When Peter saw him, he said to Jesus, “Lord, what about this man?”
v22: Jesus said, “If I want him to remain until I come, what is that to you? You follow me.”
v23: So this saying spread among the brothers that this disciple would not die. But Jesus did not tell Peter that he would not die; he said, “If I want him to remain until I come, what is that to you?”
</p>

## The Witness Behind the Gospel

<p>
v24: This is the disciple who testifies about these things and wrote these things, and we know that his testimony is true.
v25: There are also many other things Jesus did. If every one of them were written down, I suppose the world itself could not contain the books that would be written.
</p>

## Notes

v03: The disciples’ return to fishing is narrated without an explicit statement that Peter abandoned his calling. Fluent does not add motive.

v06–11: The catch is abundant and specific—153 large fish—yet the net is not torn. John does not explain the number symbolically.

v09: The charcoal fire deliberately echoes the charcoal fire at Peter’s denial in 18:18, giving the restoration scene a narrative memory.

v12–13: The risen Jesus serves breakfast. Recognition, meal, and provision are held together without requiring a sacramental explanation.

v15–17: Jesus asks Peter three times about love and gives three shepherding commands. The threefold exchange unmistakably answers Peter’s three denials, though John does not reduce it to a mechanical cancellation formula.

v15–17: John varies `agapaō` and `phileō` as well as lambs/sheep and feed/shepherd. Fluent translates both love verbs as “love” because strong evidence for a stable contrast in this dialogue is lacking, while the lexical variation is recorded here.

v15: “More than these” can refer to these other disciples, to their love, or to the fishing equipment/catch. Fluent leaves the comparison open.

v18–19: The narrator interprets Jesus’ words as indicating the kind of death by which Peter would glorify God. “Stretch out your hands” therefore has death imagery in context.

v19: Peter’s renewed commission ends where his first calling began: “Follow me.”

v22–23: Jesus refuses Peter’s attempt to make the beloved disciple’s future part of Peter’s vocation. The narrator then corrects a misunderstanding that had spread in the community.

v24: The shift from “this disciple” to “we know” preserves the Gospel’s own witness language without forcing a theory of final composition.

v25: The hyperbolic closing stresses the inexhaustibility of Jesus’ works rather than supplying a literal estimate of book capacity.
