## Warning to the Rich

<p>
v01: Come now, you rich people: weep and wail over the miseries that are coming upon you.
v02: Your riches have rotted, and your clothes have become moth-eaten.
v03: Your gold and silver have corroded, and their corrosion will testify against you and eat your flesh like fire. You have stored up treasure in the last days.
v04: Look! The wages you withheld from the workers who harvested your fields are crying out, and the cries of the harvesters have reached the ears of the Lord of hosts.
v05: You have lived on the earth in luxury and self-indulgence. You have fattened your hearts for a day of slaughter.
v06: You have condemned and murdered the righteous person, who does not resist you.
</p>

## Be Patient Until the Lord’s Coming

<p>
v07: Therefore be patient, brothers and sisters, until the coming of the Lord. See how the farmer waits for the precious fruit of the earth, being patient for it until it receives the early and late rains.
v08: You also must be patient. Strengthen your hearts, because the coming of the Lord is near.
v09: Do not grumble against one another, brothers and sisters, so that you may not be judged. Look—the Judge is standing at the door.
v10: As an example of suffering and patience, brothers and sisters, take the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord.
v11: Look, we call those blessed who endured. You have heard of Job's endurance and have seen the Lord's purpose in the end—that the Lord is deeply compassionate and merciful.
</p>

## Let Your Yes Be Yes

<p>
v12: Above all, my brothers and sisters, do not swear—not by heaven, earth, or any other oath. Let your “yes” be yes and your “no” be no, so that you do not fall under judgment.
</p>

## Prayer, Healing, and Confession

<p>
v13: Is anyone among you suffering? Let that person pray. Is anyone cheerful? Let that person sing praise.
v14: Is anyone among you sick? Let that person call for the elders of the church, and let them pray over them, anointing them with oil in the name of the Lord.
v15: The prayer of faith will save the one who is sick, and the Lord will raise them up. And if they have committed sins, they will be forgiven.
v16: Therefore confess your sins to one another and pray for one another so that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous person is very powerful in its effect.
v17: Elijah was a human being with a nature like ours, and he prayed earnestly that it would not rain. It did not rain on the land for three years and six months.
v18: Then he prayed again, and heaven gave rain, and the earth produced its fruit.
</p>

## Bring Back the One Who Wanders

<p>
v19: My brothers and sisters, if anyone among you wanders from the truth and someone brings that person back,
v20: know that whoever brings a sinner back from the error of their way will save a life from death and cover a multitude of sins.
</p>

## Notes

v01–06: James's prophetic warning is directed at wealth accumulated and used unjustly. The issue is not possession in abstraction but hoarding, withheld wages, luxury, and violence.

v03: Gold and silver do not literally rust in the ordinary sense; the Greek verb describes corrosion. James intentionally depicts even precious metal as unable to escape decay and judgment.

v04: “Lord of hosts” transliterates the traditional divine title `Kyrios Sabaōth`, echoing Israel's Scriptures and divine authority over heavenly armies/hosts.

v07–08: James shifts from denunciation to patient endurance. Agricultural waiting becomes the controlling image.

v08–09: “The coming of the Lord is near” and “the Judge is standing at the door” retain the text's immediacy without imposing a chronology.

v11: “The Lord's purpose in the end” translates `to telos kyriou`, which can refer to the outcome the Lord brought about or the Lord's intended end. The Job reference favors God's compassionate outcome/purpose.

v12: The verse simplifies speech rather than creating elaborate oath rules: yes should mean yes and no should mean no.

v14: Anointing with oil is retained without deciding in the translation whether the action is medicinal, symbolic, sacramental, or some combination.

v15: `sōzō` means save/rescue and can overlap with restoration or healing in context. Fluent keeps “save” because the next clause says the Lord will “raise” the sick person, preserving James's wording rather than collapsing the two verbs.

v15: The verse connects illness, prayer, raising, and forgiveness without asserting that every illness is caused by sin.

v16: The Greek participle modifying prayer is difficult; “very powerful in its effect” gives natural English without making prayer a mechanical force.

v17: The phrase literally intensifies the verb—Elijah “prayed with prayer.” “Prayed earnestly” conveys the idiom.

v19–20: The pronouns in v20 allow some ambiguity about whose “life/soul” is saved. The main text uses “a life” rather than forcing a referent. Restoration, not speculation about mechanism, is the focus.
