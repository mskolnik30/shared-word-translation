## Warning to the Rich

<p>
v01: Now listen, you rich people. Weep and cry out over the suffering that is coming upon you.
v02: Your wealth has rotted away. Moths have eaten your clothes.
v03: Your gold and silver are corroded. Their corrosion will stand as evidence against you and eat your flesh like fire. You have piled up treasure in the last days.
v04: Look! You have held back the wages of those who harvested your fields, and those wages cry out. The harvesters' cries have reached the ears of the Lord of hosts.
v05: You have spent your lives on earth in luxury and indulgence. You have fattened your hearts on a day of slaughter.
v06: You have condemned the righteous person and put that person to death. The righteous person does not resist you.
</p>

## Be Patient Until the Lord’s Coming

<p>
v07: So be patient, brothers and sisters, until the Lord comes. Think of a farmer waiting patiently for the land's precious crop and for the early and late rains.
v08: You too must be patient and make your hearts firm, because the Lord's coming is near.
v09: Brothers and sisters, do not complain against one another, or you will face judgment. Look! The judge is standing at the door.
v10: Brothers and sisters, take the prophets who spoke in the Lord's name as your example of patience in suffering.
v11: We call those who endured blessed. You have heard how Job endured, and you have seen the outcome the Lord brought about. The Lord is full of compassion and mercy.
</p>

## Let Your Yes Be Yes

<p>
v12: Above all, my brothers and sisters, do not take oaths by heaven or earth, or swear any other oath. Let your yes mean yes and your no mean no, so that you do not come under judgment.
</p>

## Prayer, Healing, and Confession

<p>
v13: Is anyone among you suffering? Pray. Is anyone in good spirits? Sing praise.
v14: Is anyone among you sick? Call the church's elders. They should pray over the sick person and anoint that person with oil in the Lord's name.
v15: Prayer offered in faith will save the sick person, and the Lord will raise that person up. Any sins they have committed will be forgiven.
v16: So confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, so that you may be healed. A righteous person's prayer has great power as it works.
v17: Elijah was human, just like us. He prayed earnestly for no rain, and no rain fell on the land for three years and six months.
v18: He prayed again. The sky gave rain, and the land brought forth its crop.
</p>

## Bring Back the One Who Wanders

<p>
v19: My brothers and sisters, if one of you wanders from the truth and someone brings that person back,
v20: remember this: whoever turns a sinner back from a wandering path will save a life from death and cover many sins.
</p>

## Notes

v03: The Greek describes gold and silver as corroded. The words “like fire” can attach to the eating of flesh or to the following sentence about storing treasure. Fluent retains the former reading; the pinned SBLGNT edition punctuates with the latter attachment.

v04–06: “Lord of hosts” renders the divine title Kyrios Sabaōth; hosts are armies or multitudes. Verse 5 has “on a day of slaughter”; Fluent retains “hearts” and the slaughter image. The final clause of v06 may be read as a statement or a question. Fluent retains “does not resist you”; SBLGNT punctuates it as a question, “Does he not resist you?”

v07–11: The early and late rains belong to the agricultural cycle. The Lord's coming and the judge at the door retain the passage's urgency. “The outcome the Lord brought about” in v11 can also be understood as the Lord's purpose or intended end.

v14–15: The text does not specify whether anointing with oil is medicinal, symbolic, sacramental, or a combination. “Save” and “raise” remain distinct verbs. The conditional reference to sins does not state that every illness results from a person's sin.

v16: “As it works” renders a participle that can describe prayer as effective or as being put into effect. The translation does not add an unstated agent or a mechanical explanation of prayer.

v17–18: Elijah “prayed with prayer,” an expression of intensity rendered “prayed earnestly.” The episode recalls 1 Kings 17–18; James specifies three years and six months.

v19–20: The Greek of v20 refers to saving “his life/soul,” but the possessive's referent is disputed. “A life” leaves that question open. “Cover many sins” retains the covering image rather than explaining how it happens.

## Vocabulary

v04: ἀφυστερέω (aphystereō)
Withhold or deprive; the verb read by the pinned SBLGNT text here.

v07: μακροθυμέω (makrothymeō)
Be patient, especially while waiting or suffering.

v11: ὑπομονή (hypomonē)
Endurance; the same noun used in 1:3.

v15: σῴζω (sōzō)
Save or rescue; in this context it can include healing or restoration.

v15: ἐγείρω (egeirō)
Raise or raise up.

v20: ἐπιστρέφω (epistrephō)
Turn back or bring back.
