## Greeting

<p>
v01: The elder, to beloved Gaius, whom I love in truth.
v02: Beloved, I pray that all may go well with you and that you may be in good health, just as all goes well with your soul.
</p>

## Commendation of Gaius

<p>
v03: I rejoiced greatly when brothers and sisters came and bore witness to your truth, just as you are walking in truth.
v04: I have no greater joy than hearing that my children are walking in the truth.
v05: Beloved, you act faithfully in whatever you do for the brothers and sisters, even though they are strangers.
v06: They have borne witness to your love before the church. You will do well to send them on their way in a manner worthy of God,
v07: because they went out for the sake of the Name, accepting nothing from the Gentiles.
v08: So we ought to support such people, so that we may become fellow workers for the truth.
</p>

## Diotrephes and Demetrius

<p>
v09: I wrote something to the church, but Diotrephes, who loves to be first among them, does not accept us.
v10: So if I come, I will call attention to what he is doing: talking malicious nonsense about us. Not content with that, he himself refuses to welcome the brothers and sisters, stops those who want to, and throws them out of the church.
v11: Beloved, do not imitate what is evil, but what is good. Whoever does good is of God; whoever does evil has not seen God.
v12: Everyone has borne witness to Demetrius, and so has the truth itself. We also bear witness, and you know that our testimony is true.
</p>

## Final Greetings

<p>
v13: I had much to write to you, but I do not want to write it with ink and pen.
v14: Instead, I hope to see you soon, and we will speak face to face.
v15: Peace to you. The friends greet you. Greet the friends by name.
</p>

## Notes

v02: The sentence expresses a prayer or wish for Gaius’ welfare.

v07: “The Name” is not expanded with a personal name in the Greek.

v14–15: The pinned source numbers the final greeting as verse 15; some English editions include it in verse 14.

## Vocabulary

v06: **προπέμπω (propempō)**: Send on a journey, including practical assistance for travel.

v09: **φιλοπρωτεύω (philoprōteuō)**: Love to be first or seek preeminence.
