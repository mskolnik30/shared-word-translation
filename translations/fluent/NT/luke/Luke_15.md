## Sinners Draw Near

<p>
v01: All the tax collectors and sinners were coming near to Jesus to hear him.
v02: But the Pharisees and scribes kept grumbling, “This man welcomes sinners and eats with them.”
v03: So he told them this parable:
v04: “Which one of you, having a hundred sheep and losing one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine in the wilderness and go after the lost one until he finds it?
v05: When he finds it, he joyfully puts it on his shoulders,
v06: comes home, and calls together his friends and neighbors, saying, ‘Rejoice with me, because I have found my lost sheep!’
v07: I tell you, in the same way there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous people who have no need of repentance.
v08: Or what woman, having ten silver coins and losing one, does not light a lamp, sweep the house, and search carefully until she finds it?
v09: When she finds it, she calls together her friends and neighbors and says, ‘Rejoice with me, because I have found the coin I lost!’
v10: In the same way, I tell you, there is joy before the angels of God over one sinner who repents.”
</p>

## A Father and Two Lost Sons

<p>
v11: Then Jesus said, “A man had two sons.
v12: The younger one said to his father, ‘Father, give me the share of the estate that belongs to me.’ So the father divided his property between them.
v13: Not many days later, the younger son gathered everything together and traveled to a distant country. There he squandered his estate in reckless living.
v14: After he had spent everything, a severe famine struck that country, and he began to be in need.
v15: So he went and attached himself to one of that country’s citizens, who sent him into his fields to feed pigs.
v16: He longed to fill his stomach with the pods the pigs were eating, but no one gave him anything.
v17: When he came to his senses, he said, ‘How many of my father’s hired workers have more than enough bread, while I am dying of hunger here!
v18: I will get up, go to my father, and say to him, “Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you.
v19: I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Make me like one of your hired workers.”’
v20: So he got up and went to his father. While he was still far away, his father saw him and was moved with compassion. He ran, threw his arms around his neck, and kissed him tenderly.
v21: The son said to him, ‘Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son.’
v22: But the father said to his servants, ‘Quickly, bring out the best robe and put it on him. Put a ring on his hand and sandals on his feet.
v23: Bring the fattened calf and slaughter it. Let us eat and celebrate,
v24: because this son of mine was dead and is alive again; he was lost and has been found.’ And they began to celebrate.
v25: Now his older son was in the field. As he came near the house, he heard music and dancing.
v26: He called one of the servants and asked what these things meant.
v27: The servant told him, ‘Your brother has come, and your father has slaughtered the fattened calf because he received him back safe and sound.’
v28: The older son became angry and refused to go in. So his father came out and pleaded with him.
v29: But he answered his father, ‘Look, I have served you like a slave for all these years and never disobeyed your command. Yet you never gave me even a young goat so I could celebrate with my friends.
v30: But when this son of yours came back after devouring your property with prostitutes, you slaughtered the fattened calf for him!’
v31: The father said to him, ‘Child, you are always with me, and everything I have is yours.
v32: But we had to celebrate and rejoice, because this brother of yours was dead and is alive; he was lost and has been found.’”
</p>

## Notes

v01–02: The three stories answer the complaint that Jesus welcomes sinners and eats with them.

v12: The father divides his livelihood between both sons, not only the share requested by the younger.

v20: The father sees the son at a distance, runs, embraces him, and kisses him repeatedly or tenderly before hearing the completed confession.

v21–24: The son does not complete his rehearsed request to become a hired worker. The father instead restores him publicly with robe, ring, sandals, and a feast.

v32: The story ends with the father’s appeal and does not report the older brother’s response.

## Vocabulary

v07: **μετάνοια (metanoia)**: Repentance, a turning of mind and life.

v13: **ἀσώτως (asōtōs)**: Recklessly, wastefully, or without restraint.

v20: **σπλαγχνίζομαι (splagchnizomai)**: Be moved with deep compassion.

v24: **ἀπόλλυμι (apollymi)**: Lose, destroy, or perish; repeated throughout the chapter.
