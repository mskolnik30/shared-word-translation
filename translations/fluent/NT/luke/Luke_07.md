## The Faith of a Centurion

<p>
v01: After Jesus had finished saying all this in the hearing of the people, he entered Capernaum.
v02: A centurion had a slave whom he valued highly. The slave was sick and near death.
v03: When the centurion heard about Jesus, he sent Jewish elders to ask him to come and save his slave.
v04: When they came to Jesus, they pleaded earnestly with him: “This man deserves to have you do this for him,
v05: because he loves our nation and built our synagogue for us.”
v06: So Jesus went with them. When he was not far from the house, the centurion sent friends to tell him, “Lord, do not trouble yourself, because I am not worthy to have you come under my roof.
v07: That is why I did not consider myself worthy to come to you. But say the word, and let my servant be healed.
v08: For I too am a man placed under authority, with soldiers under me. I tell one, ‘Go,’ and he goes; another, ‘Come,’ and he comes; and my slave, ‘Do this,’ and he does it.”
v09: When Jesus heard this, he marveled at him. Turning to the crowd following him, he said, “I tell you, not even in Israel have I found such great faith.”
v10: When the messengers returned to the house, they found the slave in good health.
</p>

## Jesus Raises a Widow’s Son

<p>
v11: Soon afterward, Jesus went to a town called Nain. His disciples and a large crowd went with him.
v12: As he approached the town gate, a dead man was being carried out—the only son of his mother, and she was a widow. A large crowd from the town was with her.
v13: When the Lord saw her, he had compassion for her and said, “Do not weep.”
v14: He came forward and touched the bier, and the bearers stopped. He said, “Young man, I tell you, get up!”
v15: The dead man sat up and began to speak, and Jesus gave him to his mother.
v16: Fear seized them all, and they glorified God, saying, “A great prophet has arisen among us,” and, “God has visited his people.”
v17: This report about Jesus spread throughout Judea and all the surrounding region.
</p>

## John’s Messengers

<p>
v18: John’s disciples told him about all these things. So John called two of his disciples
v19: and sent them to the Lord to ask, “Are you the one who is to come, or should we expect someone else?”
v20: When the men came to Jesus, they said, “John the Baptist sent us to ask you, ‘Are you the one who is to come, or should we expect someone else?’”
v21: At that very hour Jesus healed many people of diseases, afflictions, and evil spirits, and he graciously gave sight to many who were blind.
v22: Then he answered them, “Go and report to John what you have seen and heard: blind people see, those who are lame walk, people with leprosy are made clean, those who are deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor receive good news.
v23: Blessed is anyone who does not stumble because of me.”
</p>

## Jesus Speaks About John

<p>
v24: After John’s messengers left, Jesus began speaking to the crowds about John: “What did you go out into the wilderness to see? A reed shaken by the wind?
v25: What then did you go out to see? A man dressed in soft clothing? Look, those who wear splendid clothes and live in luxury are in royal palaces.
v26: What then did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and far more than a prophet.
v27: This is the one about whom it is written: ‘Look, I am sending my messenger ahead of you, who will prepare your way before you.’
v28: I tell you, among those born of women no one is greater than John. Yet the least in the kingdom of God is greater than he.”
v29: When all the people, including the tax collectors, heard this, they acknowledged that God was just, because they had received John’s baptism.
v30: But the Pharisees and experts in the law rejected God’s purpose for themselves, because they had not been baptized by John.
</p>

## This Generation

<p>
v31: “To what, then, shall I compare the people of this generation? What are they like?
v32: They are like children sitting in the marketplace and calling to one another: ‘We played the flute for you, but you did not dance; we sang a funeral song, but you did not weep.’
v33: For John the Baptist came neither eating bread nor drinking wine, and you say, ‘He has a demon.’
v34: The Son of Man has come eating and drinking, and you say, ‘Look, a glutton and a drunkard, a friend of tax collectors and sinners!’
v35: Yet wisdom is vindicated by all her children.”
</p>

## A Woman Anoints Jesus

<p>
v36: One of the Pharisees invited Jesus to eat with him. Jesus entered the Pharisee’s house and reclined at the table.
v37: A woman in that town who was known as a sinner learned that Jesus was reclining in the Pharisee’s house. She brought an alabaster jar of perfume
v38: and stood behind Jesus at his feet, weeping. She began to wet his feet with her tears and wiped them with her hair. She kept kissing his feet and anointing them with the perfume.
v39: When the Pharisee who had invited Jesus saw this, he said to himself, “If this man were a prophet, he would know who and what kind of woman is touching him—that she is a sinner.”
v40: Jesus answered him, “Simon, I have something to tell you.” He replied, “Teacher, say it.”
v41: “Two people owed money to a certain lender. One owed five hundred denarii, and the other fifty.
v42: When they could not repay, he freely forgave the debts of both. Which of them, then, will love him more?”
v43: Simon answered, “I suppose the one whose larger debt he forgave.” Jesus told him, “You have judged correctly.”
v44: Then, turning toward the woman, Jesus said to Simon, “Do you see this woman? I entered your house, and you gave me no water for my feet. But she wet my feet with her tears and wiped them with her hair.
v45: You gave me no kiss, but from the time I entered she has not stopped kissing my feet.
v46: You did not anoint my head with oil, but she anointed my feet with perfume.
v47: Therefore I tell you, her many sins have been forgiven, for she loved much. But the one who is forgiven little loves little.”
v48: Then he said to her, “Your sins are forgiven.”
v49: Those reclining at the table with him began saying among themselves, “Who is this who even forgives sins?”
v50: But Jesus said to the woman, “Your faith has saved you. Go in peace.”
</p>

## Notes

v02, 07–10: The centurion's household worker is called a ‘slave’ in verses 2, 3, 8, and 10 and a term that can mean child or servant in verse 7. The translation preserves ‘slave’ where Luke uses it explicitly and ‘servant’ in the reported request.

v11: The opening can mean ‘on the next day’ or more generally ‘soon afterward.’ The pinned text's variant marking does not remove the temporal connection.

v13: This is the first place in Luke where the narrator directly calls Jesus ‘the Lord.’

v16: ‘God has visited his people’ uses visitation language for God's active intervention, not merely observation.

v22: The sequence echoes prophetic promises through concrete acts: sight, walking, cleansing, hearing, resurrection, and good news for the poor.

v23: ‘Stumble because of me’ can also be rendered ‘take offense at me.’ The saying leaves the particular cause of stumbling unstated.

v29–30: The paired response says the people acknowledged God as just, while the Pharisees and legal experts rejected God's purpose for themselves. Both responses are linked with John's baptism.

v35: Wisdom is personified as a mother whose children vindicate her. The image is retained rather than replaced with an abstract explanation.

v47: The relation between forgiveness and love is expressed compactly. The preceding parable presents love as the response to forgiven debt, while the clause ‘for she loved much’ can sound causal in English; both features should inform interpretation.

## Vocabulary

v02: **δοῦλος (doulos)**: Slave; a person held under another's authority.

v13: **σπλαγχνίζομαι (splagchnizomai)**: Be moved with deep compassion.

v16: **ἐπισκέπτομαι (episkeptomai)**: Visit, attend to, or intervene for someone.

v23: **σκανδαλίζω (skandalizō)**: Cause to stumble or take offense.

v41: **δηνάριον (dēnarion)**: Denarius, a common Roman silver coin.

v50: **σῴζω (sōzō)**: Save, rescue, or make well.
