## Lord of the Sabbath

<p>
v01: On a Sabbath, Jesus was passing through grainfields. His disciples were picking heads of grain, rubbing them in their hands, and eating them.
v02: Some of the Pharisees asked, “Why are you doing what is not lawful on the Sabbath?”
v03: Jesus answered them, “Have you not read what David did when he and those with him were hungry?
v04: He entered the house of God, took and ate the consecrated bread, and gave some to those with him—bread that only the priests are permitted to eat.”
v05: Then he told them, “The Son of Man is Lord of the Sabbath.”
v06: On another Sabbath, Jesus entered the synagogue and taught. A man was there whose right hand was withered.
v07: The scribes and Pharisees were watching Jesus closely to see whether he would heal on the Sabbath, so they could find a reason to accuse him.
v08: But Jesus knew their thoughts. He said to the man with the withered hand, “Get up and stand in the middle.” The man got up and stood there.
v09: Then Jesus said to them, “I ask you: Is it lawful on the Sabbath to do good or to do harm, to save a life or to destroy it?”
v10: After looking around at all of them, he said to the man, “Stretch out your hand.” He did, and his hand was restored.
v11: But they were filled with senseless rage and discussed with one another what they might do to Jesus.
v12: In those days Jesus went out to the mountain to pray, and he spent the whole night in prayer to God.
v13: When day came, he called his disciples and chose twelve of them, whom he also named apostles:
v14: Simon, whom he also named Peter; Andrew, his brother; James; John; Philip; Bartholomew;
v15: Matthew; Thomas; James son of Alphaeus; Simon called the Zealot;
v16: Judas son of James; and Judas Iscariot, who became a traitor.
v17: Jesus came down with them and stood on a level place. A large crowd of his disciples was there, along with a great multitude of people from all Judea, Jerusalem, and the coastal region of Tyre and Sidon.
v18: They had come to hear him and to be healed of their diseases. Those troubled by unclean spirits were also healed.
v19: The whole crowd was trying to touch him, because power was coming out from him and healing everyone.
v20: Jesus looked up at his disciples and said: “Blessed are you who are poor, because the kingdom of God is yours.
v21: Blessed are you who hunger now, because you will be filled. Blessed are you who weep now, because you will laugh.
v22: Blessed are you when people hate you, exclude you, insult you, and reject your name as evil because of the Son of Man.
v23: Rejoice on that day and leap for joy, because your reward is great in heaven. Their ancestors treated the prophets in the same way.
v24: But woe to you who are rich, because you have received your comfort.
v25: Woe to you who are well fed now, because you will hunger. Woe to you who laugh now, because you will mourn and weep.
v26: Woe to you when everyone speaks well of you, for their ancestors treated the false prophets in the same way.”
v27: “But I say to you who are listening: Love your enemies; do good to those who hate you;
v28: bless those who curse you; pray for those who mistreat you.
v29: If someone strikes you on the cheek, offer the other also. If someone takes your outer garment, do not withhold your tunic either.
v30: Give to everyone who asks you, and if someone takes what belongs to you, do not demand it back.
v31: Treat others the same way you want them to treat you.
v32: If you love those who love you, what credit is that to you? Even sinners love those who love them.
v33: If you do good to those who do good to you, what credit is that to you? Even sinners do the same.
v34: If you lend to those from whom you expect repayment, what credit is that to you? Even sinners lend to sinners to receive the same amount back.
v35: Instead, love your enemies, do good, and lend, expecting nothing in return. Then your reward will be great, and you will be children of the Most High, because he is kind to the ungrateful and evil.
v36: Be merciful, just as your Father is merciful.
v37: Do not judge, and you will certainly not be judged. Do not condemn, and you will certainly not be condemned. Forgive, and you will be forgiven.
v38: Give, and it will be given to you. A good measure—pressed down, shaken together, and overflowing—will be poured into your lap. For the measure you use will be measured back to you.”
v39: He also told them a parable: “Can one blind person guide another? Will they not both fall into a pit?
v40: A disciple is not above the teacher, but everyone who is fully trained will be like their teacher.
v41: Why do you see the speck in your brother’s eye but fail to notice the beam in your own eye?
v42: How can you say to your brother, ‘Brother, let me remove the speck from your eye,’ when you do not see the beam in your own eye? Hypocrite! First remove the beam from your own eye, and then you will see clearly to remove the speck from your brother’s eye.”
v43: “No good tree produces rotten fruit, and no rotten tree produces good fruit.
v44: Each tree is known by its own fruit. People do not gather figs from thornbushes or harvest grapes from brambles.
v45: A good person brings good out of the heart’s good treasure, and an evil person brings evil out of evil treasure. For the mouth speaks from the overflow of the heart.”
v46: “Why do you call me ‘Lord, Lord,’ but do not do what I say?
v47: I will show you what everyone is like who comes to me, hears my words, and acts on them.
v48: That person is like someone building a house who dug down deep and laid its foundation on rock. When a flood came, the river crashed against that house but could not shake it, because it had been built well.
v49: But the one who hears and does not act is like someone who built a house on the ground without a foundation. The river crashed against it, and it immediately collapsed. The destruction of that house was great.”
</p>

## Notes

v20–26: Luke's blessings and woes directly name poverty, hunger, grief, wealth, fullness, laughter, exclusion, and reputation. The translation does not add qualifications from a parallel Gospel.

v29–30: The concrete commands resist retaliation and possessiveness. They should not be used to require a person to remain in abuse or to prevent protection of someone in danger; those applications are beyond the wording of these verses.

v32–34: The repeated question uses the word often translated ‘grace’ or ‘thanks.’ ‘What credit is that to you?’ conveys the contrast with merely reciprocal conduct.

v35: The phrase rendered ‘expecting nothing in return’ is difficult and has also been understood as ‘despairing of no one.’ The surrounding discussion of lending supports the main rendering.

v37: The final pair can mean ‘release, and you will be released’ or ‘forgive, and you will be forgiven.’ The context of judgment and condemnation supports ‘forgive’ here.

v38: The overflowing measure is pictured being placed into the fold of a garment used like a carrying pouch.

v48: The pinned text says the house stood because it ‘had been built well.’ Other readings more explicitly repeat its foundation on rock.

## Vocabulary

v13: **ἀπόστολος (apostolos)**: One who is sent; here Jesus names the selected Twelve apostles.

v20: **μακάριος (makarios)**: Blessed, fortunate, or flourishing under God's favor.

v24: **παράκλησις (paraklēsis)**: Comfort, consolation, or encouragement.

v36: **οἰκτίρμων (oiktirmōn)**: Merciful or compassionate.

v40: **καταρτίζω (katartizō)**: Prepare, restore, or train fully.

v42: **ὑποκριτής (hypokritēs)**: Hypocrite, originally a term associated with playing a role.
