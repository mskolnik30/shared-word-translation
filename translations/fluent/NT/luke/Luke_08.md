## Women Travel with Jesus

<p>
v01: Soon afterward, Jesus traveled from one town and village to another, proclaiming and bringing the good news of God’s kingdom. The Twelve were with him,
v02: as were some women who had been healed of evil spirits and illnesses: Mary called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out;
v03: Joanna the wife of Chuza, Herod’s household manager; Susanna; and many other women. They supported them from their own resources.
</p>

## The Sower

<p>
v04: When a large crowd gathered and people from town after town came to Jesus, he spoke in a parable:
v05: “A sower went out to sow his seed. As he sowed, some seed fell along the path, where it was trampled and eaten by the birds of the sky.
v06: Other seed fell on rock. When it grew, it withered because it had no moisture.
v07: Other seed fell among thorns. The thorns grew up with it and choked it.
v08: Other seed fell into good soil. It grew and produced a hundred times as much fruit.” As Jesus said this, he called out, “Let anyone who has ears to hear, listen!”
v09: His disciples asked him what this parable meant.
v10: He said, “You have been given knowledge of the mysteries of God’s kingdom, but to the rest it comes in parables, so that ‘seeing they may not see, and hearing they may not understand.’”
v11: “This is the meaning of the parable: The seed is the word of God.
v12: Those along the path are the ones who hear. Then the devil comes and takes the word from their hearts so that they may not believe and be saved.
v13: Those on the rock are the ones who receive the word with joy when they hear it, but they have no root. They believe for a time, but in a time of testing they fall away.
v14: The seed that fell among thorns represents those who hear, but as they go on their way they are choked by life’s worries, riches, and pleasures, and their fruit never matures.
v15: But the seed in the good soil represents those who hear the word with a noble and good heart, hold firmly to it, and bear fruit with endurance.”
</p>

## Hearing and Doing

<p>
v16: “No one lights a lamp and covers it with a container or puts it under a bed. Instead, it is put on a lampstand so that those who enter may see the light.
v17: For nothing is hidden that will not be made visible, and nothing is concealed that will not become known and come into the open.
v18: Therefore, pay attention to how you listen. Whoever has will be given more; whoever does not have, even what they think they have will be taken away.”
v19: Jesus’ mother and brothers came to him, but they could not reach him because of the crowd.
v20: He was told, “Your mother and your brothers are standing outside, wanting to see you.”
v21: But he answered them, “My mother and my brothers are those who hear the word of God and do it.”
</p>

## Jesus Calms the Storm

<p>
v22: One day, Jesus got into a boat with his disciples and said to them, “Let us cross to the other side of the lake.” So they set out.
v23: As they sailed, he fell asleep. A windstorm swept down on the lake, and the boat was filling with water, putting them in danger.
v24: They went to him and woke him, saying, “Master, Master, we are perishing!” He got up and rebuked the wind and the surging water. They ceased, and there was a calm.
v25: He said to them, “Where is your faith?” They were afraid and amazed, saying to one another, “Who then is this? He commands even the winds and the water, and they obey him!”
</p>

## A Man Freed from Demons

<p>
v26: They sailed to the region of the Gerasenes, across the lake from Galilee.
v27: When Jesus stepped onto land, a man from the town who had demons met him. For a long time he had worn no clothes and had not lived in a house but among the tombs.
v28: When he saw Jesus, he cried out, fell before him, and said loudly, “What do you want with me, Jesus, Son of the Most High God? I beg you, do not torment me!”
v29: For Jesus had commanded the unclean spirit to come out of the man. Many times it had seized him. Though he was kept under guard and bound with chains and shackles, he would break the bonds and be driven by the demon into deserted places.
v30: Jesus asked him, “What is your name?” “Legion,” he answered, because many demons had entered him.
v31: The demons kept begging Jesus not to command them to go into the abyss.
v32: A large herd of pigs was feeding there on the hillside. The demons begged Jesus to permit them to enter the pigs, and he permitted them.
v33: The demons came out of the man and entered the pigs. The herd rushed down the steep bank into the lake and drowned.
v34: When the herders saw what had happened, they fled and reported it in the town and countryside.
v35: The people went out to see what had happened. When they came to Jesus, they found the man from whom the demons had gone out sitting at Jesus’ feet, clothed and in his right mind, and they were afraid.
v36: Those who had seen it told them how the demon-possessed man had been delivered.
v37: Then all the people from the surrounding region of the Gerasenes asked Jesus to leave them, because they were gripped by great fear. So he got into the boat and returned.
v38: The man from whom the demons had gone out begged to be with Jesus, but Jesus sent him away, saying,
v39: “Return home and tell how much God has done for you.” So he went through the whole town proclaiming how much Jesus had done for him.
</p>

## A Woman Healed and a Girl Raised

<p>
v40: When Jesus returned, the crowd welcomed him, because they were all waiting for him.
v41: A man named Jairus came. He was a synagogue leader. Falling at Jesus’ feet, he pleaded with him to come to his house,
v42: because his only daughter, about twelve years old, was dying. As Jesus went, the crowds pressed in around him.
v43: A woman who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years had spent her whole livelihood on physicians, but no one had been able to heal her.
v44: She came up behind Jesus and touched the fringe of his garment. Immediately her bleeding stopped.
v45: Jesus asked, “Who touched me?” When everyone denied it, Peter said, “Master, the crowds are surrounding you and pressing against you.”
v46: But Jesus said, “Someone touched me, because I know that power has gone out from me.”
v47: When the woman saw that she had not escaped notice, she came trembling and fell before him. In front of all the people, she explained why she had touched him and how she had been healed immediately.
v48: He said to her, “Daughter, your faith has saved you. Go in peace.”
v49: While Jesus was still speaking, someone came from the synagogue leader’s house and said, “Your daughter has died. Do not trouble the teacher any longer.”
v50: When Jesus heard this, he answered Jairus, “Do not be afraid; only believe, and she will be made well.”
v51: When he came to the house, he allowed no one to enter with him except Peter, John, James, and the child’s father and mother.
v52: Everyone was weeping and mourning for her, but Jesus said, “Do not weep. She is not dead but asleep.”
v53: They laughed at him because they knew she was dead.
v54: But Jesus took her by the hand and called out, “Child, get up!”
v55: Her spirit returned, and she immediately got up. Jesus ordered that she be given something to eat.
v56: Her parents were astonished, but Jesus ordered them not to tell anyone what had happened.
</p>

## Notes

v01–03: The women travel with Jesus and the Twelve and support the group from their own resources. Their agency is part of Luke's description, not background detail.

v10: The quotation's ‘so that’ wording preserves a difficult relationship between parables, seeing, hearing, and understanding. It is not softened into a simple claim that parables always make teaching easier.

v14–15: The contrast is between fruit that never reaches maturity and fruit borne with endurance. Hearing alone appears in both descriptions.

v18: The warning concerns how people listen. The final clause distinguishes what someone has from what the person only thinks they have.

v26: The pinned Greek text reads ‘Gerasenes.’ Ancient manuscripts preserve several place-name forms in this account.

v27–29: The narrative describes prolonged nakedness, isolation among tombs, restraint, broken chains, and being driven into deserted places. The translation does not romanticize the man's suffering or equate it with a modern diagnosis.

v30: ‘Legion’ is a Roman military term and is explained in the verse by the presence of many demons.

v36: The verb can mean saved, delivered, or made well. ‘Delivered’ fits the immediate account of release from demons while retaining rescue language.

v39: Jesus tells the man to report what God has done; the narrator says he reports what Jesus has done. Luke places the statements in deliberate correspondence.

v43: The pinned text includes the woman's spending of her whole livelihood on physicians. The verse names both economic loss and the failure of available treatment without blaming her.

v48, 50: The same verb can mean save or make well. Verse 48 retains ‘saved’ in Jesus' address to the woman; verse 50 uses ‘made well’ for the dying girl while preserving the shared language of rescue.

## Vocabulary

v03: **διακονέω (diakoneō)**: Serve, provide for, or minister.

v10: **μυστήριον (mystērion)**: Mystery, a divine purpose made known rather than a puzzle solved by ingenuity.

v15: **ὑπομονή (hypomonē)**: Endurance, steadfastness, or patient persistence.

v31: **ἄβυσσος (abyssos)**: Abyss or bottomless depth, here a place feared by the demons.

v44: **κράσπεδον (kraspedon)**: Edge, fringe, or tassel of a garment.

v48: **πίστις (pistis)**: Faith or trust; Jesus names the woman's faith in connection with salvation.
