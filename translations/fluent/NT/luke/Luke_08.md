## Women Travel with Jesus

<p>
v01: Soon afterward Jesus went through towns and villages, proclaiming and announcing the good news of God’s kingdom. The twelve were with him,
v02: along with some women who had been healed of evil spirits and illnesses: Mary called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out;
v03: Joanna, the wife of Chuza, Herod’s steward; Susanna; and many others. These women were supporting them from their own resources.
</p>

## The Sower

<p>
v04: When a large crowd was gathering and people from town after town were coming to Jesus, he spoke in a parable:
v05: “A sower went out to sow his seed. As he sowed, some fell along the path, was trampled, and the birds of the sky ate it.
v06: Some fell on rock, and when it grew up it withered because it had no moisture.
v07: Some fell among thorns, and the thorns grew up with it and choked it.
v08: Some fell into good soil and, when it grew, produced a hundredfold.” As he said these things he called out, “Let anyone with ears to hear listen.”
v09: His disciples asked him what this parable meant.
v10: He said, “To you it has been given to know the mysteries of God’s kingdom, but to the rest it comes in parables, so that
‘seeing they may not see,
and hearing they may not understand.’
v11: This is what the parable means: The seed is God’s word.
v12: Those along the path are the ones who hear; then the devil comes and takes the word away from their hearts so that they may not trust and be saved.
v13: Those on the rock are the ones who receive the word with joy when they hear it, but they have no root. They trust for a time and fall away in a time of testing.
v14: What fell among thorns are those who hear, but as they go on their way they are choked by the cares, wealth, and pleasures of life, and their fruit does not mature.
v15: But what fell in good soil are those who hear the word with a good and upright heart, hold it firmly, and bear fruit with endurance.
</p>

## Hearing and Doing

<p>
v16: “No one lights a lamp and covers it with a jar or puts it under a bed. Instead, they put it on a stand so that those entering may see the light.
v17: Nothing is hidden that will not be revealed, and nothing secret that will not become known and come to light.
v18: Therefore pay attention to how you hear. Whoever has will be given more, and whoever does not have, even what they think they have will be taken away.”
v19: Jesus’ mother and brothers came to him but could not reach him because of the crowd.
v20: Someone told him, “Your mother and your brothers are standing outside wanting to see you.”
v21: He answered, “My mother and my brothers are those who hear God’s word and do it.”
</p>

## Jesus Calms the Storm

<p>
v22: One day Jesus got into a boat with his disciples and said, “Let us cross to the other side of the lake.” So they set out.
v23: As they sailed, he fell asleep. A windstorm came down on the lake, the boat began filling with water, and they were in danger.
v24: They came and woke him: “Master! Master! We are perishing!” He got up, rebuked the wind and the raging water, and they stopped, and there was a calm.
v25: He said to them, “Where is your faith?” They were afraid and amazed and said to one another, “Who then is this? He commands even the winds and the water, and they obey him!”
</p>

## A Man Freed from Demons

<p>
v26: They sailed to the region of the Gerasenes, across from Galilee.
v27: When Jesus stepped onto land, a man from the town met him who had demons. For a long time he had worn no clothes and lived not in a house but among the tombs.
v28: When he saw Jesus, he cried out, fell before him, and shouted, “What do you want with me, Jesus, Son of the Most High God? I beg you, do not torment me!”
v29: For Jesus had been commanding the unclean spirit to come out of the man. Many times it had seized him. He had been kept under guard and bound with chains and shackles, but he broke the bonds and was driven by the demon into deserted places.
v30: Jesus asked him, “What is your name?” He said, “Legion,” because many demons had entered him.
v31: They begged Jesus not to command them to go into the abyss.
v32: A large herd of pigs was feeding there on the hillside. The demons begged Jesus to allow them to enter the pigs, and he allowed them.
v33: The demons came out of the man and entered the pigs, and the herd rushed down the steep bank into the lake and drowned.
v34: When the herders saw what had happened, they fled and reported it in the town and countryside.
v35: People came out to see what had happened. They came to Jesus and found the man from whom the demons had gone out sitting at Jesus’ feet, clothed and in his right mind, and they were afraid.
v36: Those who had seen it told them how the demonized man had been saved.
v37: Then all the people of the surrounding region of the Gerasenes asked Jesus to leave them because they were seized with great fear. He got into the boat and returned.
v38: The man from whom the demons had gone out begged to be with Jesus, but Jesus sent him away:
v39: “Return home and tell what God has done for you.” The man went away, proclaiming throughout the town what Jesus had done for him.
</p>

## A Woman Healed and a Girl Raised

<p>
v40: When Jesus returned, the crowd welcomed him because they were all waiting for him.
v41: Look—a man named Jairus came. He was a synagogue ruler. Falling at Jesus’ feet, he begged him to come to his house
v42: because his only daughter, about twelve years old, was dying. As Jesus went, the crowds pressed around him.
v43: A woman had suffered from bleeding for twelve years and could not be healed by anyone.
v44: She came up behind Jesus and touched the fringe of his garment. Immediately her bleeding stopped.
v45: Jesus asked, “Who touched me?” When everyone denied it, Peter said, “Master, the crowds surround you and press against you.”
v46: But Jesus said, “Someone touched me, because I knew that power had gone out from me.”
v47: When the woman saw that she had not remained hidden, she came trembling, fell before him, and declared before all the people why she had touched him and how she had been healed immediately.
v48: He said to her, “Daughter, your faith has saved you. Go in peace.”
v49: While he was still speaking, someone came from the synagogue ruler’s house and said, “Your daughter has died. Do not trouble the teacher any longer.”
v50: But Jesus heard and answered Jairus, “Do not be afraid. Only trust, and she will be saved.”
v51: When he came to the house, he allowed no one to enter with him except Peter, John, James, and the child’s father and mother.
v52: Everyone was weeping and mourning for her, but he said, “Do not weep. She has not died but is sleeping.”
v53: They laughed at him because they knew she had died.
v54: But Jesus took her by the hand and called out, “Child, get up!”
v55: Her spirit returned, and she got up immediately. He ordered that she be given something to eat.
v56: Her parents were amazed, but he ordered them not to tell anyone what had happened.
</p>

## Notes

v01–03: Luke names women as traveling participants and financial supporters of the mission.

v10: Luke’s shortened Isaiah citation highlights the dual action of parables: revelation to some and nonperception among others.

v15: Luke adds “endurance” to the fruit-bearing description.

v21: Luke defines Jesus’ family specifically as those who “hear God’s word and do it.”

v36: Luke uses `sōzō` for the restored man, connecting deliverance from demonic oppression with the Gospel’s broader salvation vocabulary.

v39: The command is to tell what “God” has done; the man then proclaims what “Jesus” has done. Luke places the two statements side by side without commentary.

v43: Some manuscript traditions add that the woman spent all her resources on physicians. SBLGNT’s main text is shorter; Fluent follows the pinned source.

v48,50: The same `sōzō` word is used in the intertwined stories, joining the woman’s healing and the girl’s rescue from death.
