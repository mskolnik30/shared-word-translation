## Jesus Teaches Prayer

<p>
v01: One day Jesus was praying in a certain place. When he finished, one of his disciples said, “Lord, teach us to pray, just as John taught his disciples.”
v02: He said to them, “When you pray, say: ‘Father, may your name be kept holy. May your kingdom come.
v03: Give us each day our daily bread.
v04: Forgive us our sins, for we ourselves forgive everyone indebted to us. And do not bring us into testing.’”
</p>

## Ask, Seek, Knock

<p>
v05: Then Jesus said to them, “Suppose one of you has a friend and goes to him at midnight, saying, ‘Friend, lend me three loaves of bread,
v06: because a friend of mine has arrived from a journey, and I have nothing to set before him.’
v07: Then the friend inside answers, ‘Do not bother me. The door is already locked, and my children are in bed with me. I cannot get up and give you anything.’
v08: I tell you, even if he will not get up and give him bread because he is his friend, he will get up because of the man’s shameless persistence and give him whatever he needs.
v09: So I tell you: Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and the door will be opened to you.
v10: For everyone who asks receives; the one who seeks finds; and to the one who knocks, the door will be opened.
v11: Which father among you, if his son asks for a fish, will give him a snake instead?
v12: Or if he asks for an egg, will give him a scorpion?
v13: If you, though you are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will the Father from heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him!”
</p>

## The Finger of God

<p>
v14: Jesus was driving out a demon that kept a man from speaking. When the demon came out, the man who had been mute spoke, and the crowds were amazed.
v15: But some of them said, “He drives out demons by Beelzebul, the ruler of demons.”
v16: Others tested him by demanding a sign from heaven.
v17: Knowing their thoughts, Jesus said to them, “Every kingdom divided against itself is laid waste, and house falls upon house.
v18: If Satan is divided against himself, how will his kingdom stand? I ask because you say that I drive out demons by Beelzebul.
v19: If I drive out demons by Beelzebul, by whom do your followers drive them out? Therefore, they will be your judges.
v20: But if I drive out demons by the finger of God, then the kingdom of God has come upon you.
v21: When a strong man, fully armed, guards his own courtyard, his possessions are secure.
v22: But when someone stronger attacks and overcomes him, he takes away the armor the man trusted and distributes his plunder.
v23: Whoever is not with me is against me, and whoever does not gather with me scatters.
</p>

## Hearing and Keeping God’s Word

<p>
v24: When an unclean spirit comes out of a person, it travels through waterless places seeking rest. Finding none, it says, ‘I will return to my house that I left.’
v25: When it arrives, it finds the house swept and put in order.
v26: Then it goes and brings seven other spirits more evil than itself. They enter and live there, and that person’s final condition becomes worse than the first.”
v27: As Jesus was saying these things, a woman in the crowd raised her voice and said, “Blessed is the womb that carried you and the breasts that nursed you!”
v28: But he said, “Rather, blessed are those who hear the word of God and keep it.”
</p>

## The Sign of Jonah and Light

<p>
v29: As the crowds grew larger, Jesus began to say, “This generation is an evil generation. It seeks a sign, but no sign will be given to it except the sign of Jonah.
v30: For just as Jonah became a sign to the people of Nineveh, so the Son of Man will be to this generation.
v31: The queen of the South will rise at the judgment with the people of this generation and condemn them, because she came from the ends of the earth to hear Solomon’s wisdom—and look, something greater than Solomon is here.
v32: The people of Nineveh will rise at the judgment with this generation and condemn it, because they repented at Jonah’s proclamation—and look, something greater than Jonah is here.
v33: No one lights a lamp and puts it in a hidden place or under a basket. Instead, it is put on a lampstand so those who enter may see the light.
v34: Your eye is the lamp of your body. When your eye is healthy, your whole body is full of light, but when it is bad, your body is full of darkness.
v35: Therefore, watch carefully that the light in you is not darkness.
v36: If your whole body is full of light, with no part dark, it will be entirely illuminated, as when a lamp shines its light on you.”
</p>

## Woes at the Table

<p>
v37: When Jesus finished speaking, a Pharisee invited him to dine with him. Jesus went in and reclined at the table.
v38: The Pharisee was surprised when he saw that Jesus did not first wash ceremonially before the meal.
v39: The Lord said to him, “You Pharisees clean the outside of the cup and dish, but inside you are full of greed and wickedness.
v40: Fools! Did not the one who made the outside make the inside as well?
v41: Instead, give what is inside as alms, and then everything will be clean for you.
v42: But woe to you Pharisees! You give a tenth of mint, rue, and every herb, yet you neglect justice and the love of God. You should have practiced these without neglecting the others.
v43: Woe to you Pharisees! You love the front seats in the synagogues and greetings in the marketplaces.
v44: Woe to you! You are like unmarked graves that people walk over without knowing it.”
v45: One of the experts in the law responded, “Teacher, when you say these things, you insult us too.”
v46: Jesus replied, “Woe to you experts in the law as well! You load people with burdens hard to carry, while you yourselves do not touch the burdens with one of your fingers.
v47: Woe to you! You build the tombs of the prophets whom your ancestors killed.
v48: So you are witnesses who approve of your ancestors’ deeds: they killed the prophets, and you build their tombs.
v49: For this reason the Wisdom of God said, ‘I will send them prophets and apostles. They will kill some of them and persecute others,’
v50: so that this generation may be held accountable for the blood of all the prophets shed since the foundation of the world,
v51: from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zechariah, who was killed between the altar and the sanctuary. Yes, I tell you, this generation will be held accountable for it all.
v52: Woe to you experts in the law! You have taken away the key of knowledge. You did not enter yourselves, and you hindered those who were entering.”
v53: When Jesus left there, the scribes and Pharisees began to oppose him fiercely and question him closely about many things,
v54: lying in wait to catch him in something he might say.
</p>

## Notes

v02–04: Luke preserves a shorter form of the prayer than the familiar form in Matthew and later worship traditions. Added petitions are not imported into the main text.

v03: The adjective describing bread is rare and can mean bread needed for the day or bread for the coming day. Luke also explicitly says ‘each day.’

v04: ‘Testing’ can also mean trial or temptation. The prayer asks not to be brought into it without explaining the agent or circumstances.

v08: The noun can describe shamelessness, bold persistence, or refusal to be deterred. It is not fully clear whether the emphasis falls on the requester's persistence or the sleeper's avoidance of shame.

v13: Luke names the Father's gift as the Holy Spirit, rather than the more general ‘good things’ found in a parallel saying.

v20: ‘Finger of God’ retains a concrete scriptural image for divine action and power.

v27–28: A woman blesses the womb and breasts that sustained Jesus. Jesus redirects the blessing toward hearing and keeping God's word without denying his mother.

v29–30: Luke identifies Jonah himself as the sign to Nineveh. The text here does not add a reference to three days and nights.

v34: The adjective describing the eye can mean healthy, clear, single, or generous; its opposite can mean bad, evil, or unhealthy. The light-darkness image is retained without forcing one nuance.

v38: The verb is related to baptism and here refers to ceremonial washing before a meal, not Christian baptism.

v41: ‘Give what is inside as alms’ is compressed and difficult. The wording has also been understood as giving alms from what one has or giving the contents of the dishes.

v42: Jesus names justice and the love of God as neglected obligations while also saying the detailed acts should not simply be abandoned.

v44: Unmarked graves made people unknowingly encounter ritual impurity. The image criticizes hidden rather than visible corruption.

v51: The Zechariah named here is not further identified in the verse. The translation does not silently choose among historical identifications.

## Vocabulary

v04: **πειρασμός (peirasmos)**: Testing, trial, or temptation.

v08: **ἀναίδεια (anaideia)**: Shamelessness, boldness, or unembarrassed persistence.

v15: **Βεελζεβούλ (Beelzeboul)**: Beelzebul, named in the accusation as ruler of demons.

v20: **δάκτυλος θεοῦ (daktylos theou)**: Finger of God.

v28: **φυλάσσω (phylassō)**: Keep, guard, or observe.

v41: **ἐλεημοσύνη (eleēmosynē)**: Alms or merciful giving.

v52: **γνῶσις (gnōsis)**: Knowledge or understanding.
