## Jesus’ Authority Is Challenged

<p>
v01: One day while Jesus was teaching the people in the temple and announcing the good news, the chief priests and scribes came with the elders
v02: and said, “Tell us: By what authority are you doing these things, or who gave you this authority?”
v03: He answered, “I also will ask you a question. Tell me:
v04: John’s baptism—was it from heaven or from people?”
v05: They reasoned among themselves, “If we say, ‘From heaven,’ he will ask, ‘Why did you not trust him?’
v06: But if we say, ‘From people,’ all the people will stone us, because they are convinced John was a prophet.”
v07: So they answered that they did not know where it came from.
v08: Jesus said to them, “Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things.”
</p>

## The Wicked Tenants

<p>
v09: He began telling the people this parable: “A man planted a vineyard, leased it to tenants, and went away for a long time.
v10: At harvest time he sent a servant to the tenants so they would give him some of the vineyard’s fruit. But the tenants beat him and sent him away empty-handed.
v11: He sent another servant, but they beat and dishonored that one too and sent him away empty-handed.
v12: He sent a third, and they wounded him and threw him out.
v13: The owner of the vineyard said, ‘What should I do? I will send my beloved son. Perhaps they will respect him.’
v14: But when the tenants saw him, they reasoned together, ‘This is the heir. Let us kill him so the inheritance will become ours.’
v15: They threw him out of the vineyard and killed him. What then will the owner of the vineyard do to them?
v16: He will come, destroy those tenants, and give the vineyard to others.” When the people heard this, they said, “May it never happen!”
v17: But Jesus looked at them and said, “Then what does this Scripture mean:
‘The stone the builders rejected
has become the cornerstone’?
v18: Everyone who falls on that stone will be broken to pieces, and anyone on whom it falls will be crushed.”
v19: The scribes and chief priests wanted to lay hands on him that very hour, but they were afraid of the people, because they knew he had spoken this parable against them.
</p>

## Caesar and God

<p>
v20: They watched Jesus closely and sent spies who pretended to be righteous so they could catch him in something he said and hand him over to the authority and jurisdiction of the governor.
v21: They asked him, “Teacher, we know that you speak and teach rightly and show no partiality but teach God’s way truthfully.
v22: Is it lawful for us to pay tribute to Caesar or not?”
v23: But Jesus recognized their craftiness and said,
v24: “Show me a denarius. Whose image and inscription does it have?” They answered, “Caesar’s.”
v25: He said to them, “Then give back to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s.”
v26: They could not catch him in anything he said before the people. Amazed by his answer, they became silent.
</p>

## Resurrection and the Living God

<p>
v27: Some Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, came and questioned him:
v28: “Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if a man’s brother dies leaving a wife but no child, his brother should take the wife and raise up offspring for his brother.
v29: There were seven brothers. The first took a wife and died childless.
v30: The second
v31: and then the third took her, and in the same way all seven died without leaving children.
v32: Finally the woman also died.
v33: In the resurrection, whose wife will she be? For all seven had her as wife.”
v34: Jesus said to them, “The children of this age marry and are given in marriage,
v35: but those considered worthy to attain that age and the resurrection from the dead neither marry nor are given in marriage.
v36: They cannot die anymore, because they are like angels and are children of God, being children of the resurrection.
v37: But that the dead are raised, even Moses showed in the passage about the bush, where he calls the Lord ‘the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.’
v38: He is not God of the dead but of the living, because to him all are alive.”
v39: Some scribes answered, “Teacher, you have spoken well.”
v40: And no one dared ask him anything else.
</p>

## David’s Son and Lord

<p>
v41: Then Jesus said to them, “How can people say that the Christ is David’s son?
v42: David himself says in the book of Psalms:
‘The Lord said to my Lord,
Sit at my right hand
v43: until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet.’
v44: David calls him Lord, so how is he his son?”
</p>

## Beware of the Scribes

<p>
v45: While all the people were listening, Jesus said to his disciples,
v46: “Beware of the scribes who like to walk around in long robes, love greetings in the marketplaces, the best seats in synagogues, and places of honor at banquets.
v47: They devour widows’ houses and, for appearance, make long prayers. They will receive greater condemnation.”
</p>

## Notes

v01: Luke explicitly says Jesus is “announcing good news” in the temple when authorities challenge his authority.

v13: “Beloved son” echoes Luke’s baptism and transfiguration identity language inside the parable.

v15: Luke has the son thrown out of the vineyard before being killed, a sequence that differs from some Synoptic parallels.

v20: The spies’ stated purpose is political transfer to the governor; Luke makes the trap’s legal aim explicit.

v25: `apodidōmi` means give back or repay. The saying concerns what is owed to Caesar and what belongs to God without reducing the latter to a limited religious sphere.

v34–36: Resurrection life is transformed rather than a continuation of present marriage structures.

v36: “Like angels” concerns immortality in context, not humans becoming angels.

v38: “To him all are alive” is distinctive Lukan wording.

v47: The condemnation of scribes explicitly joins visible piety with exploitation of widows.
