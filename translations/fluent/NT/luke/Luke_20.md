## Jesus’ Authority Is Challenged

<p>
v01: One day, while Jesus was teaching the people in the temple courts and proclaiming the good news, the chief priests and scribes came up with the elders
v02: and said to him, “Tell us by what authority you are doing these things, or who gave you this authority.”
v03: He answered them, “I will also ask you a question. Tell me:
v04: Was John’s baptism from heaven or from people?”
v05: They reasoned among themselves, “If we say, ‘From heaven,’ he will ask, ‘Why did you not believe him?’
v06: But if we say, ‘From people,’ all the people will stone us, because they are convinced that John was a prophet.”
v07: So they answered that they did not know where it came from.
v08: Jesus told them, “Then neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things.”
</p>

## The Wicked Tenants

<p>
v09: Then he began telling the people this parable: “A man planted a vineyard, leased it to tenant farmers, and went away for a long time.
v10: At harvest time he sent a servant to the tenants so they would give him some of the vineyard’s fruit. But the tenants beat the servant and sent him away empty-handed.
v11: He sent another servant, but they beat and humiliated him too and sent him away empty-handed.
v12: Then he sent a third, but they wounded this one and threw him out.
v13: The vineyard owner said, ‘What should I do? I will send my beloved son. Perhaps they will respect him.’
v14: But when the tenants saw him, they discussed it together and said, ‘This is the heir. Let us kill him so that the inheritance will become ours.’
v15: So they threw him out of the vineyard and killed him. What, then, will the vineyard owner do to them?
v16: He will come, destroy those tenants, and give the vineyard to others.” When the people heard this, they said, “May it never happen!”
v17: But Jesus looked directly at them and said, “Then what does this Scripture mean: ‘The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone’?
v18: Everyone who falls on that stone will be broken to pieces, and anyone on whom it falls will be crushed.”
v19: The scribes and chief priests wanted to lay hands on him that very hour, because they knew he had told this parable against them—but they were afraid of the people.
</p>

## Caesar and God

<p>
v20: So they watched him closely and sent spies pretending to be righteous, hoping to catch him in something he said and hand him over to the governor’s rule and authority.
v21: They asked him, “Teacher, we know that you speak and teach correctly and show no partiality, but teach God’s way truthfully.
v22: Is it lawful for us to pay tribute to Caesar or not?”
v23: But Jesus perceived their craftiness and said to them,
v24: “Show me a denarius. Whose image and inscription does it have?” They answered, “Caesar’s.”
v25: He told them, “Then give back to Caesar what belongs to Caesar, and to God what belongs to God.”
v26: They could not catch him in his words before the people. Astonished by his answer, they became silent.
</p>

## Resurrection and the Living God

<p>
v27: Some Sadducees, who deny that there is a resurrection, came to him and asked,
v28: “Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if a man’s married brother dies childless, the man should take the widow and raise up offspring for his brother.
v29: Now there were seven brothers. The first married a woman and died childless.
v30: Then the second married her,
v31: and then the third. In the same way, all seven married her, left no children, and died.
v32: Finally, the woman also died.
v33: So in the resurrection, whose wife will she be? For all seven had married her.”
v34: Jesus told them, “The people of this age marry and are given in marriage.
v35: But those considered worthy to take part in that age and in the resurrection from the dead neither marry nor are given in marriage.
v36: Indeed, they can no longer die, because they are like angels and are children of God, being children of the resurrection.
v37: But even Moses showed that the dead are raised, in the passage about the bush, where he calls the Lord ‘the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.’
v38: He is not God of the dead but of the living, for to him all are alive.”
v39: Some of the scribes responded, “Teacher, you have spoken well.”
v40: For they no longer dared to ask him anything.
</p>

## David’s Son and Lord

<p>
v41: Then Jesus said to them, “How can people say that the Messiah is David’s son?
v42: For David himself says in the book of Psalms, ‘The Lord said to my Lord, “Sit at my right hand
v43: until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet.”’
v44: David therefore calls him ‘Lord.’ So how is he David’s son?”
</p>

## Beware of the Scribes

<p>
v45: While all the people were listening, Jesus said to his disciples,
v46: “Beware of the scribes, who like to walk around in long robes, love greetings in the marketplaces, the best seats in the synagogues, and places of honor at banquets.
v47: They devour widows’ houses and, as a pretense, make long prayers. They will receive a more severe judgment.”
</p>

## Notes

v09–16: The tenant story reports escalating assaults and murder. Its critique is directed within the narrated leadership conflict and must not be generalized into hostility toward Jewish people.

v25: The verb means give back or render what is due. The saying leaves hearers to discern the claims represented by Caesar’s image and by God.

v30: The pinned source gives only the terse phrase ‘and the second’; the English supplies ‘married her’ from the continuing sentence without importing additional events.

v34–38: Jesus answers the Sadducees’ test by distinguishing this age from the resurrection age and grounding the living God’s relation to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

v46–47: The warning joins conspicuous religious status with the economic devouring of widows’ households; long prayer functions as a pretense.

## Vocabulary

v02: **ἐξουσία (exousia)**: Authority, right, or delegated power.

v25: **ἀποδίδωμι (apodidōmi)**: Give back, repay, or render what is due.

v27: **ἀνάστασις (anastasis)**: Resurrection, rising from the dead.

v47: **κρίμα (krima)**: Judgment or a judicial verdict.
