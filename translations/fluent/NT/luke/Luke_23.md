## Jesus Before Pilate and Herod

<p>
v01: The whole assembly got up and led Jesus to Pilate.
v02: They began accusing him: “We found this man misleading our nation, forbidding payment of tribute to Caesar, and saying that he himself is Christ, a king.”
v03: Pilate asked him, “Are you the king of the Jews?” Jesus answered, “You say so.”
v04: Pilate said to the chief priests and crowds, “I find no guilt in this man.”
v05: But they insisted, “He stirs up the people, teaching throughout all Judea, beginning from Galilee all the way here.”
v06: When Pilate heard this, he asked whether the man was a Galilean.
v07: Learning that Jesus was under Herod’s jurisdiction, Pilate sent him to Herod, who was also in Jerusalem during those days.
v08: Herod was very glad to see Jesus. For a long time he had wanted to see him because he had heard about him, and he hoped to see Jesus perform some sign.
v09: Herod questioned him at length, but Jesus gave him no answer.
v10: The chief priests and scribes stood there accusing him forcefully.
v11: Herod and his soldiers treated Jesus with contempt and mocked him. They dressed him in splendid clothing and sent him back to Pilate.
v12: Herod and Pilate became friends with each other that very day; before this they had been enemies.
</p>

## Pilate Hands Jesus Over

<p>
v13: Pilate called together the chief priests, rulers, and people
v14: and said, “You brought me this man as one who misleads the people. Look, I examined him in front of you and found no guilt in him concerning the charges you bring.
v15: Neither did Herod, because he sent him back to us. Look, he has done nothing deserving death.
v16: Therefore I will have him punished and release him.”
v18: But they all cried out together, “Take this man away! Release Barabbas to us!”
v19: Barabbas had been thrown into prison for an insurrection in the city and for murder.
v20: Pilate addressed them again because he wanted to release Jesus,
v21: but they kept shouting, “Crucify! Crucify him!”
v22: A third time Pilate said to them, “Why? What evil has he done? I have found no reason for a death sentence. Therefore I will have him punished and release him.”
v23: But they pressed him with loud voices, demanding that Jesus be crucified, and their voices prevailed.
v24: Pilate decided that their demand should be granted.
v25: He released the man who had been thrown into prison for insurrection and murder, the one they requested, but he handed Jesus over to their will.
</p>

## The Way to the Cross

<p>
v26: As they led Jesus away, they seized Simon of Cyrene, who was coming in from the countryside, and laid the cross on him to carry behind Jesus.
v27: A large crowd of people followed him, including women who were mourning and lamenting him.
v28: Jesus turned to them and said, “Daughters of Jerusalem, do not weep for me. Weep for yourselves and for your children.
v29: Look, days are coming when people will say, ‘Blessed are the women unable to have children, the wombs that never gave birth, and the breasts that never nursed!’
v30: Then they will begin saying to the mountains, ‘Fall on us!’ and to the hills, ‘Cover us!’
v31: For if they do these things when the tree is green, what will happen when it is dry?”
v32: Two others, both criminals, were also led away with him to be executed.
</p>

## Jesus Is Crucified

<p>
v33: When they came to the place called the Skull, they crucified Jesus there, along with the criminals, one on his right and one on his left.
v34: Jesus said, “Father, forgive them, because they do not know what they are doing.” Then they divided his clothing by casting lots.
v35: The people stood watching. The rulers sneered at him: “He saved others; let him save himself if this is God’s Christ, the Chosen One.”
v36: The soldiers also mocked him, coming up and offering him sour wine.
v37: They said, “If you are the king of the Jews, save yourself!”
v38: There was also an inscription over him: “This is the king of the Jews.”
v39: One of the criminals hanging there insulted him: “Are you not the Christ? Save yourself and us!”
v40: But the other rebuked him: “Do you not even fear God, since you are under the same sentence?
v41: We are punished justly, because we are receiving what our actions deserve. But this man has done nothing wrong.”
v42: Then he said, “Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom.”
v43: Jesus said to him, “Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in paradise.”
</p>

## Jesus Dies

<p>
v44: It was now about the sixth hour, and darkness came over the whole land until the ninth hour.
v45: The sun’s light failed, and the curtain of the temple was torn down the middle.
v46: Jesus cried out with a loud voice, “Father, into your hands I entrust my spirit.” After saying this, he breathed his last.
v47: When the centurion saw what happened, he glorified God and said, “Certainly this man was righteous.”
v48: All the crowds who had gathered for this spectacle, when they saw what happened, returned home beating their chests.
v49: All Jesus’ acquaintances and the women who had followed him from Galilee stood at a distance watching these things.
</p>

## Jesus Is Buried

<p>
v50: Look—there was a man named Joseph, a council member, a good and righteous man.
v51: He had not agreed with their plan and action. He was from Arimathea, a Jewish town, and he was waiting for God’s kingdom.
v52: This man went to Pilate and asked for Jesus’ body.
v53: He took it down, wrapped it in linen cloth, and placed him in a tomb cut from rock where no one had yet been laid.
v54: It was Preparation Day, and the Sabbath was beginning.
v55: The women who had come with Jesus from Galilee followed and saw the tomb and how his body was laid.
v56: Then they returned and prepared spices and perfumes. On the Sabbath they rested according to the commandment.
</p>

## Notes

v04,14–16,22: Luke repeatedly states Pilate’s finding that Jesus is not guilty of a capital offense, while still narrating Pilate’s decision to hand him over.

v08–12: Herod seeks a sign and receives silence. Luke alone narrates this Herod hearing and the new friendship between Herod and Pilate.

v17: Public verse 17 is absent from SBLGNT’s main text. Later manuscripts add an explanation that Pilate was obligated to release one prisoner at the festival.

v26: Simon carries the cross “behind Jesus,” wording that recalls Luke’s discipleship language about coming after Jesus with the cross.

v34: “Father, forgive them…” is printed in SBLGNT but bracketed as textually disputed. Fluent includes it with this note.

v35–43: “Save” language is repeated mockingly around Jesus, but the scene culminates with Jesus promising salvation-presence to the criminal rather than saving himself from the cross.

v43: Luke’s “today” theme reaches the crucifixion: “today you will be with me in paradise.”

v46: Luke gives Jesus a final prayer of trust from Psalm 31 rather than Mark and Matthew’s cry of abandonment.

v47: Luke’s centurion calls Jesus “righteous,” differing from the “Son of God” confession in Mark and Matthew.

v55–56: The women witness the burial location, prepare burial materials, and still rest on the Sabbath according to the commandment.
