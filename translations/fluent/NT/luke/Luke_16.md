## The Shrewd Steward

<p>
v01: Jesus also said to his disciples, “There was a rich man who had a manager, and charges were brought to him that this manager was wasting his possessions.
v02: So he called him in and said, ‘What is this I hear about you? Give an account of your management, because you can no longer be manager.’
v03: The manager said to himself, ‘What will I do, since my master is taking the management away from me? I am not strong enough to dig, and I am ashamed to beg.
v04: I know what I will do so that, when I am removed from management, people will welcome me into their homes.’
v05: He summoned each of his master’s debtors and asked the first, ‘How much do you owe my master?’
v06: He answered, ‘A hundred measures of olive oil.’ The manager told him, ‘Take your bill, sit down quickly, and write fifty.’
v07: Then he asked another, ‘And how much do you owe?’ He replied, ‘A hundred measures of wheat.’ The manager told him, ‘Take your bill and write eighty.’
v08: The master praised the dishonest manager because he had acted shrewdly. For the children of this age are more shrewd in dealing with their own generation than the children of light are.
v09: And I tell you, make friends for yourselves by means of dishonest wealth, so that when it fails they may welcome you into eternal dwellings.
v10: Whoever is faithful with very little is also faithful with much, and whoever is dishonest with very little is also dishonest with much.
v11: So if you have not been faithful with dishonest wealth, who will entrust true riches to you?
v12: And if you have not been faithful with what belongs to another, who will give you what is your own?
v13: No household servant can serve two masters. Either he will hate one and love the other, or he will be devoted to one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and wealth.”
</p>

## God Knows the Heart

<p>
v14: The Pharisees, who loved money, heard all this and ridiculed him.
v15: He said to them, “You are the ones who justify yourselves before people, but God knows your hearts. What people highly esteem is detestable before God.
v16: The Law and the Prophets were proclaimed until John. Since then the good news of God’s kingdom is announced, and everyone presses forcefully into it.
v17: But it is easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for one stroke of a letter in the Law to fall.
v18: Everyone who divorces his wife and marries another woman commits adultery, and a man who marries a woman divorced from her husband commits adultery.
</p>

## The Rich Man and Lazarus

<p>
v19: “There was a rich man who dressed in purple and fine linen and feasted splendidly every day.
v20: A poor man named Lazarus, covered with sores, had been laid at his gate.
v21: He longed to be filled with what fell from the rich man’s table. Even the dogs came and licked his sores.
v22: The poor man died and was carried by the angels to Abraham’s side. The rich man also died and was buried.
v23: In Hades, where he was in torment, the rich man looked up and saw Abraham far away with Lazarus at his side.
v24: He called out, ‘Father Abraham, have mercy on me! Send Lazarus to dip the tip of his finger in water and cool my tongue, because I am in agony in this flame.’
v25: But Abraham said, ‘Child, remember that during your life you received your good things, while Lazarus likewise received bad things. But now he is comforted here, and you are in agony.
v26: Besides all this, a great chasm has been fixed between us and you, so that those who want to cross from here to you cannot, nor can anyone cross from there to us.’
v27: He said, ‘Then I beg you, father, send Lazarus to my father’s house,
v28: because I have five brothers. Let him warn them so that they do not also come to this place of torment.’
v29: But Abraham said, ‘They have Moses and the Prophets; let them listen to them.’
v30: He replied, ‘No, Father Abraham, but if someone from the dead goes to them, they will repent.’
v31: Abraham told him, ‘If they do not listen to Moses and the Prophets, neither will they be persuaded even if someone rises from the dead.’”
</p>

## Notes

v08: The master praises the manager’s shrewdness, not his dishonesty. The story’s difficult moral force is developed in the sayings that follow.

v09: ‘Dishonest wealth’ is literally ‘the mammon of injustice.’ The saying recognizes wealth’s compromised setting while directing it toward relationships that outlast it.

v16: The final verb can suggest forceful entry, urgent pressing, or violence. The main text preserves the force without making the kingdom something seized by merit.

v19–31: The story names the poor man Lazarus but leaves the rich man unnamed. Their reversal exposes the rich man’s failure to see the suffering at his gate; the imagery should not be made into a detailed map of the afterlife.

## Vocabulary

v01: **οἰκονόμος (oikonomos)**: Manager or steward entrusted with a household’s affairs.

v09: **μαμωνᾶς (mamōnas)**: Mammon, wealth treated as a power or master.

v23: **ᾅδης (hadēs)**: Hades, the realm of the dead.

v25: **παρακαλέω (parakaleō)**: Comfort, encourage, appeal, or call alongside.
