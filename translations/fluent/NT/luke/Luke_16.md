## The Shrewd Steward

<p>
v01: Jesus also said to his disciples, “There was a rich man who had a steward, and accusations were brought to him that this steward was wasting his possessions.
v02: He called him and said, ‘What is this I hear about you? Give an account of your stewardship, because you can no longer be steward.’
v03: The steward said to himself, ‘What should I do? My master is taking the stewardship away from me. I am not strong enough to dig, and I am ashamed to beg.
v04: I know what I will do so that when I am removed from the stewardship, people will welcome me into their homes.’
v05: He called in each of his master’s debtors. He asked the first, ‘How much do you owe my master?’
v06: The man said, ‘A hundred measures of olive oil.’ The steward told him, ‘Take your bill, sit down quickly, and write fifty.’
v07: Then he asked another, ‘And how much do you owe?’ The man said, ‘A hundred measures of wheat.’ He told him, ‘Take your bill and write eighty.’
v08: The master commended the unrighteous steward because he had acted shrewdly. For the children of this age are more shrewd toward their own generation than the children of light.
v09: And I tell you, make friends for yourselves by means of unrighteous wealth so that when it fails, they may welcome you into eternal dwellings.
v10: Whoever is faithful in very little is also faithful in much, and whoever is unrighteous in very little is also unrighteous in much.
v11: Therefore, if you have not been faithful with unrighteous wealth, who will entrust true riches to you?
v12: And if you have not been faithful with what belongs to another, who will give you what is your own?
v13: No servant can serve two masters. Either they will hate one and love the other, or be devoted to one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and wealth.”
</p>

## God Knows the Heart

<p>
v14: The Pharisees, who loved money, heard all these things and ridiculed Jesus.
v15: He said to them, “You are the ones who justify yourselves before people, but God knows your hearts. What is highly valued among people is detestable before God.
v16: The Law and the Prophets were until John. Since then God’s kingdom is announced as good news, and everyone is pressing into it.
v17: But it is easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for one stroke of a letter of the Law to fall.
v18: Everyone who divorces his wife and marries another commits adultery, and whoever marries a woman divorced from her husband commits adultery.
</p>

## The Rich Man and Lazarus

<p>
v19: “There was a rich man who dressed in purple and fine linen and feasted splendidly every day.
v20: A poor man named Lazarus had been laid at his gate, covered with sores.
v21: He longed to be fed with what fell from the rich man’s table. Even the dogs came and licked his sores.
v22: The poor man died and was carried by the angels to Abraham’s side. The rich man also died and was buried.
v23: In Hades, while in torment, he looked up and saw Abraham far away, with Lazarus at his side.
v24: He called out, ‘Father Abraham, have mercy on me! Send Lazarus to dip the tip of his finger in water and cool my tongue, because I am in anguish in this flame.’
v25: But Abraham said, ‘Child, remember that during your life you received your good things, while Lazarus received bad things. Now he is comforted here, and you are in anguish.
v26: Besides all this, a great chasm has been fixed between us and you so that those wanting to cross from here to you cannot, nor can anyone cross from there to us.’
v27: The rich man said, ‘Then I beg you, father, send him to my father’s house,
v28: because I have five brothers. Let him warn them so they do not also come to this place of torment.’
v29: Abraham said, ‘They have Moses and the Prophets. Let them listen to them.’
v30: He said, ‘No, Father Abraham. But if someone goes to them from the dead, they will repent.’
v31: Abraham said to him, ‘If they do not listen to Moses and the Prophets, neither will they be persuaded if someone rises from the dead.’”
</p>

## Notes

v01–08: The steward is called unrighteous, and his shrewdness—not his dishonesty—is the feature commended.

v09: “Unrighteous wealth” is Luke’s deliberately suspicious language for money in a world marked by unjust systems. The command is to deploy it toward relationships and eternal ends.

v15: The Pharisees “justify themselves”; God’s knowledge of the heart exposes the limits of public moral status.

v16: The difficult “everyone is pressing into it” can be heard as forceful entry or pressure around the kingdom. Fluent leaves the active image rather than explaining it away.

v18: The divorce saying stands abruptly among teachings about Law, kingdom, and wealth; Luke gives no exception clause here.

v19–31: The story reverses rich and poor conditions after death but does not name the rich man.

v22: “Abraham’s side” renders an intimacy and banquet image rather than a technical map of the afterlife.

v29–31: The climax concerns hearing Scripture. Even resurrection is said not to compel those unwilling to hear Moses and the Prophets.
