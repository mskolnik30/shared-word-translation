## Healing at a Sabbath Meal

<p>
v01: One Sabbath, Jesus went to eat at the home of a leading Pharisee, and they were watching him closely.
v02: There in front of him was a man whose body was swollen with fluid.
v03: Jesus asked the legal experts and Pharisees, “Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath or not?”
v04: But they remained silent. Jesus took hold of the man, healed him, and sent him away.
v05: Then he said to them, “If your son or your ox falls into a well, which of you will not immediately pull him out on the Sabbath day?”
v06: They were unable to answer him about these things.
</p>

## Choosing the Lower Place

<p>
v07: When Jesus noticed how the invited guests were choosing the places of honor, he told them a parable:
v08: “When someone invites you to a wedding feast, do not recline in the place of honor. Someone more distinguished than you may have been invited,
v09: and the host who invited both of you may come and say, ‘Give this person your place.’ Then in shame you will begin to take the lowest place.
v10: Instead, when you are invited, go and recline in the lowest place. Then when your host comes, he will say to you, ‘Friend, move up higher.’ You will be honored in front of all who are reclining with you.
v11: For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted.”
</p>

## Invite Those Who Cannot Repay

<p>
v12: Then Jesus said to the man who had invited him, “When you give a luncheon or dinner, do not invite your friends, brothers, relatives, or rich neighbors, in case they invite you in return and you are repaid.
v13: But when you give a banquet, invite people who are poor, disabled, lame, or blind.
v14: Then you will be blessed, because they cannot repay you; you will be repaid at the resurrection of the righteous.”
</p>

## The Great Banquet

<p>
v15: When one of the guests heard this, he said to Jesus, “Blessed is the one who will eat bread in the kingdom of God!”
v16: Jesus replied, “A man was preparing a great dinner and invited many guests.
v17: At dinner time he sent his servant to tell those who had been invited, ‘Come, because everything is now ready.’
v18: But they all alike began to make excuses. The first said, ‘I bought a field and must go see it. Please excuse me.’
v19: Another said, ‘I bought five pairs of oxen and am going to test them. Please excuse me.’
v20: Still another said, ‘I just got married, so I cannot come.’
v21: The servant returned and reported these things to his master. Then the homeowner became angry and told his servant, ‘Go quickly into the city’s streets and lanes and bring in here the poor, disabled, blind, and lame.’
v22: The servant said, ‘Sir, what you ordered has been done, and there is still room.’
v23: Then the master told the servant, ‘Go out to the roads and hedges and urge people to come in, so that my house will be filled.
v24: For I tell you, not one of those men who were invited will taste my dinner.’”
</p>

## Count the Cost

<p>
v25: Large crowds were traveling with Jesus. He turned and said to them,
v26: “If anyone comes to me and does not hate his own father and mother, wife and children, brothers and sisters—yes, even his own life—he cannot be my disciple.
v27: Whoever does not carry his own cross and come after me cannot be my disciple.
v28: Which of you, wanting to build a tower, does not first sit down and calculate the cost to see whether he has enough to finish it?
v29: Otherwise, after he lays a foundation but cannot finish, everyone watching will begin to ridicule him,
v30: saying, ‘This man began to build but could not finish.’
v31: Or what king, going to meet another king in war, will not first sit down and consider whether he can oppose with ten thousand the one coming against him with twenty thousand?
v32: If he cannot, while the other is still far away he sends a delegation to ask for terms of peace.
v33: In the same way, none of you who does not renounce all his possessions can be my disciple.
v34: Salt is good, but if even salt loses its taste, how will its saltiness be restored?
v35: It is fit neither for the soil nor for the manure pile; people throw it out. Let anyone who has ears to hear listen.”
</p>

## Notes

v02: The ancient term describes swelling caused by retained fluid. The main text describes the body without assigning a modern diagnosis.

v13: The Greek names people who are poor, disabled, lame, and blind. These are invited guests, not illustrations detached from real people.

v23: The verb can mean urge strongly or compel. In this banquet setting it expresses the host’s determination to welcome people from beyond the expected circles; it does not authorize coercion.

v26: ‘Hate’ retains the saying’s deliberately severe language of comparative allegiance. It should not be weakened into a claim that family ties are insignificant.

## Vocabulary

v11: **ταπεινόω (tapeinoō)**: Humble or bring low.

v14: **ἀνάστασις (anastasis)**: Resurrection, rising from the dead.

v26: **μαθητής (mathētēs)**: Disciple or learner who follows a teacher.

v33: **ἀποτάσσομαι (apotassomai)**: Take leave of, renounce, or give up claim to.
