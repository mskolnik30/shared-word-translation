## Beware of Hypocrisy

<p>
v01: Meanwhile, a crowd of many thousands gathered, so that they were trampling one another. Jesus began by speaking first to his disciples: “Guard yourselves against the yeast of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy.
v02: Nothing is concealed that will not be revealed, or hidden that will not become known.
v03: So whatever you have said in the dark will be heard in the light, and what you have whispered in private rooms will be proclaimed from the rooftops.
v04: I tell you, my friends, do not fear those who kill the body and after that can do nothing more.
v05: But I will show you whom to fear: fear the one who, after killing, has authority to throw into Gehenna. Yes, I tell you, fear him.
v06: Are not five sparrows sold for two small coins? Yet not one of them is forgotten before God.
v07: Why, even the hairs of your head are all counted. Do not be afraid; you are worth more than many sparrows.
v08: I tell you, everyone who acknowledges me before others, the Son of Man will also acknowledge before God’s angels.
v09: But whoever denies me before others will be denied before God’s angels.
v10: Everyone who speaks a word against the Son of Man will be forgiven, but whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven.
v11: When they bring you before synagogues, rulers, and authorities, do not worry about how to defend yourselves or what to say,
v12: for the Holy Spirit will teach you at that very hour what you must say.”
</p>

## The Rich Fool

<p>
v13: Someone in the crowd said to him, “Teacher, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me.”
v14: But Jesus replied, “Man, who appointed me judge or arbitrator over you?”
v15: Then he said to them, “Watch out and guard yourselves against every kind of greed, because a person’s life does not come from having an abundance of possessions.”
v16: He told them a parable: “A rich man’s land produced an abundant crop.
v17: He thought to himself, ‘What should I do? I have nowhere to store my crops.’
v18: Then he said, ‘This is what I will do: I will tear down my barns and build larger ones, and there I will store all my grain and goods.
v19: Then I will say to myself, “You have many goods stored up for many years. Rest, eat, drink, and enjoy yourself.”’
v20: But God said to him, ‘Fool! This very night your life is being demanded back from you. Then who will have what you prepared?’
v21: So it is with the one who stores up treasure for himself but is not rich toward God.”
</p>

## Seek the Kingdom

<p>
v22: Then Jesus said to his disciples, “Therefore I tell you, do not worry about your life—what you will eat—or about your body—what you will wear.
v23: For life is more than food, and the body more than clothing.
v24: Consider the ravens: they neither sow nor reap; they have no storeroom or barn, yet God feeds them. How much more valuable you are than birds!
v25: Which of you by worrying can add a cubit to your span of life?
v26: If, then, you cannot do even this very small thing, why worry about the rest?
v27: Consider how the lilies grow: they neither labor nor spin. Yet I tell you, not even Solomon in all his glory was clothed like one of these.
v28: If God clothes the grass this way—alive in the field today and thrown into an oven tomorrow—how much more will he clothe you, you of little faith!
v29: So do not keep seeking what you will eat and drink, and do not be anxious.
v30: For all the nations of the world pursue these things, and your Father knows that you need them.
v31: Instead, seek his kingdom, and these things will be given to you as well.
v32: Do not be afraid, little flock, because your Father has been pleased to give you the kingdom.
v33: Sell your possessions and give to those in need. Make purses for yourselves that do not wear out—an unfailing treasure in heaven, where no thief comes near and no moth destroys.
v34: For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.
</p>

## Be Ready and Faithful

<p>
v35: Be dressed and ready, with your lamps burning,
v36: like servants waiting for their master to return from a wedding feast, so that when he comes and knocks they can open the door for him at once.
v37: Blessed are those servants whom the master finds awake when he comes. Truly I tell you, he will dress himself to serve, have them recline at the table, and come serve them.
v38: Even if he comes in the second or third watch of the night and finds them ready, those servants are blessed.
v39: But understand this: if the homeowner had known at what hour the thief was coming, he would not have let his house be broken into.
v40: You also must be ready, because the Son of Man is coming at an hour you do not expect.”
v41: Peter asked, “Lord, are you telling this parable to us, or to everyone?”
v42: The Lord replied, “Who then is the faithful and wise manager whom the master will put in charge of his household servants to give them their food allowance at the proper time?
v43: Blessed is that servant whom his master finds doing so when he comes.
v44: Truly I tell you, he will put that servant in charge of all his possessions.
v45: But suppose that servant says in his heart, ‘My master is taking a long time to come,’ and begins to beat the male and female servants, and to eat, drink, and get drunk.
v46: That servant’s master will come on a day he does not expect and at an hour he does not know. He will cut him in two and assign him a place with the unfaithful.
v47: That servant who knew his master’s will but did not prepare or act according to it will receive many blows.
v48: But the one who did not know and did things deserving blows will receive few. From everyone who has been given much, much will be required; and from the one entrusted with much, even more will be demanded.
</p>

## Fire, Division, and Discernment

<p>
v49: “I came to cast fire on the earth, and how I wish it were already kindled!
v50: But I have a baptism to undergo, and how distressed I am until it is completed!
v51: Do you think I came to bring peace on the earth? No, I tell you, but division.
v52: From now on, five people in one household will be divided, three against two and two against three.
v53: They will be divided: father against son and son against father, mother against daughter and daughter against mother, mother-in-law against her daughter-in-law and daughter-in-law against mother-in-law.”
v54: He also said to the crowds, “When you see a cloud rising in the west, you immediately say, ‘A rainstorm is coming,’ and so it happens.
v55: And when the south wind blows, you say, ‘It will be scorching hot,’ and it happens.
v56: Hypocrites! You know how to interpret the appearance of earth and sky, but why do you not know how to interpret this present time?
v57: Why do you not judge for yourselves what is right?
v58: As you go with your accuser before a ruler, make every effort along the way to settle with him. Otherwise he may drag you before the judge, the judge hand you over to the officer, and the officer throw you into prison.
v59: I tell you, you will certainly not get out until you have paid the very last small coin.”
</p>

## Notes

v05: Gehenna is the Greek form of the Valley of Hinnom, used here as an image of final judgment.

v25: The Greek can refer to adding a cubit to one’s height or to one’s span of life. The context of anxious provision favors the life-span sense while the ancient measurement remains visible.

v46–48: The household warning retains its severe violence and differing degrees of accountability. The imagery is not transferred into an instruction for human punishment.

v49–50: Fire and baptism are difficult images for Jesus’ disruptive mission and approaching suffering; the main text does not reduce them to one explanation.

## Vocabulary

v01: **ὑπόκρισις (hypokrisis)**: Hypocrisy, play-acting, or presenting a false appearance.

v15: **πλεονεξία (pleonexia)**: Greed or the grasping desire to have more.

v32: **ποίμνιον (poimnion)**: A small flock.

v50: **βάπτισμα (baptisma)**: Baptism or immersion; here an ordeal Jesus must undergo.
