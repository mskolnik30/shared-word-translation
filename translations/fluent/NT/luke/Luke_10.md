## The Seventy-Two Are Sent

<p>
v01: After this, the Lord appointed seventy-two others and sent them ahead of him in pairs to every town and place he was about to visit.
v02: He told them, “The harvest is plentiful, but the workers are few. Therefore ask the Lord of the harvest to send out workers into his harvest.
v03: Go! I am sending you out like lambs among wolves.
v04: Carry no moneybag, travel bag, or sandals, and greet no one along the road.
v05: Whenever you enter a house, first say, ‘Peace to this house.’
v06: If a person of peace is there, your peace will rest on them; if not, it will return to you.
v07: Stay in that same house, eating and drinking whatever they provide, because the worker deserves their wages. Do not move from house to house.
v08: Whenever you enter a town and they welcome you, eat what is set before you.
v09: Heal the sick there and tell them, ‘The kingdom of God has come near to you.’
v10: But whenever you enter a town and they do not welcome you, go out into its streets and say,
v11: ‘Even the dust from your town that clings to our feet we wipe off against you. Yet know this: the kingdom of God has come near.’
v12: I tell you, on that day it will be more bearable for Sodom than for that town.
v13: Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! If the miracles done among you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago, sitting in sackcloth and ashes.
v14: But at the judgment it will be more bearable for Tyre and Sidon than for you.
v15: And you, Capernaum, will you be lifted up to heaven? You will be brought down to Hades.
v16: Whoever listens to you listens to me; whoever rejects you rejects me; and whoever rejects me rejects the one who sent me.”
</p>

## The Seventy-Two Return

<p>
v17: The seventy-two returned with joy, saying, “Lord, even the demons submit to us in your name!”
v18: He said to them, “I watched Satan fall from heaven like lightning.
v19: Look, I have given you authority to tread on snakes and scorpions and over all the power of the enemy. Nothing will ever harm you.
v20: Nevertheless, do not rejoice that the spirits submit to you; rejoice that your names are written in heaven.”
</p>

## Jesus Rejoices in the Holy Spirit

<p>
v21: At that very hour Jesus rejoiced in the Holy Spirit and said, “I praise you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you hid these things from the wise and intelligent and revealed them to infants. Yes, Father, because this was your gracious will.
v22: Everything has been handed over to me by my Father. No one knows who the Son is except the Father, or who the Father is except the Son and anyone to whom the Son chooses to reveal him.”
v23: Then Jesus turned privately to the disciples and said, “Blessed are the eyes that see what you see.
v24: For I tell you, many prophets and kings wanted to see what you see but did not see it, and to hear what you hear but did not hear it.”
</p>

## A Samaritan Becomes the Neighbor

<p>
v25: An expert in the law stood up to test Jesus. “Teacher,” he asked, “what must I do to inherit eternal life?”
v26: Jesus said to him, “What is written in the law? How do you read it?”
v27: He answered, “‘Love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, with all your strength, and with all your mind,’ and ‘your neighbor as yourself.’”
v28: Jesus told him, “You have answered correctly. Do this, and you will live.”
v29: But wanting to justify himself, he asked Jesus, “And who is my neighbor?”
v30: Jesus replied, “A man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho when he fell into the hands of robbers. They stripped him, beat him, and went away, leaving him half dead.
v31: By chance a priest was going down that road. When he saw the man, he passed by on the other side.
v32: In the same way, a Levite came to the place, saw him, and passed by on the other side.
v33: But a Samaritan traveling that way came to him, saw him, and was moved with compassion.
v34: He went to him, bandaged his wounds, and poured on oil and wine. Then he put him on his own animal, brought him to an inn, and cared for him.
v35: The next day he took out two denarii, gave them to the innkeeper, and said, ‘Take care of him. Whatever more you spend, I will repay you when I return.’
v36: Which of these three do you think became a neighbor to the man who fell among the robbers?”
v37: The expert said, “The one who showed him mercy.” Jesus told him, “Go and do likewise.”
</p>

## Mary and Martha

<p>
v38: As they continued on their way, Jesus entered a village. A woman named Martha welcomed him into her home.
v39: She had a sister named Mary, who sat at the Lord’s feet and listened to his word.
v40: But Martha was distracted by her many tasks. She came to Jesus and said, “Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to serve alone? Tell her to help me.”
v41: The Lord answered her, “Martha, Martha, you are worried and troubled about many things,
v42: but few things are needed—or only one. Mary has chosen the good portion, and it will not be taken away from her.”
</p>

## Notes

v01, 17: The pinned Greek text reads ‘seventy-two.’ Other ancient witnesses read ‘seventy.’

v06: ‘Person of peace’ is literally ‘son of peace,’ an idiom for someone characterized by or receptive to peace.

v11: The kingdom's nearness is announced both to towns that welcome the messengers and to a town that rejects them.

v19: The saying grants authority over snakes, scorpions, and the enemy's power. It is not a command to handle dangerous animals or test God.

v21: The pinned reading explicitly says Jesus rejoiced ‘in the Holy Spirit.’

v29–37: Jesus answers ‘Who is my neighbor?’ by asking who became a neighbor to the assaulted man. The Samaritan's identity, compassion, material care, and continuing obligation remain central to the reversal.

v38–42: Martha's service is real and necessary; the scene contrasts distracted anxiety with Mary's protected attention. It does not condemn hospitality or service as such.

v42: The Greek reads ‘few things are needed, or one.’ The wording may concern a simple meal, a single necessary thing, or both. The main text preserves the unusual alternatives.

## Vocabulary

v02: **θερισμός (therismos)**: Harvest.

v06: **εἰρήνη (eirēnē)**: Peace, well-being, or wholeness.

v15: **ᾅδης (hadēs)**: Hades, the realm of the dead.

v25: **ζωὴ αἰώνιος (zōē aiōnios)**: Eternal life or the life of the age to come.

v33: **σπλαγχνίζομαι (splagchnizomai)**: Be moved with deep compassion.

v37: **ἔλεος (eleos)**: Mercy or compassionate action.
