## Jesus and Zacchaeus

<p>
v01: Jesus entered Jericho and was passing through.
v02: There was a man named Zacchaeus. He was a chief tax collector and was wealthy.
v03: He was trying to see who Jesus was, but he could not because of the crowd, since he was short.
v04: So he ran ahead and climbed a sycamore-fig tree to see Jesus, who was about to pass that way.
v05: When Jesus reached the place, he looked up and said to him, “Zacchaeus, hurry and come down, because today I must stay at your house.”
v06: So he hurried down and welcomed Jesus joyfully.
v07: When everyone saw this, they began to grumble, “He has gone in to lodge with a sinful man.”
v08: But Zacchaeus stood and said to the Lord, “Look, Lord, I give half of my possessions to the poor, and if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I repay four times as much.”
v09: Jesus said to him, “Today salvation has come to this house, because this man too is a son of Abraham.
v10: For the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost.”
</p>

## The Parable of the Minas

<p>
v11: As they were listening to these things, Jesus went on to tell a parable because he was near Jerusalem and they thought God’s kingdom was about to appear immediately.
v12: He said, “A nobleman went to a distant country to receive royal authority for himself and then return.
v13: He called ten of his servants, gave them ten minas, and said, ‘Conduct business with these until I return.’
v14: But his citizens hated him and sent a delegation after him to say, ‘We do not want this man to rule over us.’
v15: After receiving royal authority, he returned and summoned the servants to whom he had given the money, so that he could find out what they had earned through business.
v16: The first came and said, ‘Lord, your mina has earned ten more minas.’
v17: The nobleman told him, ‘Well done, good servant! Because you have been faithful in a very small matter, take authority over ten cities.’
v18: The second came and said, ‘Your mina, Lord, has made five minas.’
v19: He told this servant too, ‘You are to be over five cities.’
v20: Then another servant came and said, ‘Lord, here is your mina, which I kept put away in a cloth.
v21: I was afraid of you because you are a harsh man: you take what you did not deposit and reap what you did not sow.’
v22: The nobleman replied, ‘I will judge you by your own words, wicked servant. You knew, did you, that I am a harsh man, taking what I did not deposit and reaping what I did not sow?
v23: Then why did you not put my money in a bank, so that when I returned I could have collected it with interest?’
v24: Then he said to those standing nearby, ‘Take the mina from him and give it to the one who has ten minas.’
v25: They said to him, ‘Lord, he already has ten minas!’
v26: ‘I tell you, to everyone who has, more will be given; but from the one who does not have, even what he has will be taken away.
v27: But as for these enemies of mine who did not want me to rule over them, bring them here and slaughter them in front of me.’”
</p>

## Jesus Enters Jerusalem

<p>
v28: After Jesus said these things, he went on ahead, going up to Jerusalem.
v29: As he approached Bethphage and Bethany near the hill called the Mount of Olives, he sent two of his disciples,
v30: saying, “Go into the village opposite you. As you enter it, you will find a colt tied there that no one has ever sat on. Untie it and bring it here.
v31: If anyone asks you, ‘Why are you untying it?’ say this: ‘The Lord needs it.’”
v32: Those who were sent went and found everything just as he had told them.
v33: As they were untying the colt, its owners asked them, “Why are you untying the colt?”
v34: They replied, “The Lord needs it.”
v35: They brought it to Jesus, threw their cloaks on the colt, and helped Jesus mount it.
v36: As he rode along, people spread their cloaks on the road.
v37: As he came near the road down the Mount of Olives, the whole multitude of disciples began rejoicing and praising God with a loud voice for all the mighty works they had seen.
v38: They said, “Blessed is the king who comes in the name of the Lord! Peace in heaven and glory in the highest!”
v39: Some Pharisees in the crowd said to him, “Teacher, rebuke your disciples.”
v40: He replied, “I tell you, if these people become silent, the stones will cry out.”
</p>

## Jesus Weeps over Jerusalem

<p>
v41: As Jesus came near and saw the city, he wept over it
v42: and said, “If only you too had recognized on this day what makes for peace! But now it has been hidden from your eyes.
v43: For days will come upon you when your enemies will build a siege ramp around you, encircle you, and press in on you from every side.
v44: They will crush you to the ground, you and the children within you. They will not leave one stone on another within you, because you did not recognize the time when God visited you.”
</p>

## Jesus in the Temple

<p>
v45: Then Jesus entered the temple courts and began driving out those who were selling.
v46: He told them, “It is written, ‘My house will be a house of prayer,’ but you have made it ‘a den of robbers.’”
v47: Every day Jesus was teaching in the temple courts. But the chief priests, scribes, and leaders of the people were seeking to destroy him.
v48: Yet they could not find anything they could do, because all the people were hanging on his every word.
</p>

## Notes

v08: Zacchaeus speaks in the present tense: ‘I give’ and ‘I repay.’ This can describe a new commitment or declare a practice; the text does not explicitly settle the timing.

v11–27: The nobleman’s political setting and final slaughter command remain part of the character’s story. The translation does not soften the violence or silently turn every action of the ruler into a direct command from God.

v42: ‘What makes for peace’ is literally ‘the things toward peace,’ gathering the path or conditions of peace without reducing them to a slogan.

v44: The word rendered ‘visited’ can describe God’s gracious inspection or visitation. Jerusalem fails to recognize the decisive time.

v45: The pinned text names those selling in the temple courts; it does not add buyers or money changers in this verse.

## Vocabulary

v02: **ἀρχιτελώνης (architelōnēs)**: Chief tax collector.

v08: **συκοφαντέω (sykophanteō)**: Defraud, extort, or accuse falsely for gain.

v13: **μνᾶ (mna)**: Mina, a substantial unit of money.

v44: **ἐπισκοπή (episkopē)**: Visitation, oversight, or inspection.
