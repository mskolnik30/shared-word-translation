## Jesus and Zacchaeus

<p>
v01: Jesus entered Jericho and was passing through.
v02: Look—a man named Zacchaeus was there. He was a chief tax collector, and he was rich.
v03: He was trying to see who Jesus was but could not because of the crowd, since he was short.
v04: So he ran ahead and climbed a sycamore tree to see Jesus, because he was about to pass that way.
v05: When Jesus came to the place, he looked up and said, “Zacchaeus, hurry and come down, because today I must stay at your house.”
v06: Zacchaeus hurried down and welcomed him with joy.
v07: When everyone saw this, they began grumbling, “He has gone in to stay with a sinful man.”
v08: But Zacchaeus stood and said to the Lord, “Look, Lord, I give half of my possessions to the poor, and if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I repay four times as much.”
v09: Jesus said to him, “Today salvation has come to this house, because this man too is a son of Abraham.
v10: For the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost.”
</p>

## The Parable of the Minas

<p>
v11: As they were listening to these things, Jesus went on to tell a parable because he was near Jerusalem and they thought God’s kingdom was about to appear immediately.
v12: He said, “A nobleman went to a distant country to receive a kingdom for himself and then return.
v13: He called ten of his servants, gave them ten minas, and said, ‘Do business with these until I return.’
v14: But his citizens hated him and sent a delegation after him, saying, ‘We do not want this man to reign over us.’
v15: When he returned after receiving the kingdom, he ordered the servants to whom he had given the money to be called so he could learn what they had gained through business.
v16: The first came and said, ‘Lord, your mina has made ten more minas.’
v17: He said, ‘Well done, good servant! Because you were faithful in very little, have authority over ten cities.’
v18: The second came and said, ‘Your mina, Lord, has made five minas.’
v19: He said to him, ‘And you, be over five cities.’
v20: Another came and said, ‘Lord, look—here is your mina, which I kept laid away in a cloth.
v21: I was afraid of you because you are a severe man. You take what you did not deposit and reap what you did not sow.’
v22: He said to him, ‘I will judge you by your own words, evil servant. You knew that I am a severe man, taking what I did not deposit and reaping what I did not sow?
v23: Why then did you not put my money in a bank so that when I returned I could collect it with interest?’
v24: He said to those standing nearby, ‘Take the mina from him and give it to the one who has ten minas.’
v25: They said, ‘Lord, he already has ten minas!’
v26: ‘I tell you, to everyone who has, more will be given; but from the one who does not have, even what they have will be taken away.
v27: But these enemies of mine who did not want me to reign over them—bring them here and slaughter them in front of me.’”
</p>

## Jesus Enters Jerusalem

<p>
v28: After saying these things, Jesus went on ahead, going up to Jerusalem.
v29: When he approached Bethphage and Bethany near the mountain called Olivet, he sent two disciples,
v30: saying, “Go into the village ahead of you. As you enter, you will find a colt tied there that no one has ever ridden. Untie it and bring it.
v31: If anyone asks, ‘Why are you untying it?’ say, ‘The Lord needs it.’”
v32: Those who were sent went and found it just as he had told them.
v33: As they were untying the colt, its owners asked, “Why are you untying the colt?”
v34: They answered, “The Lord needs it.”
v35: They brought it to Jesus, threw their cloaks on the colt, and put Jesus on it.
v36: As he went along, people spread their cloaks on the road.
v37: When he was already approaching the descent of the Mount of Olives, the whole multitude of disciples began rejoicing and praising God loudly for all the works of power they had seen:
v38: “Blessed is the king who comes in the name of the Lord!
Peace in heaven
and glory in the highest!”
v39: Some Pharisees in the crowd said to him, “Teacher, rebuke your disciples.”
v40: He answered, “I tell you, if they become silent, the stones will cry out.”
</p>

## Jesus Weeps over Jerusalem

<p>
v41: As Jesus drew near and saw the city, he wept over it,
v42: saying, “If only you had known on this day the things that make for peace! But now they are hidden from your eyes.
v43: Days will come upon you when your enemies will build a barricade around you, surround you, and press you in from every side.
v44: They will crush you to the ground, you and your children within you, and they will not leave one stone on another, because you did not recognize the time of your visitation.”
</p>

## Jesus in the Temple

<p>
v45: Jesus entered the temple and began driving out those who were selling.
v46: He said to them, “It is written, ‘My house will be a house of prayer,’ but you have made it a den of robbers.”
v47: He was teaching daily in the temple. The chief priests, scribes, and leaders of the people were looking for a way to destroy him,
v48: but they could not find what to do, because all the people were hanging on his words.
</p>

## Notes

v05: “Today” and `dei`, “must,” bring together Luke’s present-salvation and divine-necessity themes.

v08: Zacchaeus describes concrete redistribution and restitution. The text does not explicitly say whether every act is future intention or present practice; the scene clearly presents economic transformation.

v09: Jesus restores Zacchaeus to the Abraham-family identity that the crowd’s label “sinner” threatens to obscure.

v10: Seeking and saving the lost summarizes Jesus’ mission and links directly with Luke 15.

v11: Luke explicitly says the minas parable addresses expectation that God’s kingdom will appear immediately.

v21–22: The servant’s description of the nobleman as severe is used against him in the reply; it need not be treated as an exhaustive description of God.

v27: The violent ending remains inside the parable’s royal-judgment world. It is not a command to Jesus’ followers to kill opponents.

v38: Luke’s acclamation says “peace in heaven,” echoing the heavenly peace/glory language of 2:14.

v41: Jesus’ tears prevent the coming judgment oracle from being heard as detached satisfaction.

v44: `episkopē` can mean visitation or inspection. Here the tragedy is failure to recognize God’s saving visitation.
