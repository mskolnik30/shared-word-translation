## The Twelve Are Sent

<p>
v01: Jesus called the twelve together and gave them power and authority over all demons and to heal diseases.
v02: He sent them to proclaim God’s kingdom and to heal.
v03: He told them, “Take nothing for the journey—no staff, bag, bread, or money, and do not have two tunics.
v04: Whatever house you enter, stay there until you leave that place.
v05: Wherever people do not welcome you, leave that town and shake the dust off your feet as a testimony against them.”
v06: They went out through the villages, announcing good news and healing everywhere.
v07: Herod the tetrarch heard about everything that was happening and was perplexed because some people were saying that John had been raised from the dead,
v08: others that Elijah had appeared, and others that one of the ancient prophets had arisen.
v09: Herod said, “John I beheaded. Who then is this about whom I hear such things?” And he kept trying to see Jesus.
</p>

## Jesus Feeds Five Thousand

<p>
v10: When the apostles returned, they told Jesus everything they had done. He took them with him and withdrew privately toward a town called Bethsaida.
v11: But the crowds learned about it and followed him. He welcomed them, spoke to them about God’s kingdom, and healed those who needed healing.
v12: The day began to decline. The twelve came and said, “Send the crowd away so they can go into the surrounding villages and countryside to find lodging and food, because we are here in a deserted place.”
v13: But he said, “You give them something to eat.” They replied, “We have only five loaves and two fish—unless we go and buy food for all these people.”
v14: There were about five thousand men. Jesus told his disciples, “Have them sit down in groups of about fifty.”
v15: They did so and had everyone sit down.
v16: Jesus took the five loaves and two fish, looked up to heaven, blessed them, broke them, and kept giving them to the disciples to set before the crowd.
v17: Everyone ate and was satisfied, and twelve baskets of broken pieces were collected from what remained.
</p>

## Peter’s Confession and the Cross

<p>
v18: Once, while Jesus was praying alone and the disciples were with him, he asked them, “Who do the crowds say I am?”
v19: They answered, “John the Baptist; others say Elijah; and others that one of the ancient prophets has arisen.”
v20: He asked them, “But you—who do you say I am?” Peter answered, “God’s Christ.”
v21: Jesus strictly warned them and commanded them to tell this to no one.
v22: He said, “The Son of Man must suffer many things, be rejected by the elders, chief priests, and scribes, be killed, and on the third day be raised.”
v23: He said to everyone, “If anyone wants to come after me, let them deny themselves, take up their cross daily, and follow me.
v24: For whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life because of me will save it.
v25: What does it benefit a person to gain the whole world but lose or forfeit themselves?
v26: Whoever is ashamed of me and my words, the Son of Man will be ashamed of that person when he comes in his glory and the glory of the Father and the holy angels.
v27: But truly I tell you, some standing here will certainly not taste death before they see God’s kingdom.”
</p>

## The Transfiguration

<p>
v28: About eight days after these sayings, Jesus took Peter, John, and James and went up the mountain to pray.
v29: As he was praying, the appearance of his face changed, and his clothing became dazzling white.
v30: Look—two men were talking with him: Moses and Elijah.
v31: Appearing in glory, they were speaking about his departure, which he was about to accomplish in Jerusalem.
v32: Peter and those with him were heavy with sleep, but when they became fully awake they saw Jesus’ glory and the two men standing with him.
v33: As the men were leaving Jesus, Peter said, “Master, it is good for us to be here. Let us make three shelters: one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah.” He did not know what he was saying.
v34: While he was saying this, a cloud came and overshadowed them, and they were afraid as they entered the cloud.
v35: A voice came from the cloud: “This is my Son, the Chosen One. Listen to him!”
v36: When the voice had spoken, Jesus was found alone. They kept silent and told no one in those days anything they had seen.
</p>

## A Boy Is Healed

<p>
v37: The next day, when they came down from the mountain, a large crowd met Jesus.
v38: Look—a man from the crowd cried out, “Teacher, I beg you to look at my son, because he is my only child.
v39: Look—a spirit seizes him, and suddenly he cries out. It convulses him so that he foams, and it barely leaves him after crushing him.
v40: I begged your disciples to cast it out, but they could not.”
v41: Jesus answered, “Faithless and twisted generation! How long will I be with you and put up with you? Bring your son here.”
v42: While the boy was still coming, the demon threw him down and convulsed him. But Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit, healed the boy, and gave him back to his father.
v43: Everyone was astonished at God’s greatness. While everyone was marveling at everything Jesus was doing, he said to his disciples,
v44: “Let these words sink into your ears: the Son of Man is about to be handed over into human hands.”
v45: But they did not understand this saying. It was concealed from them so that they could not grasp it, and they were afraid to ask him about it.
</p>

## Greatness and the Road to Jerusalem

<p>
v46: An argument arose among the disciples about which of them might be greatest.
v47: Jesus, knowing the reasoning of their hearts, took a child and stood the child beside him.
v48: He said, “Whoever welcomes this child in my name welcomes me, and whoever welcomes me welcomes the one who sent me. For the one who is least among all of you—this one is great.”
v49: John answered, “Master, we saw someone casting out demons in your name, and we tried to stop him because he does not follow with us.”
v50: Jesus said, “Do not stop him, because whoever is not against you is for you.”
v51: When the days were approaching for him to be taken up, Jesus set his face to go to Jerusalem.
v52: He sent messengers ahead of him. They entered a Samaritan village to prepare for him,
v53: but the people did not welcome him because his face was set toward Jerusalem.
v54: When James and John saw this, they said, “Lord, do you want us to command fire to come down from heaven and consume them?”
v55: But Jesus turned and rebuked them.
v56: Then they went to another village.
v57: As they were traveling along the road, someone said to Jesus, “I will follow you wherever you go.”
v58: Jesus said, “Foxes have dens, and birds of the sky have nests, but the Son of Man has nowhere to lay his head.”
v59: He said to another, “Follow me.” But the man replied, “Lord, first allow me to go and bury my father.”
v60: Jesus said, “Let the dead bury their own dead, but you go and proclaim God’s kingdom.”
v61: Another said, “I will follow you, Lord, but first allow me to say goodbye to those in my house.”
v62: Jesus said, “No one who puts a hand to the plow and looks back is fit for God’s kingdom.”
</p>

## Notes

v18,28: Luke places both Peter’s confession and the transfiguration explicitly in settings of Jesus’ prayer.

v23: Luke uniquely includes “daily” in the call to take up the cross.

v31: `exodos`, “departure,” describes what Jesus will accomplish in Jerusalem, linking death and resurrection with biblical deliverance language.

v35: SBLGNT reads “the Chosen One.” “Beloved” is an important variant familiar from the Synoptic parallels; Fluent follows the pinned Luke text.

v43–45: Luke places the second passion saying at the height of public amazement and then says its meaning was concealed from the disciples.

v51: “He set his face to go to Jerusalem” marks the major structural turn of Luke’s Gospel.

v54–55: Later manuscripts add a saying explaining that the Son of Man came to save rather than destroy lives. SBLGNT’s main text has only Jesus’ rebuke.

v57–62: Luke collects three severe sayings about following Jesus at the beginning of the Jerusalem journey.
