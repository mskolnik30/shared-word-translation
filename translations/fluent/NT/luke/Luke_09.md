## The Twelve Are Sent

<p>
v01: Jesus called the Twelve together and gave them power and authority over all demons and power to heal diseases.
v02: He sent them out to proclaim the kingdom of God and to heal the sick.
v03: He told them, “Take nothing for the journey—no staff, bag, bread, or money, and do not have two tunics apiece.
v04: Whatever house you enter, stay there until you leave that place.
v05: Wherever people do not welcome you, leave that town and shake the dust from your feet as a testimony against them.”
v06: So they set out and traveled from village to village, bringing the good news and healing everywhere.
v07: Herod the tetrarch heard about everything that was happening, and he was perplexed because some were saying that John had been raised from the dead,
v08: others that Elijah had appeared, and still others that one of the ancient prophets had risen.
v09: Herod said, “I beheaded John. Who, then, is this man I hear such things about?” And he kept trying to see Jesus.
</p>

## Jesus Feeds Five Thousand

<p>
v10: When the apostles returned, they reported to Jesus everything they had done. He took them with him and withdrew privately to a town called Bethsaida.
v11: But the crowds learned about it and followed him. He welcomed them, spoke to them about the kingdom of God, and healed those who needed healing.
v12: As the day began to draw to a close, the Twelve came to him and said, “Send the crowd away so they can go into the surrounding villages and countryside to find lodging and food, because we are here in a deserted place.”
v13: But he said to them, “You give them something to eat.” They replied, “We have no more than five loaves and two fish—unless we go and buy food for all these people.”
v14: There were about five thousand men. Jesus told his disciples, “Have them sit down in groups of about fifty each.”
v15: They did so and had everyone sit down.
v16: Taking the five loaves and two fish, Jesus looked up to heaven, blessed them, broke them, and kept giving them to the disciples to set before the crowd.
v17: They all ate and were satisfied. The leftovers were gathered up—twelve baskets of broken pieces.
</p>

## Peter’s Confession and the Cross

<p>
v18: Once, while Jesus was praying alone and the disciples were with him, he asked them, “Who do the crowds say I am?”
v19: They answered, “John the Baptist; others say Elijah; and still others say that one of the ancient prophets has risen.”
v20: “But you,” he asked, “who do you say I am?” Peter answered, “God’s Messiah.”
v21: Jesus warned them sternly and ordered them not to tell this to anyone.
v22: He said, “The Son of Man must suffer many things, be rejected by the elders, chief priests, and scribes, be killed, and be raised on the third day.”
v23: Then he said to everyone, “If anyone wants to come after me, they must deny themselves, take up their cross every day, and follow me.
v24: For whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life because of me will save it.
v25: What does it benefit someone to gain the whole world but lose or forfeit themselves?
v26: Whoever is ashamed of me and my words, the Son of Man will be ashamed of that person when he comes in his glory and the glory of the Father and of the holy angels.
v27: Truly I tell you, some standing here will certainly not taste death before they see the kingdom of God.”
</p>

## The Transfiguration

<p>
v28: About eight days after these sayings, Jesus took Peter, John, and James and went up the mountain to pray.
v29: As he was praying, the appearance of his face changed, and his clothing became dazzling white.
v30: Suddenly two men were talking with him: Moses and Elijah.
v31: They appeared in glory and spoke about his departure, which he was about to fulfill in Jerusalem.
v32: Peter and those with him were weighed down with sleep, but when they became fully awake they saw Jesus’ glory and the two men standing with him.
v33: As the men were leaving Jesus, Peter said to him, “Master, it is good for us to be here. Let us make three shelters: one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah.” He did not know what he was saying.
v34: While he was saying this, a cloud came and overshadowed them. They were afraid as they entered the cloud.
v35: A voice came from the cloud, saying, “This is my Son, my Chosen One. Listen to him!”
v36: When the voice had spoken, Jesus was found alone. The disciples kept silent and, in those days, told no one anything they had seen.
</p>

## A Boy Is Healed

<p>
v37: The next day, when they came down from the mountain, a large crowd met Jesus.
v38: A man in the crowd shouted, “Teacher, I beg you to look at my son, because he is my only child.
v39: A spirit seizes him, and he suddenly screams. It convulses him until he foams and scarcely leaves him, crushing him as it goes.
v40: I begged your disciples to drive it out, but they could not.”
v41: Jesus answered, “You unbelieving and corrupt generation! How long must I be with you and put up with you? Bring your son here.”
v42: Even while the boy was approaching, the demon threw him down and convulsed him. But Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit, healed the child, and gave him back to his father.
v43: Everyone was astonished at the majesty of God. While they were all marveling at everything Jesus was doing, he said to his disciples,
v44: “Let these words sink into your ears: The Son of Man is about to be handed over into human hands.”
v45: But they did not understand this saying. Its meaning was concealed from them so they could not grasp it, and they were afraid to ask him about it.
</p>

## Greatness and the Road to Jerusalem

<p>
v46: An argument arose among the disciples about which of them would be the greatest.
v47: But Jesus knew the reasoning in their hearts. He took a child and had the child stand beside him.
v48: He said to them, “Whoever welcomes this child in my name welcomes me, and whoever welcomes me welcomes the one who sent me. For the one who is least among all of you is the one who is great.”
v49: John answered, “Master, we saw someone driving out demons in your name, and we tried to stop him because he does not follow with us.”
v50: Jesus told him, “Do not stop him, for whoever is not against you is for you.”
v51: As the days approached for Jesus to be taken up, he set his face firmly toward Jerusalem.
v52: He sent messengers ahead of him. They entered a Samaritan village to make preparations for him,
v53: but the people did not welcome him because he was heading toward Jerusalem.
v54: When the disciples James and John saw this, they said, “Lord, do you want us to call down fire from heaven to consume them?”
v55: But Jesus turned and rebuked them.
v56: Then they went to another village.
v57: As they were traveling along the road, someone said to Jesus, “I will follow you wherever you go.”
v58: Jesus told him, “Foxes have dens and birds of the sky have nests, but the Son of Man has nowhere to lay his head.”
v59: He said to another man, “Follow me.” But the man replied, “Lord, first let me go and bury my father.”
v60: Jesus told him, “Let the dead bury their own dead, but you go and proclaim the kingdom of God.”
v61: Another said, “I will follow you, Lord, but first let me say goodbye to those in my household.”
v62: Jesus told him, “No one who puts a hand to the plow and looks back is fit for the kingdom of God.”
</p>

## Notes

v03: The pinned text prohibits a staff as well as a bag, bread, money, and two tunics. Parallel mission accounts differ in some travel details.

v14: The count explicitly says about five thousand men. The verse does not provide a separate count of women or children.

v23: Luke explicitly includes ‘every day’ in the saying about taking up one's cross.

v25: Luke's form says a person may lose or forfeit ‘himself,’ emphasizing damage to the person rather than using only the word ‘soul.’

v28: Luke says ‘about eight days.’ Parallel accounts use a different way of marking the interval.

v31: The word rendered ‘departure’ is *exodos*. In this setting it gathers Jesus' coming death, resurrection, and departure into the journey he will fulfill at Jerusalem.

v35: The pinned text reads ‘my Chosen One.’ Other manuscript traditions have wording closer to ‘my beloved Son.’

v38–42: The child is the father's only son. The violent symptoms and harm are reported as the action of a spirit; the translation does not impose a modern medical diagnosis.

v51: ‘Set his face’ is an ancient image of firm resolve. Jesus' journey toward Jerusalem unfolds under the horizon of his being ‘taken up.’

v54–56: James and John propose consuming the Samaritan village with fire. Jesus rebukes them and the group goes elsewhere. The pinned text does not include the longer rebuke found in some manuscripts.

## Vocabulary

v01: **δύναμις / ἐξουσία (dynamis / exousia)**: Power or ability / authority or right.

v20: **χριστός (christos)**: Messiah or Christ, the anointed one.

v23: **σταυρός (stauros)**: Cross, a Roman instrument of execution and public shame.

v31: **ἔξοδος (exodos)**: Departure or exodus.

v35: **ἐκλέγομαι (eklegomai)**: Choose or select.

v51: **ἀνάλημψις (analēmpsis)**: Being taken up or ascension.
