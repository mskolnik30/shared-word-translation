## Simon and the Great Catch

<p>
v01: One day, while the crowd pressed around Jesus to hear the word of God, he was standing beside Lake Gennesaret.
v02: He saw two boats at the water’s edge. The fishermen had gotten out of them and were washing their nets.
v03: Jesus got into one of the boats, which belonged to Simon, and asked him to put out a little from shore. Then he sat down and taught the crowds from the boat.
v04: When he finished speaking, he said to Simon, “Put out into deep water, and let down your nets for a catch.”
v05: Simon answered, “Master, we worked hard all night and caught nothing. But because you say so, I will let down the nets.”
v06: When they did this, they enclosed such a large number of fish that their nets began to tear.
v07: They signaled to their partners in the other boat to come and help them. They came and filled both boats so full that they began to sink.
v08: When Simon Peter saw this, he fell at Jesus’ knees and said, “Go away from me, Lord, because I am a sinful man.”
v09: For astonishment had seized him and everyone with him because of the catch of fish they had taken.
v10: James and John, the sons of Zebedee, Simon’s partners, were astonished too. Jesus said to Simon, “Do not be afraid. From now on you will be catching people alive.”
v11: Then they brought the boats to shore, left everything, and followed him.
</p>

## A Man Is Made Clean

<p>
v12: While Jesus was in one of the towns, a man covered with leprosy saw him, fell facedown, and pleaded, “Lord, if you are willing, you can make me clean.”
v13: Jesus reached out his hand and touched him. “I am willing,” he said. “Be made clean.” Immediately the leprosy left him.
v14: Then Jesus ordered him not to tell anyone: “Instead, go and show yourself to the priest, and offer what Moses commanded for your cleansing, as a testimony to them.”
v15: But the news about Jesus spread even more, and large crowds gathered to hear him and to be healed of their illnesses.
v16: Yet Jesus would withdraw to deserted places and pray.
</p>

## A Paralytic Forgiven and Healed

<p>
v17: One day, as Jesus was teaching, Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there. They had come from every village of Galilee and Judea and from Jerusalem, and the Lord’s power was with him to heal.
v18: Some men came carrying on a mat a man who was paralyzed. They tried to bring him inside and place him before Jesus.
v19: When they could not find a way to bring him in because of the crowd, they went up on the roof and lowered him on his mat through the tiles into the middle of the room, right in front of Jesus.
v20: When Jesus saw their faith, he said, “Friend, your sins are forgiven.”
v21: The scribes and Pharisees began reasoning, “Who is this man who speaks blasphemies? Who can forgive sins except God alone?”
v22: Jesus knew what they were reasoning and answered them, “Why are you reasoning this way in your hearts?
v23: Which is easier: to say, ‘Your sins are forgiven,’ or to say, ‘Get up and walk’?
v24: But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins”—he said to the paralyzed man—“I tell you, get up, pick up your mat, and go home.”
v25: Immediately he stood up in front of them, picked up what he had been lying on, and went home glorifying God.
v26: Amazement seized them all, and they glorified God. Filled with fear, they said, “We have seen extraordinary things today.”
</p>

## Levi Is Called

<p>
v27: After this, Jesus went out and saw a tax collector named Levi sitting at the tax booth. He said to him, “Follow me.”
v28: Levi got up, left everything, and followed him.
v29: Then Levi gave a great banquet for Jesus in his house. A large crowd of tax collectors and others were reclining at the table with them.
v30: The Pharisees and their scribes complained to Jesus’ disciples, “Why do you eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners?”
v31: Jesus answered them, “Those who are healthy do not need a physician, but those who are sick do.
v32: I have not come to call righteous people, but sinners to repentance.”
</p>

## Fasting and the New

<p>
v33: They said to him, “John’s disciples often fast and pray, and so do the disciples of the Pharisees, but yours eat and drink.”
v34: Jesus said to them, “You cannot make the wedding guests fast while the bridegroom is with them, can you?
v35: But days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them. Then, in those days, they will fast.”
v36: He also told them a parable: “No one tears a patch from a new garment and puts it on an old one. Otherwise, the new garment will be torn, and the patch from the new will not match the old.
v37: And no one pours new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise, the new wine will burst the skins; it will spill out, and the skins will be ruined.
v38: Instead, new wine must be poured into new wineskins.
v39: And no one who has drunk old wine wants new, for he says, ‘The old is good.’”
</p>

## Notes

v10: The verb translated ‘catching people alive’ differs from ordinary fishing language and explicitly includes keeping the catch alive. The image is retained without turning people into prey.

v12: ‘Leprosy’ represents a range of ancient skin conditions and does not establish a modern medical diagnosis.

v14: ‘As a testimony to them’ does not state whether ‘them’ refers specifically to the priests or more broadly to the people. The open pronoun is retained.

v17: The wording connects the Lord's power with Jesus' healing. It does not portray healing as an impersonal technique.

v20: Jesus sees ‘their faith,’ including the action of those who carried the man. The address translated ‘Friend’ is literally ‘human being’ or ‘man.’

v34: ‘Wedding guests’ is literally ‘sons of the bridal chamber,’ an idiom for those accompanying the bridegroom.

v36–39: Luke's paired images emphasize the damage caused by joining new and old in the wrong way, then add the old-wine drinker's preference. The saying itself does not identify every item in the images.

## Vocabulary

v08: **ἁμαρτωλός (hamartōlos)**: Sinful or a sinner; Peter applies the term to himself.

v10: **ζωγρέω (zōgreō)**: Catch alive or take captive alive.

v20: **πίστις (pistis)**: Faith, trust, or faithfulness.

v24: **ἐξουσία (exousia)**: Authority or delegated right.

v32: **μετάνοια (metanoia)**: Repentance, a turning of mind and direction.

v37: **ἀσκός (askos)**: A skin container used for liquids such as wine.
