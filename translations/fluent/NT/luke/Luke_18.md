## A Widow Who Does Not Give Up

<p>
v01: Jesus told them a parable about the need always to pray and not lose heart.
v02: He said, “In a certain town there was a judge who did not fear God and did not respect people.
v03: A widow in that town kept coming to him and saying, ‘Give me justice against my opponent.’
v04: For a while he was unwilling. But afterward he said to himself, ‘Even though I do not fear God or respect people,
v05: because this widow keeps causing me trouble, I will give her justice so she does not wear me out by continually coming.’”
v06: The Lord said, “Listen to what the unrighteous judge says.
v07: Will God not certainly bring justice for his chosen ones who cry out to him day and night? Will he keep delaying over them?
v08: I tell you, he will bring them justice quickly. Nevertheless, when the Son of Man comes, will he find faith on the earth?”
</p>

## A Pharisee and a Tax Collector Pray

<p>
v09: Jesus also told this parable to some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous and looked down on everyone else:
v10: “Two men went up to the temple to pray, one a Pharisee and the other a tax collector.
v11: The Pharisee stood apart and prayed these things about himself: ‘God, I thank you that I am not like everyone else—extortioners, unrighteous people, adulterers—or even like this tax collector.
v12: I fast twice a week. I give a tenth of everything I acquire.’
v13: But the tax collector stood far away and would not even lift his eyes toward heaven. He beat his chest and said, ‘God, be merciful to me, the sinner!’
v14: I tell you, this man went down to his house set right rather than the other. For everyone who exalts themselves will be humbled, and the one who humbles themselves will be exalted.”
</p>

## Receive the Kingdom Like a Child

<p>
v15: People were bringing even infants to Jesus so that he might touch them. When the disciples saw this, they rebuked them.
v16: But Jesus called the children to him and said, “Let the children come to me, and do not stop them, because God’s kingdom belongs to such as these.
v17: Truly I tell you, whoever does not receive God’s kingdom like a child will certainly not enter it.”
</p>

## The Rich Ruler

<p>
v18: A ruler asked Jesus, “Good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?”
v19: Jesus said to him, “Why do you call me good? No one is good except God alone.
v20: You know the commandments: ‘Do not commit adultery. Do not murder. Do not steal. Do not bear false witness. Honor your father and mother.’”
v21: The man said, “I have kept all these from my youth.”
v22: When Jesus heard this, he said to him, “You still lack one thing: sell everything you have and distribute it to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. Then come, follow me.”
v23: When the man heard this, he became deeply sorrowful because he was extremely rich.
v24: Jesus saw him and said, “How difficult it is for those who have wealth to enter God’s kingdom!
v25: It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich person to enter God’s kingdom.”
v26: Those who heard this asked, “Then who can be saved?”
v27: Jesus said, “What is impossible with people is possible with God.”
v28: Peter said, “Look, we have left what was ours and followed you.”
v29: Jesus said to them, “Truly I tell you, no one who has left house, wife, brothers, parents, or children because of God’s kingdom
v30: will fail to receive many times more in this time, and in the age to come eternal life.”
</p>

## The Son of Man Will Be Handed Over

<p>
v31: Jesus took the twelve aside and said, “Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and everything written through the prophets about the Son of Man will be fulfilled.
v32: He will be handed over to the Gentiles and mocked, mistreated, and spit on.
v33: After flogging him, they will kill him, and on the third day he will rise.”
v34: They understood none of these things. The saying was hidden from them, and they did not know what he was talking about.
</p>

## A Blind Man Sees and Follows

<p>
v35: As Jesus approached Jericho, a blind man was sitting beside the road begging.
v36: When he heard a crowd passing, he asked what was happening.
v37: They told him, “Jesus the Nazarene is passing by.”
v38: He cried out, “Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!”
v39: Those in front rebuked him and told him to be quiet, but he cried out even more loudly, “Son of David, have mercy on me!”
v40: Jesus stopped and ordered that the man be brought to him. When he came near, Jesus asked,
v41: “What do you want me to do for you?” He said, “Lord, I want to see again.”
v42: Jesus said, “See again. Your faith has saved you.”
v43: Immediately he received his sight and followed Jesus, glorifying God. When all the people saw it, they gave praise to God.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Luke explains the parable’s purpose before telling it: persistent prayer and refusal to lose heart.

v05: `hypōpiazō` literally evokes striking under the eye and can idiomatically mean wear out. Fluent uses the idiomatic force.

v07–08: The judge is a contrast, not a picture of God. If even an unjust judge acts, how much more will God bring justice.

v13: `hilaskomai` is an atonement and mercy verb. The tax collector asks God to deal mercifully with him as “the sinner.”

v14: `dikaioō` is rendered “set right,” consistent with the project’s lexical policy. Here the passive describes the tax collector’s status before God.

v15: Luke specifies that even infants, `brephē`, were being brought to Jesus.

v22: Luke has “distribute to the poor,” preserving the economic specificity of Jesus’ demand.

v31: Luke emphasizes fulfillment of everything written through the prophets about the Son of Man.

v35: Luke locates the healing as Jesus approaches Jericho, differing from Mark and Matthew’s placement as Jesus leaves Jericho.

v42: `sōzō` again joins restored sight with salvation language.
