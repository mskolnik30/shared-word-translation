## A Widow Who Does Not Give Up

<p>
v01: Then Jesus told them a parable about their need always to pray and not lose heart.
v02: He said, “In a certain city there was a judge who neither feared God nor respected people.
v03: There was also a widow in that city who kept coming to him, saying, ‘Give me justice against my opponent.’
v04: For some time he refused. But afterward he said to himself, ‘Although I neither fear God nor respect people,
v05: because this widow keeps bothering me, I will give her justice, so that she will not wear me out by continually coming.’”
v06: The Lord said, “Listen to what the unjust judge says.
v07: Will God not certainly give justice to his chosen people who cry out to him day and night? Will he delay long over them?
v08: I tell you, he will give them justice quickly. Nevertheless, when the Son of Man comes, will he find faith on the earth?”
</p>

## A Pharisee and a Tax Collector Pray

<p>
v09: He also told this parable to some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous and treated everyone else with contempt:
v10: “Two men went up to the temple to pray, one a Pharisee and the other a tax collector.
v11: The Pharisee stood by himself and prayed these things: ‘God, I thank you that I am not like other people—swindlers, unjust, adulterers—or even like this tax collector.
v12: I fast twice a week and give a tenth of everything I acquire.’
v13: But the tax collector stood far off and would not even lift his eyes to heaven. Instead, he kept beating his chest and saying, ‘God, be merciful to me, the sinner.’
v14: I tell you, this man went down to his home justified rather than the other. For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, but whoever humbles himself will be exalted.”
</p>

## Receive the Kingdom Like a Child

<p>
v15: People were bringing even infants to Jesus for him to touch them. When the disciples saw this, they rebuked them.
v16: But Jesus called the children to him and said, “Let the children come to me; do not stop them, because the kingdom of God belongs to people like these.
v17: Truly I tell you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God like a child will certainly not enter it.”
</p>

## The Rich Ruler

<p>
v18: A ruler asked him, “Good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?”
v19: Jesus said to him, “Why do you call me good? No one is good except God alone.
v20: You know the commandments: Do not commit adultery; do not murder; do not steal; do not give false testimony; honor your father and mother.”
v21: He replied, “I have kept all these since my youth.”
v22: When Jesus heard this, he told him, “You still lack one thing: sell everything you have and distribute the proceeds to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. Then come, follow me.”
v23: But when the man heard this, he became deeply sorrowful, because he was extremely rich.
v24: Seeing him, Jesus said, “How difficult it is for those who have wealth to enter the kingdom of God!
v25: Indeed, it is easier for a camel to enter through the eye of a needle than for a rich person to enter the kingdom of God.”
v26: Those who heard asked, “Then who can be saved?”
v27: Jesus replied, “What is impossible for people is possible with God.”
v28: Peter said, “Look, we have left what was ours and followed you.”
v29: Jesus told them, “Truly I tell you, no one who has left house, wife, brothers, parents, or children for the sake of God’s kingdom
v30: will fail to receive many times more in this present time, and in the coming age eternal life.”
</p>

## The Son of Man Will Be Handed Over

<p>
v31: Taking the Twelve aside, Jesus told them, “Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and everything written by the prophets about the Son of Man will be fulfilled.
v32: He will be handed over to the Gentiles, mocked, abused, and spat on.
v33: After flogging him, they will kill him, and on the third day he will rise.”
v34: But they understood none of these things. This saying was hidden from them, and they did not grasp what was said.
</p>

## A Blind Man Sees and Follows

<p>
v35: As Jesus approached Jericho, a blind man was sitting beside the road begging.
v36: When he heard a crowd going by, he asked what was happening.
v37: They told him, “Jesus the Nazarene is passing by.”
v38: He cried out, “Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!”
v39: Those leading the way rebuked him and told him to be quiet, but he shouted all the more, “Son of David, have mercy on me!”
v40: Jesus stopped and ordered that the man be brought to him. When he came near, Jesus asked him,
v41: “What do you want me to do for you?” He replied, “Lord, let me see again.”
v42: Jesus told him, “See again. Your faith has saved you.”
v43: Immediately he regained his sight and followed Jesus, glorifying God. When all the people saw it, they gave praise to God.
</p>

## Notes

v03–08: The widow asks for justice against an opponent. The judge acts from self-interest, while Jesus argues from the unjust judge to God rather than comparing God’s character to his.

v05: The final idiom may mean wear me out, give me a black eye, or damage my reputation. The main text keeps the judge’s fear of her relentless return.

v13: The tax collector calls himself ‘the sinner,’ concentrating his confession on his own need rather than comparing himself with others.

v22: Jesus’ command joins total divestment, distribution to the poor, heavenly treasure, and personal following; none of these movements is omitted.

v35–43: The man is blind and begs beside the road. The translation keeps his agency, persistent voice, request, restored sight, and decision to follow Jesus.

## Vocabulary

v03: **ἐκδικέω (ekdikeō)**: Give justice, vindicate, or avenge.

v13: **ἱλάσκομαι (hilaskomai)**: Show mercy or make atonement.

v18: **ζωὴ αἰώνιος (zōē aiōnios)**: Eternal life or the life of the age to come.

v42: **πίστις (pistis)**: Faith, trust, or faithfulness.
