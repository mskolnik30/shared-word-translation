## Repent or Perish

<p>
v01: At that very time, some people arrived and told Jesus about the Galileans whose blood Pilate had mixed with their sacrifices.
v02: He answered them, “Do you think these Galileans were worse sinners than all other Galileans because they suffered this way?
v03: No, I tell you. But unless you repent, you will all perish in the same way.
v04: Or those eighteen people on whom the tower in Siloam fell and killed them—do you think they were more guilty than everyone else living in Jerusalem?
v05: No, I tell you. But unless you repent, you will all perish likewise.”
</p>

## The Barren Fig Tree

<p>
v06: Then he told this parable: “A man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard. He came looking for fruit on it but found none.
v07: He said to the vineyard worker, ‘Look, for three years I have come looking for fruit on this fig tree and found none. Cut it down! Why should it keep wasting the soil?’
v08: But the worker answered, ‘Sir, leave it alone for one more year, until I dig around it and spread manure.
v09: If it bears fruit next year, well and good. If not, you can cut it down.’”
</p>

## A Daughter of Abraham Released

<p>
v10: Jesus was teaching in one of the synagogues on the Sabbath.
v11: There was a woman who for eighteen years had a spirit that caused weakness. She was bent over and could not straighten up at all.
v12: When Jesus saw her, he called her over and said, “Woman, you are released from your weakness.”
v13: He placed his hands on her, and immediately she stood straight and began glorifying God.
v14: But the synagogue leader, indignant because Jesus had healed on the Sabbath, said to the crowd, “There are six days on which work should be done. Come and be healed on those days, not on the Sabbath day.”
v15: The Lord answered him, “Hypocrites! Does not each of you untie his ox or donkey from the feeding trough on the Sabbath and lead it out to give it water?
v16: Then should not this woman—a daughter of Abraham whom Satan bound for eighteen years—be released from this bondage on the Sabbath day?”
v17: When he said these things, all his opponents were put to shame, while the whole crowd rejoiced at all the glorious things he was doing.
</p>

## Mustard Seed and Yeast

<p>
v18: Then Jesus said, “What is the kingdom of God like, and to what can I compare it?
v19: It is like a mustard seed that a man took and planted in his garden. It grew and became a tree, and the birds of the sky nested in its branches.”
v20: Again he said, “To what can I compare the kingdom of God?
v21: It is like yeast that a woman took and hid in three measures of flour until all of it was leavened.”
</p>

## The Narrow Door

<p>
v22: Jesus traveled through towns and villages, teaching as he made his way toward Jerusalem.
v23: Someone asked him, “Lord, will only a few be saved?” He said to them,
v24: “Strive to enter through the narrow door, because many, I tell you, will try to enter and will not be able.
v25: Once the homeowner gets up and shuts the door, you will stand outside and begin knocking, saying, ‘Lord, open the door for us!’ But he will answer, ‘I do not know where you come from.’
v26: Then you will begin to say, ‘We ate and drank in your presence, and you taught in our streets.’
v27: But he will tell you, ‘I do not know where you come from. Get away from me, all you workers of injustice!’
v28: There will be weeping and grinding of teeth when you see Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and all the prophets in the kingdom of God, but yourselves thrown outside.
v29: People will come from east and west, from north and south, and recline at the table in the kingdom of God.
v30: And look: some who are last will be first, and some who are first will be last.”
</p>

## Jesus Laments Jerusalem

<p>
v31: At that time some Pharisees came and told him, “Leave and go away from here, because Herod wants to kill you.”
v32: He replied, “Go tell that fox, ‘Look, I am driving out demons and completing healings today and tomorrow, and on the third day I reach my goal.’
v33: Nevertheless, I must continue on my way today, tomorrow, and the following day, because it is not possible for a prophet to perish outside Jerusalem.
v34: Jerusalem, Jerusalem, you who kill the prophets and stone those sent to you! How often I wanted to gather your children as a hen gathers her brood under her wings, but you were unwilling.
v35: Look, your house is left to you. I tell you, you will certainly not see me until the time comes when you say, ‘Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord.’”
</p>

## Notes

v01–05: Jesus rejects the inference that violent or accidental deaths prove that the victims were greater sinners. He turns both reports into a warning addressed to everyone.

v11–16: The woman is described as having a spirit of weakness, as a daughter of Abraham, and as bound by Satan. The translation preserves Luke’s account without imposing a modern diagnosis.

v32: ‘Reach my goal’ can also be rendered ‘be completed’ or ‘be perfected’; it gathers Jesus’ works and approaching death into the three-day saying.

v34: The hen gathering her brood is a maternal image for Jesus’ desired protection of Jerusalem’s children.

## Vocabulary

v03: **μετανοέω (metanoeō)**: Repent; turn in mind, allegiance, and life.

v12: **ἀπολύω (apolyō)**: Release, set free, or dismiss.

v19: **σινάπεως (sinapeōs)**: Mustard, known for a very small seed and vigorous growth.

v29: **ἀνακλίνω (anaklinō)**: Recline at a meal, here the kingdom banquet.
