## Repent or Perish

<p>
v01: At that time some people were present who told Jesus about the Galileans whose blood Pilate had mixed with their sacrifices.
v02: Jesus answered them, “Do you think these Galileans were worse sinners than all the other Galileans because they suffered these things?
v03: No, I tell you. But unless you repent, you will all perish in the same way.
v04: Or those eighteen people on whom the tower in Siloam fell and killed them—do you think they were worse offenders than everyone else living in Jerusalem?
v05: No, I tell you. But unless you repent, you will all perish in the same way.”
</p>

## The Barren Fig Tree

<p>
v06: He told this parable: “A man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard. He came looking for fruit on it and found none.
v07: He said to the gardener, ‘Look, for three years I have come looking for fruit on this fig tree and found none. Cut it down. Why should it keep using up the soil?’
v08: The gardener answered, ‘Sir, leave it for this year too, until I dig around it and put manure on it.
v09: If it bears fruit in the future, good. If not, you can cut it down.’”
</p>

## A Daughter of Abraham Released

<p>
v10: Jesus was teaching in one of the synagogues on the Sabbath.
v11: Look—a woman there had a spirit of infirmity for eighteen years. She was bent over and completely unable to straighten herself.
v12: When Jesus saw her, he called her forward and said, “Woman, you are released from your infirmity.”
v13: He laid his hands on her, and immediately she stood straight and began glorifying God.
v14: But the synagogue ruler was indignant because Jesus had healed on the Sabbath. He told the crowd, “There are six days on which people must work. Come on those days and be healed, not on the Sabbath.”
v15: The Lord answered him, “Hypocrites! Does not each of you untie an ox or donkey from the stall on the Sabbath and lead it away to drink?
v16: And this woman, a daughter of Abraham whom Satan bound for eighteen years—should she not be released from this bond on the Sabbath?”
v17: As he said these things, all his opponents were put to shame, and the whole crowd rejoiced over all the glorious things being done by him.
</p>

## Mustard Seed and Yeast

<p>
v18: Jesus said, “What is God’s kingdom like? What can I compare it to?
v19: It is like a mustard seed that a man took and threw into his garden. It grew and became a tree, and the birds of the sky nested in its branches.”
v20: Again he said, “What can I compare God’s kingdom to?
v21: It is like yeast that a woman took and hid in three measures of flour until all of it was leavened.”
</p>

## The Narrow Door

<p>
v22: Jesus traveled through towns and villages, teaching as he made his way toward Jerusalem.
v23: Someone asked him, “Lord, will only a few be saved?” He said to them,
v24: “Strive to enter through the narrow door, because many, I tell you, will seek to enter and will not be able.
v25: Once the master of the house gets up and shuts the door, you will begin standing outside and knocking, saying, ‘Lord, open for us!’ He will answer, ‘I do not know where you are from.’
v26: Then you will begin saying, ‘We ate and drank in your presence, and you taught in our streets.’
v27: He will say, ‘I tell you, I do not know where you are from. Go away from me, all you workers of unrighteousness.’
v28: There will be weeping and grinding of teeth when you see Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and all the prophets in God’s kingdom, but you yourselves thrown outside.
v29: People will come from east and west, north and south, and recline at the table in God’s kingdom.
v30: Look—there are last who will be first, and there are first who will be last.”
</p>

## Jesus Laments Jerusalem

<p>
v31: At that very hour some Pharisees came and told Jesus, “Leave and go away from here, because Herod wants to kill you.”
v32: He said to them, “Go tell that fox, ‘Look, I cast out demons and complete healings today and tomorrow, and on the third day I reach completion.
v33: Nevertheless, I must continue on today, tomorrow, and the following day, because it is not possible for a prophet to perish outside Jerusalem.’
v34: Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets and stones those sent to her! How often I wanted to gather your children the way a hen gathers her brood under her wings, but you were unwilling!
v35: Look, your house is left to you. I tell you, you will certainly not see me until the time comes when you say, ‘Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord.’”
</p>

## Notes

v01–05: Jesus refuses the inference that victims of violence or disaster suffered because they were morally worse than others. He redirects the discussion toward universal repentance.

v06–09: Judgment on fruitlessness is delayed, not cancelled; mercy creates additional time for fruit.

v16: Luke explicitly calls the healed woman a “daughter of Abraham” and frames Sabbath healing as release from bondage.

v21: The woman “hides” yeast in the flour. Luke allows a domestic image of hidden permeation to picture God’s kingdom.

v24: `agōnizomai` means strive or struggle. The saying addresses urgency of entry rather than giving a numerical answer to the question in v23.

v32–33: Jesus answers Herod’s threat with mission-time language: today, tomorrow, third day, and divine necessity.

v35: “Your house” remains open to temple, Jerusalem, and community resonances.
