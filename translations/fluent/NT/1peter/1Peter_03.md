## Wives and Husbands

<p>
v01: In the same way, wives, submit to your own husbands so that even if some disobey the word, they may be won without a word by their wives' conduct
v02: when they observe your pure conduct marked by reverent fear.
v03: Do not let your adornment be merely external—braided hair, wearing gold or putting on fine clothes—
v04: but let it be the hidden person of the heart, with the imperishable quality of a gentle and quiet spirit, which is very precious before God.
v05: For this is how the holy women who hoped in God once adorned themselves, submitting to their own husbands,
v06: as Sarah obeyed Abraham and called him lord. You have become her daughters if you do good and do not fear any terror.
v07: Husbands, in the same way, live considerately with your wives, showing honor to the woman as the physically weaker partner and as a coheir of the grace of life, so that your prayers are not hindered.
</p>

## Suffering for Righteousness’ Sake

<p>
v08: Finally, all of you, be harmonious, sympathetic, loving as family, compassionate and humble.
v09: Do not repay evil for evil or insult for insult. Instead, bless, because you were called to this so that you may inherit blessing.
v10: For “the one who wants to love life and see good days must keep the tongue from evil and the lips from speaking deceit;
v11: turn away from evil and do good; seek peace and pursue it.
v12: For the Lord's eyes are on the righteous and his ears attend to their prayer, but the Lord's face is against those who do evil.”
</p>

<p>
v13: Who will harm you if you become eager for what is good?
v14: But even if you should suffer for righteousness, you are blessed. Do not fear what they fear or be troubled,
v15: but honor Christ as Lord in your hearts. Always be ready to give a defense to anyone who asks you for the reason for the hope within you,
v16: yet do so with gentleness and reverent fear, maintaining a good conscience, so that those who slander your good conduct in Christ may be put to shame for their abuse.
v17: For it is better to suffer for doing good, if that should be God's will, than for doing evil.
</p>

## Christ’s Suffering and Victory

<p>
v18: For Christ also suffered once for sins, the righteous one for the unrighteous, to bring you to God. He was put to death in flesh but made alive in spirit,
v19: in which he also went and proclaimed to the spirits in prison,
v20: who disobeyed long ago when God waited patiently in Noah's days while the ark was being built. In it a few—that is, eight people—were brought safely through water.
v21: Baptism, which corresponds to this, now saves you—not as removal of dirt from the body, but as an appeal to God for a good conscience—through Jesus Christ's resurrection.
v22: He has gone into heaven and is at God's right hand, with angels, authorities and powers subjected to him.
</p>

## Notes

v01–07: The household instructions are gender-specific and arise in an unequal ancient setting; verse 7 warns husbands that abuse of honor disrupts prayer.

v19: The identity of the spirits in prison and the time and manner of Christ’s proclamation remain disputed.

v21: The text relates baptism to salvation while denying that mere removal of bodily dirt is the point.
