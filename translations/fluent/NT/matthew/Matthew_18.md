## Greatness and the little ones

<p>
v01: At that time the disciples came to Jesus and asked, “Who, then, is greatest in the kingdom of heaven?”
v02: He called a child over and stood the child among them.
v03: “Truly I tell you,” he said, “unless you turn and become like children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven.
v04: So whoever humbles themselves like this child is greatest in the kingdom of heaven.
v05: And whoever welcomes one such child in my name welcomes me.
</p>

## Stumbling blocks

<p>
v06: “But if anyone causes one of these little ones who believe in me to stumble, it would be better for that person to have a great millstone hung around their neck and be drowned in the open sea.
v07: Woe to the world because of stumbling blocks! Stumbling blocks are bound to come, but woe to the person through whom one comes.
v08: If your hand or foot causes you to stumble, cut it off and throw it away. It is better for you to enter life maimed or lame than to have two hands or two feet and be thrown into eternal fire.
v09: And if your eye causes you to stumble, tear it out and throw it away. It is better for you to enter life with one eye than to have two eyes and be thrown into fiery Gehenna.
</p>

## The wandering sheep

<p>
v10: “See that you do not despise one of these little ones. I tell you, their angels in heaven continually see the face of my Father in heaven.
v12: What do you think? If a man has a hundred sheep and one wanders away, won’t he leave the ninety-nine on the mountains and go looking for the stray?
v13: And if he finds it, truly I tell you, he rejoices over it more than over the ninety-nine that did not stray.
v14: In the same way, your Father in heaven does not will that one of these little ones should perish.
</p>

## Restoring a brother or sister

<p>
v15: “If your brother or sister sins against you, go and point out the wrong privately, just between the two of you. If they listen, you have gained your brother or sister.
v16: But if they do not listen, take one or two others with you, so that every matter may be established by the testimony of two or three witnesses.
v17: If they refuse to listen to them, tell the church. If they refuse to listen even to the church, let that person be to you as a Gentile and a tax collector.
v18: Truly I tell you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you release on earth will be released in heaven.
v19: Again, truly I tell you, if two of you on earth agree about any matter they ask for, my Father in heaven will do it for them.
v20: For where two or three gather in my name, I am there among them.”
</p>

## Forgiveness without counting

<p>
v21: Then Peter came and asked him, “Lord, how many times should I forgive my brother or sister when they sin against me? As many as seven?”
v22: Jesus told him, “Not seven times, I tell you, but seventy-seven times.
</p>

<p>
v23: “That is why the kingdom of heaven is like a king who wanted to settle accounts with his slaves.
v24: As he began settling them, a man who owed ten thousand talents was brought before him.
v25: Since he could not repay it, his master ordered that he, his wife, his children, and everything he owned be sold to pay the debt.
v26: The slave fell down and bowed before him. ‘Be patient with me,’ he said, ‘and I will repay you everything.’
v27: Moved with compassion, the master released that slave and forgave his debt.
v28: But when that slave went out, he found a fellow slave who owed him a hundred denarii. He seized him and began choking him. ‘Pay what you owe!’ he said.
v29: His fellow slave fell down and begged him, ‘Be patient with me, and I will repay you.’
v30: But he refused. Instead, he went and had him thrown into prison until he paid what he owed.
v31: When the other slaves saw what had happened, they were deeply distressed. They went and told their master everything.
v32: Then his master summoned him. ‘You evil slave,’ he said, ‘I forgave you that whole debt because you begged me.
v33: Shouldn’t you have shown your fellow slave mercy, just as I showed mercy to you?’
v34: In anger, his master handed him over to the torturers until he paid everything he owed.
v35: So my heavenly Father will also do to you unless each of you forgives your brother or sister from your heart.”
</p>

## Notes

v08–11: The bodily cutting images are forceful hyperbole about stumbling, not instructions for self-injury. The pinned source omits verse 11; numbering continues from 10 to 12.

v15,18,22: “Against you” is printed in the pinned verse 15. Binding and releasing address the disciples in the plural here. “Seventy-seven” can also be understood as seventy times seven.

v24–35: The immense ten-thousand-talent debt contrasts with a hundred denarii. Family sale, choking, imprisonment, and torture are all retained as elements of the severe parable.

## Vocabulary

v06: **Great millstone**: A heavy stone of the kind turned by a donkey, rather than a small hand mill.

v24: **Talent**: A large weight and monetary unit; the amount in the story is deliberately enormous.
