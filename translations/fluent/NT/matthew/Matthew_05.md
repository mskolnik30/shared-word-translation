## The Beatitudes

<p>
v01: When Jesus saw the crowds, he went up the mountain. After he sat down, his disciples came to him.
v02: He began teaching them:
v03: “Blessed are the poor in spirit,
because theirs is the kingdom of heaven.
v04: Blessed are those who mourn,
because they will be comforted.
v05: Blessed are the meek,
because they will inherit the earth.
v06: Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness,
because they will be filled.
v07: Blessed are the merciful,
because they will receive mercy.
v08: Blessed are the pure in heart,
because they will see God.
v09: Blessed are the peacemakers,
because they will be called children of God.
v10: Blessed are those who are persecuted because of righteousness,
because theirs is the kingdom of heaven.
v11: Blessed are you when people insult you, persecute you, and falsely say every kind of evil against you because of me.
v12: Rejoice and be glad, because your reward is great in heaven. In the same way they persecuted the prophets who came before you.
</p>

## Salt and Light

<p>
v13: “You are the salt of the earth. But if salt becomes tasteless, how can its saltiness be restored? It is no longer good for anything except to be thrown outside and trampled underfoot.
v14: You are the light of the world. A city set on a hill cannot be hidden.
v15: Neither do people light a lamp and put it under a basket. They put it on a lampstand, and it gives light to everyone in the house.
v16: In the same way, let your light shine before people so that they may see your good works and glorify your Father in heaven.
</p>

## Jesus and the Law

<p>
v17: “Do not think that I came to abolish the Law or the Prophets. I did not come to abolish but to fulfill.
v18: Truly I tell you, until heaven and earth pass away, not one iota or one small stroke will pass from the Law until everything takes place.
v19: Therefore, whoever breaks one of the least of these commandments and teaches others to do the same will be called least in the kingdom of heaven. But whoever practices and teaches them will be called great in the kingdom of heaven.
v20: For I tell you that unless your righteousness exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will certainly not enter the kingdom of heaven.
</p>

## Anger and Reconciliation

<p>
v21: “You have heard that it was said to those of old, ‘Do not murder,’ and ‘Whoever murders will be liable to judgment.’
v22: But I tell you that everyone who is angry with a brother or sister will be liable to judgment; whoever says to a brother or sister, ‘Raca,’ will be liable to the council; and whoever says, ‘Fool!’ will be liable to the fire of Gehenna.
v23: Therefore, if you are offering your gift at the altar and there remember that your brother or sister has something against you,
v24: leave your gift there before the altar. First go and be reconciled to your brother or sister; then come and offer your gift.
v25: Come to terms quickly with your accuser while you are still on the way to court, or your accuser may hand you over to the judge, the judge to the officer, and you may be thrown into prison.
v26: Truly I tell you, you will certainly not get out until you have paid the last penny.
</p>

## Adultery and Integrity

<p>
v27: “You have heard that it was said, ‘Do not commit adultery.’
v28: But I tell you that everyone who looks at a woman in order to desire her has already committed adultery with her in the heart.
v29: If your right eye causes you to stumble, tear it out and throw it away. It is better for you to lose one part of your body than for your whole body to be thrown into Gehenna.
v30: And if your right hand causes you to stumble, cut it off and throw it away. It is better for you to lose one part of your body than for your whole body to go into Gehenna.
v31: “It was also said, ‘Whoever divorces his wife must give her a certificate of divorce.’
v32: But I tell you that everyone who divorces his wife, except on account of sexual immorality, causes her to be subjected to adultery; and whoever marries a divorced woman commits adultery.
</p>

## Oaths

<p>
v33: “Again, you have heard that it was said to those of old, ‘Do not break your oath, but fulfill your oaths to the Lord.’
v34: But I tell you: do not swear at all—neither by heaven, because it is God's throne;
v35: nor by the earth, because it is his footstool; nor by Jerusalem, because it is the city of the great King.
v36: Do not swear by your head, because you cannot make one hair white or black.
v37: Let your word be simply ‘Yes, yes’ or ‘No, no.’ Anything more than this comes from evil.
</p>

## Retaliation and Enemy-Love

<p>
v38: “You have heard that it was said, ‘Eye for eye and tooth for tooth.’
v39: But I tell you: do not retaliate against an evildoer. Instead, if anyone strikes you on the right cheek, turn the other to them also.
v40: If anyone wants to sue you and take your tunic, let them have your cloak as well.
v41: If anyone forces you to go one mile, go with them two.
v42: Give to the one who asks you, and do not turn away from the one who wants to borrow from you.
v43: “You have heard that it was said, ‘Love your neighbor and hate your enemy.’
v44: But I tell you: love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you,
v45: so that you may be children of your Father in heaven. For he makes his sun rise on evil and good people and sends rain on righteous and unrighteous people.
v46: If you love those who love you, what reward do you have? Do not even tax collectors do the same?
v47: And if you greet only your own people, what are you doing beyond others? Do not even the Gentiles do the same?
v48: Therefore, be complete, as your heavenly Father is complete.
</p>

## Notes

v03–10: The repeated “blessed” statements announce God's favor before they issue any direct command.

v05: “Earth” can also be rendered “land.” The wording echoes Psalm 37 and should not be narrowed prematurely.

v17: “Fulfill” is central to Matthew. Jesus does not set Law and Prophets aside; he brings their purpose and scriptural trajectory to fulfillment.

v18: “Iota” and “small stroke” refer to the smallest features of written Scripture.

v22: Manuscript traditions vary around an added phrase equivalent to “without cause.” Fluent follows the pinned critical text.

v28: The phrase means looking with the purpose of desiring; the translation avoids turning involuntary perception itself into the stated offense.

v32: The wording and social implications of divorce law are difficult. Fluent preserves Matthew's exception phrase without expanding it into a full divorce ethic.

v39: `antistēnai` can mean resist or retaliate against. “Do not retaliate against an evildoer” aims to preserve the concrete examples that follow without making the verse prohibit every form of resistance to evil.

v48: `teleios` means complete, mature, or whole. “Complete” preserves the connection with undivided love even toward enemies rather than suggesting flawlessness in every respect.
