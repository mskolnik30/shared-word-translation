## A demand for a sign

<p>
v01: The Pharisees and Sadducees came to test him. They asked him to show them a sign from heaven.
v02: He answered, “In the evening you say, ‘Fair weather—the sky is red.’
v03: And in the morning, ‘Stormy weather today—the sky is red and threatening.’ You know how to read the sky’s appearance, but you cannot read the signs of the times.
v04: An evil and adulterous generation demands a sign, but no sign will be given to it except the sign of Jonah.” Then he left them and went away.
</p>

## The leaven of the teachers

<p>
v05: The disciples reached the other side but had forgotten to bring bread.
v06: Jesus told them, “Be watchful. Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and Sadducees.”
v07: They discussed it among themselves: “We didn’t bring bread.”
v08: Jesus knew what they were saying. “You of little faith,” he said, “why are you discussing among yourselves that you didn’t bring bread?
v09: Do you still not understand? Don’t you remember the five loaves for the five thousand, and how many baskets you gathered?
v10: Or the seven loaves for the four thousand, and how many large baskets you gathered?
v11: How can you not understand that I wasn’t speaking to you about bread? Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and Sadducees.”
v12: Then they understood: he had told them to beware of the teaching of the Pharisees and Sadducees, not the leaven in bread.
</p>

## Peter names the Messiah

<p>
v13: When Jesus came to the region of Caesarea Philippi, he asked his disciples, “Who do people say the Son of Man is?”
v14: They answered, “Some say John the Baptist; others, Elijah; still others, Jeremiah or one of the prophets.”
v15: “But you,” he asked, “who do you say I am?”
v16: Simon Peter answered, “You are the Messiah, the Son of the living God.”
v17: Jesus answered him, “Blessed are you, Simon son of Jonah. Flesh and blood did not reveal this to you, but my Father in heaven.
v18: And I tell you: you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church. The gates of Hades will not overpower it.
v19: I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven. Whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you release on earth will be released in heaven.”
v20: Then he strictly instructed the disciples to tell no one that he was the Messiah.
</p>

## Jesus speaks of his death

<p>
v21: From then on Jesus began showing his disciples that he must go to Jerusalem, suffer much from the elders, chief priests, and scribes, be killed, and be raised on the third day.
v22: Peter took him aside and began rebuking him: “God forbid, Lord! This will never happen to you.”
v23: But he turned and told Peter, “Get behind me, Satan! You are a stumbling block to me, because your mind is on human concerns, not God’s.”
</p>

## Taking up the cross

<p>
v24: Then Jesus told his disciples, “If anyone wants to come after me, they must deny themselves, take up their cross, and follow me.
v25: For whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life because of me will find it.
v26: What good will it do a person to gain the whole world but forfeit their life? What can anyone give in exchange for their life?
v27: For the Son of Man is going to come in his Father’s glory with his angels. Then he will repay each person according to what that person has done.
v28: Truly I tell you, some standing here will certainly not taste death before they see the Son of Man coming in his kingdom.”
</p>

## Notes

v02–03: The weather sayings are printed with variant markers in the pinned source and are translated here; the markers are retained in the exact source record.

v18–19: Peter and rock form a wordplay. Church renders ekklesia, an assembly or gathered community. Binding and releasing remain images; the Greek future-perfect construction can also be rendered “will have been bound/released.”

v28: The saying’s timing is retained without identifying one particular later event as its fulfillment.

## Vocabulary

v18: **Gates of Hades**: An image joining the realm of the dead with its gates or power.

v24: **Cross**: An instrument of execution, used in the call to costly following.
