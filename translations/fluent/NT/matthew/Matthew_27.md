## Jesus Handed Over to Pilate

<p>
v01: Early in the morning, all the chief priests and elders of the people made a plan against Jesus to put him to death.
v02: They bound him, led him away, and handed him over to Pilate the governor.
</p>

## Judas Dies

<p>
v03: When Judas, the one who handed Jesus over, saw that Jesus had been condemned, he regretted what he had done and returned the thirty silver coins to the chief priests and elders,
v04: saying, “I sinned by handing over innocent blood.” They said, “What is that to us? You see to it.”
v05: He threw the silver coins into the temple, left, and went away and hanged himself.
v06: The chief priests took the silver coins and said, “It is not lawful to put them into the temple treasury, because they are blood money.”
v07: After consulting together, they used the money to buy the potter's field as a burial place for foreigners.
v08: Therefore that field has been called Field of Blood to this day.
v09: Then what was spoken through Jeremiah the prophet was fulfilled: “They took the thirty silver coins, the price of the one whose price had been set by some of the sons of Israel,
v10: and they gave them for the potter's field, as the Lord directed me.”
</p>

## Jesus Before Pilate

<p>
v11: Jesus stood before the governor, and the governor asked him, “Are you the king of the Jews?” Jesus said, “You say so.”
v12: When he was accused by the chief priests and elders, he gave no answer.
v13: Then Pilate said to him, “Do you not hear how many things they are testifying against you?”
v14: But Jesus did not answer him—not even one charge—so that the governor was greatly amazed.
</p>

## Barabbas or Jesus

<p>
v15: At the festival the governor was accustomed to release for the crowd one prisoner whom they wanted.
v16: At that time they had a notorious prisoner named Jesus Barabbas.
v17: So when they had gathered, Pilate asked them, “Whom do you want me to release for you: Jesus Barabbas or Jesus who is called Christ?”
v18: For he knew they had handed Jesus over because of envy.
v19: While Pilate was sitting on the judgment seat, his wife sent him a message: “Have nothing to do with that righteous man, because today I suffered greatly in a dream because of him.”
v20: But the chief priests and elders persuaded the crowds to ask for Barabbas and have Jesus killed.
v21: The governor asked them, “Which of the two do you want me to release for you?” They said, “Barabbas.”
v22: Pilate asked them, “What then should I do with Jesus who is called Christ?” They all said, “Let him be crucified!”
v23: He asked, “Why? What evil has he done?” But they shouted even louder, “Let him be crucified!”
</p>

<p>
v24: When Pilate saw that he was getting nowhere, but instead a riot was beginning, he took water, washed his hands before the crowd, and said, “I am innocent of this man's blood. You see to it.”
v25: All the people answered, “His blood be on us and on our children!”
v26: Then Pilate released Barabbas for them. After having Jesus flogged, he handed him over to be crucified.
</p>

## Jesus Mocked

<p>
v27: Then the governor's soldiers took Jesus into the governor's headquarters and gathered the whole cohort around him.
v28: They stripped him and put a scarlet cloak on him.
v29: They twisted together a crown of thorns, put it on his head, and placed a reed in his right hand. Kneeling before him, they mocked him: “Hail, king of the Jews!”
v30: They spit on him, took the reed, and kept striking him on the head.
v31: After mocking him, they took off the cloak, put his own clothes back on him, and led him away to crucify him.
</p>

## The Crucifixion

<p>
v32: As they went out, they found a man from Cyrene named Simon. They compelled him to carry Jesus' cross.
v33: When they came to a place called Golgotha, which means Place of a Skull,
v34: they gave Jesus wine mixed with gall to drink. After tasting it, he refused to drink.
v35: After crucifying him, they divided his clothes by casting lots.
v36: Then they sat down and kept watch over him there.
v37: Above his head they placed the written charge against him: “THIS IS JESUS, THE KING OF THE JEWS.”
v38: Then two robbers were crucified with him, one on his right and one on his left.
</p>

<p>
v39: Those passing by reviled him, shaking their heads
v40: and saying, “You who would destroy the temple and build it in three days, save yourself! If you are God's Son, come down from the cross!”
v41: In the same way, the chief priests with the scribes and elders mocked him:
v42: “He saved others, but he cannot save himself! He is Israel's king. Let him come down now from the cross, and we will trust in him.
v43: He trusted in God; let God rescue him now, if God wants him. For he said, ‘I am God's Son.’”
v44: In the same way, the robbers crucified with him also insulted him.
</p>

## Jesus Dies

<p>
v45: From the sixth hour until the ninth hour, darkness came over all the land.
v46: About the ninth hour Jesus cried out with a loud voice, “Eli, Eli, lema sabachthani?” that is, “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?”
v47: When some of those standing there heard this, they said, “This man is calling Elijah.”
v48: Immediately one of them ran, took a sponge, filled it with sour wine, put it on a reed, and gave it to him to drink.
v49: But the others said, “Leave him alone. Let us see whether Elijah comes to save him.”
v50: Jesus cried out again with a loud voice and gave up his spirit.
</p>

<p>
v51: And look—the curtain of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom. The earth shook, and the rocks split.
v52: The tombs were opened, and many bodies of the holy ones who had fallen asleep were raised.
v53: After Jesus' resurrection, they came out of the tombs, entered the holy city, and appeared to many.
v54: When the centurion and those with him guarding Jesus saw the earthquake and what happened, they were terrified and said, “Truly this man was God's Son.”
</p>

<p>
v55: Many women were there watching from a distance. They had followed Jesus from Galilee and served him.
v56: Among them were Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James and Joseph, and the mother of the sons of Zebedee.
</p>

## Jesus Is Buried

<p>
v57: When evening came, a rich man from Arimathea named Joseph came; he too had become a disciple of Jesus.
v58: He went to Pilate and asked for Jesus' body. Then Pilate ordered it to be given to him.
v59: Joseph took the body, wrapped it in a clean linen cloth,
v60: and placed it in his own new tomb, which he had cut out of rock. He rolled a large stone against the entrance of the tomb and went away.
v61: Mary Magdalene and the other Mary were there, sitting opposite the tomb.
</p>

## The Guard at the Tomb

<p>
v62: The next day, which was after the day of Preparation, the chief priests and Pharisees gathered before Pilate.
v63: They said, “Sir, we remember that while that deceiver was still alive he said, ‘After three days I will be raised.’
v64: Therefore order the tomb to be made secure until the third day, or his disciples may come, steal him away, and tell the people, ‘He was raised from the dead.’ Then the last deception will be worse than the first.”
v65: Pilate told them, “You have a guard. Go, make it as secure as you know how.”
v66: So they went and made the tomb secure by sealing the stone and posting the guard.
</p>

## Notes

v03: `metamelomai` describes regret/change of mind, not Matthew's usual `metanoeō` repentance verb. Fluent preserves the distinction.

v09–10: Matthew attributes the citation to Jeremiah, though the wording strongly echoes Zechariah 11 with additional scriptural associations. Fluent does not “correct” Matthew's attribution.

v16–17: SBLGNT reads “Jesus Barabbas,” an ancient and striking textual reading. Many manuscripts omit “Jesus” before Barabbas, likely because scribes were uncomfortable with the criminal sharing Jesus' name.

v19: Pilate's wife's dream is unique to Matthew and continues the Gospel's dream motif from the infancy narrative.

v24–25: Pilate's handwashing does not remove his narrative responsibility: he still orders the release and execution. Verse 25 has a long and tragic history of antisemitic misuse; the scene concerns the particular crowd in Matthew's passion narrative and must not be weaponized against Jewish people collectively.

v35: SBLGNT does not include the longer fulfillment quotation often added to v35 in later manuscripts.

v49: SBLGNT's main text does not include the variant sentence about another person piercing Jesus' side before his death. The important variant is recorded in the textual ledger.

v51–53: The opened tombs and raised holy ones are unique to Matthew. The strange temporal sequence is preserved rather than rationalized.

v62–66: The tomb-guard scene is also distinctive to Matthew and sets up the counter-story in 28:11–15.
