## Jesus Handed Over to Pilate

<p>
v01: Early in the morning, all the chief priests and elders of the people made plans to put Jesus to death.
v02: They bound him, led him away, and handed him over to Pilate the governor.
</p>

## Judas Dies

<p>
v03: When Judas, who had handed him over, saw that Jesus was condemned, he was filled with remorse. He returned the thirty silver coins to the chief priests and elders.
v04: “I have sinned,” he said. “I have handed over innocent blood.” They replied, “What is that to us? That is your concern.”
v05: He threw the silver coins into the sanctuary and left. Then he went away and hanged himself.
v06: The chief priests picked up the coins. “It is not lawful to put these into the treasury,” they said, “because they are blood money.”
v07: After discussing it, they used the money to buy the potter’s field as a burial place for foreigners.
v08: That is why the field has been called the Field of Blood to this day.
v09: Then what was spoken through the prophet Jeremiah was fulfilled:
“They took the thirty silver coins,
the price set on him by some of the people of Israel,
v10: and gave them for the potter’s field,
as the Lord directed me.”
</p>

## Jesus Before Pilate

<p>
v11: Jesus stood before the governor, who asked him, “Are you the king of the Jews?” Jesus answered, “You say so.”
v12: When the chief priests and elders accused him, he gave no answer.
v13: Then Pilate asked him, “Do you not hear how much they are testifying against you?”
v14: But Jesus did not answer him even a single word, to the governor’s great amazement.
</p>

## Barabbas or Jesus

<p>
v15: At the festival, the governor customarily released one prisoner chosen by the crowd.
v16: At that time they had a notorious prisoner called Jesus Barabbas.
v17: So when the crowd gathered, Pilate asked, “Whom do you want me to release for you: Jesus Barabbas, or Jesus who is called the Christ?”
v18: For he knew they had handed Jesus over out of envy.
v19: While Pilate was sitting on the judgment seat, his wife sent him a message: “Have nothing to do with that righteous man. I have suffered greatly today in a dream because of him.”
v20: But the chief priests and elders persuaded the crowds to ask for Barabbas and to have Jesus killed.
v21: The governor asked them, “Which of the two do you want me to release for you?” “Barabbas,” they replied.
v22: Pilate asked, “Then what should I do with Jesus who is called the Christ?” They all said, “Let him be crucified!”
v23: “Why?” he asked. “What evil has he done?” But they shouted all the louder, “Let him be crucified!”
</p>

<p>
v24: Pilate saw that he was getting nowhere and that a riot was beginning. He took water and washed his hands in front of the crowd. “I am innocent of this blood,” he said. “That is your concern.”
v25: All the people answered, “His blood be on us and on our children!”
v26: Then he released Barabbas for them. After having Jesus flogged, he handed him over to be crucified.
</p>

## Jesus Mocked

<p>
v27: The governor’s soldiers took Jesus into the governor’s headquarters and gathered the whole cohort around him.
v28: They stripped him and put a scarlet cloak on him.
v29: They wove a crown of thorns, placed it on his head, and put a reed in his right hand. Kneeling before him, they mocked him: “Hail, king of the Jews!”
v30: They spat on him, took the reed, and struck him on the head.
v31: When they had finished mocking him, they removed the cloak, dressed him in his own clothes, and led him away to crucify him.
</p>

## The Crucifixion

<p>
v32: As they went out, they came upon a man from Cyrene named Simon and forced him to carry Jesus’ cross.
v33: They came to a place called Golgotha, which means “Place of a Skull.”
v34: They offered Jesus wine mixed with gall. After tasting it, he refused to drink.
v35: After crucifying him, they divided his clothes by casting lots.
v36: Then they sat down and kept watch over him there.
v37: Above his head they placed the written charge against him: “This is Jesus, the king of the Jews.”
v38: Two bandits were crucified with him, one on his right and one on his left.
</p>

<p>
v39: Those passing by insulted him, shaking their heads
v40: and saying, “You who would destroy the sanctuary and rebuild it in three days, save yourself! If you are God’s Son, come down from the cross!”
v41: Likewise the chief priests, with the scribes and elders, mocked him, saying,
v42: “He saved others; he cannot save himself! He is the king of Israel. Let him come down from the cross now, and we will believe in him.
v43: He trusts in God. Let God rescue him now, if he wants him, for he said, ‘I am God’s Son.’”
v44: The bandits crucified with him also insulted him in the same way.
</p>

## Jesus Dies

<p>
v45: From noon until three in the afternoon, darkness covered the whole land.
v46: About three in the afternoon, Jesus cried out loudly, “Eli, Eli, lema sabachthani?” That means, “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?”
v47: Some of those standing there heard him and said, “This man is calling Elijah.”
v48: Immediately one of them ran, took a sponge, filled it with sour wine, put it on a reed, and offered him a drink.
v49: The others said, “Leave him alone. Let us see whether Elijah comes to save him.”
v50: Jesus cried out loudly once more and gave up his spirit.
</p>

<p>
v51: Then the sanctuary curtain was torn in two from top to bottom. The earth shook, and the rocks split.
v52: The tombs were opened, and many bodies of the holy people who had fallen asleep were raised.
v53: After Jesus’ resurrection, they came out of the tombs, entered the holy city, and appeared to many.
v54: When the centurion and those guarding Jesus with him saw the earthquake and what had happened, they were terrified. “This man truly was God’s Son!” they said.
</p>

<p>
v55: Many women were there, watching from a distance. They had followed Jesus from Galilee and cared for him.
v56: Among them were Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James and Joseph, and the mother of Zebedee’s sons.
</p>

## Jesus Is Buried

<p>
v57: When evening came, a rich man from Arimathea named Joseph arrived. He too had become a disciple of Jesus.
v58: He went to Pilate and asked for Jesus’ body. Pilate ordered it to be given to him.
v59: Joseph took the body and wrapped it in a clean linen cloth.
v60: He laid it in his own new tomb, which he had cut into the rock. Then he rolled a large stone across the entrance and left.
v61: Mary Magdalene and the other Mary were there, sitting opposite the tomb.
</p>

## The Guard at the Tomb

<p>
v62: The next day, the day after Preparation, the chief priests and Pharisees gathered before Pilate.
v63: “Sir,” they said, “we remember that while that deceiver was still alive, he said, ‘After three days I will be raised.’
v64: So order the tomb to be secured until the third day. Otherwise his disciples may come and steal him, then tell the people, ‘He has been raised from the dead.’ This last deception would be worse than the first.”
v65: Pilate told them, “You have a guard. Go and secure it as best you know how.”
v66: They went and secured the tomb, sealing the stone and posting the guard.
</p>

## Notes

v03: The Greek describes remorse or a change of mind. It uses a different verb from Matthew’s usual verb for repentance.

v09–10: Matthew attributes the quotation to Jeremiah. Its wording closely recalls Zechariah 11:12–13, alongside imagery associated with Jeremiah; the attribution is preserved.

v16–17: The pinned Greek includes “Jesus” in Barabbas’s name. Many manuscripts omit it.

v24–26: The blood declaration is spoken by the crowd in this scene. It does not make Jewish people across later generations its speakers. Pilate’s subsequent order to flog and crucify Jesus remains explicit.

v35: The longer fulfillment quotation found in some manuscripts is absent from the pinned Greek here.

v45: Greek “sixth hour” and “ninth hour” are rendered approximately as noon and three in the afternoon. “Land” can also mean “earth.”

v46: Jesus’ cry quotes Psalm 22:1. Matthew preserves the words and supplies their meaning.

v49: Some manuscripts add a sentence about piercing Jesus’ side. The pinned Greek does not include it here.

v52–53: The account places the appearance of the raised holy people after Jesus’ resurrection. It gives no further account of their lives.

v65: “You have a guard” can also be read as the instruction “Take a guard.”

## Vocabulary

v06: **Blood money**: Payment associated with bringing about a person’s death.

v27: **Cohort**: A Roman military unit; its actual size could vary.

v34: **Gall**: A bitter substance. Matthew does not use Mark’s word “myrrh.”

v46: **Forsaken**: Abandoned or left behind; the cry’s force is retained.

v52: **Fallen asleep**: A familiar image for death.

v54: **Centurion**: A Roman officer commanding a unit conventionally associated with a hundred soldiers.

v62: **Preparation**: The day of preparation before the Sabbath.
