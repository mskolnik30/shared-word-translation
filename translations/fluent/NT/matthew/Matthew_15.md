## Tradition and the Command of God

<p>
v01: Then Pharisees and scribes came to Jesus from Jerusalem and asked,
v02: “Why do your disciples break the tradition of the elders? They do not wash their hands when they eat bread.”
v03: Jesus answered them, “And why do you break God's commandment because of your tradition?
v04: For God said, ‘Honor your father and your mother,’ and, ‘Whoever speaks evil of father or mother must surely die.’
v05: But you say, ‘If anyone tells father or mother, “Whatever benefit you might have received from me is a gift devoted to God,”
v06: that person does not need to honor their father.’ So you have nullified God's word because of your tradition.
v07: Hypocrites! Isaiah prophesied rightly about you:
v08: ‘This people honors me with their lips,
but their heart is far from me.
v09: They worship me in vain,
teaching human commands as doctrines.’”
</p>

## What Defiles a Person

<p>
v10: Jesus called the crowd and said to them, “Listen and understand:
v11: it is not what enters the mouth that defiles a person, but what comes out of the mouth—this defiles a person.”
v12: Then the disciples came and said to him, “Do you know that the Pharisees were offended when they heard this saying?”
v13: He answered, “Every plant that my heavenly Father did not plant will be uprooted.
v14: Leave them. They are blind guides of the blind. If one blind person guides another, both will fall into a pit.”
v15: Peter said to him, “Explain this parable to us.”
v16: Jesus said, “Are you still without understanding?
v17: Do you not see that whatever enters the mouth goes into the stomach and then passes into the latrine?
v18: But what comes out of the mouth comes from the heart, and these things defile a person.
v19: For from the heart come evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, sexual immoralities, thefts, false testimonies, and slanders.
v20: These are the things that defile a person. But eating with unwashed hands does not defile a person.”
</p>

## A Canaanite Woman's Trust

<p>
v21: Jesus left there and withdrew to the region of Tyre and Sidon.
v22: And look—a Canaanite woman from that region came out and cried, “Have mercy on me, Lord, Son of David! My daughter is severely tormented by a demon.”
v23: But he did not answer her a word. His disciples came and urged him, “Send her away, because she keeps crying out after us.”
v24: He answered, “I was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.”
v25: But she came, bowed before him, and said, “Lord, help me.”
v26: He answered, “It is not right to take the children's bread and throw it to the dogs.”
v27: She said, “Yes, Lord, yet even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their masters' table.”
v28: Then Jesus answered her, “Woman, great is your faith! Let it happen for you as you desire.” And her daughter was healed from that hour.
</p>

## Jesus Heals Many

<p>
v29: Jesus left there, went along the Sea of Galilee, went up the mountain, and sat down.
v30: Large crowds came to him, bringing those who were lame, blind, disabled, unable to speak, and many others. They laid them at his feet, and he healed them.
v31: The crowd was amazed when they saw those unable to speak speaking, disabled people made whole, the lame walking, and the blind seeing. And they glorified the God of Israel.
</p>

## Jesus Feeds Four Thousand

<p>
v32: Jesus called his disciples and said, “I have compassion on the crowd, because they have stayed with me three days now and have nothing to eat. I do not want to send them away hungry, or they may collapse on the way.”
v33: The disciples asked him, “Where in this deserted place could we get enough bread to feed such a large crowd?”
v34: Jesus asked them, “How many loaves do you have?” They said, “Seven, and a few small fish.”
v35: He told the crowd to sit down on the ground.
v36: He took the seven loaves and the fish, gave thanks, broke them, and kept giving them to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the crowds.
v37: Everyone ate and was satisfied. They picked up seven baskets full of the broken pieces left over.
v38: Those who ate were four thousand men, besides women and children.
v39: After dismissing the crowds, Jesus got into the boat and went to the region of Magadan.
</p>

## Notes

v02–06: As in Mark, the dispute concerns inherited tradition when it functions to override concrete obligations of God's command.

v11,18–20: Matthew emphasizes the mouth because speech expresses what comes from the heart; the defilement teaching is moral and relational rather than merely ritual.

v14: “Blind guides” is a severe polemical image. Fluent does not generalize the saying beyond the opponents in the scene.

v22: Matthew calls the woman “Canaanite,” an archaic scriptural identity marker, whereas Mark calls her Greek/Syrophoenician. The difference is preserved.

v24: Jesus' statement about Israel's lost sheep echoes the limited mission of 10:5–6 and creates the tension the woman's response engages.

v26–28: The exchange retains its sharpness. Fluent does not soften “dogs” into a different animal or insert motives Jesus does not state.

v28: Matthew explicitly praises the woman's “great faith,” contrasting with repeated “little faith” language among disciples.

v32–38: The second feeding remains distinct rather than being harmonized with the five-thousand scene.
