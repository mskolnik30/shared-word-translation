## Tradition and God’s command

<p>
v01: Then Pharisees and scribes came to Jesus from Jerusalem and asked,
v02: “Why do your disciples break the tradition of the elders? They don’t wash their hands when they eat.”
v03: He answered them, “Why do you break God’s command because of your tradition?
v04: For God said, ‘Honor your father and mother,’ and, ‘Whoever curses father or mother must be put to death.’
v05: But you say that anyone may tell father or mother, ‘Whatever help you might have received from me is a gift devoted to God,’
v06: and need not honor their father. So you have nullified God’s word because of your tradition.
v07: Hypocrites! Isaiah prophesied well about you when he said,
v08: ‘This people honors me with their lips,
but their heart is far from me.
v09: Their worship of me is empty,
for they teach human commands as doctrines.’”
</p>

## What defiles a person?

<p>
v10: He called the crowd to him and said, “Listen and understand.
v11: What goes into the mouth does not defile a person. What comes out of the mouth is what defiles a person.”
v12: Then the disciples came and asked him, “Do you know the Pharisees took offense when they heard that?”
v13: He answered, “Every plant my heavenly Father did not plant will be uprooted.
v14: Leave them alone. They are blind guides of the blind. If one blind person leads another, both will fall into a pit.”
v15: Peter said to him, “Explain this parable to us.”
v16: He answered, “Are you still without understanding too?
v17: Don’t you understand that everything entering the mouth goes into the stomach and is expelled into the latrine?
v18: But what comes out of the mouth comes from the heart, and that is what defiles a person.
v19: For from the heart come evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, sexual immoralities, thefts, false testimonies, and slanders.
v20: These are what defile a person. Eating with unwashed hands does not defile a person.”
</p>

## A Canaanite woman’s faith

<p>
v21: Jesus left that place and withdrew to the region of Tyre and Sidon.
v22: A Canaanite woman from that region came out, crying, “Have mercy on me, Lord, Son of David! My daughter is suffering terribly from a demon.”
v23: He did not answer her a word. His disciples came and urged him, “Send her away; she keeps shouting after us.”
v24: He answered, “I was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.”
v25: But she came and bowed before him. “Lord, help me,” she said.
v26: He answered, “It is not right to take the children’s bread and throw it to the little dogs.”
v27: “Yes, Lord,” she said, “for even the little dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their masters’ table.”
v28: Then Jesus answered her, “Woman, your faith is great! Let it happen for you as you wish.” And her daughter was healed from that moment.
</p>

## Healing beside the sea

<p>
v29: Jesus moved on from there and came beside the Sea of Galilee. He went up the mountain and sat down.
v30: Large crowds came to him, bringing people unable to speak, blind people, lame people, people with impaired limbs, and many others. They laid them at his feet, and he healed them.
v31: The crowd was amazed to see those unable to speak speaking, impaired limbs made healthy, the lame walking, and the blind seeing. They glorified the God of Israel.
</p>

## Four thousand fed

<p>
v32: Jesus called his disciples to him. “I have compassion for the crowd,” he said. “They have stayed with me for three days now and have nothing to eat. I don’t want to send them away hungry; they might collapse on the road.”
v33: The disciples asked him, “Where could we find enough bread in this wilderness to satisfy a crowd this large?”
v34: Jesus asked them, “How many loaves do you have?” “Seven,” they said, “and a few small fish.”
v35: He instructed the crowd to recline on the ground.
v36: He took the seven loaves and the fish, gave thanks, broke them, and kept giving them to the disciples. The disciples gave them to the crowds.
v37: Everyone ate and was satisfied. They gathered the leftover pieces—seven full large baskets.
v38: Four thousand men ate, besides women and children.
v39: After dismissing the crowds, he got into the boat and came to the region of Magadan.
</p>

## Notes

v02–20: The dispute concerns ritual handwashing and defilement, not a medical claim about hygiene. Matthew’s argument returns to unwashed hands in verse 20.

v26–28: The “little dogs” comparison remains uncomfortable; the diminutive does not erase its hierarchy. The woman answers within that image, and Jesus calls her faith great.

v37–39: The seven large baskets and four thousand men distinguish this feeding from chapter 14. The pinned destination is Magadan.

## Vocabulary

v11: **Defile**: Make ritually or morally unclean, with the meaning developed in Jesus’ explanation.

v22: **Canaanite**: Matthew’s designation for the woman from the Tyre and Sidon region.
