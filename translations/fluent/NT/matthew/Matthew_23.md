## Teaching and conduct

<p>
v01: Then Jesus spoke to the crowds and to his disciples:
v02: “The scribes and Pharisees have taken their seat in Moses’s chair.
v03: So do and observe everything they tell you, but do not act as they act. They speak but do not do.
v04: They tie up heavy loads and put them on people’s shoulders, but will not move them with their own finger.
v05: They do everything to be seen by people. They make their phylacteries broad and their garment fringes long.
v06: They love the place of honor at dinners and the front seats in synagogues,
v07: greetings in the marketplaces, and being called ‘Rabbi’ by people.
</p>

## Greatness through humility

<p>
v08: But you must not be called ‘Rabbi,’ for you have one teacher, and you are all brothers and sisters.
v09: And call no one on earth your father, for you have one Father, the heavenly one.
v10: Nor are you to be called instructors, for you have one instructor, the Messiah.
v11: The greatest among you will be your servant.
v12: Whoever exalts themselves will be humbled, and whoever humbles themselves will be exalted.
</p>

## Woes against hypocrisy

<p>
v13: “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! You shut the kingdom of heaven in people’s faces. You do not enter yourselves, and you do not let those trying to enter go in.
v15: “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! You travel over sea and land to make one convert, and when you do, you make that person twice as much a child of Gehenna as yourselves.
</p>

<p>
v16: “Woe to you, blind guides! You say, ‘If anyone swears by the sanctuary, it means nothing; but if anyone swears by the sanctuary’s gold, the oath is binding.’
v17: Blind fools! Which is greater: the gold, or the sanctuary that made the gold holy?
v18: You also say, ‘If anyone swears by the altar, it means nothing; but if anyone swears by the gift on it, the oath is binding.’
v19: Blind people! Which is greater: the gift, or the altar that makes the gift holy?
v20: So whoever swears by the altar swears by it and by everything on it.
v21: Whoever swears by the sanctuary swears by it and by the one who dwells in it.
v22: And whoever swears by heaven swears by God’s throne and by the one seated on it.
</p>

<p>
v23: “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! You tithe mint, dill, and cumin, but have neglected the weightier matters of the Law: justice, mercy, and faithfulness. You should have practiced these without neglecting the others.
v24: Blind guides! You strain out a gnat but swallow a camel.
</p>

<p>
v25: “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! You clean the outside of the cup and dish, but inside they are full of plunder and self-indulgence.
v26: Blind Pharisee! First clean the inside of the cup and dish, so that its outside may become clean too.
</p>

<p>
v27: “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! You are like whitewashed tombs, beautiful on the outside but full of dead people’s bones and every kind of uncleanness within.
v28: In the same way, you appear righteous to people on the outside, but inside you are full of hypocrisy and lawlessness.
</p>

## The prophets’ blood

<p>
v29: “Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! You build the prophets’ tombs and decorate the monuments of the righteous,
v30: and say, ‘If we had lived in our ancestors’ days, we would not have shared with them in shedding the prophets’ blood.’
v31: So you testify against yourselves that you are descendants of those who murdered the prophets.
v32: You too—fill up the measure of your ancestors!
v33: Snakes! Brood of vipers! How will you escape the judgment of Gehenna?
</p>

<p>
v34: That is why I am sending you prophets, wise people, and scribes. Some you will kill and crucify; others you will flog in your synagogues and persecute from town to town.
v35: So all the righteous blood shed on earth will come upon you, from the blood of righteous Abel to the blood of Zechariah son of Berechiah, whom you murdered between the sanctuary and the altar.
v36: Truly I tell you, all these things will come upon this generation.
</p>

## Lament over Jerusalem

<p>
v37: “Jerusalem, Jerusalem! You kill the prophets and stone those sent to you. How often I wanted to gather your children as a hen gathers her chicks beneath her wings, but you were unwilling!
v38: Look, your house is left to you deserted.
v39: For I tell you, from now on you will not see me until you say, ‘Blessed is the one who comes in the Lord’s name.’”
</p>

## Notes

v02–03: The positive instruction concerning teaching and the criticism of conduct are both retained. The chapter’s sharp polemic is directed at the named opponents within the story, not a license to generalize about all Jews.

v14,23: The pinned source has no verse 14, so the numbering jumps from 13 to 15. Faithfulness in verse 23 renders pistis in its ethical list with justice and mercy.

v35,37–38: The text names Zechariah son of Berechiah; the difficult identification is not silently changed. The hen image is maternal, and “your house” is left without a supplied referent.

## Vocabulary

v05: **Phylacteries**: Small containers for scriptural passages worn in religious observance; garment fringes are a separate practice.

v23: **Tithe**: Give a tenth; the critique concerns neglected weightier matters while retaining the smaller obligation.
