## Ten Bridesmaids

<p>
v01: “Then the kingdom of heaven will be like ten bridesmaids who took their lamps and went out to meet the bridegroom.
v02: Five were foolish and five were wise.
v03: The foolish ones brought their lamps but no oil with them.
v04: The wise ones brought jars of oil along with their lamps.
v05: The bridegroom was delayed, and they all grew drowsy and fell asleep.
v06: At midnight a shout rang out: ‘Here is the bridegroom! Come out to meet him!’
v07: Then all the bridesmaids got up and put their lamps in order.
v08: The foolish ones said to the wise, ‘Give us some of your oil; our lamps are going out.’
v09: But the wise ones answered, ‘There may not be enough for all of us. Go to the sellers and buy some for yourselves.’
v10: While they were away buying oil, the bridegroom arrived. Those who were ready went in with him to the wedding banquet, and the door was shut.
v11: Later the other bridesmaids came too. ‘Lord, lord,’ they said, ‘open the door for us!’
v12: But he replied, ‘I tell you the truth: I do not know you.’
v13: “So stay awake, because you know neither the day nor the hour.
</p>

## Entrusted Talents

<p>
v14: “It is also like a man about to travel. He called his servants and entrusted his property to them.
v15: He gave one five talents, another two, and another one, each according to his ability. Then he left. At once,
v16: the servant who received five talents went to work with them and gained five more.
v17: In the same way, the servant with two gained two more.
v18: But the servant who received one went off, dug a hole, and hid his master’s money in the ground.
v19: After a long time, the servants’ master returned and settled accounts with them.
v20: The servant who had received five talents came forward with five more. ‘Master,’ he said, ‘you entrusted five talents to me. Look, I have gained five more.’
v21: His master said, ‘Well done, good and faithful servant! You have been faithful with a little; I will put you in charge of much. Come and share your master’s joy.’
v22: The servant with two talents also came forward. ‘Master,’ he said, ‘you entrusted two talents to me. Look, I have gained two more.’
v23: His master said, ‘Well done, good and faithful servant! You have been faithful with a little; I will put you in charge of much. Come and share your master’s joy.’
v24: Then the servant who had received one talent came forward. ‘Master,’ he said, ‘I knew you were a hard man. You reap where you have not sown and gather where you have not scattered seed.
v25: I was afraid, so I went and hid your talent in the ground. Here, you have what is yours.’
v26: His master replied, ‘You wicked, lazy servant! You knew I reap where I have not sown and gather where I have not scattered seed?
v27: Then you should have put my money with the bankers, so that when I returned I could have received it with interest.
v28: So take the talent from him and give it to the one who has ten talents.
v29: For everyone who has will be given more and will have plenty. But whoever does not have will lose even what they have.
v30: Throw this useless servant into the outer darkness. There will be weeping and grinding of teeth.’
</p>

## The Son of Man Judges the Nations

<p>
v31: “When the Son of Man comes in his glory with all the angels, he will sit on his glorious throne.
v32: All the nations will be gathered before him. He will separate people from one another as a shepherd separates sheep from goats.
v33: He will place the sheep on his right and the goats on his left.
v34: Then the king will say to those on his right, ‘Come, you who are blessed by my Father. Inherit the kingdom prepared for you since the foundation of the world.
v35: For I was hungry and you gave me food. I was thirsty and you gave me something to drink. I was a stranger and you welcomed me.
v36: I was naked and you clothed me. I was sick and you cared for me. I was in prison and you came to me.’
v37: Then the righteous will ask, ‘Lord, when did we see you hungry and feed you, or thirsty and give you something to drink?
v38: When did we see you a stranger and welcome you, or naked and clothe you?
v39: When did we see you sick or in prison and come to you?’
v40: The king will answer, ‘I tell you the truth: whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers and sisters of mine, you did for me.’
</p>

<p>
v41: “Then he will say to those on his left, ‘Go away from me, you who are cursed, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels.
v42: For I was hungry and you gave me no food. I was thirsty and you gave me nothing to drink.
v43: I was a stranger and you did not welcome me. I was naked and you did not clothe me. I was sick and in prison and you did not care for me.’
v44: They too will ask, ‘Lord, when did we see you hungry or thirsty, a stranger or naked, sick or in prison, and fail to serve you?’
v45: He will answer, ‘I tell you the truth: whatever you failed to do for one of the least of these, you failed to do for me.’
v46: And they will go away into eternal punishment, but the righteous into eternal life.”
</p>

## Notes

v01–13: All ten bridesmaids fall asleep. The story distinguishes their readiness when the bridegroom arrives.

v15–16: “At once” stands at the verse boundary in the pinned Greek. Here it describes the first servant going to work; it can also be connected with the master’s departure.

v24–26: The description of the master as harsh begins in the servant’s speech. Verse 26 repeats it as a question.

v40: “These brothers and sisters of mine” may refer specifically to Jesus’ disciples or more broadly to vulnerable people. The phrase is retained without substituting either interpretation.

v46: The same Greek adjective, aiōnios, modifies both punishment and life.

## Vocabulary

v01: **Bridesmaids**: Greek parthenoi: virgins or unmarried young women; here participants awaiting the bridegroom.

v01: **Lamps**: Greek lampades may denote lamps or torches; the story requires a supply of oil.

v15: **Talent**: A large weight used as a monetary unit, not an innate skill. Its value depended on the metal and local standard.

v21: **Faithful**: Greek pistos: faithful or trustworthy.

v32: **Nations**: Greek ethnē: nations or peoples.

v46: **Eternal**: Greek aiōnios: eternal, or pertaining to an age; the parallel in this verse is preserved.
