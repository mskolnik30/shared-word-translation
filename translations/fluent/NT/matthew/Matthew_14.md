## John is killed

<p>
v01: At that time Herod the tetrarch heard the reports about Jesus.
v02: He told his servants, “This is John the Baptist. He has been raised from the dead, and that is why miraculous powers are at work in him.”
v03: Herod had arrested John, bound him, and put him in prison because of Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip.
v04: For John had been telling him, “You are not permitted to have her.”
v05: Herod wanted to kill him but feared the crowd, because they regarded John as a prophet.
v06: At Herod’s birthday celebration, Herodias’s daughter danced before the company and pleased Herod.
v07: So he promised under oath to give her whatever she asked.
v08: Prompted by her mother, she said, “Give me John the Baptist’s head here on a platter.”
v09: The king was distressed, but because of his oaths and his dinner guests, he ordered it to be given.
v10: He sent orders and had John beheaded in prison.
v11: John’s head was brought on a platter and given to the girl, and she carried it to her mother.
v12: John’s disciples came, took the body, and buried him. Then they went and told Jesus.
</p>

## Five thousand fed

<p>
v13: When Jesus heard, he withdrew by boat to a deserted place to be alone. The crowds heard about it and followed him on foot from the towns.
v14: When he came ashore, he saw a large crowd. Moved with compassion for them, he healed their sick.
v15: When evening came, the disciples approached him. “This is a deserted place, and it is already late,” they said. “Send the crowds away so they can go to the villages and buy food for themselves.”
v16: Jesus told them, “They don’t need to go away. You give them something to eat.”
v17: They answered, “All we have here are five loaves and two fish.”
v18: “Bring them here to me,” he said.
v19: He told the crowds to recline on the grass. Taking the five loaves and two fish, he looked up to heaven and spoke a blessing. He broke the loaves and gave them to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the crowds.
v20: Everyone ate and was satisfied. They gathered the leftover pieces—twelve full baskets.
v21: About five thousand men ate, besides women and children.
</p>

## Jesus walks on the sea

<p>
v22: Immediately he made the disciples get into the boat and go ahead of him to the other side while he dismissed the crowds.
v23: After dismissing the crowds, he went up the mountain alone to pray. When evening came, he was there by himself.
v24: The boat was already many stadia from land, battered by waves because the wind was against it.
v25: In the fourth watch of the night, he came toward them, walking on the sea.
v26: When the disciples saw him walking on the sea, they were terrified. “It’s a ghost!” they said, and cried out in fear.
v27: Jesus spoke to them at once: “Take courage! It is I. Do not be afraid.”
v28: Peter answered him, “Lord, if it is you, command me to come to you on the water.”
v29: “Come,” he said. Peter climbed out of the boat, walked on the water, and came toward Jesus.
v30: But when he saw the strong wind, he became afraid. Beginning to sink, he cried, “Lord, save me!”
v31: Jesus immediately reached out his hand and caught him. “You of little faith,” he said, “why did you doubt?”
v32: When they climbed into the boat, the wind died down.
v33: Those in the boat worshiped him, saying, “Truly you are God’s Son.”
</p>

## Healing at Gennesaret

<p>
v34: They crossed over and came ashore at Gennesaret.
v35: The men of that place recognized him and sent word throughout the surrounding region. They brought him everyone who was sick
v36: and begged him to let them touch just the fringe of his garment. Everyone who touched it was made well.
</p>

## Notes

v21,24–25: Five thousand counts men, with women and children additional. The distance is many stadia; the fourth watch is the final portion of the night.

v27: “It is I” translates ego eimi. The words identify the speaker; possible further resonances are not forced into the translation.

## Vocabulary

v01: **Tetrarch**: A ruler of a territorial division; the narrative also calls Herod king in verse 9.

v24: **Stadion**: An ancient distance unit of roughly 185 meters; the plural is stadia.
