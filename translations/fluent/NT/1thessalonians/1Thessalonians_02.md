## Our Coming to You Was Not Empty

<p>
v01: You yourselves know, brothers and sisters, that our coming to you was not empty.
v02: Although we had already suffered and been mistreated in Philippi, as you know, our God gave us courage to speak his good news to you amid great struggle.
v03: For our appeal does not spring from error, impurity or deceit.
v04: Instead, just as God approved us to be entrusted with the good news, so we speak—not to please people, but God, who tests our hearts.
v05: For we never came with flattering speech, as you know, or with a pretext for greed—God is our witness.
v06: Nor did we seek glory from people, whether from you or from others.
v07: Though we could have asserted our weight as Christ's apostles, we became gentle among you, like a nursing mother tenderly caring for her own children.
v08: Because we yearned for you, we were pleased to share with you not only God's good news but our own lives as well, since you had become dear to us.
</p>

## Working Night and Day

<p>
v09: You remember our labor and hardship, brothers and sisters. Working night and day so as not to burden any of you, we proclaimed God's good news to you.
v10: You are witnesses, and so is God, of how holy, just and blameless our conduct was toward you who believe.
v11: You know that we treated each one of you as a father treats his own children:
v12: encouraging, comforting and urging you to walk worthy of God, who calls you into his own kingdom and glory.
</p>

## You Received God’s Word as God’s Word

<p>
v13: We also thank God without ceasing because, when you received God's word that you heard from us, you accepted it not as a human word but as what it truly is, God's word, which is also at work in you who believe.
v14: For you, brothers and sisters, became imitators of God's churches in Judea that are in Christ Jesus: you suffered the same things from your own compatriots as they did from the Judeans
v15: who killed both the Lord Jesus and the prophets and drove us out. They displease God and oppose everyone,
v16: hindering us from speaking to the nations so that they may be saved. In this way they always fill up their sins. But wrath has overtaken them at last.
</p>

## Our Longing to See You

<p>
v17: But when we were orphaned from you, brothers and sisters, for a short time—in person, not in heart—we made every effort, with great longing, to see you face to face.
v18: We wanted to come to you—I, Paul, tried again and again—but Satan blocked us.
v19: For who is our hope, joy or crown of boasting before our Lord Jesus at his coming? Is it not you?
v20: Indeed, you are our glory and joy.
</p>

## Notes

v07: The pinned reading is ‘gentle’ and the following comparison is explicitly to a nursing mother.

v14–16: This severe first-century polemic is tied to named actions and Judean opponents; it must not be generalized to Jewish people.
