## Greeting

<p>
v01: Paul, an apostle—not from human beings or through a human being, but through Jesus Christ and God the Father who raised him from the dead—
v02: and all the brothers and sisters with me, to the churches of Galatia:
v03: Grace to you and peace from God our Father and our Lord Jesus Christ,
v04: who gave himself for our sins to rescue us from the present evil age, according to the will of our God and Father,
v05: to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen.
</p>

## No Other Good News

<p>
v06: I am amazed that you are turning so quickly from the one who called you by Christ’s grace to a different good news—
v07: not that there is another one. Rather, some people are troubling you and want to distort the good news of Christ.
v08: But even if we or an angel from heaven should announce to you a good news contrary to what we announced to you, let that one be under a curse.
v09: As we have said before, I now say again: if anyone announces to you a good news contrary to what you received, let that one be under a curse.
v10: Am I now trying to win human approval or God’s? Or am I trying to please people? If I were still trying to please people, I would not be Christ’s servant.
</p>

## Paul’s Good News Came through Revelation

<p>
v11: I want you to know, brothers and sisters, that the good news I announced is not of human origin.
v12: I did not receive it from a human being, nor was I taught it; I received it through a revelation of Jesus Christ.
v13: You have heard about my former way of life in Judaism: how intensely I persecuted God’s church and tried to destroy it.
v14: I was advancing in Judaism beyond many people my own age among my people, being far more zealous for the traditions of my ancestors.
v15: But when God, who set me apart from my mother’s womb and called me through his grace, was pleased
v16: to reveal his Son in me so that I might announce the good news about him among the Gentiles, I did not immediately consult flesh and blood,
v17: nor did I go up to Jerusalem to those who were apostles before me. Instead, I went away to Arabia and then returned to Damascus.
v18: Then after three years I went up to Jerusalem to get acquainted with Cephas and stayed with him fifteen days.
v19: I saw none of the other apostles except James, the Lord’s brother.
v20: As for what I am writing to you—look, before God, I am not lying.
v21: Afterward I went into the regions of Syria and Cilicia.
v22: I was still unknown by sight to the churches of Judea that are in Christ.
v23: They only kept hearing, “The man who once persecuted us is now announcing the faith he once tried to destroy.”
v24: And they glorified God because of me.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Paul’s opening insistence that his apostleship is “not from human beings or through a human being” anticipates the letter’s defense of divine calling and gospel authority.

v04: “Present evil age” preserves Paul’s apocalyptic contrast between the current order and God’s rescuing action in Christ.

v06–09: Paul repeats `euangelion`, good news or gospel, and denies that the rival message is truly another gospel. Fluent keeps the repetition rather than varying the term.

v08–09: `anathema` is a severe curse formula. Fluent does not soften the force into simple disapproval.

v12: “Revelation of Jesus Christ” can mean revelation from Jesus Christ or revelation in which Jesus Christ is revealed. Fluent preserves the compact wording in the note while the main text uses the natural source sense.

v13–14: Paul names his former life “in Judaism” positively as a real tradition in which he advanced; the problem described is his persecution of the church, not Jewish identity itself.

v15–16: Paul uses prophetic-call language—set apart from the womb, called by grace, commissioned among the nations.

v18: Paul names Cephas rather than Peter in the pinned Greek text here.

## Vocabulary

“Apostle, one sent.”

v04: ἐξαιρέω (exaireō)
“To rescue, deliver.”

v06: μετατίθημι (metatithēmi)
“To turn away, transfer.”

v07: ταράσσω (tarassō)
“To trouble, disturb.”

v08: ἀνάθεμα (anathema)
“Accursed, devoted to judgment.”

v12: ἀποκάλυψις (apokalypsis)
“Revelation, unveiling.”

v14: Ἰουδαϊσμός (Ioudaismos)
“Judaism, Jewish way of life.”

v15: ἀφορίζω (aphorizō)
“To set apart.”
