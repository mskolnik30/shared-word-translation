## Spirit, Trust, and Abraham

<p>
v01: O foolish Galatians! Who bewitched you? Before your eyes Jesus Christ was publicly portrayed as crucified.
v02: I want to learn only this from you: did you receive the Spirit by works of the law, or by hearing with faith?
v03: Are you so foolish? Having begun by the Spirit, are you now being completed by the flesh?
v04: Did you experience so much in vain—if it really was in vain?
v05: So does the one who supplies the Spirit to you and works miracles among you do so by works of the law, or by hearing with faith?
v06: Just as “Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness.”
v07: Know, then, that those who belong to faith are Abraham's children.
v08: And Scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the Gentiles by faith, announced the gospel beforehand to Abraham: “All the nations will be blessed in you.”
v09: So those who belong to faith are blessed together with faithful Abraham.
</p>

## The Curse and the Promise

<p>
v10: For all who rely on works of the law are under a curse, because it is written, “Cursed is everyone who does not continue in everything written in the book of the law, to do it.”
v11: Now it is clear that no one is justified before God by the law, because “the righteous one will live by faith.”
v12: But the law does not operate by faith; instead, “The one who does these things will live by them.”
v13: Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us, because it is written, “Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree,”
v14: so that in Christ Jesus Abraham's blessing might come to the Gentiles, so that we might receive the promised Spirit through faith.
</p>

## The Promise Came before the Law

<p>
v15: Brothers and sisters, I speak in human terms: even a human covenant, once ratified, cannot be annulled or supplemented by anyone.
v16: Now the promises were spoken to Abraham and to his offspring. It does not say, “and to offsprings,” as referring to many, but as to one: “and to your offspring,” who is Christ.
v17: What I mean is this: the law, which came four hundred thirty years later, does not annul a covenant previously ratified by God and so cancel the promise.
v18: For if the inheritance comes by the law, it no longer comes by promise. But God graciously gave it to Abraham through a promise.
</p>

## Why Then the Law?

<p>
v19: Why, then, the law? It was added because of transgressions, until the offspring to whom the promise had been made would come. It was arranged through angels by the hand of a mediator.
v20: Now a mediator is not for one party alone, but God is one.
v21: Is the law, then, opposed to God's promises? Absolutely not! For if a law had been given that could impart life, righteousness would indeed have come by the law.
v22: But Scripture imprisoned everything under sin, so that the promise by the faithfulness of Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe.
v23: Before this faith came, we were held under guard by the law, imprisoned until the coming faith would be revealed.
v24: Thus the law became our guardian until Christ, so that we might be justified by faith.
v25: But now that faith has come, we are no longer under a guardian.
</p>

## One in Christ Jesus

<p>
v26: For all of you are God's sons through faith in Christ Jesus.
v27: For all of you who were baptized into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ.
v28: There is no Jew or Greek, there is no enslaved or free person, there is no male and female, for all of you are one in Christ Jesus.
v29: And if you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham's offspring, heirs according to promise.
</p>

## Notes

v16: Paul builds an argument on the collective singular ‘offspring,’ identifying the promised offspring with Christ.

v19–20: The statement about the mediator is exceptionally compressed and has many interpretations; the translation does not supply an unstated explanation.

v24: The paidagōgos was a household guardian or supervisor, not simply a modern schoolteacher.

v28: The verse retains enslaved/free and male/female as real social and gendered distinctions while declaring unity in Christ.

## Vocabulary

v24: **παιδαγωγός (paidagōgos)**: A household guardian supervising a minor.

v28: **δοῦλος (doulos)**: An enslaved person or slave.
