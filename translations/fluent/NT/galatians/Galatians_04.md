## From Slavery to Sonship

<p>
v01: What I mean is that as long as an heir is a minor, he differs in no way from an enslaved person, though he is owner of everything.
v02: Instead, he is under guardians and estate managers until the date set by his father.
v03: In the same way, when we were minors, we were enslaved under the elemental powers of the world.
v04: But when the fullness of time came, God sent out his Son, born from a woman, born under the law,
v05: to redeem those under the law, so that we might receive adoption as sons.
v06: And because you are sons, God sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying, “Abba, Father!”
v07: So you are no longer enslaved but a son; and if a son, then also an heir through God.
</p>

## Do Not Return to Slavery

<p>
v08: But formerly, when you did not know God, you were enslaved to beings that by nature are not gods.
v09: But now that you know God—or rather, are known by God—how can you turn back again to the weak and impoverished elemental powers? Do you want to be enslaved to them all over again?
v10: You carefully observe days and months and seasons and years.
v11: I fear for you that perhaps I have labored over you in vain.
</p>

## Paul’s Appeal

<p>
v12: Become as I am, because I also became as you are, brothers and sisters—I plead with you. You did me no wrong.
v13: You know that it was because of a bodily weakness that I first proclaimed the gospel to you.
v14: Although my bodily condition was a trial for you, you did not despise or spit at me. Instead, you welcomed me as an angel of God, as Christ Jesus.
v15: Where, then, is that sense of blessing you had? For I testify that, if possible, you would have torn out your eyes and given them to me.
v16: So have I become your enemy by telling you the truth?
v17: Those people are zealous for you, but not for a good purpose. They want to shut you out so that you will be zealous for them.
v18: Now it is good to be sought eagerly for a good purpose at all times, and not only when I am present with you.
v19: My children, for whom I am again in labor pains until Christ is formed in you,
v20: I wish I could be present with you now and change my tone, because I am perplexed about you.
</p>

## Hagar and Sarah

<p>
v21: Tell me, you who want to be under the law: do you not listen to the law?
v22: For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one by the enslaved woman and one by the free woman.
v23: The one by the enslaved woman was born according to the flesh, but the one by the free woman through promise.
v24: These things are being interpreted allegorically, for these women represent two covenants. One is from Mount Sinai, bearing children into slavery; this is Hagar.
v25: Now Hagar is Mount Sinai in Arabia and corresponds to the present Jerusalem, for she is enslaved with her children.
v26: But the Jerusalem above is free, and she is our mother.
v27: For it is written, “Rejoice, barren woman who does not give birth; break out and cry aloud, woman who has no labor pains, because the deserted woman's children are more numerous than those of the woman who has a husband.”
v28: Now you, brothers and sisters, are children of promise, like Isaac.
v29: But just as then the one born according to the flesh persecuted the one born according to the Spirit, so it is now.
v30: But what does Scripture say? “Drive out the enslaved woman and her son, for the son of the enslaved woman will certainly not inherit with the son of the free woman.”
v31: Therefore, brothers and sisters, we are not children of the enslaved woman, but of the free woman.
</p>

## Notes

v01–7: The heir is male in the analogy because ‘son’ names full inheritance status in its ancient setting; Paul applies that status to the whole community.

v13–15: Paul identifies a bodily weakness but does not diagnose it. The eye language expresses extreme devotion and is not sufficient evidence of a particular illness.

v22–31: Paul explicitly calls his reading allegorical. The translation retains Hagar, slavery, expulsion, and the two Jerusalems without turning the analogy into a general judgment about ethnic groups.

## Vocabulary

v05: **υἱοθεσία (huiothesia)**: Adoption as a son, with full inheritance status.

v24: **ἀλληγορέω (allēgoreō)**: Speak or interpret allegorically.
