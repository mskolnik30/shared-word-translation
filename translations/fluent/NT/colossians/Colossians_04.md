## Masters, Prayer, and Wise Speech

<p>
v01: Masters, give your slaves what is right and fair, knowing that you also have a Master in heaven.
v02: Devote yourselves to prayer, staying alert in it with thanksgiving.
v03: At the same time pray for us too, that God may open a door for the word so that we may speak Christ’s mystery, for which I am in chains,
v04: so that I may make it clear, as I ought to speak.
v05: Walk wisely toward outsiders, making the most of the opportunity.
v06: Let your speech always be gracious, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how you should answer each person.
</p>

## Final Greetings

<p>
v07: Tychicus, our beloved brother, faithful servant, and fellow slave in the Lord, will tell you everything about me.
v08: I am sending him to you for this very reason, so that you may know about us and he may encourage your hearts,
v09: together with Onesimus, our faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you. They will tell you everything happening here.
v10: Aristarchus, my fellow prisoner, greets you, as does Mark, Barnabas’ cousin. You received instructions about him: if he comes to you, welcome him.
v11: Jesus who is called Justus also greets you. These are the only ones from the circumcision who are my fellow workers for God’s kingdom, and they have been a comfort to me.
v12: Epaphras, who is one of you, a slave of Christ Jesus, greets you. He is always struggling for you in his prayers so that you may stand mature and fully assured in everything God wills.
v13: For I testify about him that he works hard for you and for those in Laodicea and Hierapolis.
v14: Luke, the beloved physician, greets you, and so does Demas.
v15: Greet the brothers and sisters in Laodicea, and Nympha and the church in her house.
v16: When this letter has been read among you, make sure it is also read in the church of the Laodiceans, and you in turn should read the letter from Laodicea.
v17: Tell Archippus, “Pay attention to the ministry you received in the Lord, so that you fulfill it.”
v18: I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand. Remember my chains. Grace be with you.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Masters remain under a heavenly Master and are commanded to give enslaved people what is “right and fair.” The verse constrains ancient masters but does not by itself dismantle the institution.

v03–04: Paul asks not first for release from prison but for an open door for the word and clarity in speaking.

v05: “Making the most of the opportunity” uses the same buy-up-the-time idiom found in Ephesians 5:16.

v06: “Seasoned with salt” is retained as a vivid speech metaphor; it suggests fitting, appealing, and discerning communication without reducing it to mere cleverness.

v07: Tychicus is called both `diakonos` and `syndoulos`, servant and fellow slave, language of trusted ministry rather than rank.

v09: Onesimus is “faithful and beloved brother” before any social status is mentioned, an important frame for reading Philemon.

v10–11: Paul identifies these coworkers as being “from the circumcision,” again showing ongoing Jewish participation in his Gentile mission.

v12: Epaphras “struggles” in prayer, using athletic/conflict language for intercession.

v15: SBLGNT reads Nympha in the feminine and “her house.” Fluent follows that reading and names her as host of a house church.

v16: The “letter from Laodicea” is not preserved in the New Testament under that title; Fluent does not identify it with another canonical letter.

v18: Paul’s own handwriting and “remember my chains” make imprisonment physically present in the closing grace wish.
