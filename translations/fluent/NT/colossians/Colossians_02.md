## Rooted and Built Up in Christ

<p>
v01: I want you to know how great a struggle I have for you, for those in Laodicea, and for all who have not seen me face to face,
v02: so that their hearts may be encouraged, joined together in love, and brought into all the riches of full assurance that comes from understanding, to know God’s mystery, Christ,
v03: in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden.
v04: I say this so that no one deceives you with persuasive speech.
v05: For although I am absent in body, I am with you in spirit, rejoicing to see your order and the firmness of your trust in Christ.
v06: Therefore, just as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, walk in him,
v07: rooted and built up in him, established in the trust just as you were taught, overflowing with thanksgiving.
</p>

## Fullness in Christ

<p>
v08: Watch out that no one takes you captive through philosophy and empty deception, according to human tradition, according to the elemental powers of the world, and not according to Christ.
v09: For in him all the fullness of deity dwells bodily,
v10: and you have been filled in him, who is the head over every ruler and authority.
v11: In him you were also circumcised with a circumcision not made by hands, in the removal of the body of flesh, in the circumcision of Christ,
v12: having been buried with him in baptism, in which you were also raised with him through trust in the powerful working of God, who raised him from the dead.
v13: And when you were dead in trespasses and the uncircumcision of your flesh, God made you alive together with him, forgiving us all our trespasses.
v14: He erased the record of debt with its decrees that stood against us. He removed it from the way, nailing it to the cross.
v15: He stripped the rulers and authorities and made a public example of them, triumphing over them in the cross.
</p>

## Do Not Let Anyone Disqualify You

<p>
v16: Therefore do not let anyone judge you about food or drink, or with respect to a festival, new moon, or Sabbath.
v17: These are a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ.
v18: Do not let anyone disqualify you, delighting in self-abasement and worship of angels, dwelling on visions, puffed up without reason by a fleshly mind,
v19: and not holding fast to the head, from whom the whole body, supported and held together by joints and ligaments, grows with growth from God.
</p>

## Died with Christ to the Elemental Powers

<p>
v20: If you died with Christ to the elemental powers of the world, why, as though still living in the world, do you submit to regulations:
v21: “Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch”?
v22: These things are all destined to perish with use; they are based on human commands and teachings.
v23: Such practices have an appearance of wisdom in self-made religion, self-abasement, and severe treatment of the body, but they have no value in restraining fleshly indulgence.
</p>

## Notes

v02: The wording around “God’s mystery, Christ” has important manuscript variation. Fluent follows SBLGNT’s compact identification of the mystery with Christ.

v06–07: Receiving Christ leads to “walking in him.” Root, building, strengthening, and thanksgiving metaphors accumulate around continuing trust.

v08: `philosophia` is not a blanket condemnation of disciplined philosophical thinking. Paul attacks a particular captivity-producing system described as empty deception and human tradition “not according to Christ.”

v08,20: `stoicheia` again can mean elemental powers, elements, or basic principles. Fluent uses “elemental powers” because the surrounding argument treats them as ruling realities from which believers die with Christ.

v09: “Fullness of deity” and “bodily” are both retained directly, resisting reductions of Christ to one spiritual intermediary among many.

v11–12: Paul links spiritual circumcision, baptismal burial, resurrection, and God’s power without making physical Jewish circumcision itself the object of contempt.

v14: `cheirographon` is a handwritten record of indebtedness. Fluent uses “record of debt” while preserving the legal-metaphor force.

v15: The verb can mean disarm or strip. The cross is portrayed as God’s public triumph over hostile powers.

v16: Food, drink, festival, new moon, and Sabbath form a Jewish calendrical cluster. Paul forbids the Colossians from being judged by these practices; Fluent does not translate the verse into anti-Jewish contempt.

v18: The exact practice behind “worship of angels” and “dwelling on visions” is uncertain. Fluent leaves the language concrete and does not reconstruct a full “Colossian heresy.”

v23: Severe bodily discipline is not praised merely because it is difficult; Paul says it lacks power against fleshly indulgence.
