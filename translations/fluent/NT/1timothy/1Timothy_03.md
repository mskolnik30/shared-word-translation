## Overseers

<p>
v01: This saying is trustworthy: if anyone aspires to oversight, they desire a good work.
v02: Therefore an overseer must be above accusation, faithful to one spouse, sober, self-controlled, respectable, hospitable, able to teach,
v03: not addicted to wine, not violent but gentle, not quarrelsome, not a lover of money,
v04: managing their own household well, having children who are respectful and obedient,
v05: because if someone does not know how to care for their own household, how will they care for God’s church?
v06: The overseer must not be a recent convert, so that they do not become conceited and fall into the devil’s judgment.
v07: They must also have a good reputation with outsiders so that they do not fall into disgrace and the devil’s trap.
</p>

## Deacons

<p>
v08: Deacons likewise must be dignified, not double-tongued, not given to much wine, not greedy for dishonest gain,
v09: holding the mystery of the faith with a clear conscience.
v10: Let them also be tested first; then let them serve as deacons if they are above accusation.
v11: Women likewise must be dignified, not slanderers, sober, faithful in everything.
v12: Deacons must be faithful to one spouse, managing their children and their households well.
v13: For those who serve well as deacons gain a good standing for themselves and great boldness in the trust that is in Christ Jesus.
</p>

## The Mystery of Godliness

<p>
v14: I am writing these things to you, hoping to come to you soon.
v15: But if I am delayed, I am writing so that you may know how people should conduct themselves in God’s household, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and foundation of the truth.
v16: And without question, great is the mystery of godliness:
He was revealed in flesh,
set right in Spirit,
seen by angels,
proclaimed among the nations,
trusted in throughout the world,
taken up in glory.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Oversight is described as “a good work,” emphasizing service and character before status.

v02: `mias gynaikos andra` is literally “a one-woman man.” It likely emphasizes marital faithfulness; Fluent uses “faithful to one spouse” rather than making the phrase mechanically exclude every unmarried or remarried person.

v02: Hospitality and ability to teach sit beside sobriety, gentleness, and financial integrity as leadership qualifications.

v04–05: Household management is used analogically for care of the church, but the household itself is not treated as a private sphere exempt from ethical scrutiny.

v08: `diakonos` is retained as the office-like term “deacon,” though the same word elsewhere means servant or minister more broadly.

v11: “Women likewise” may refer to women deacons or wives of male deacons. The Greek does not include “their,” so Fluent does not insert “their wives.”

v13: Good service produces boldness in trust, not merely higher organizational rank.

v15: The church is called God’s household and “pillar and foundation of the truth,” giving communal conduct theological importance.

v16: SBLGNT reads “He who was revealed in flesh,” not the later reading “God was revealed in flesh.” Fluent follows the pinned source.

v16: `dikaioō` is rendered “set right” even here, where the sense is vindicated or shown right by the Spirit; the note preserves that semantic nuance.
