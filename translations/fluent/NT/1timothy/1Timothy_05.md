## Treat the Community as Family

<p>
v01: Do not rebuke an older man harshly, but appeal to him as a father, younger men as brothers,
v02: older women as mothers, and younger women as sisters, with complete purity.
</p>

## Honor Widows Who Are Truly Widows

<p>
v03: Honor widows who are truly widows.
v04: But if a widow has children or grandchildren, let them first learn to practice godliness toward their own household and repay their parents and grandparents, because this is acceptable before God.
v05: The woman who is truly a widow and left alone has placed her hope in God and continues in petitions and prayers night and day.
v06: But the one who lives for pleasure is dead even while she lives.
v07: Command these things so that they may be above accusation.
v08: But if anyone does not provide for their own, especially members of their household, they have denied the faith and are worse than an unbeliever.
</p>

## The Widows’ List

<p>
v09: Let a widow be enrolled only if she is at least sixty years old, faithful to one husband,
v10: well attested for good works: if she raised children, showed hospitality, washed the feet of the holy ones, helped those in distress, and devoted herself to every good work.
v11: But refuse to enroll younger widows, because when their desires draw them away from Christ, they want to marry,
v12: and so incur judgment because they have abandoned their earlier commitment.
v13: At the same time they learn to be idle, going from house to house—and not only idle but gossips and busybodies, saying things they should not.
v14: Therefore I want younger widows to marry, have children, manage a household, and give the opponent no opportunity for slander.
v15: For some have already turned aside after Satan.
v16: If any believing woman has widows in her family, let her help them so the church is not burdened and can help those who are truly widows.
</p>

## Elders and Accountability

<p>
v17: Let elders who lead well be considered worthy of double honor, especially those who labor in word and teaching.
v18: For Scripture says, “You shall not muzzle an ox while it is threshing,” and, “The worker deserves wages.”
v19: Do not accept an accusation against an elder unless it is supported by two or three witnesses.
v20: Those who persist in sin, rebuke in front of everyone so that the rest may have fear.
v21: I solemnly charge you before God, Christ Jesus, and the chosen angels to keep these instructions without prejudgment, doing nothing from partiality.
v22: Do not lay hands on anyone hastily or share in the sins of others. Keep yourself pure.
v23: No longer drink only water, but use a little wine because of your stomach and your frequent illnesses.
v24: The sins of some people are obvious, going ahead of them into judgment, but the sins of others follow later.
v25: In the same way good works are obvious, and even those that are otherwise cannot remain hidden.
</p>

## Notes

v01–02: Timothy is instructed to exercise authority through family-like appeal and purity rather than harshness.

v03: “Honor” includes material support in the chapter’s context, not respect alone.

v04,08,16: Family responsibility and church responsibility are coordinated so that vulnerable widows are supported without turning the community into a substitute for avoidable family neglect.

v09–10: The “list” appears to be an enrolled group of widows recognized for both need and a history of service. The text does not fully describe its institutional function.

v09: “Faithful to one husband” translates the feminine counterpart to the leadership phrase in 3:2 and emphasizes marital faithfulness.

v11–15: The comments about younger widows are situational and polemical, not a description of all young women. Fluent avoids converting them into universal character claims.

v17: “Double honor” likely includes both respect and financial support, as the wage quotations in v18 suggest.

v18: Paul calls both Deuteronomy and a Jesus saying “Scripture,” an important window into early Christian authoritative tradition.

v19–21: Elders receive procedural protection from uncorroborated accusations but are not exempt from public accountability for persistent sin.

v22: Laying on hands likely concerns appointment or commissioning; haste can implicate Timothy in another person’s wrongdoing.

v23: The practical medical aside resists an ascetic interpretation of Timothy’s personal habits.
