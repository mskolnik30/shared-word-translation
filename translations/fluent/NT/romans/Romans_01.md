## Greeting

<p>
v01: Paul, a slave of Christ Jesus, called to be an apostle and set apart for God's good news—
v02: the good news God promised beforehand through his prophets in the holy Scriptures—
v03: concerning his Son, who in terms of human descent came from David's offspring,
v04: and who in terms of the Spirit of holiness was appointed Son of God in power by resurrection from the dead: Jesus Christ our Lord.
v05: Through him we received grace and apostleship to bring about the obedience of faith among all the Gentiles for the sake of his name,
v06: including you, who are also called to belong to Jesus Christ.
v07: To all in Rome who are loved by God and called to be holy ones: Grace and peace to you from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.
</p>

## Paul Wants to Visit Rome

<p>
v08: First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for all of you, because your faith is being proclaimed throughout the whole world.
v09: God, whom I serve in my spirit by proclaiming the good news of his Son, is my witness that I remember you constantly,
v10: always asking in my prayers that somehow, at last, by God's will the way may be opened for me to come to you.
v11: For I long to see you so that I may share some spiritual gift with you to strengthen you—
v12: that is, so that we may be mutually encouraged among you through one another's faith, both yours and mine.
v13: I do not want you to be unaware, brothers and sisters, that I often planned to come to you—but have been prevented until now—so that I might have some fruit among you as I have among the rest of the Gentiles.
v14: I am indebted both to Greeks and to non-Greeks, both to the wise and to the foolish.
v15: That is why I am eager to proclaim the good news also to you who are in Rome.
</p>

## God’s Righteousness Revealed in the Good News

<p>
v16: For I am not ashamed of the good news, because it is God's power for salvation to everyone who believes—to the Jewish person first and also to the Greek.
v17: For in it God's righteousness is revealed from faith to faith, as it is written: “The righteous person will live by faith.”
</p>

## Humanity’s Exchange of God’s Glory

<p>
v18: For God's wrath is revealed from heaven against every form of human ungodliness and injustice by which people suppress the truth through injustice.
v19: This is because what can be known about God is evident among them, since God made it evident to them.
v20: For ever since the creation of the world, God's invisible qualities—his eternal power and divine nature—have been clearly seen, being understood from what he has made. So they are without excuse.
v21: Though they knew God, they did not glorify him as God or give thanks. Instead, their reasoning became futile, and their senseless heart was darkened.
v22: Claiming to be wise, they became fools
v23: and exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images resembling mortal humanity, birds, four-footed animals, and reptiles.
</p>

<p>
v24: Therefore God handed them over, in the desires of their hearts, to impurity, so that their bodies were dishonored among them.
v25: They exchanged the truth of God for the lie and revered and served the creation instead of the Creator, who is blessed forever. Amen.
</p>

<p>
v26: For this reason God handed them over to dishonorable passions. Their females exchanged natural sexual relations for those contrary to nature.
v27: Likewise, the males abandoned natural sexual relations with females and burned in their desire for one another. Males committed shameless acts with males and received in themselves the consequence their error required.
</p>

<p>
v28: And because they did not consider God worth retaining in their knowledge, God handed them over to an unfit mind to do what is not fitting.
v29: They were filled with every kind of injustice, wickedness, greed, and malice; full of envy, murder, strife, deceit, and spite; they became whisperers,
v30: slanderers, God-haters, insolent, arrogant, boastful, inventors of evil, disobedient to parents,
v31: without understanding, untrustworthy, without natural affection, and without mercy.
v32: Although they know God's righteous decree that those who practice such things deserve death, they not only do them but also approve of those who practice them.
</p>

## Notes

v01: ‘Slave’ retains Paul’s ownership language; it is not softened to hired service.

v17: ‘From faith to faith’ is compact and disputed. The main text preserves the phrase rather than choosing one exhaustive explanation.

v26–27: Paul’s female/male and natural/contrary language is translated directly. The passage is situated within the argument that turns in 2:1 toward the judging reader; the note does not add a modern political or doctrinal verdict.

## Vocabulary

v01: **δοῦλος (doulos)**: Slave, a person owned by another; also used metaphorically for complete allegiance.

v16: **εὐαγγέλιον (euangelion)**: Good news or gospel.

v17: **δικαιοσύνη (dikaiosynē)**: Righteousness, justice, or right relationship.
