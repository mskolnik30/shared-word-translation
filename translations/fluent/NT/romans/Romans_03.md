## God Remains Faithful

<p>
v01: What advantage, then, does the Jew have? Or what is the value of circumcision?
v02: Much in every way. First of all, they were entrusted with God’s words.
v03: What if some were unfaithful? Will their unfaithfulness cancel God’s faithfulness?
v04: Absolutely not! Let God be true even if every human being is a liar, as it is written,
“So that you may be shown right in your words
and prevail when you are judged.”
v05: But if our unrighteousness demonstrates God’s righteousness, what should we say? Is God unjust to bring wrath? I am speaking in human terms.
v06: Absolutely not! Otherwise, how will God judge the world?
v07: But if through my falsehood God’s truth overflows to his glory, why am I still judged as a sinner?
v08: And why not say—as some slanderously claim we say—“Let us do evil so that good may come”? Their condemnation is deserved.
</p>

## All Are Under Sin

<p>
v09: What then? Are we any better? Not at all. For we have already charged that both Jews and Greeks are all under sin,
v10: as it is written:
“There is no one righteous,
not even one.
v11: There is no one who understands;
there is no one who seeks God.
v12: All have turned aside;
together they have become worthless.
There is no one who does good,
not even one.”
v13: “Their throat is an open grave;
with their tongues they deceive.”
“The venom of snakes is under their lips.”
v14: “Their mouth is full of cursing and bitterness.”
v15: “Their feet are swift to shed blood;
v16: ruin and misery are in their paths,
v17: and they have not known the way of peace.”
v18: “There is no fear of God before their eyes.”
</p>

<p>
v19: We know that whatever the Law says, it speaks to those under the Law, so that every mouth may be silenced and the whole world become accountable to God.
v20: For no human being will be set right in God’s sight by works of the Law, because through the Law comes knowledge of sin.
</p>

## God’s Righteousness Revealed

<p>
v21: But now, apart from the Law, God’s righteousness has been revealed, though the Law and the Prophets testify to it—
v22: God’s righteousness through the faithfulness of Jesus Christ, for all who trust. For there is no distinction:
v23: all have sinned and fall short of God’s glory,
v24: and they are set right freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.
v25: God presented him as a place of atonement by his blood, received through trust. This was to demonstrate God’s righteousness, because in his restraint God had passed over sins committed beforehand.
v26: It was to demonstrate his righteousness at the present time, so that God may be just and the one who sets right the person who lives from Jesus’ faithfulness.
</p>

## Boasting Is Excluded

<p>
v27: Where, then, is boasting? It is excluded. By what kind of law? A law of works? No, but by the law of trust.
v28: For we hold that a person is set right by trust apart from works of the Law.
v29: Or is God the God of Jews only? Is he not also the God of Gentiles? Yes, of Gentiles also,
v30: since God is one, and he will set right the circumcised from trust and the uncircumcised through trust.
v31: Do we then overthrow the Law through trust? Absolutely not! Instead, we uphold the Law.
</p>

## Notes

v02: Being entrusted with God’s words names responsibility as well as privilege.

v03: Human `apistia` is set against God’s `pistis`; Fluent uses unfaithfulness/faithfulness to make Paul’s lexical contrast visible.

v09: “Under sin” treats sin as a ruling power, preparing the dominion/slavery language of Romans 5–8.

v19–20: The Law stops every mouth and gives knowledge of sin; Paul denies that works of the Law set humanity right before God.

v21: “But now” marks a decisive disclosure while Paul insists this righteousness is attested by the Law and Prophets.

v22: `pistis Iēsou Christou` permits both “faith in Jesus Christ” and “faithfulness of Jesus Christ.” Fluent follows the project’s frozen decision: **“through the faithfulness of Jesus Christ, for all who trust.”** Christ’s faithful action is the ground; human trust is the response.

v25: `hilastērion` can evoke the mercy seat/place of atonement and sacrificial atonement language. Fluent uses **“a place of atonement”** rather than selecting one later atonement theory.

v26: The compressed final phrase is difficult. Fluent keeps Jesus’ faithfulness central while avoiding an added doctrinal explanation inside the verse.

v27: “Law of trust” uses `nomos` metaphorically as a governing principle and intentionally excludes boasting.

v31: Paul ends by saying trust upholds the Law, blocking an interpretation in which his good news simply treats Israel’s Scriptures as discarded.
