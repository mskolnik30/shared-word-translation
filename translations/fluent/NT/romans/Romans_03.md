## God Remains Faithful

<p>
v01: What advantage, then, is there in being Jewish? Or what benefit is there in circumcision?
v02: Much in every way! First of all, the Jewish people were entrusted with the words of God.
v03: What if some were unfaithful? Will their unfaithfulness nullify God's faithfulness?
v04: Absolutely not! Let God be shown true, even if every human is a liar, as it is written: “So that you may be proved right in your words and prevail when you are judged.”
v05: But if our injustice demonstrates God's righteousness, what shall we say? Is God unjust to inflict wrath? I am speaking in human terms.
v06: Absolutely not! Otherwise, how could God judge the world?
v07: But if God's truth abounds to his glory through my falsehood, why am I still judged as a sinner?
v08: And why not say, “Let us do evil so that good may come”—as we are slanderously reported and as some claim we say? Their condemnation is just.
</p>

## All Are Under Sin

<p>
v09: What then? Are we any better? Not at all. For we have already charged that both Jewish and Greek people are all under sin,
v10: as it is written: “There is no one righteous, not even one;
v11: there is no one who understands; there is no one who seeks God.
v12: All have turned away; together they have become worthless. There is no one who does good, not even one.
v13: Their throat is an open grave; they practice deceit with their tongues. Viper's venom is under their lips.
v14: Their mouth is full of cursing and bitterness.
v15: Their feet are swift to shed blood;
v16: ruin and misery are in their paths,
v17: and they have not known the way of peace.
v18: There is no fear of God before their eyes.”
</p>

<p>
v19: Now we know that whatever the Law says, it speaks to those under the Law, so that every mouth may be silenced and the whole world may become accountable to God.
v20: For no human being will be declared righteous before him by works of the Law, since through the Law comes knowledge of sin.
</p>

## God’s Righteousness Revealed

<p>
v21: But now, apart from the Law, God's righteousness has been revealed—though the Law and the Prophets bear witness to it—
v22: God's righteousness through the faithfulness of Jesus Christ for all who believe. For there is no distinction,
v23: because all have sinned and fall short of God's glory,
v24: and they are declared righteous freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.
v25: God presented him as an atoning sacrifice, effective through faith in his blood, to demonstrate God's righteousness, because in his restraint he had passed over sins committed beforehand.
v26: He did this to demonstrate his righteousness in the present time, so that he might be righteous and declare righteous the one who has faith in Jesus.
</p>

## Boasting Is Excluded

<p>
v27: Where, then, is boasting? It has been excluded. By what kind of law—one of works? No, but by a law of faith.
v28: For we conclude that a person is declared righteous by faith apart from works of the Law.
v29: Or is God the God of Jewish people only? Is he not also the God of Gentiles? Yes, of Gentiles too,
v30: since God is one, and he will declare the circumcised righteous by faith and the uncircumcised through the same faith.
v31: Do we then nullify the Law through faith? Absolutely not! Instead, we uphold the Law.
</p>

## Notes

v03: The same word-family can describe human unfaithfulness and God’s faithfulness; the repetition is retained.

v22: The Greek phrase can mean ‘faithfulness of Jesus Christ’ or ‘faith in Jesus Christ.’ The main text chooses the former while the following phrase explicitly names all who believe.

v25: The term translated ‘atoning sacrifice’ can also evoke the mercy seat, the place of atonement. The main text does not exhaust the image.

v30: The prepositions differ, but Paul identifies one God and one faith rather than two ways of justification.

## Vocabulary

v20: **δικαιόω (dikaioō)**: Declare righteous, vindicate, or set right.

v24: **ἀπολύτρωσις (apolytrōsis)**: Redemption or release secured at a cost.

v25: **ἱλαστήριον (hilastērion)**: Atoning means or mercy seat.
