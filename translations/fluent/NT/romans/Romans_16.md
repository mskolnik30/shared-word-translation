## Phoebe and the Roman House Churches

<p>
v01: I commend to you our sister Phoebe, a deacon of the church in Cenchreae.
v02: Welcome her in the Lord in a way worthy of the holy ones, and help her in whatever matter she may need from you, because she has been a benefactor of many people, including me.
</p>

<p>
v03: Greet Prisca and Aquila, my fellow workers in Christ Jesus,
v04: who risked their own necks for my life. Not only I but all the Gentile churches give thanks for them.
v05: Greet also the church in their house. Greet my beloved Epaenetus, the firstfruits of Asia for Christ.
v06: Greet Mary, who worked very hard for you.
v07: Greet Andronicus and Junia, my relatives and fellow prisoners. They are outstanding among the apostles, and they were in Christ before me.
v08: Greet Ampliatus, my beloved in the Lord.
v09: Greet Urbanus, our fellow worker in Christ, and my beloved Stachys.
v10: Greet Apelles, who is approved in Christ. Greet those from Aristobulus’ household.
v11: Greet my relative Herodion. Greet those from Narcissus’ household who are in the Lord.
v12: Greet Tryphaena and Tryphosa, who work hard in the Lord. Greet the beloved Persis, who has worked very hard in the Lord.
v13: Greet Rufus, chosen in the Lord, and his mother, who has been a mother to me as well.
v14: Greet Asyncritus, Phlegon, Hermes, Patrobas, Hermas, and the brothers and sisters with them.
v15: Greet Philologus and Julia, Nereus and his sister, Olympas, and all the holy ones with them.
v16: Greet one another with a holy kiss. All the churches of Christ greet you.
</p>

## Watch for Those Who Divide

<p>
v17: I urge you, brothers and sisters, to watch out for those who cause divisions and put obstacles in the way contrary to the teaching you learned. Turn away from them.
v18: For such people do not serve our Lord Christ but their own appetites, and through smooth talk and flattery they deceive the hearts of unsuspecting people.
v19: Your obedience has become known to everyone, so I rejoice over you. But I want you to be wise about what is good and innocent about what is evil.
v20: The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet. The grace of our Lord Jesus be with you.
</p>

## Greetings from Paul’s Companions

<p>
v21: Timothy, my fellow worker, greets you, as do Lucius, Jason, and Sosipater, my relatives.
v22: I, Tertius, the one who wrote down this letter, greet you in the Lord.
v23: Gaius, who is host to me and to the whole church, greets you. Erastus, the city treasurer, greets you, along with our brother Quartus.
v24: The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with all of you. Amen.
</p>

## Notes

v01: `diakonos` is the same word used for servant/minister/deacon elsewhere. Because Phoebe is linked to a specific congregation, Fluent uses “deacon” while not importing a later fixed job description into the first-century term.

v02: `prostatis` describes a patron/benefactor/protector. Phoebe is presented as a person of recognized material and social support, including support of Paul.

v03: Paul uses Prisca’s name before Aquila’s here, as in several early-Christian references, without explanation.

v07: Junia is the normal feminine form of the name. Fluent follows the strong grammatical and historical case for a woman named Junia.

v07: “Outstanding among the apostles” naturally says Andronicus and Junia were notable members of the apostolic group. A reading “well known to the apostles” has been argued; Fluent follows the more direct construction while recording the debate here.

v13: Paul can call Rufus’ mother “his mother and mine,” using kinship language for shared care rather than biological maternity.

v14–15: The clustered names likely represent multiple household congregations or networks; the text does not provide a single centralized Roman church structure.

v17–18: The warning is against teaching that produces divisions and obstacles contrary to what the believers learned, not a command to avoid every disagreement.

v20: The God “of peace” is the one who crushes Satan, deliberately combining peace and final victory imagery and echoing Genesis 3.

v22: Tertius identifies himself as the person physically writing the letter, distinguishing Paul’s authorship from scribal activity.

v24: The pinned SBLGNT main text includes this grace benediction. Its apparatus notes that Westcott-Hort, Tregelles, and NA28 omit it, while the Robinson-Pierpont tradition includes it. Fluent follows the pinned SBLGNT main text.

v25–27: These public verse numbers are absent from the pinned SBLGNT main text. The familiar doxology beginning “Now to the one who is able to strengthen you…” is a major ancient textual tradition whose placement varies across witnesses and critical editions. The SBLGNT apparatus records the doxology after Romans 14:23 in the Robinson-Pierpont tradition. Because Fluent’s production source is the pinned SBLGNT main text, the doxology is not printed as continuous Fluent Scripture here; its textual history is preserved in the apparatus.

v25–27: The traditional doxology contains the important “obedience of faith/trust” inclusio with Romans 1:5, but that phrase is not part of the pinned SBLGNT main text used for this Fluent edition.
