## Life in the Spirit

<p>
v01: Therefore there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.
v02: For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has set you free from the law of sin and death.
v03: What the Law could not do, weakened as it was through the flesh, God did: he sent his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh and concerning sin, and condemned sin in the flesh,
v04: so that the Law’s righteous requirement might be fulfilled in us who walk not according to the flesh but according to the Spirit.
</p>

<p>
v05: Those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, but those who live according to the Spirit on the things of the Spirit.
v06: The mindset of the flesh is death, but the mindset of the Spirit is life and peace.
v07: The mindset of the flesh is hostile to God because it does not submit to God’s Law—indeed, it cannot.
v08: Those who are in the flesh cannot please God.
v09: But you are not in the flesh; you are in the Spirit, if indeed God’s Spirit dwells in you. Anyone who does not have Christ’s Spirit does not belong to him.
v10: But if Christ is in you, the body is dead because of sin, yet the Spirit is life because of righteousness.
v11: And if the Spirit of the one who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, the one who raised Christ Jesus from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies through his Spirit dwelling in you.
</p>

## Children and Heirs

<p>
v12: So then, brothers and sisters, we are debtors—but not to the flesh, to live according to the flesh.
v13: For if you live according to the flesh, you are going to die. But if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
v14: For all who are led by God’s Spirit are God’s children.
v15: You did not receive a spirit of slavery leading back into fear. Instead, you received the Spirit of adoption, by whom we cry, “Abba! Father!”
v16: The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that we are God’s children,
v17: and if children, then heirs—heirs of God and co-heirs with Christ, if indeed we suffer with him so that we may also be glorified with him.
</p>

## Creation Groans in Hope

<p>
v18: I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worth comparing with the glory about to be revealed to us.
v19: For creation waits with eager longing for the revealing of God’s children.
v20: Creation was subjected to futility, not willingly but because of the one who subjected it, in hope
v21: that creation itself will also be set free from slavery to decay into the freedom of the glory of God’s children.
v22: We know that the whole creation has been groaning together and suffering labor pains together until now.
v23: And not only creation, but we ourselves, who have the firstfruits of the Spirit, groan inwardly while we wait for adoption, the redemption of our bodies.
v24: For we were saved in hope. But hope that is seen is not hope. Who hopes for what they see?
v25: But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with endurance.
</p>

## The Spirit Helps Our Weakness

<p>
v26: In the same way, the Spirit helps us in our weakness. We do not know what we should pray for as we ought, but the Spirit himself intercedes with groanings too deep for words.
v27: The one who searches hearts knows the Spirit’s mind, because the Spirit intercedes for the holy ones according to God’s will.
v28: We know that God works all things together for good for those who love God, who are called according to his purpose.
v29: For those God foreknew, he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son, so that the Son might be firstborn among many brothers and sisters.
v30: Those he predestined, he also called; those he called, he also set right; and those he set right, he also glorified.
</p>

## Nothing Can Separate Us

<p>
v31: What then should we say about these things? If God is for us, who can be against us?
v32: He did not spare his own Son but handed him over for us all. How will he not also, with him, freely give us all things?
v33: Who will bring a charge against God’s chosen ones? God is the one who sets right.
v34: Who is the one who condemns? Christ Jesus is the one who died—more than that, who was raised—who is at God’s right hand and who also intercedes for us.
v35: Who will separate us from Christ’s love? Will affliction, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, danger, or sword?
v36: As it is written,
“For your sake we are being put to death all day long;
we are considered sheep for slaughter.”
v37: But in all these things we overwhelmingly conquer through the one who loved us.
v38: For I am convinced that neither death nor life, neither angels nor rulers, neither things present nor things to come, nor powers,
v39: neither height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation will be able to separate us from God’s love in Christ Jesus our Lord.
</p>

## Notes

v01: SBLGNT ends the sentence after “Christ Jesus.” Later expansions add phrases about walking according to the Spirit from v04. Fluent follows the shorter critical text.

v03: “In the likeness of sinful flesh” protects both realities in Paul’s wording: the Son genuinely shares human flesh, while “sinful” belongs to the condition he enters rather than a statement that Jesus sinned.

v05–09: `sarx`, flesh, describes a human sphere/orientation resistant to God, not a condemnation of physical embodiment.

v15: Adoption is received belonging. “Abba” is retained as Paul’s own transliterated family address followed by “Father.”

v18–23: Human salvation and creation’s liberation are bound together; the chapter does not reduce redemption to escape from creation.

v23: Adoption is already received in v15 yet still awaited as “the redemption of our bodies.” Paul holds present and future adoption together.

v26: The Spirit’s groaning meets human inability to formulate adequate prayer; weakness is not a failure condition outside grace.

v28: The subject is God: God works all things together toward good. The verse does not call all events good.

v29–30: The sequence is kept as assurance-centered divine action rather than expanded into a systematic account of every question about predestination.

v33: `dikaioō` remains “set right,” keeping Romans 3–5 lexical continuity in the courtroom assurance passage.

v35–39: Paul names severe suffering rather than promising its avoidance. The promise is inseparability from divine love through it.
