## Abraham Was Set Right by Trust

<p>
v01: What then shall we say that Abraham, our ancestor according to the flesh, discovered?
v02: For if Abraham was declared righteous by works, he has something to boast about—but not before God.
v03: For what does Scripture say? “Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness.”
v04: Now to the one who works, wages are not counted as a gift but as something owed.
v05: But to the one who does not work but believes in the God who declares the ungodly righteous, that person's faith is counted as righteousness.
v06: Likewise, David speaks of the blessing of the person to whom God counts righteousness apart from works:
v07: “Blessed are those whose lawless acts are forgiven and whose sins are covered.
v08: Blessed is the person whose sin the Lord will never count against them.”
</p>

## Counted Before Circumcision

<p>
v09: Does this blessing, then, apply only to the circumcised, or also to the uncircumcised? For we say, “Faith was counted to Abraham as righteousness.”
v10: Under what circumstances was it counted? After he was circumcised, or before? It was not after, but before he was circumcised.
v11: He received the sign of circumcision as a seal of the righteousness he had by faith while still uncircumcised. This was so that he might be the father of all who believe without being circumcised, so that righteousness might be counted to them as well,
v12: and the father of the circumcised who are not merely circumcised but also walk in the footsteps of the faith our father Abraham had before he was circumcised.
</p>

## The Promise Comes Through Trust

<p>
v13: For the promise that Abraham or his offspring would inherit the world did not come through the Law, but through the righteousness of faith.
v14: For if those who depend on the Law are heirs, faith has been emptied and the promise nullified.
v15: For the Law produces wrath; but where there is no Law, neither is there transgression.
v16: For this reason the promise comes through faith, so that it may rest on grace and be guaranteed to all Abraham's offspring—not only to those who depend on the Law but also to those who share Abraham's faith. He is the father of us all,
v17: as it is written, “I have made you the father of many nations.” He is our father in the sight of the God he believed—the God who gives life to the dead and calls into being things that do not exist.
</p>

## Trust in the God Who Gives Life

<p>
v18: Against all hope, Abraham believed in hope, so that he became the father of many nations, according to what was said: “So shall your offspring be.”
v19: Without weakening in faith, he considered his own body as good as dead—since he was about a hundred years old—and the deadness of Sarah's womb.
v20: Yet he did not waver in unbelief at God's promise but was strengthened in faith and gave glory to God,
v21: fully convinced that God was able to do what he had promised.
v22: Therefore “it was counted to him as righteousness.”
</p>

<p>
v23: Now the words “it was counted to him” were not written for him alone,
v24: but also for us, to whom righteousness will be counted—for us who believe in the one who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead.
v25: He was handed over because of our trespasses and raised because of our justification.
</p>

## Notes

v01: ‘According to the flesh’ can modify Abraham as physical ancestor; the sentence is rendered with that natural connection.

v11–12: Circumcision is a sign and seal received after Abraham’s faith, allowing Paul to describe him as father of believing people in both groups.

v19: The verse names Abraham’s aged body and Sarah’s womb without blaming either person for infertility.

v25: Both clauses use ‘because of’; the compact parallel does not require that Jesus’ resurrection was caused by an already completed human justification.

## Vocabulary

v03: **λογίζομαι (logizomai)**: Count, reckon, calculate, or consider.

v11: **σφραγίς (sphragis)**: Seal, a mark of confirmation or ownership.

v13: **κληρονόμος (klēronomos)**: Heir, one who receives an inheritance.
