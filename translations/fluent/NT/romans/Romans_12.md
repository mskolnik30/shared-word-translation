## A Living Sacrifice

<p>
v01: Therefore I urge you, brothers and sisters, by God’s mercies, to present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God. This is your fitting worship.
v02: Do not be conformed to this age, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, so that you may discern what God’s will is—what is good, pleasing, and complete.
</p>

## Many Members, One Body

<p>
v03: By the grace given to me, I tell everyone among you not to think more highly of yourself than you ought to think, but to think with sober judgment, each according to the measure of trust God has assigned.
v04: For just as we have many parts in one body, and all the parts do not have the same function,
v05: so we who are many are one body in Christ and individually belong to one another.
v06: We have different gifts according to the grace given to us: if prophecy, use it according to the proportion of trust;
v07: if service, in serving; if teaching, in teaching;
v08: if encouragement, in encouraging; the one who gives, with generosity; the one who leads, with diligence; the one who shows mercy, with cheerfulness.
</p>

## Love Without Hypocrisy

<p>
v09: Let love be genuine. Hate what is evil; cling to what is good.
v10: Love one another with family affection. Take the lead in honoring one another.
v11: Do not become lazy in diligence. Be fervent in spirit. Serve the Lord.
v12: Rejoice in hope, endure affliction, persist in prayer.
v13: Share in the needs of the holy ones; pursue hospitality.
</p>

<p>
v14: Bless those who persecute you; bless and do not curse.
v15: Rejoice with those who rejoice; weep with those who weep.
v16: Live in harmony with one another. Do not set your mind on high things, but associate with the lowly. Do not become wise in your own estimation.
v17: Repay no one evil for evil. Give careful thought to what is good in the sight of everyone.
v18: If possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone.
v19: Beloved, do not avenge yourselves, but leave room for God’s wrath, because it is written, “Vengeance is mine; I will repay, says the Lord.”
v20: Instead,
“If your enemy is hungry, feed them;
if they are thirsty, give them something to drink.
For by doing this you will heap burning coals on their head.”
v21: Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.
</p>

## Notes

v01: “Therefore” makes embodied worship the response to the mercies unfolded in Romans 1–11, not a new route to earn them.

v01: `logikos` can mean reasonable, rational, spiritual, or fitting. “Fitting worship” keeps the sense of an appropriate whole-life response.

v02: “This age” is more precise than “the world” here and preserves Paul’s eschatological contrast.

v03: The repeated `phroneō` language stresses disciplined perception rather than low self-esteem.

v05: Paul goes beyond saying members share one body: they are “members of one another.”

v06: “According to the proportion of trust/faith” is compressed and debated; Fluent does not convert it into a detailed theory of prophetic inspiration.

v10: The Greek can mean “outdo one another in showing honor” or “take the lead in honoring.” Fluent uses the latter while preserving the reversal of status-seeking.

v13: Hospitality is something believers are told to “pursue,” an unusually active verb.

v18: Peace is commanded with realistic qualifiers: “if possible” and “as far as it depends on you.”

v19–21: Refusing vengeance does not deny justice; it relocates vengeance to God and replaces retaliatory action with concrete care for enemies.

v20: The burning-coals image remains ambiguous and is not explained as either secret retaliation or guaranteed repentance.
