## Dying and Living with Christ

<p>
v01: What then shall we say? Should we continue in sin so that grace may increase?
v02: Absolutely not! How can we who died to sin still live in it?
v03: Or do you not know that all of us who were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death?
v04: Therefore we were buried with him through baptism into death, so that just as Christ was raised from the dead through the Father's glory, we too might walk in newness of life.
v05: For if we have become united with him in the likeness of his death, we will certainly also be united with him in the likeness of his resurrection.
v06: We know that our old self was crucified with him so that the body ruled by sin might be rendered powerless and we might no longer be enslaved to sin.
v07: For whoever has died has been freed from sin's claim.
</p>

<p>
v08: Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we will also live with him,
v09: knowing that Christ, having been raised from the dead, will never die again; death no longer rules over him.
v10: For the death he died, he died to sin once for all; but the life he lives, he lives to God.
v11: In the same way, consider yourselves dead to sin but alive to God in Christ Jesus.
</p>

## Do Not Let Sin Reign

<p>
v12: Therefore do not let sin reign in your mortal body, making you obey its desires.
v13: Do not present the parts of your body to sin as weapons of injustice. Instead, present yourselves to God as people alive from the dead, and present the parts of your body to God as weapons of righteousness.
v14: For sin will not rule over you, because you are not under Law but under grace.
</p>

## Slaves of Righteousness

<p>
v15: What then? Should we sin because we are not under Law but under grace? Absolutely not!
v16: Do you not know that when you present yourselves to someone as slaves for obedience, you are slaves of the one you obey—either of sin, which leads to death, or of obedience, which leads to righteousness?
v17: But thanks be to God! Though you were slaves of sin, you became obedient from the heart to the pattern of teaching to which you were entrusted.
v18: Having been set free from sin, you became enslaved to righteousness.
</p>

<p>
v19: I am speaking in human terms because of the weakness of your flesh. Just as you once presented the parts of your body as slaves to impurity and to lawlessness leading to more lawlessness, so now present them as slaves to righteousness leading to holiness.
v20: For when you were slaves of sin, you were free from the claims of righteousness.
v21: So what fruit did you have then from the things of which you are now ashamed? Their outcome is death.
v22: But now that you have been set free from sin and enslaved to God, the fruit you receive leads to holiness, and its outcome is eternal life.
v23: For sin pays wages of death, but God's gracious gift is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.
</p>

## Notes

v03–5: Baptism is described as participation in Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection-shaped new life.

v06–07: The old self and body ruled by sin are crucified so that slavery to sin ends; ‘freed’ in verse 7 represents a legal justification verb in this context.

v16–22: Paul deliberately uses ancient slavery as a severe metaphor for mastery and obedience. The translation retains the image while not treating the brutal institution as morally harmless.

v23: The contrast is between wages earned from sin and God’s unearned gracious gift.

## Vocabulary

v04: **καινότης (kainotēs)**: Newness or a new quality of life.

v13: **ὅπλον (hoplon)**: Weapon, tool, or instrument.

v22: **ἁγιασμός (hagiasmos)**: Holiness or sanctification.
