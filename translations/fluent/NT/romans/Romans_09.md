## Paul’s Anguish for Israel

<p>
v01: I am speaking the truth in Christ—I am not lying; my conscience bears witness with me in the Holy Spirit—
v02: that I have great sorrow and unceasing anguish in my heart.
v03: For I could wish that I myself were accursed and cut off from Christ for the sake of my brothers and sisters, my relatives according to the flesh.
v04: They are Israelites. To them belong the adoption as sons, the glory, the covenants, the giving of the law, the worship, and the promises.
v05: To them belong the patriarchs, and from them, according to the flesh, comes the Messiah, who is over all, God blessed forever. Amen.
</p>

## God’s Promise Has Not Failed

<p>
v06: But it is not as though God's word has failed. For not all who are descended from Israel are Israel,
v07: nor are they all children because they are Abraham's offspring. Rather, “Through Isaac your offspring will be named.”
v08: That is, it is not the children of the flesh who are God's children; instead, the children of the promise are counted as offspring.
v09: For this is the word of promise: “At the appointed time I will come, and Sarah will have a son.”
</p>

<p>
v10: And not only that, but Rebekah also conceived children by one man, our father Isaac.
v11: Before the twins were born or had done anything good or bad—so that God's purpose according to election might remain,
v12: not because of works but because of the one who calls—she was told, “The older will serve the younger.”
v13: As it is written: “Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated.”
</p>

## Mercy and God’s Freedom

<p>
v14: What then shall we say? Is there injustice with God? Absolutely not!
v15: For he says to Moses, “I will have mercy on whom I have mercy, and I will have compassion on whom I have compassion.”
v16: So then, it does not depend on human desire or effort, but on God who shows mercy.
v17: For Scripture says to Pharaoh, “I raised you up for this very purpose: that I might display my power in you and that my name might be proclaimed throughout the earth.”
v18: So then, he has mercy on whom he wishes, and he hardens whom he wishes.
</p>

<p>
v19: You will say to me then, “Why does he still find fault? For who has resisted his will?”
v20: But who are you, a human being, to answer back to God? Will what is formed say to the one who formed it, “Why did you make me like this?”
v21: Or does the potter not have authority over the clay, to make from the same lump one vessel for honored use and another for dishonored use?
v22: And what if God, wishing to display his wrath and make his power known, endured with great patience vessels of wrath prepared for destruction,
v23: and did so to make known the riches of his glory upon vessels of mercy, which he prepared beforehand for glory—
v24: even us, whom he called not only from the Jews but also from the Gentiles?
</p>

## Called from Jews and Gentiles

<p>
v25: As he also says in Hosea: “I will call those who were not my people ‘my people,’ and the one who was not loved ‘beloved.’”
v26: “And in the very place where they were told, ‘You are not my people,’ there they will be called ‘sons of the living God.’”
</p>

<p>
v27: And Isaiah cries out concerning Israel: “Though the number of the sons of Israel is like the sand of the sea, only the remnant will be saved,
v28: for the Lord will carry out his sentence on the earth completely and swiftly.”
v29: And just as Isaiah foretold: “If the Lord of Armies had not left us offspring, we would have become like Sodom and been made like Gomorrah.”
</p>

## Righteousness and the Stumbling Stone

<p>
v30: What then shall we say? Gentiles who were not pursuing righteousness obtained righteousness—a righteousness that comes from faith.
v31: But Israel, pursuing a law of righteousness, did not reach that law.
v32: Why not? Because they pursued it not by faith but as if by works. They stumbled over the stumbling stone,
v33: as it is written: “Look, I am placing in Zion a stone that causes stumbling and a rock that causes offense, and the one who believes in him will not be put to shame.”
</p>

## Notes

v03: Paul's impossible-sounding wish to be accursed expresses anguish for his own Israelite kin; it is not a curse pronounced on them.

v05: The compact Greek punctuation can connect ‘God blessed forever’ directly to the Messiah, as in the main text, or take it as a separate blessing of God.

v13–23: The language of hatred, hardening, wrath, and vessels is translated directly. The passage's objections and unanswered pressures are not softened into a complete theological system.

v26: ‘Sons of the living God’ retains the gendered title in Hosea's quotation.

## Vocabulary

v03: **ἀνάθεμα (anathema)**: Accursed, devoted to destruction, or cut off.

v18: **σκληρύνω (sklērynō)**: Harden or make resistant.

v21: **κεραμεύς (kerameus)**: Potter, one who shapes clay vessels.
