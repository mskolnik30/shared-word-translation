## Governing Authorities

<p>
v01: Let every person be subject to the governing authorities, because there is no authority except from God, and the authorities that exist have been put in place by God.
v02: Therefore, whoever resists the authority opposes what God has ordered, and those who oppose it will bring judgment on themselves.
v03: For rulers are not a terror to good conduct but to bad. Do you want to have no fear of the authority? Do what is good, and you will receive praise from it,
v04: because it is God’s servant for your good. But if you do evil, be afraid, because it does not carry the sword for nothing. It is God’s servant, an agent of wrath against the one who practices evil.
v05: Therefore it is necessary to be subject, not only because of wrath but also because of conscience.
v06: This is also why you pay taxes, because the authorities are God’s public servants, devoting themselves to this very task.
v07: Give everyone what is owed: tax to whom tax is owed, revenue to whom revenue, fear to whom fear, honor to whom honor.
</p>

## Love Fulfills the Law

<p>
v08: Owe no one anything except to love one another, because the one who loves another has fulfilled the Law.
v09: For the commandments, “Do not commit adultery, do not murder, do not steal, do not covet,” and any other commandment, are summed up in this word: “Love your neighbor as yourself.”
v10: Love does no harm to a neighbor. Therefore love is the fulfillment of the Law.
</p>

## The Day Has Drawn Near

<p>
v11: And do this knowing the time: the hour has already come for you to wake from sleep, because our salvation is nearer now than when we first trusted.
v12: The night is nearly over, and the day has drawn near. So let us lay aside the works of darkness and put on the weapons of light.
v13: Let us walk honorably, as in daylight—not in wild parties and drunkenness, not in sexual immorality and sensual excess, not in strife and jealousy.
v14: Instead, put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh to satisfy its desires.
</p>

## Notes

v01–07: Paul describes governing authority in terms of an ordered vocation toward good and the restraint of evil. The passage states a norm and obligation of subjection; it does not explicitly address every case in which rulers themselves become agents of evil.

v01: “Put in place/ordered by God” does not mean every decision or ruler is morally approved by God. Paul’s own description in vv03–04 defines authority by service toward good and judgment of evil.

v02: Resistance here is resistance to the ordered authority Paul has just described. Other biblical texts depict faithful resistance to rulers when commands directly oppose God; Fluent does not insert those other texts into Paul’s sentence, but this passage should not be isolated from them.

v04: `diakonos`—the same family used for Christian servants/ministers—describes governing authority as God’s servant, emphasizing accountable function rather than autonomous sovereignty.

v04: “Sword” names coercive punitive authority in the Roman setting. The verse does not develop a comprehensive theory of state violence.

v07: Paul echoes the language of giving what is owed, distinguishing tax, revenue, fear, and honor without saying any human authority is owed worship.

v08–10: Love is not set against the Law; love fulfills what the commandments seek in relation to the neighbor.

v11–14: Ethical urgency is eschatological: believers live as people of the approaching day.

v12: `hopla` is “weapons,” continuing the embodied conflict imagery used in Romans 6.

v14: “Flesh” remains the rebellious orientation/sphere of life, not the physical body as inherently evil.
