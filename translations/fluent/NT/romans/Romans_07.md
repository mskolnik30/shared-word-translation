## Released from the Law

<p>
v01: Or do you not know, brothers and sisters—for I am speaking to people who know the law—that the law rules over a person only as long as that person lives?
v02: For a married woman is bound by law to her husband while he is alive, but if her husband dies, she is released from the law concerning her husband.
v03: So while her husband is alive, she will be called an adulteress if she belongs to another man. But if her husband dies, she is free from that law and is not an adulteress if she belongs to another man.
v04: In the same way, my brothers and sisters, you also were put to death with respect to the law through the body of Christ, so that you might belong to another—to the one raised from the dead—in order that we might bear fruit for God.
v05: For when we were in the flesh, sinful passions aroused through the law were at work in our members to bear fruit for death.
v06: But now we have been released from the law, having died to what held us, so that we serve in the newness of the Spirit and not in the oldness of the written code.
</p>

## The Law and Sin

<p>
v07: What then shall we say? Is the law sin? Absolutely not! Yet I would not have known sin except through the law. For I would not have known coveting if the law had not said, “Do not covet.”
v08: But sin seized an opportunity through the commandment and produced in me every kind of coveting. For apart from the law, sin is dead.
v09: I once was alive apart from the law, but when the commandment came, sin sprang to life,
v10: and I died. The very commandment intended for life proved to bring death to me.
v11: For sin seized an opportunity through the commandment, deceived me, and through it killed me.
v12: So the law is holy, and the commandment is holy, righteous, and good.
v13: Did what is good, then, become death to me? Absolutely not! Rather, sin did, so that it might be shown to be sin by producing death in me through what is good, and through the commandment sin might become sinful beyond measure.
</p>

## The Conflict Within

<p>
v14: For we know that the law is spiritual, but I am made of flesh, sold under sin.
v15: I do not understand what I am doing. For I do not practice what I want, but I do what I hate.
v16: But if I do what I do not want, I agree that the law is good.
v17: So now it is no longer I who do it, but sin dwelling in me.
v18: For I know that nothing good dwells in me—that is, in my flesh. The desire to do what is good is present with me, but the ability to carry it out is not.
v19: For I do not do the good I want, but I practice the evil I do not want.
v20: But if I do what I do not want, it is no longer I who do it, but sin dwelling in me.
</p>

<p>
v21: So I find this law at work: when I want to do good, evil is present with me.
v22: For in my inner self I delight in God's law,
v23: but I see another law at work in my members, waging war against the law of my mind and taking me captive under the law of sin that is in my members.
v24: What a miserable man I am! Who will rescue me from this body of death?
v25: Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord! So then, I myself serve God's law with my mind, but with my flesh I serve sin's law.
</p>

## Notes

v07–13: Paul distinguishes the holy commandment from sin, which exploits the commandment to deceive and kill.

v14–25: The identity and life-setting of the first-person ‘I’ are disputed. The translation preserves Paul's present-tense conflict rather than assigning it to one doctrinal scheme.

v24: ‘Body of death’ is retained as Paul's severe bodily image, not reduced to an abstract problem.

## Vocabulary

v06: **γράμμα (gramma)**: Letter or written code.

v14: **σάρκινος (sarkinos)**: Made of flesh; fleshly or mortal.

v23: **αἰχμαλωτίζω (aichmalōtizō)**: Take captive as a prisoner of war.
