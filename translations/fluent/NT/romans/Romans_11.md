## God Has Not Rejected His People

<p>
v01: So I ask: Has God rejected his people? Absolutely not! For I too am an Israelite, a descendant of Abraham, from the tribe of Benjamin.
v02: God has not rejected his people whom he foreknew. Or do you not know what Scripture says in the passage about Elijah, how he appeals to God against Israel?
v03: “Lord, they have killed your prophets and torn down your altars. I alone am left, and they are seeking my life.”
v04: But what does the divine response say to him? “I have kept for myself seven thousand men who have not bowed the knee to Baal.”
v05: In the same way, then, at the present time there is a remnant chosen by grace.
v06: But if it is by grace, it is no longer by works; otherwise grace would no longer be grace.
</p>

## Israel’s Stumbling Is Not the End

<p>
v07: What then? Israel did not obtain what it was seeking, but the chosen remnant obtained it. The rest were hardened,
v08: as it is written: “God gave them a spirit of stupor, eyes that would not see and ears that would not hear, to this very day.”
v09: And David says, “Let their table become a snare, a trap, a stumbling block, and a retribution for them.
v10: Let their eyes be darkened so they cannot see, and bend their backs continually.”
</p>

<p>
v11: So I ask: Did they stumble in order to fall beyond recovery? Absolutely not! Rather, through their trespass salvation has come to the Gentiles, to make Israel jealous.
v12: Now if their trespass means riches for the world, and their loss means riches for the Gentiles, how much more will their fullness mean?
</p>

## Paul’s Ministry and Israel’s Future

<p>
v13: Now I am speaking to you Gentiles. Insofar as I am an apostle to the Gentiles, I honor my ministry,
v14: in the hope that I may somehow make my own people jealous and save some of them.
v15: For if their rejection means the reconciliation of the world, what will their acceptance mean but life from the dead?
v16: If the first portion of dough is holy, so is the whole batch; and if the root is holy, so are the branches.
</p>

## The Olive Tree

<p>
v17: But if some of the branches were broken off, and you, though a wild olive shoot, were grafted in among them and became a sharer in the rich root of the olive tree,
v18: do not boast over the branches. If you do boast, remember: you do not support the root; the root supports you.
v19: Then you will say, “Branches were broken off so that I might be grafted in.”
v20: True enough. They were broken off because of unbelief, but you stand by faith. Do not become arrogant; instead, be afraid.
v21: For if God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you.
v22: Consider, then, God's kindness and severity: severity toward those who fell, but God's kindness toward you, if you remain in his kindness. Otherwise, you too will be cut off.
v23: And even they, if they do not remain in unbelief, will be grafted in, for God is able to graft them in again.
v24: For if you were cut from what is by nature a wild olive tree and, contrary to nature, grafted into a cultivated olive tree, how much more readily will these natural branches be grafted back into their own olive tree?
</p>

## All Israel Will Be Saved

<p>
v25: For I do not want you to be unaware of this mystery, brothers and sisters, so that you will not be wise in your own estimation: a partial hardening has come upon Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles comes in,
v26: and in this way all Israel will be saved, as it is written: “The Deliverer will come from Zion; he will turn godlessness away from Jacob.
v27: And this will be my covenant with them when I take away their sins.”
v28: With respect to the good news, they are enemies for your sake; but with respect to election, they are beloved because of the patriarchs.
v29: For God's gracious gifts and calling are irrevocable.
v30: For just as you once disobeyed God but now have received mercy because of their disobedience,
v31: so they too have now disobeyed because of the mercy shown to you, so that they too may now receive mercy.
v32: For God has confined everyone under disobedience so that he may have mercy on everyone.
</p>

## Doxology

<p>
v33: Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable his judgments and untraceable his ways!
v34: “For who has known the mind of the Lord? Or who has become his counselor?”
v35: “Or who has first given to him, that it should be repaid?”
v36: For from him and through him and to him are all things. To him be the glory forever. Amen.
</p>

## Notes

v17–24: Gentile believers are pictured as a wild shoot grafted among Israel's branches. The root supports them, and the warning explicitly rules out boasting over the branches.

v25–27: ‘All Israel’ and ‘in this way’ have several substantial interpretations. The main text preserves Paul's wording without identifying Israel with one later theory.

v28: ‘Enemies’ describes a relation ‘with respect to the gospel’ in Paul's argument; the same people remain beloved with respect to election.

v32: The two occurrences of ‘everyone’ are retained: all are confined in disobedience so that God may show mercy to all.

## Vocabulary

v05: **λεῖμμα (leimma)**: A remnant, what remains.

v17: **ἐγκεντρίζω (enkentrizō)**: Graft a shoot into a tree.

v25: **μυστήριον (mystērion)**: A divine purpose once hidden and now disclosed.
