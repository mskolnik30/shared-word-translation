## God Has Not Rejected His People

<p>
v01: I ask, then: Has God rejected his people? Absolutely not! I myself am an Israelite, a descendant of Abraham, from the tribe of Benjamin.
v02: God has not rejected his people whom he foreknew. Or do you not know what Scripture says in the story of Elijah, how he appeals to God against Israel?
v03: “Lord, they killed your prophets, tore down your altars, and I alone am left, and they are seeking my life.”
v04: But what does the divine answer say to him? “I have kept for myself seven thousand who have not bowed the knee to Baal.”
v05: In the same way, then, at the present time there is a remnant according to the choosing of grace.
v06: But if it is by grace, it is no longer from works; otherwise grace would no longer be grace.
</p>

## Israel’s Stumbling Is Not the End

<p>
v07: What then? Israel did not obtain what it was seeking, but the chosen obtained it. The rest were hardened,
v08: just as it is written,
“God gave them a spirit of stupor,
eyes that would not see
and ears that would not hear,
to this very day.”
v09: And David says,
“Let their table become a snare and a trap,
a stumbling block and a repayment for them.
v10: Let their eyes be darkened so they cannot see,
and keep their backs bent down.”
</p>

<p>
v11: I ask, then: Did they stumble so that they would fall beyond recovery? Absolutely not! Rather, through their trespass salvation has come to the Gentiles, to make Israel jealous.
v12: Now if their trespass means riches for the world, and their loss means riches for the Gentiles, how much more will their fullness mean!
</p>

## Paul’s Ministry and Israel’s Future

<p>
v13: I am speaking to you Gentiles. Since I am an apostle to the Gentiles, I honor my ministry,
v14: in the hope that somehow I may make my own people jealous and save some of them.
v15: For if their rejection means reconciliation for the world, what will their acceptance mean but life from the dead?
v16: If the first portion of dough is holy, so is the whole batch; and if the root is holy, so are the branches.
</p>

## The Olive Tree

<p>
v17: But if some of the branches were broken off, and you, though a wild olive shoot, were grafted in among them and became a sharer in the rich root of the olive tree,
v18: do not boast over the branches. If you do boast, remember: you do not support the root; the root supports you.
v19: You will say, “Branches were broken off so that I might be grafted in.”
v20: True. They were broken off because of lack of trust, and you stand through trust. Do not become arrogant, but fear.
v21: For if God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you.
v22: Consider, then, God’s kindness and severity: severity toward those who fell, but God’s kindness toward you, if you continue in his kindness. Otherwise you too will be cut off.
v23: And they also, if they do not continue in lack of trust, will be grafted in, because God is able to graft them in again.
v24: For if you were cut from a wild olive tree by nature and, contrary to nature, were grafted into a cultivated olive tree, how much more will these natural branches be grafted back into their own olive tree!
</p>

## All Israel Will Be Saved

<p>
v25: I do not want you to be unaware of this mystery, brothers and sisters, so that you do not become wise in your own eyes: a partial hardening has come upon Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles comes in,
v26: and in this way all Israel will be saved, just as it is written,
“The Deliverer will come from Zion;
he will turn ungodliness away from Jacob.
v27: And this will be my covenant with them
when I take away their sins.”
v28: With respect to the good news, they are enemies for your sake; but with respect to election, they are beloved because of the ancestors.
v29: For God’s gifts and calling are irrevocable.
v30: Just as you once disobeyed God but now have received mercy because of their disobedience,
v31: so they too have now disobeyed so that, through the mercy shown to you, they too may now receive mercy.
v32: For God has enclosed everyone in disobedience so that he may show mercy to everyone.
</p>

## Doxology

<p>
v33: Oh, the depth of God’s riches and wisdom and knowledge!
How unsearchable are his judgments
and beyond tracing out his ways!
v34: “For who has known the Lord’s mind?
Or who has been his counselor?”
v35: “Or who has first given to him,
so that it must be repaid?”
v36: For from him and through him and to him are all things.
To him be the glory forever. Amen.
</p>

## Notes

v01–02: Paul asks the controlling question directly and answers it emphatically: God has not rejected his people. Readings of Romans 9–11 must not contradict this statement.

v05: Remnant language describes continuity within Israel, not replacement of Israel by a non-Jewish people.

v11: The Greek question expects Paul’s emphatic denial. Fluent makes the force explicit with “fall beyond recovery” while retaining the contrast between stumbling and final fall.

v12,15: Paul imagines a positive future “fullness” and “acceptance” for Israel beyond the present situation.

v17–24: Gentile believers are grafted into Israel’s cultivated olive tree and warned explicitly against boasting over Jewish branches. The root supports the graft, not the reverse.

v20: `apistia` can be unbelief/unfaithfulness/lack of trust. “Lack of trust” keeps the relational contrast with Gentiles standing through trust.

v23–24: Re-grafting is not a marginal possibility; Paul stresses God’s ability and the natural branches’ belonging to “their own olive tree.”

v25: The hardening is partial and temporally bounded “until” the fullness of Gentiles comes in.

v26: “And in this way all Israel will be saved” is preserved rather than changed to “then” or reduced to a remnant. The identity/scope of “all Israel” is debated, but Paul’s immediate subject remains Israel in contrast with Gentiles.

v28–29: Paul can describe present opposition to the gospel while simultaneously declaring Israel beloved according to election and God’s calling irrevocable.

v32: The symmetrical movement from disobedience to mercy climaxes the argument before doxology.
