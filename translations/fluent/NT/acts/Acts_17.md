## Thessalonica: The Christ Had to Suffer and Rise

<p>
v01: Paul and Silas traveled through Amphipolis and Apollonia and came to Thessalonica, where there was a Jewish synagogue.
v02: As was his custom, Paul went in to them. For three Sabbaths he reasoned with them from the Scriptures,
v03: explaining and demonstrating that the Messiah had to suffer and rise from the dead. “This Jesus I am proclaiming to you is the Messiah,” he said.
v04: Some of them were persuaded and joined Paul and Silas, along with a large number of devout Greeks and not a few prominent women.
v05: But some Jewish people became jealous. They recruited wicked men from the marketplace, formed a mob, and set the city in an uproar. They attacked Jason’s house, trying to bring Paul and Silas out to the public assembly.
v06: When they could not find them, they dragged Jason and some other believers before the city officials, shouting, “These men who have turned the world upside down have come here too,
v07: and Jason has welcomed them. They all act against Caesar’s decrees, saying there is another king—Jesus.”
v08: When the crowd and city officials heard these things, they were disturbed.
v09: After taking security from Jason and the others, the officials released them.
</p>

## Berea: Receptive and Examining

<p>
v10: That very night the brothers and sisters sent Paul and Silas away to Berea. When they arrived, they went into the Jewish synagogue.
v11: The people there were more receptive than those in Thessalonica. They welcomed the word with great eagerness and examined the Scriptures every day to see whether these things were so.
v12: Many of them believed, including not a few prominent Greek women and men.
v13: But when some Jewish people from Thessalonica learned that Paul was also proclaiming God’s word in Berea, they came there too, agitating and stirring up the crowds.
v14: The brothers and sisters immediately sent Paul away toward the sea, but Silas and Timothy remained there.
v15: Those escorting Paul brought him as far as Athens. Then they left with instructions for Silas and Timothy to join him as soon as possible.
</p>

## Paul in Athens

<p>
v16: While Paul waited for them in Athens, his spirit was deeply troubled when he saw that the city was full of idols.
v17: So he reasoned in the synagogue with Jewish people and others who worshiped God, and every day in the marketplace with anyone who happened to be there.
v18: Some Epicurean and Stoic philosophers also debated with him. Some asked, “What is this babbler trying to say?” Others said, “He seems to be proclaiming foreign gods.” They said this because he was proclaiming the good news about Jesus and the resurrection.
v19: They took him and brought him to the Areopagus, asking, “May we know what this new teaching is that you are presenting?
v20: You are bringing some strange things to our ears, and we want to know what they mean.”
v21: Now all the Athenians and visiting foreigners spent their time doing nothing but telling or hearing something new.
</p>

## Paul at the Areopagus

<p>
v22: Paul stood in the middle of the Areopagus and said, “People of Athens, I see that you are very religious in every way.
v23: For as I walked around and examined your objects of worship, I even found an altar inscribed, ‘To an unknown god.’ Therefore, what you worship without knowing, this I proclaim to you.
v24: The God who made the world and everything in it is Lord of heaven and earth and does not live in temples made by human hands.
v25: Nor is he served by human hands as though he needed anything, since he himself gives everyone life, breath, and everything else.
v26: From one ancestor he made every nation of humanity to live over the whole face of the earth, determining their appointed times and the boundaries of where they would live.
v27: He did this so that they might seek God and perhaps reach out for him and find him, though he is not far from each one of us.
v28: ‘For in him we live and move and exist.’ As some of your own poets have said, ‘For we too are his offspring.’
v29: Since we are God’s offspring, we should not think that the divine nature is like gold, silver, or stone—an image shaped by human art and imagination.
v30: God overlooked the times of ignorance, but now he commands all people everywhere to repent,
v31: because he has set a day when he will judge the world with justice through a man he has appointed. He has given everyone assurance of this by raising him from the dead.”
v32: When they heard about the resurrection of the dead, some mocked, but others said, “We will hear you again about this.”
v33: So Paul left them.
v34: But some people joined him and believed, including Dionysius the Areopagite, a woman named Damaris, and others with them.
</p>

## Notes

v18: ‘Babbler’ literally evokes a seed-picker and functions as a dismissive insult.

v22: The description can mean very religious or superstitious; Paul begins with an observation rather than a simple compliment.

v28: Paul quotes language familiar from Greek poets while directing the argument toward the Creator.

## Vocabulary

v18: **ἀνάστασις (anastasis)**: Resurrection.

v23: **ἄγνωστος (agnōstos)**: Unknown.
