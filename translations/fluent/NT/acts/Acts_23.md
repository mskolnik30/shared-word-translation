## Paul Before the Council

<p>
v01: Paul looked intently at the council and said, “Brothers, I have lived before God with an entirely clear conscience to this very day.”
v02: At this, the high priest Ananias ordered those standing near Paul to strike him on the mouth.
v03: Then Paul said to him, “God is about to strike you, you whitewashed wall! You sit there judging me according to the Law, yet contrary to the Law you order me to be struck?”
v04: Those standing nearby said, “Do you insult God's high priest?”
v05: Paul said, “Brothers, I did not know he was the high priest, for it is written: ‘You must not speak evil of a ruler of your people.’”
v06: When Paul realized that one part of the council were Sadducees and the other Pharisees, he called out, “Brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees. I am on trial concerning the hope and resurrection of the dead!”
v07: When he said this, a dispute broke out between the Pharisees and Sadducees, and the assembly was divided.
v08: For the Sadducees say there is no resurrection, angel, or spirit, while the Pharisees affirm them all.
v09: A great outcry arose, and some scribes from the Pharisees' party stood up and argued forcefully: “We find nothing wrong with this man. What if a spirit or an angel spoke to him?”
v10: As the dispute became violent, the commander feared that Paul would be torn apart by them. He ordered the troops to go down, take him from their midst by force, and bring him into the barracks.
v11: The following night the Lord stood beside Paul and said, “Take courage! For just as you have testified about me in Jerusalem, so you must also testify in Rome.”
</p>

## A Plot to Kill Paul

<p>
v12: When day came, some Jewish men formed a conspiracy and bound themselves under a curse, saying they would neither eat nor drink until they had killed Paul.
v13: More than forty men joined this plot.
v14: They went to the chief priests and elders and said, “We have bound ourselves under a solemn curse to taste nothing until we have killed Paul.
v15: Now then, you and the council should notify the commander to bring him down to you, as though you intended to examine his case more carefully. We are ready to kill him before he comes near.”
</p>

## Paul’s Nephew Reports the Ambush

<p>
v16: But Paul's sister's son heard about the ambush. He went to the barracks, entered, and told Paul.
v17: Paul called one of the centurions and said, “Take this young man to the commander, for he has something to report to him.”
v18: So the centurion took him to the commander and said, “The prisoner Paul called me and asked me to bring this young man to you because he has something to tell you.”
v19: The commander took him by the hand, drew him aside privately, and asked, “What is it that you have to report to me?”
v20: He said, “The Jewish leaders have agreed to ask you to bring Paul down to the council tomorrow, pretending they intend to inquire more carefully about him.
v21: Do not be persuaded by them, because more than forty of their men are waiting in ambush for him. They have bound themselves under a curse neither to eat nor drink until they kill him. They are ready now, waiting for your consent.”
v22: The commander dismissed the young man, instructing him, “Tell no one that you have disclosed these things to me.”
</p>

## Paul Is Sent to Felix

<p>
v23: Then he summoned two centurions and said, “Prepare two hundred soldiers, seventy horsemen, and two hundred spearmen to go as far as Caesarea at nine tonight.
v24: Provide mounts for Paul so they may bring him safely to Governor Felix.”
v25: He wrote a letter in this form:
v26: “Claudius Lysias, to His Excellency Governor Felix: Greetings.
v27: This man was seized by some Jewish people and was about to be killed by them. I came with the troops and rescued him when I learned that he was a Roman citizen.
v28: Wanting to know the reason they were accusing him, I brought him down before their council.
v29: I found that he was accused about questions of their Law, but there was no charge against him deserving death or imprisonment.
v30: When I was informed of a plot against the man, I sent him to you at once and ordered his accusers also to state their case against him before you.”
v31: So the soldiers followed their orders, took Paul, and brought him by night to Antipatris.
v32: The next day they let the horsemen continue with him and returned to the barracks.
v33: When the horsemen entered Caesarea, they delivered the letter to the governor and presented Paul to him.
v34: After reading the letter, the governor asked which province Paul was from. Learning that he was from Cilicia,
v35: he said, “I will hear your case when your accusers arrive as well.” Then he ordered Paul to be kept under guard in Herod's headquarters.
</p>

## Notes

v05: Paul says he did not know Ananias was high priest; the text does not explain why.

v08: ‘Both’ gathers the disputed realities of angel and spirit alongside resurrection; the concise Greek need not define their relation.

v23: The escort comprises two hundred infantry, seventy cavalry, and two hundred spearmen.

## Vocabulary

v06: **ἀνάστασις (anastasis)**: Resurrection.

v23: **δεξιολάβος (dexiolabos)**: A soldier described by a rare term, probably a spearman or light-armed guard.
