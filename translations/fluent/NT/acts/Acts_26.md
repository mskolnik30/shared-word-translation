## Paul’s Defense Before Agrippa

<p>
v01: Agrippa said to Paul, “You have permission to speak for yourself.” Then Paul stretched out his hand and began his defense:
v02: “King Agrippa, I consider myself fortunate that today I can defend myself before you against all the accusations made by some Jewish people,
v03: especially because you are knowledgeable about all the Jewish customs and controversies. Therefore I ask you to listen to me patiently.
v04: All the Jewish people know how I lived from my youth, from the beginning among my own nation and in Jerusalem.
v05: They have known me for a long time and can testify, if they are willing, that I lived as a Pharisee according to the strictest party of our religion.
v06: And now I stand on trial because of the hope of the promise God made to our ancestors,
v07: the promise our twelve tribes hope to attain as they earnestly worship God night and day. It is because of this hope, O king, that I am accused by some Jewish people.
v08: Why should any of you consider it unbelievable that God raises the dead?
</p>

## Paul Describes His Former Persecution

<p>
v09: “I myself was convinced that I ought to do many things against the name of Jesus the Nazarene.
v10: And that is what I did in Jerusalem. With authority from the chief priests, I locked many of the holy ones in prison, and when they were put to death, I cast my vote against them.
v11: In synagogue after synagogue I often punished them and tried to force them to blaspheme. I was so violently enraged against them that I persecuted them even in foreign cities.
</p>

## The Heavenly Vision and Commission

<p>
v12: “While doing this, I traveled to Damascus with authority and commission from the chief priests.
v13: At midday, O king, I saw on the road a light from heaven brighter than the sun, shining around me and those traveling with me.
v14: We all fell to the ground, and I heard a voice saying to me in the Hebrew language, ‘Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads.’
v15: I asked, ‘Who are you, Lord?’ The Lord answered, ‘I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.
v16: But get up and stand on your feet. I have appeared to you for this purpose: to appoint you as a servant and witness both to what you have seen of me and to what I will show you.
v17: I will rescue you from your own people and from the Gentiles. I am sending you to them
v18: to open their eyes so they may turn from darkness to light and from Satan’s authority to God, so that they may receive forgiveness of sins and an inheritance among those made holy by trust in me.’
</p>

## Paul Was Not Disobedient to the Vision

<p>
v19: “Therefore, King Agrippa, I was not disobedient to the heavenly vision.
v20: Instead, first to those in Damascus, then in Jerusalem and throughout all Judea, and also to the Gentiles, I proclaimed that they should repent, turn to God, and do works worthy of repentance.
v21: This is why some Jewish people seized me in the temple and tried to kill me.
v22: But with help from God I have stood to this day, testifying to both small and great. I say nothing beyond what the Prophets and Moses said was going to happen:
v23: that the Christ would suffer and, as the first to rise from the dead, would proclaim light both to our people and to the Gentiles.”
</p>

## Festus and Agrippa Respond

<p>
v24: As Paul was saying these things in his defense, Festus shouted, “Paul, you are out of your mind! Your great learning is driving you insane!”
v25: Paul answered, “I am not out of my mind, most excellent Festus. I am speaking words of truth and sound judgment.
v26: The king knows about these matters, and I speak to him boldly because I am convinced none of this has escaped his notice. This was not done in a corner.
v27: King Agrippa, do you trust the Prophets? I know that you do.”
v28: Agrippa said to Paul, “In such a short time are you trying to persuade me to become a Christian?”
v29: Paul replied, “Whether in a short time or a long time, I pray to God that not only you but everyone listening to me today might become as I am—except for these chains.”
v30: Then the king stood up, along with the governor, Bernice, and those sitting with them.
v31: After withdrawing, they said to one another, “This man is doing nothing deserving death or imprisonment.”
v32: Agrippa said to Festus, “This man could have been released if he had not appealed to Caesar.”
</p>

## Notes

v02–08: Paul frames the case as an intra-Jewish dispute over Israel’s ancestral promise and resurrection hope, not as abandonment of his people.

v05: Paul explicitly calls Pharisaic Judaism “our religion,” preserving his self-location within Jewish identity.

v06–08: The “hope of the promise” culminates in the question, “Why should it be unbelievable that God raises the dead?”

v09–11: Paul’s testimony does not minimize his own violence. He names imprisonment, death votes, coercion, and rage.

v14: The proverb about kicking against goads appears in this third retelling of Paul’s encounter and is not supplied to Acts 9 or 22.

v16–18: The commission is described in prophetic vocation language: servant, witness, opening eyes, turning darkness to light, liberation from Satan’s authority, forgiveness, and inheritance.

v18: `pistis eis eme` is translated “trust in me” here; the phrase explicitly locates the sanctified community’s inheritance in relation to Jesus.

v20: Repentance is expected to become visible in actions “worthy of repentance,” echoing John the Baptist in Luke–Acts.

v22: Paul insists his message says nothing beyond Moses and the Prophets, continuing Acts’ scriptural-continuity claim.

v23: Jesus is described as suffering Messiah and first to rise, bringing light to both Israel and Gentiles.

v28: Agrippa’s sentence can be heard as sincere, ironic, or evasive. Fluent preserves the question without choosing his tone.

v29: Paul turns his legal defense into personal appeal but explicitly wishes his hearers to share his faith without sharing his chains.

v31–32: Agrippa, Festus, and the others agree that Paul has done nothing deserving death or imprisonment.
