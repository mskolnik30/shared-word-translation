## Paul’s Defense Before Agrippa

<p>
v01: Agrippa said to Paul, “You have permission to speak for yourself.” Then Paul stretched out his hand and began his defense:
v02: “King Agrippa, I consider myself fortunate that I am to make my defense before you today against all the accusations brought by Jewish leaders,
v03: especially because you are well acquainted with all the Jewish customs and controversies. Therefore I beg you to listen to me patiently.
v04: “All the Jewish people know how I have lived from my youth, from the beginning among my own nation and in Jerusalem.
v05: They have known me from long ago—if they are willing to testify—that according to the strictest party of our religion, I lived as a Pharisee.
v06: And now I stand on trial because of my hope in the promise God made to our ancestors,
v07: the promise our twelve tribes hope to attain as they earnestly serve God night and day. It is for this hope, O king, that I am accused by Jewish leaders.
v08: Why is it considered unbelievable among you that God raises the dead?
</p>

## Paul Describes His Former Persecution

<p>
v09: “I myself was convinced that I ought to do many things in opposition to the name of Jesus the Nazarene.
v10: And that is what I did in Jerusalem. With authority from the chief priests, I locked up many of the holy ones in prison, and when they were put to death, I cast my vote against them.
v11: I punished them often in all the synagogues and tried to force them to blaspheme. I was so furiously enraged against them that I persecuted them even in foreign cities.
</p>

## The Heavenly Vision and Commission

<p>
v12: “On one such occasion I was traveling to Damascus with authority and a commission from the chief priests.
v13: At midday on the road, O king, I saw a light from heaven brighter than the sun shining around me and those traveling with me.
v14: When we had all fallen to the ground, I heard a voice saying to me in the Hebrew language, ‘Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads.’
v15: I said, ‘Who are you, Lord?’ The Lord answered, ‘I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.
v16: But get up and stand on your feet. I have appeared to you for this purpose: to appoint you as a servant and witness both of what you have seen of me and of what I will show you.
v17: I will rescue you from your own people and from the Gentiles—to whom I am sending you
v18: to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light and from the authority of Satan to God, and receive forgiveness of sins and a place among those sanctified by faith in me.’
</p>

## Paul Was Not Disobedient to the Vision

<p>
v19: “Therefore, King Agrippa, I was not disobedient to the heavenly vision.
v20: Instead, I declared first to those in Damascus, then in Jerusalem and throughout all Judea, and also to the Gentiles, that they should repent and turn to God, doing deeds worthy of repentance.
v21: For this reason, some Jewish leaders seized me in the temple and tried to kill me.
v22: But I have received help from God to this day, and so I stand here testifying to both small and great. I say nothing beyond what the Prophets and Moses said would happen:
v23: that the Messiah would suffer and, as the first to rise from the dead, would proclaim light both to our people and to the Gentiles.”
</p>

## Festus and Agrippa Respond

<p>
v24: As Paul was making this defense, Festus said loudly, “You are out of your mind, Paul! Your great learning is driving you insane.”
v25: But Paul said, “I am not out of my mind, Most Excellent Festus. I am speaking words of truth and sound judgment.
v26: The king knows about these matters, and I speak boldly to him. I am convinced that none of this has escaped his notice, for it was not done in a corner.
v27: King Agrippa, do you believe the Prophets? I know that you believe.”
v28: Agrippa said to Paul, “In such a short time, are you trying to persuade me to become a Christian?”
v29: Paul replied, “Whether in a short time or a long time, I pray to God that not only you but also everyone listening to me today may become as I am—except for these chains.”
v30: Then the king stood up, along with the governor, Bernice, and those seated with them.
v31: After withdrawing, they said to one another, “This man is doing nothing deserving death or imprisonment.”
v32: Agrippa said to Festus, “This man could have been released if he had not appealed to Caesar.”
</p>

## Notes

v14: ‘Kick against the goads’ is a proverb for futile resistance to forceful direction.

v18: The commission uses sight, darkness and light, dominion, forgiveness, and inheritance imagery together.

v28: Agrippa’s phrase can be heard as a question or assertion about a short time; the main text makes the rhetorical force explicit without claiming certainty.

## Vocabulary

v18: **ἄφεσις (aphesis)**: Release or forgiveness.

v29: **δεσμός (desmos)**: Bond or chain.
