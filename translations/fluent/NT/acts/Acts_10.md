## Cornelius Sees a Vision

<p>
v01: In Caesarea there was a man named Cornelius, a centurion in what was called the Italian Cohort.
v02: He and his whole household were devout and feared God. He gave generously to the people and prayed to God continually.
v03: One day at about three in the afternoon, he clearly saw in a vision an angel of God come to him and say, “Cornelius!”
v04: Cornelius stared at him in fear and asked, “What is it, Lord?” The angel replied, “Your prayers and gifts to the poor have risen before God as a memorial.
v05: Now send men to Joppa and bring back a man named Simon who is called Peter.
v06: He is staying with Simon, a tanner whose house is by the sea.”
v07: When the angel who spoke to him had left, Cornelius called two of his household servants and a devout soldier who was one of his attendants.
v08: After explaining everything to them, he sent them to Joppa.
</p>

## Peter Sees a Vision

<p>
v09: The next day, as they were traveling and approaching the city, Peter went up on the roof to pray at about noon.
v10: He became hungry and wanted something to eat. While the meal was being prepared, he fell into a trance
v11: and saw heaven opened and something like a large sheet being lowered to earth by its four corners.
v12: In it were all kinds of four-footed animals, reptiles of the earth, and birds of the sky.
v13: Then a voice said to him, “Get up, Peter. Kill and eat.”
v14: But Peter replied, “Certainly not, Lord! I have never eaten anything defiled or unclean.”
v15: The voice spoke to him a second time: “What God has made clean, you must not call defiled.”
v16: This happened three times, and immediately the object was taken back up into heaven.
</p>

## The Spirit Sends Peter

<p>
v17: While Peter was deeply puzzled about what the vision might mean, the men Cornelius had sent found Simon’s house and stood at the gate.
v18: They called out and asked whether Simon called Peter was staying there.
v19: As Peter continued thinking about the vision, the Spirit said to him, “Look, three men are searching for you.
v20: Get up, go downstairs, and go with them without hesitation, because I have sent them.”
v21: Peter went down to the men and said, “I am the one you are looking for. Why have you come?”
v22: They replied, “Cornelius, a centurion who is righteous and fears God and is well spoken of by the whole Jewish nation, was directed by a holy angel to send for you to come to his house and hear what you have to say.”
v23: So Peter invited them in and gave them lodging. The next day he got up and went with them, and some believers from Joppa accompanied him.
</p>

## Peter Enters Cornelius’ House

<p>
v24: The following day he entered Caesarea. Cornelius was waiting for them and had called together his relatives and close friends.
v25: As Peter entered, Cornelius met him, fell at his feet, and bowed down before him.
v26: But Peter raised him up and said, “Stand up. I too am only a human being.”
v27: Talking with him, Peter went inside and found many people gathered.
v28: He told them, “You know that it is considered unlawful for a Jewish man to associate with or visit a foreigner. But God has shown me that I must not call any person defiled or unclean.
v29: That is why I came without objection when I was sent for. So I ask: Why did you send for me?”
v30: Cornelius replied, “Four days ago at this very hour, about three in the afternoon, I was praying in my house. Suddenly a man in shining clothing stood before me
v31: and said, ‘Cornelius, your prayer has been heard, and your gifts to the poor have been remembered before God.
v32: Send to Joppa and invite Simon called Peter. He is staying by the sea in the house of Simon the tanner.’
v33: So I sent for you immediately, and you have been kind enough to come. Now all of us are here before God to hear everything the Lord has commanded you to tell us.”
</p>

## God Shows No Partiality

<p>
v34: Peter began to speak: “Now I truly understand that God does not show favoritism,
v35: but in every nation anyone who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him.
v36: This is the message God sent to the people of Israel, proclaiming the good news of peace through Jesus Christ—he is Lord of all.
v37: You know what happened throughout Judea, beginning in Galilee after the baptism John proclaimed:
v38: how God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and power. He went around doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, because God was with him.
v39: We are witnesses of everything he did in the land of the Jewish people and in Jerusalem. They killed him by hanging him on a tree,
v40: but God raised him on the third day and allowed him to be seen,
v41: not by all the people, but by witnesses God had chosen beforehand—by us, who ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead.
v42: He commanded us to proclaim to the people and testify that he is the one appointed by God as judge of the living and the dead.
v43: All the prophets testify about him that everyone who believes in him receives forgiveness of sins through his name.”
</p>

## The Holy Spirit Falls on Gentiles

<p>
v44: While Peter was still speaking these words, the Holy Spirit fell on everyone who was listening to the message.
v45: The circumcised believers who had come with Peter were astonished because the gift of the Holy Spirit had been poured out even on the Gentiles.
v46: For they heard them speaking in other languages and magnifying God. Then Peter said,
v47: “Can anyone withhold water and prevent these people from being baptized, since they have received the Holy Spirit just as we have?”
v48: So he ordered them to be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ. Then they asked him to stay for several days.
</p>

## Notes

v01: A centurion ordinarily commanded about one hundred soldiers; the Italian Cohort identifies Cornelius’s military unit.

v14–15: Peter’s vision uses food categories, but Peter later states its human implication: he must not call any person defiled or unclean.

v28: Peter describes a serious inherited boundary around association with foreigners, then explicitly attributes its overturning to God.

v34–35: God’s impartiality is expressed in welcome across nations. The lines do not claim that human merit purchases grace.

v45–48: The Spirit is poured out before these Gentile hearers are baptized in water, removing the circumcised believers’ stated reason to withhold baptism.

## Vocabulary

v34: **προσωπολήμπτης (prosōpolēmptēs)**: One who shows partiality or judges by status.

v36: **εἰρήνη (eirēnē)**: Peace, wholeness, and restored well-being.

v43: **ἄφεσις (aphesis)**: Release or forgiveness.
