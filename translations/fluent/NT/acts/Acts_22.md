## Paul’s Defense to the Jerusalem Crowd

<p>
v01: “Brothers and fathers, listen now to my defense before you.”
v02: When they heard him addressing them in the Hebrew language, they became even quieter. Then he said:
v03: “I am a Jewish man, born in Tarsus of Cilicia but brought up in this city at the feet of Gamaliel. I was educated in strict conformity with the law of our ancestors and was zealous for God, just as all of you are today.
v04: I persecuted this Way to the death, binding and delivering to prison both men and women,
v05: as the high priest and the whole council of elders can testify about me. I even received letters from them to our fellow Jews in Damascus, and I went there to bring those who were there as prisoners to Jerusalem to be punished.
</p>

## Paul Retells His Encounter with Jesus

<p>
v06: “As I was traveling and approaching Damascus, about noon a brilliant light from heaven suddenly flashed around me.
v07: I fell to the ground and heard a voice saying to me, ‘Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?’
v08: I answered, ‘Who are you, Lord?’ He said to me, ‘I am Jesus the Nazarene, whom you are persecuting.’
v09: Those who were with me saw the light but did not understand the voice of the one speaking to me.
v10: I asked, ‘What should I do, Lord?’ The Lord said to me, ‘Get up and go into Damascus. There you will be told everything you have been appointed to do.’
v11: Since I could not see because of the glory of that light, those with me led me by the hand, and I came into Damascus.
v12: “A man named Ananias, devout according to the Law and well spoken of by all the Jewish people living there,
v13: came to me, stood beside me, and said, ‘Brother Saul, regain your sight.’ At that very moment I looked up and saw him.
v14: He said, ‘The God of our ancestors has chosen you to know his will, to see the Righteous One, and to hear a voice from his own mouth,
v15: because you will be his witness to all people of what you have seen and heard.
v16: And now why delay? Get up, be baptized, and wash away your sins, calling on his name.’
</p>

## Paul Is Sent to the Gentiles

<p>
v17: “After I returned to Jerusalem and was praying in the temple, I fell into a trance
v18: and saw him saying to me, ‘Hurry and leave Jerusalem quickly, because they will not accept your testimony about me.’
v19: I said, ‘Lord, they themselves know that in synagogue after synagogue I imprisoned and beat those who believed in you.
v20: And when the blood of your witness Stephen was being shed, I myself stood there approving and guarding the clothes of those who killed him.’
v21: Then he said to me, ‘Go, for I will send you far away to the Gentiles.’”
</p>

## Paul’s Roman Citizenship Stops the Scourging

<p>
v22: They listened to him up to this statement. Then they raised their voices and said, “Remove such a man from the earth! He should not be allowed to live!”
v23: As they were shouting, throwing off their cloaks, and tossing dust into the air,
v24: the commander ordered Paul to be brought into the barracks and said he should be interrogated by flogging, so that he might learn why they were shouting against him like this.
v25: But when they stretched him out with the straps, Paul said to the centurion standing there, “Is it lawful for you to flog a Roman citizen who has not been condemned?”
v26: When the centurion heard this, he went to the commander and reported it, saying, “What are you about to do? This man is a Roman citizen.”
v27: The commander came and asked Paul, “Tell me, are you a Roman citizen?” He said, “Yes.”
v28: The commander replied, “I acquired this citizenship for a large sum of money.” Paul said, “But I was born a citizen.”
v29: Immediately those who were about to interrogate him withdrew. The commander was also afraid when he learned that Paul was a Roman citizen and that he had bound him.
v30: The next day, wanting to know for certain why Paul was being accused by the Jewish leaders, the commander released him and ordered the chief priests and the entire council to meet. Then he brought Paul down and set him before them.
</p>

## Notes

v09: The companions see the light but do not understand the voice; the translation distinguishes hearing a sound from comprehending speech.

v25–29: The threatened flogging and Paul’s Roman citizenship are stated as legal and bodily realities.

v16: Baptism, washing, sins, and calling on the name appear together without the note imposing a later ritual theory.

## Vocabulary

v14: **δίκαιος (dikaios)**: Righteous or just; here a title.

v28: **πολιτεία (politeia)**: Citizenship.
