## Barnabas and Saul Are Sent

<p>
v01: In the church at Antioch there were prophets and teachers: Barnabas; Simeon called Niger; Lucius of Cyrene; Manaen, a close companion of Herod the tetrarch; and Saul.
v02: While they were worshiping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said, “Set apart Barnabas and Saul for me for the work to which I have called them.”
v03: Then, after fasting and praying, they laid their hands on them and sent them away.
</p>

## Cyprus: Saul Is Also Called Paul

<p>
v04: So, sent out by the Holy Spirit, they went down to Seleucia and sailed from there to Cyprus.
v05: When they arrived at Salamis, they proclaimed the word of God in the Jewish synagogues. They also had John as their assistant.
v06: After traveling across the whole island as far as Paphos, they found a Jewish man named Bar-Jesus who was a magician and false prophet.
v07: He was with the proconsul Sergius Paulus, an intelligent man. The proconsul summoned Barnabas and Saul because he wanted to hear the word of God.
v08: But Elymas the magician—for that is how his name is translated—opposed them and tried to turn the proconsul away from the faith.
v09: Then Saul, who was also called Paul, filled with the Holy Spirit, stared at him
v10: and said, “You are full of every kind of deceit and fraud, you son of the devil and enemy of everything right! Will you never stop twisting the Lord’s straight ways?
v11: Now look—the Lord’s hand is against you. You will be blind and unable to see the sun for a time.” Immediately mist and darkness fell over him, and he went around seeking someone to lead him by the hand.
v12: When the proconsul saw what had happened, he believed, astonished at the teaching about the Lord.
</p>

## Paul Speaks in Pisidian Antioch

<p>
v13: Paul and his companions sailed from Paphos and came to Perga in Pamphylia. But John left them and returned to Jerusalem.
v14: They continued from Perga and reached Pisidian Antioch. On the Sabbath day they entered the synagogue and sat down.
v15: After the reading from the Law and the Prophets, the synagogue leaders sent word to them: “Brothers, if you have a word of encouragement for the people, speak.”
v16: Paul stood, motioned with his hand, and said, “People of Israel and you who fear God, listen.
v17: The God of this people Israel chose our ancestors and made the people great while they lived as foreigners in Egypt. With uplifted arm he led them out of it.
v18: For about forty years he put up with them in the wilderness.
v19: After destroying seven nations in the land of Canaan, he gave their land to Israel as an inheritance.
v20: All this took about 450 years. Afterward God gave them judges until the time of Samuel the prophet.
v21: Then they asked for a king, and God gave them Saul son of Kish, a man from the tribe of Benjamin, for forty years.
v22: After removing him, God raised up David as their king and testified about him: ‘I have found David son of Jesse, a man after my own heart, who will do everything I want.’
v23: From this man’s descendants, according to promise, God brought Israel a Savior, Jesus.
v24: Before Jesus’ coming, John proclaimed a baptism of repentance to all the people of Israel.
v25: As John was completing his course, he said, ‘Who do you suppose I am? I am not the one. But look—one is coming after me whose sandals I am not worthy to untie.’
</p>

## The Word of This Salvation

<p>
v26: “Brothers and sisters, children of Abraham’s family, and those among you who fear God, this message of salvation has been sent to us.
v27: The residents of Jerusalem and their rulers did not recognize Jesus or understand the words of the prophets read every Sabbath. Yet by condemning him, they fulfilled those words.
v28: Though they found no grounds for a death sentence, they asked Pilate to have him killed.
v29: When they had carried out everything written about him, they took him down from the tree and laid him in a tomb.
v30: But God raised him from the dead,
v31: and for many days he appeared to those who had come up with him from Galilee to Jerusalem. They are now his witnesses to the people.
v32: We proclaim to you the good news that the promise made to our ancestors
v33: God has fulfilled for us, their children, by raising up Jesus. As it is written in the second psalm: ‘You are my Son; today I have begotten you.’
v34: God raised him from the dead, never again to return to decay. He expressed it this way: ‘I will give you the holy and sure blessings promised to David.’
v35: So he also says in another psalm, ‘You will not let your Holy One see decay.’
v36: Now David served God’s purpose in his own generation, then fell asleep, was laid with his ancestors, and saw decay.
v37: But the one God raised did not see decay.
</p>

## Forgiveness and Being Set Right

<p>
v38: Therefore, brothers and sisters, understand that through Jesus forgiveness of sins is proclaimed to you.
v39: Through him everyone who believes is justified from everything from which you could not be justified by the law of Moses.
v40: So take care that what was spoken in the Prophets does not happen to you:
v41: ‘Look, you scoffers! Be amazed and perish, because I am doing a work in your days, a work you would never believe even if someone explained it to you.’”
</p>

## The Word Spreads Through the Region

<p>
v42: As Paul and Barnabas were leaving, the people urged them to speak about these things again the next Sabbath.
v43: After the synagogue gathering ended, many Jewish people and devout converts followed Paul and Barnabas. They spoke with them and urged them to continue in God’s grace.
v44: On the next Sabbath almost the whole city gathered to hear the word of the Lord.
v45: But when some Jewish people saw the crowds, they were filled with jealousy. They contradicted what Paul was saying and slandered him.
v46: Then Paul and Barnabas spoke boldly: “It was necessary that God’s word be spoken to you first. But since you reject it and judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life, we are now turning to the Gentiles.
v47: For this is what the Lord has commanded us: ‘I have appointed you as a light for the Gentiles, to bring salvation to the ends of the earth.’”
v48: When the Gentiles heard this, they rejoiced and honored the word of the Lord, and all who had been appointed to eternal life believed.
v49: The word of the Lord spread throughout the whole region.
v50: But some Jewish people stirred up the devout women of high standing and the city’s leading men. They incited persecution against Paul and Barnabas and drove them out of their region.
v51: So Paul and Barnabas shook the dust from their feet against them and went to Iconium.
v52: And the disciples were filled with joy and with the Holy Spirit.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Manaen’s description indicates that he was raised with or closely associated with Herod the tetrarch; the precise social arrangement is uncertain.

v18: The pinned Greek supports ‘put up with them,’ a difficult description of God’s patience in the wilderness. A closely related textual form can mean ‘carried them.’

v19–20: The syntax places the approximately 450 years with the preceding sequence before the judges. The main text makes that punctuation choice visible rather than disguising it.

v33: The psalm’s ‘today I have begotten you’ retains a royal birth image. Paul uses the quotation here while announcing God’s fulfillment in raising up Jesus.

v48: ‘Appointed to eternal life’ is retained without adding an explanation of how divine appointment and human response relate.

## Vocabulary

v07: **ἀνθύπατος (anthypatos)**: Proconsul, a Roman provincial governor.

v22: **θέλημα (thelēma)**: Will, desire, or purpose.

v39: **δικαιόω (dikaioō)**: Declare or set right; justify.
