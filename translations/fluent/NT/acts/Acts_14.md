## Iconium: Trust and Opposition

<p>
v01: In Iconium, Paul and Barnabas entered the Jewish synagogue together and spoke so effectively that a great number of both Jewish and Greek people believed.
v02: But the Jewish people who refused to believe stirred up the Gentiles and poisoned their minds against the brothers and sisters.
v03: So Paul and Barnabas stayed there a long time, speaking boldly for the Lord, who confirmed the message of his grace by granting signs and wonders through their hands.
v04: The people of the city were divided: some sided with the Jewish leaders, others with the apostles.
v05: Then Gentiles and Jewish leaders, together with their rulers, made an attempt to mistreat and stone them.
v06: When the apostles learned of it, they fled to the Lycaonian cities of Lystra and Derbe and the surrounding region,
v07: and there they continued proclaiming the good news.
</p>

## Lystra: From Healing to Attempted Worship

<p>
v08: In Lystra a man was sitting who had no strength in his feet. He had been unable to walk from birth and had never walked.
v09: He listened as Paul spoke. Paul looked intently at him and saw that he had faith to be healed.
v10: Paul said in a loud voice, “Stand upright on your feet!” The man leaped up and began to walk.
v11: When the crowds saw what Paul had done, they shouted in the Lycaonian language, “The gods have come down to us in human form!”
v12: They called Barnabas Zeus and Paul Hermes, because Paul was the chief speaker.
v13: The priest of Zeus, whose temple stood just outside the city, brought bulls and wreaths to the gates. He and the crowds wanted to offer sacrifices.
v14: But when the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard about it, they tore their clothes, rushed into the crowd, and shouted,
v15: “Men, why are you doing this? We too are human beings with the same nature as you. We are bringing you good news, telling you to turn from these worthless things to the living God, who made heaven, earth, sea, and everything in them.
v16: In past generations he allowed all nations to follow their own ways.
v17: Yet he did not leave himself without witness: he did good by giving you rain from heaven and fruitful seasons, filling your hearts with food and joy.”
v18: Even with these words, they barely stopped the crowds from sacrificing to them.
</p>

## Paul Is Stoned and Returns

<p>
v19: Then some Jewish people came from Antioch and Iconium and won over the crowds. They stoned Paul and dragged him outside the city, thinking he was dead.
v20: But when the disciples gathered around him, he got up and went into the city. The next day he left with Barnabas for Derbe.
</p>

## Strengthening the New Churches

<p>
v21: After proclaiming the good news in that city and making many disciples, they returned to Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch.
v22: They strengthened the disciples and encouraged them to remain in the faith, saying, “We must enter the kingdom of God through many troubles.”
v23: They appointed elders for them in every church and, with prayer and fasting, entrusted them to the Lord in whom they had believed.
v24: Then they passed through Pisidia and came to Pamphylia.
v25: After speaking the word in Perga, they went down to Attalia.
v26: From there they sailed back to Antioch, where they had been entrusted to God’s grace for the work they had now completed.
v27: When they arrived, they gathered the church and reported everything God had done with them and how he had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.
v28: They stayed there with the disciples for a long time.
</p>

## Notes

v08: The man’s inability to walk is lifelong, from birth. Person-first language preserves his humanity without hiding the condition.

v12–15: The crowd identifies Barnabas with Zeus and Paul with Hermes. The apostles reject the honor and insist on their shared humanity.

v17: Rain, fruitful seasons, food, and joy are named as God’s witness in ordinary provision.

v19–20: The crowd stones Paul and drags him outside the city believing him dead. The narrative records severe collective violence without stating explicitly that he died.

v22: Entry into God’s kingdom through many troubles describes the disciples’ costly path; it does not call the violence against them good.

## Vocabulary

v15: **ματαῖος (mataios)**: Empty, futile, or worthless; here describing idols and their worship.

v22: **θλῖψις (thlipsis)**: Pressure, trouble, affliction, or persecution.

v23: **πρεσβύτερος (presbyteros)**: Elder, a recognized leader in a local church.
