## James Is Killed and Peter Is Imprisoned

<p>
v01: About that time King Herod began to mistreat some who belonged to the church.
v02: He had James, the brother of John, killed with the sword.
v03: When he saw that this pleased some Jewish leaders, he went on to arrest Peter as well. This happened during the Festival of Unleavened Bread.
v04: After arresting Peter, he put him in prison and assigned four squads of four soldiers each to guard him. Herod intended to bring him out before the people after Passover.
v05: So Peter was kept in prison, but the church was praying fervently to God for him.
</p>

## Peter Is Rescued from Prison

<p>
v06: On the night before Herod was to bring him out, Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains, while guards in front of the door watched the prison.
v07: Suddenly an angel of the Lord appeared, and a light shone in the cell. The angel struck Peter on the side and woke him, saying, “Get up quickly!” The chains fell from his hands.
v08: The angel told him, “Get dressed and put on your sandals.” Peter did so. Then the angel said, “Wrap your cloak around you and follow me.”
v09: Peter went out and followed him. He did not realize that what the angel was doing was real; he thought he was seeing a vision.
v10: They passed the first and second guard posts and came to the iron gate leading into the city. It opened for them by itself, and they went out and walked the length of one street. Suddenly the angel left him.
v11: Then Peter came to himself and said, “Now I know for certain that the Lord sent his angel and rescued me from Herod’s power and from everything the Jewish people were expecting.”
</p>

## Peter Comes to the Praying Community

<p>
v12: When he understood this, he went to the house of Mary, the mother of John called Mark, where many people had gathered and were praying.
v13: When Peter knocked at the outer gate, a servant girl named Rhoda came to answer.
v14: She recognized Peter’s voice and was so overjoyed that she did not open the gate but ran inside and announced that Peter was standing outside.
v15: They told her, “You are out of your mind!” But she kept insisting it was true. Then they said, “It must be his angel.”
v16: Meanwhile Peter kept knocking. When they opened the gate and saw him, they were astonished.
v17: He motioned with his hand for them to be quiet and described how the Lord had brought him out of prison. He said, “Tell James and the brothers and sisters about this.” Then he left and went to another place.
v18: At daybreak there was no small disturbance among the soldiers over what had become of Peter.
v19: Herod searched for him but did not find him. After questioning the guards, he ordered them to be taken away for execution. Then Herod went down from Judea to Caesarea and stayed there.
</p>

## Herod Dies

<p>
v20: Herod had been furious with the people of Tyre and Sidon. They came to him together, and after winning over Blastus, the king’s chamberlain, they asked for peace because their country depended on the king’s country for food.
v21: On the appointed day, Herod dressed in royal robes, sat on the judgment seat, and delivered a public speech to them.
v22: The crowd shouted, “This is the voice of a god, not a man!”
v23: Immediately an angel of the Lord struck him because he did not give glory to God. He was eaten by worms and died.
</p>

## God’s Word Continues to Grow

<p>
v24: But the word of God continued to grow and multiply.
v25: Barnabas and Saul returned after completing their relief mission in Jerusalem, taking with them John who was called Mark.
</p>

## Notes

v02: Herod has James killed with the sword. The narrative states the political violence plainly.

v04: Four squads of four soldiers underline the security around Peter; ‘Passover’ is the festival setting, not a Christian Easter observance.

v15: ‘His angel’ is the gathered group’s proposed explanation. The narrator reports it without endorsing a complete theory of guardian angels.

v19: The guards’ removal follows an official examination and carries the threat of execution. The translation names the lethal consequence.

v23: Herod’s death is described graphically as being eaten by worms. The harm is retained without embellishment.

## Vocabulary

v04: **πάσχα (pascha)**: Passover, the festival remembering Israel’s deliverance from Egypt.

v05: **ἐκτενῶς (ektenōs)**: Earnestly, fervently, or with sustained intensity.

v20: **κοιτών (koitōn)**: Bedchamber; by extension, a trusted royal chamberlain or household official.
