## Peter Explains the Gentile Inclusion

<p>
v01: The apostles and the brothers and sisters throughout Judea heard that the Gentiles also had received God’s word.
v02: So when Peter went up to Jerusalem, those from the circumcision group criticized him,
v03: saying, “You went into the house of uncircumcised men and ate with them.”
v04: Peter began explaining everything to them step by step:
v05: “I was in the city of Joppa praying, and in a trance I saw a vision: something like a large sheet was being lowered from heaven by its four corners, and it came down to me.
v06: I looked closely at it and saw four-footed animals of the earth, wild animals, reptiles, and birds of the sky.
v07: I also heard a voice saying to me, ‘Get up, Peter. Kill and eat.’
v08: But I said, ‘Certainly not, Lord! Nothing common or unclean has ever entered my mouth.’
v09: The voice answered from heaven a second time, ‘What God has made clean, you must not call common.’
v10: This happened three times, and everything was pulled back into heaven.
v11: At that very moment three men who had been sent to me from Caesarea stood at the house where we were.
v12: The Spirit told me to go with them without making distinctions. These six brothers also went with me, and we entered the man’s house.
v13: He told us how he had seen an angel standing in his house who said, ‘Send to Joppa and bring Simon who is called Peter.
v14: He will speak words to you by which you and your whole household will be saved.’
v15: As I began to speak, the Holy Spirit fell on them just as he had fallen on us at the beginning.
v16: Then I remembered the Lord’s word: ‘John baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit.’
v17: Therefore, if God gave them the same gift he gave us when we trusted in the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I to stand in God’s way?”
v18: When they heard these things, they became quiet and glorified God, saying, “Then God has granted even to the Gentiles repentance that leads to life.”
</p>

## The Church in Antioch

<p>
v19: Those who had been scattered because of the persecution that began over Stephen traveled as far as Phoenicia, Cyprus, and Antioch, speaking the word only to Jewish people.
v20: But some of them, men from Cyprus and Cyrene, came to Antioch and began speaking also to Greeks, announcing the good news of the Lord Jesus.
v21: The Lord’s hand was with them, and a large number trusted and turned to the Lord.
v22: News about them reached the church in Jerusalem, and they sent Barnabas to Antioch.
v23: When he arrived and saw God’s grace, he rejoiced and encouraged everyone to remain faithful to the Lord with steadfast purpose of heart.
v24: Barnabas was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and faith, and a considerable crowd was added to the Lord.
v25: Then Barnabas went to Tarsus to look for Saul,
v26: and when he found him, he brought him to Antioch. For a whole year they met with the church and taught a considerable crowd. It was in Antioch that the disciples were first called Christians.
</p>

## Relief Is Sent to Judea

<p>
v27: In those days prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch.
v28: One of them, named Agabus, stood up and indicated through the Spirit that a severe famine was about to come over the whole inhabited world. This happened during the reign of Claudius.
v29: So the disciples decided, each according to their ability, to send relief to the brothers and sisters living in Judea.
v30: They did this, sending it to the elders through Barnabas and Saul.
</p>

## Notes

v01–03: The controversy is not whether Gentiles heard about Jesus but whether Peter entered their house and ate with uncircumcised people. Table fellowship is the visible pressure point.

v04: Peter’s defense is narrative rather than abstract. He recounts the vision, the Spirit’s command, Cornelius’ report, and the Spirit’s arrival.

v09: The divine declaration about clean and common is repeated, emphasizing that the boundary change originates with God.

v12: `diakrinō` again carries the sense of making distinctions or hesitating. Peter says the Spirit told him to cross the boundary without doing so.

v15: Peter interprets the Gentile Spirit-event by comparison with “us at the beginning,” making Pentecost the controlling analogy.

v17: The decisive question becomes whether Peter could “hinder God.” Human authorization follows divine action rather than creating it.

v18: The Jerusalem believers’ conclusion is not merely that Gentiles may join socially but that God has granted them “repentance that leads to life.”

v19–21: The Stephen persecution continues to drive mission expansion. Some scattered believers cross another boundary by speaking to Greeks in Antioch.

v23: Barnabas sees “God’s grace” in the new community before demanding that it reproduce Jerusalem culturally.

v25–26: Barnabas intentionally retrieves Saul for sustained teaching in Antioch. The term “Christian” first appears as an identifying label for disciples there.

v29–30: The Gentile-mission center responds to Judean need with material solidarity. Mission expansion does not sever responsibility to the Jerusalem and Judean church.
