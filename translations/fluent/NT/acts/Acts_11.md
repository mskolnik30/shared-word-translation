## Peter Explains the Gentile Inclusion

<p>
v01: The apostles and the brothers and sisters throughout Judea heard that the Gentiles also had received the word of God.
v02: So when Peter went up to Jerusalem, the circumcised believers criticized him,
v03: saying, “You went into the homes of uncircumcised men and ate with them!”
v04: Peter began and explained everything to them in order:
v05: “I was in the city of Joppa praying, and in a trance I saw a vision. Something like a large sheet was being lowered from heaven by its four corners, and it came down to me.
v06: I looked closely into it and saw four-footed animals of the earth, wild animals, reptiles, and birds of the sky.
v07: Then I heard a voice tell me, ‘Get up, Peter. Kill and eat.’
v08: But I said, ‘Certainly not, Lord! Nothing defiled or unclean has ever entered my mouth.’
v09: The voice answered a second time from heaven, ‘What God has made clean, you must not call defiled.’
v10: This happened three times, and then everything was pulled back up into heaven.
v11: “At that very moment, three men sent to me from Caesarea arrived at the house where we were staying.
v12: The Spirit told me to go with them without making distinctions. These six brothers also went with me, and we entered the man’s house.
v13: He told us how he had seen the angel standing in his house and saying, ‘Send to Joppa and bring back Simon who is called Peter.
v14: He will speak words by which you and your whole household will be saved.’
v15: As I began to speak, the Holy Spirit fell on them just as on us at the beginning.
v16: Then I remembered the Lord’s words: ‘John baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit.’
v17: So if God gave them the same gift he gave us when we believed in the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I to stand in God’s way?”
v18: When they heard this, they stopped objecting and glorified God, saying, “Then God has granted even the Gentiles repentance that leads to life.”
</p>

## The Church in Antioch

<p>
v19: Now those scattered by the persecution that arose over Stephen traveled as far as Phoenicia, Cyprus, and Antioch, speaking the word only to Jewish people.
v20: But some of them, men from Cyprus and Cyrene, came to Antioch and also spoke to Greek-speaking people, proclaiming the good news about the Lord Jesus.
v21: The Lord’s hand was with them, and a great number believed and turned to the Lord.
v22: News of this reached the church in Jerusalem, and they sent Barnabas as far as Antioch.
v23: When he arrived and saw God’s grace, he rejoiced and encouraged everyone to remain devoted to the Lord with steadfast hearts.
v24: Barnabas was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and faith, and a large crowd was added to the Lord.
v25: Then Barnabas went to Tarsus to look for Saul,
v26: and when he found him, he brought him to Antioch. For a whole year they met with the church and taught a large crowd. It was in Antioch that the disciples were first called Christians.
</p>

## Relief Is Sent to Judea

<p>
v27: During those days prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch.
v28: One of them, named Agabus, stood up and indicated through the Spirit that a severe famine would come over the whole inhabited world. This happened during the reign of Claudius.
v29: So each of the disciples, according to their means, decided to send relief to the brothers and sisters living in Judea.
v30: They did this, sending their gift to the elders through Barnabas and Saul.
</p>

## Notes

v02–03: The criticism centers on Peter entering the homes of uncircumcised men and eating with them, not merely on his preaching.

v12: Peter says the Spirit told him to go without ‘making distinctions.’ Six fellow believers accompany him as witnesses.

v17: Peter’s question frames resistance to the Gentiles’ reception of the Spirit as standing in God’s way.

v20: The pinned text reads ‘Greek-speaking people,’ a form that can refer to Hellenists; the note does not silently substitute a different textual reading.

v28–30: The anticipated famine leads the disciples to concrete, ability-based relief for believers in Judea.

## Vocabulary

v18: **μετάνοια (metanoia)**: Repentance, a changed mind and direction.

v23: **χάρις (charis)**: Grace or favor freely given.

v26: **Χριστιανός (Christianos)**: Christian, a person identified with Christ.
