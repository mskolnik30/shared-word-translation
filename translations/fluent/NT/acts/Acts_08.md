## Persecution Scatters the Church

<p>
v01: Saul approved of Stephen’s death. On that day a severe persecution broke out against the church in Jerusalem, and everyone except the apostles was scattered throughout the regions of Judea and Samaria.
v02: Devout men buried Stephen and mourned deeply for him.
v03: But Saul was ravaging the church, entering house after house, dragging away both men and women, and handing them over to prison.
</p>

## Philip Announces the Christ in Samaria

<p>
v04: Those who had been scattered went from place to place announcing the good news of the word.
v05: Philip went down to a city of Samaria and proclaimed the Christ to them.
v06: The crowds paid close attention together to what Philip said as they heard and saw the signs he was doing.
v07: Unclean spirits came out of many people with loud cries, and many who were paralyzed or unable to walk were healed.
v08: So there was great joy in that city.
</p>

## Simon and the Gift of God

<p>
v09: A man named Simon had previously practiced magic in the city and amazed the people of Samaria, claiming that he himself was someone great.
v10: Everyone, from the least to the greatest, paid attention to him and said, “This man is the power of God called Great.”
v11: They paid attention to him because for a long time he had amazed them with his magic.
v12: But when they trusted Philip as he announced the good news about God’s kingdom and the name of Jesus Christ, both men and women were baptized.
v13: Simon himself also trusted and was baptized. He stayed close to Philip and was amazed as he saw signs and great works of power happening.
v14: When the apostles in Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received God’s word, they sent Peter and John to them.
v15: When they arrived, they prayed for the Samaritan believers so they might receive the Holy Spirit,
v16: because the Spirit had not yet fallen on any of them; they had only been baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.
v17: Then Peter and John laid their hands on them, and they received the Holy Spirit.
v18: When Simon saw that the Spirit was given through the laying on of the apostles’ hands, he offered them money
v19: and said, “Give me this authority too, so that anyone I lay my hands on may receive the Holy Spirit.”
v20: But Peter said to him, “May your silver perish with you because you thought you could obtain God’s gift with money!
v21: You have no part or share in this matter because your heart is not right before God.
v22: Therefore repent of this evil and pray to the Lord that, if possible, the intention of your heart may be forgiven.
v23: For I see that you are poisoned by bitterness and bound by unrighteousness.”
v24: Simon answered, “You pray to the Lord for me so that none of the things you said will happen to me.”
v25: After Peter and John had testified and spoken the Lord’s word, they returned to Jerusalem, announcing the good news in many Samaritan villages along the way.
</p>

## Philip and the Ethiopian Eunuch

<p>
v26: An angel of the Lord spoke to Philip: “Get up and go south to the road that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza. It is a desert road.”
v27: So Philip got up and went. Look—an Ethiopian man, a eunuch and court official of Candace, queen of the Ethiopians, who was in charge of all her treasury, had come to Jerusalem to worship.
v28: He was returning home, sitting in his chariot and reading the prophet Isaiah.
v29: The Spirit said to Philip, “Go over and stay close to this chariot.”
v30: Philip ran up and heard him reading Isaiah the prophet. He asked, “Do you understand what you are reading?”
v31: The man answered, “How could I unless someone guides me?” He invited Philip to come up and sit with him.
v32: The passage of Scripture he was reading was:
“Like a sheep he was led to slaughter,
and like a lamb silent before its shearer,
he does not open his mouth.
v33: In his humiliation justice was denied him.
Who will describe his generation?
For his life is taken from the earth.”
v34: The eunuch asked Philip, “Please tell me, who is the prophet talking about—himself or someone else?”
v35: Philip began with this Scripture and announced the good news about Jesus to him.
v36: As they traveled along the road, they came to some water. The eunuch said, “Look—water! What prevents me from being baptized?”
v38: He ordered the chariot to stop. Philip and the eunuch both went down into the water, and Philip baptized him.
v39: When they came up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord carried Philip away. The eunuch did not see him again but continued on his way rejoicing.
v40: Philip found himself at Azotus, and as he traveled through the region he announced the good news in all the towns until he came to Caesarea.
</p>

## Notes

v01–04: Persecution scatters the Jerusalem community into Judea and Samaria, unintentionally advancing the geographic mission announced in 1:8.

v03: Saul’s violence explicitly targets both men and women.

v05–08: Samaria receives proclamation, signs, healing, and great joy. The move into Samaria is a major boundary crossing in Luke–Acts.

v12: Philip announces both God’s kingdom and Jesus Christ’s name, preserving continuity with Jesus’ kingdom proclamation.

v14–17: Acts narrates Samaritan baptism before reception of the Spirit through Peter and John. Fluent preserves this unusual sequence rather than forcing every conversion scene into one fixed order.

v18–23: Simon tries to acquire divine gift and authority as a controllable commodity. Peter’s rebuke targets the heart and the attempted purchase, not money as such.

v26–35: The Spirit directs Philip toward one particular traveler, and Scripture interpretation becomes the path into proclamation of Jesus.

v27: The Ethiopian is simultaneously an African royal official, a eunuch, and a worshiper returning from Jerusalem. Fluent retains all three social markers without speculating about his precise covenantal status.

v32–35: Philip begins from Isaiah’s suffering figure and announces Jesus, but Acts does not preserve the full interpretive argument.

v36: The eunuch’s question “What prevents me?” becomes especially significant in Acts’ widening inclusion narrative.

v37: Public verse 37 is absent from SBLGNT’s main text. Later manuscripts add a confession of faith before baptism.

v38: Fluent therefore moves directly from the question in v36 to the stopping of the chariot in v38, preserving public numbering.

v39: The eunuch’s final response is joy; Acts does not narrate a return to Jerusalem for further authorization.
