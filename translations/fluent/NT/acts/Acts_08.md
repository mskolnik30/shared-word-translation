## Persecution Scatters the Church

<p>
v01: Saul approved of Stephen’s killing. That day a severe persecution began against the church in Jerusalem, and everyone except the apostles was scattered throughout the regions of Judea and Samaria.
v02: Devout men buried Stephen and mourned deeply for him.
v03: But Saul was ravaging the church. Going from house to house, he dragged off both men and women and put them in prison.
</p>

## Philip Announces the Christ in Samaria

<p>
v04: Those who had been scattered went from place to place proclaiming the good news of the word.
v05: Philip went down to a city of Samaria and proclaimed the Messiah to them.
v06: The crowds listened closely and with one accord to what Philip said when they heard him and saw the signs he performed.
v07: Unclean spirits came out of many people, crying with a loud voice, and many who were paralyzed or unable to walk were healed.
v08: So there was great joy in that city.
</p>

## Simon and the Gift of God

<p>
v09: Now a man named Simon had previously practiced magic in the city and amazed the people of Samaria, claiming to be someone great.
v10: Everyone, from the least to the greatest, paid attention to him and said, “This man is the power of God called Great.”
v11: They paid attention to him because for a long time he had amazed them with his magic.
v12: But when they believed Philip as he proclaimed the good news about the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ, both men and women were baptized.
v13: Even Simon himself believed and was baptized. He stayed close to Philip, and he was amazed when he saw the great signs and miracles taking place.
v14: When the apostles in Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God, they sent Peter and John to them.
v15: After they went down, they prayed for the Samaritan believers to receive the Holy Spirit,
v16: because the Spirit had not yet fallen on any of them. They had only been baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.
v17: Then Peter and John laid their hands on them, and they received the Holy Spirit.
v18: When Simon saw that the Spirit was given through the apostles’ laying on of hands, he offered them money
v19: and said, “Give me this authority too, so that anyone on whom I lay my hands may receive the Holy Spirit.”
v20: But Peter told him, “May your silver perish with you, because you thought you could acquire God’s gift with money!
v21: You have no part or share in this matter, because your heart is not right before God.
v22: So repent of this wickedness and pray to the Lord that, if possible, the intention of your heart may be forgiven.
v23: For I see that you are poisoned by bitterness and bound by injustice.”
v24: Simon answered, “Pray to the Lord for me, so that nothing you have said will happen to me.”
v25: After Peter and John had testified and spoken the word of the Lord, they returned to Jerusalem, proclaiming the good news in many Samaritan villages along the way.
</p>

## Philip and the Ethiopian Eunuch

<p>
v26: An angel of the Lord spoke to Philip: “Get up and go south to the road that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza—the desert road.”
v27: So he got up and went. There he saw an Ethiopian man, a eunuch and high official of Candace, queen of the Ethiopians, who was in charge of all her treasury. He had come to Jerusalem to worship
v28: and was returning home. Seated in his chariot, he was reading the prophet Isaiah.
v29: The Spirit told Philip, “Go over and stay close to that chariot.”
v30: Philip ran up and heard him reading Isaiah the prophet. He asked, “Do you understand what you are reading?”
v31: He replied, “How could I unless someone guides me?” Then he invited Philip to come up and sit with him.
v32: The passage of Scripture he was reading was this: “He was led like a sheep to slaughter, and like a lamb silent before its shearer, he does not open his mouth.
v33: In his humiliation justice was denied him. Who can describe his generation? For his life is taken from the earth.”
v34: The eunuch asked Philip, “Please tell me, who is the prophet speaking about—himself or someone else?”
v35: Then Philip began with this Scripture and proclaimed the good news about Jesus to him.
v36: As they traveled along the road, they came to some water. The eunuch said, “Look, here is water! What prevents me from being baptized?”
v38: He ordered the chariot to stop. Both Philip and the eunuch went down into the water, and Philip baptized him.
v39: When they came up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord suddenly took Philip away. The eunuch did not see him again but went on his way rejoicing.
v40: Philip found himself at Azotus. As he traveled through the region, he proclaimed the good news in every town until he came to Caesarea.
</p>

## Notes

v09–24: Simon’s belief and baptism are narrated, but Peter exposes his attempt to buy divine authority. The account does not invite readers to treat the Spirit as a commodity.

v23: The Greek uses the vivid images ‘gall of bitterness’ and ‘bond of injustice.’ The main text communicates their moral force without turning them into a medical diagnosis.

v27: The traveler is both an Ethiopian court official and a eunuch. The translation preserves these identities rather than reducing him to his occupation.

v37: Verse 37 is absent from the pinned Greek text, so the public verse numbering moves from 36 to 38.

## Vocabulary

v05: **χριστός (christos)**: Messiah or Anointed One.

v20: **δωρεά (dōrea)**: Gift, especially something freely bestowed.

v27: **εὐνοῦχος (eunouchos)**: Eunuch; a man whose embodied and social status could be associated with royal service.
