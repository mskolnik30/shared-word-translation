## Paul Sails for Italy

<p>
v01: When it was decided that we would sail for Italy, Paul and some other prisoners were handed over to a centurion named Julius of the Imperial Cohort.
v02: We boarded a ship from Adramyttium that was about to sail to ports along the coast of Asia, and we put out to sea. Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica, was with us.
v03: The next day we put in at Sidon. Julius treated Paul kindly and allowed him to visit his friends and receive their care.
v04: From there we put out to sea and sailed under the shelter of Cyprus because the winds were against us.
v05: After sailing across the open sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came to Myra in Lycia.
v06: There the centurion found an Alexandrian ship sailing for Italy and put us aboard it.
v07: We sailed slowly for many days and with difficulty came near Cnidus. Since the wind would not allow us to continue directly, we sailed under the shelter of Crete near Salmone.
v08: Coasting along it with difficulty, we came to a place called Fair Havens, near the city of Lasea.
</p>

## Paul Warns against Sailing On

<p>
v09: A great deal of time had passed, and sailing had already become dangerous because even the Fast was now over. So Paul warned them,
v10: “Men, I can see that this voyage is going to bring disaster and great loss, not only to the cargo and ship but also to our lives.”
v11: But the centurion was persuaded more by the pilot and the shipowner than by what Paul said.
v12: Since the harbor was unsuitable for wintering, the majority decided to sail on from there, hoping somehow to reach Phoenix, a harbor of Crete facing southwest and northwest, and spend the winter there.
</p>

## The Northeaster Drives the Ship

<p>
v13: When a gentle south wind began to blow, they thought they had secured their purpose. They weighed anchor and sailed close along the coast of Crete.
v14: But before long a hurricane-force wind called the Northeaster swept down from the island.
v15: The ship was caught and could not face into the wind, so we gave way to it and were driven along.
v16: Running under the shelter of a small island called Cauda, we barely managed to get the ship's boat under control.
v17: After hoisting it aboard, the sailors used cables to brace the ship. Fearing they would run aground on the Syrtis, they lowered the sea anchor and let the ship be driven along.
v18: Because the storm was battering us so violently, the next day they began throwing the cargo overboard.
v19: On the third day, they threw the ship's gear overboard with their own hands.
v20: When neither sun nor stars appeared for many days and a severe storm continued to rage, all hope that we would be saved was finally taken away.
</p>

## God Promises the Lives of Everyone on Board

<p>
v21: After they had gone a long time without food, Paul stood among them and said, “Men, you should have listened to me and not sailed from Crete; then you would have avoided this disaster and loss.
v22: But now I urge you to take courage, because not one of you will lose your life—only the ship will be lost.
v23: For last night an angel of the God to whom I belong and whom I serve stood beside me
v24: and said, ‘Do not be afraid, Paul. You must stand before Caesar, and look: God has graciously given you everyone sailing with you.’
v25: So take courage, men! For I trust God that it will happen exactly as I have been told.
v26: But we must run aground on some island.”
</p>

## The Sailors Must Remain

<p>
v27: On the fourteenth night, as we were being driven across the Adriatic Sea, around midnight the sailors sensed that land was drawing near.
v28: They took soundings and found the depth was twenty fathoms. A little farther on they took soundings again and found it was fifteen fathoms.
v29: Fearing we might run aground on rocks, they dropped four anchors from the stern and prayed for daylight.
v30: The sailors tried to escape from the ship and lowered the boat into the sea, pretending they intended to lay out anchors from the bow.
v31: Paul said to the centurion and soldiers, “Unless these men remain in the ship, you cannot be saved.”
v32: Then the soldiers cut the ropes of the boat and let it fall away.
</p>

## Everyone Eats and Takes Courage

<p>
v33: Until day was about to dawn, Paul urged everyone to eat, saying, “Today is the fourteenth day you have kept watch and gone without food, taking nothing.
v34: Therefore I urge you to eat, for this is necessary for your survival. Not a hair from any of your heads will be lost.”
v35: After saying this, Paul took bread, gave thanks to God in front of them all, broke it, and began to eat.
v36: Then everyone took courage and ate some food themselves.
v37: There were 276 people in all on the ship.
v38: When they had eaten enough, they lightened the ship by throwing the grain into the sea.
</p>

## The Ship Is Lost but Everyone Reaches Land

<p>
v39: When daylight came, they did not recognize the land, but they noticed a bay with a beach and planned to run the ship ashore there if they could.
v40: They cut away the anchors and left them in the sea, at the same time loosening the ropes that held the rudders. Then they raised the foresail to the wind and headed for the beach.
v41: But they struck a sandbar between two seas and ran the ship aground. The bow stuck fast and remained immovable, while the stern began to break apart under the pounding waves.
v42: The soldiers planned to kill the prisoners so that none could swim away and escape.
v43: But the centurion, wanting to save Paul, prevented them from carrying out their plan. He ordered those who could swim to jump overboard first and make for land,
v44: and the rest to follow, some on planks and others on pieces of the ship. In this way everyone reached land safely.
</p>

## Notes

v17: ‘Sea anchor’ renders a general gear term whose exact nautical referent is uncertain.

v37: The pinned text numbers everyone aboard as 276 people.

v42–43: The soldiers plan to kill the prisoners; the centurion prevents it because he wants to save Paul.

## Vocabulary

v14: **Εὐρακύλων (Eurakylōn)**: Northeaster, the storm wind’s name.

v28: **ὀργυιά (orguia)**: Fathom, a nautical depth measure.
