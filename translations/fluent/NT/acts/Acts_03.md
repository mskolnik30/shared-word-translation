## A Man Unable to Walk Is Healed

<p>
v01: Peter and John were going up to the temple courts at the ninth-hour time of prayer, about three in the afternoon.
v02: A man who had been unable to walk from his mother’s womb was being carried there. Every day people placed him at the temple gate called Beautiful so he could ask those entering for alms.
v03: When he saw Peter and John about to enter the temple courts, he asked to receive alms.
v04: Peter, along with John, looked directly at him and said, “Look at us.”
v05: So he gave them his attention, expecting to receive something from them.
v06: But Peter said, “Silver and gold I do not have. What I do have, I give you: in the name of Jesus the Messiah, the Nazarene, walk!”
v07: Taking him by the right hand, Peter raised him up. Immediately the man’s feet and ankles became strong.
v08: He leaped up, stood, and began walking. Then he entered the temple courts with them, walking, leaping, and praising God.
v09: All the people saw him walking and praising God.
v10: They recognized him as the man who used to sit asking for alms at the Beautiful Gate of the temple, and they were filled with wonder and amazement at what had happened to him.
</p>

## Peter Speaks in Solomon’s Portico

<p>
v11: While he held on to Peter and John, all the people ran together to them in the portico called Solomon’s, utterly amazed.
v12: When Peter saw this, he responded to the people: “People of Israel, why are you amazed at this? Why do you stare at us as though we had made him walk by our own power or godliness?
v13: The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob—the God of our ancestors—has glorified his servant Jesus. You handed him over and denied him before Pilate, even though Pilate had decided to release him.
v14: You denied the Holy and Righteous One and asked for a murderer to be released to you.
v15: You killed the Author of life, but God raised him from the dead. We are witnesses of this.
v16: By faith in Jesus’ name, his name has strengthened this man whom you see and know. The faith that comes through Jesus has given him this complete healing in front of all of you.
</p>

## Repent and Turn Back

<p>
v17: “And now, brothers and sisters, I know that you acted in ignorance, as did your leaders.
v18: But in this way God fulfilled what he had announced beforehand through all the prophets: that his Messiah would suffer.
v19: Therefore repent and turn back, so that your sins may be wiped away,
v20: so that seasons of refreshing may come from the Lord’s presence and he may send Jesus, the Messiah appointed for you.
v21: Heaven must receive him until the times when everything is restored, which God spoke about through his holy prophets from long ago.
v22: Moses said, ‘The Lord your God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among your own brothers and sisters. Listen to him in everything he tells you.
v23: Every person who does not listen to that prophet will be completely cut off from the people.’
v24: Indeed, all the prophets who have spoken, from Samuel onward, announced these days.
v25: You are the children of the prophets and of the covenant God made with your ancestors when he told Abraham, ‘Through your offspring all the families of the earth will be blessed.’
v26: God raised up his servant and sent him first to you, to bless you by turning each of you away from your evil ways.”
</p>

## Notes

v02: The man has been unable to walk since birth. Person-first wording preserves his humanity without hiding his lifelong condition.

v13, 26: The word for Jesus can mean servant or child. ‘Servant’ connects Jesus with the servant language of Scripture; the filial title is expressed elsewhere.

v14: The crowd asks for a murderer to be released. The narrative names the lethal harm plainly.

v15: ‘Author’ can also mean leader, pioneer, or originator. The title creates a deliberate paradox: people kill the source or leader of life.

v16: The relationships among Jesus’ name, faith in the name, and faith through Jesus are dense. The translation retains each phrase rather than assigning healing to human confidence alone.

v19–21: Repentance and return lead into erased sins and seasons of refreshing; heaven receives Jesus until the promised restoration of all things.

v22–23: The quotation’s ‘brothers’ means the people’s own kin. Refusal to hear the prophet brings severe exclusion from the people.

v25: ‘Offspring’ is singular in form and can be heard corporately or with messianic focus. The main text preserves the collective promise to all earth’s families.

## Vocabulary

v06: **ὄνομα (onoma)**: Name, including identity, authority, and reputation.

v15: **ἀρχηγός (archēgos)**: Leader, pioneer, author, or originator.

v19: **μετανοέω (metanoeō)**: Repent, change one’s mind and direction.
