## Tertullus Accuses Paul Before Felix

<p>
v01: Five days later the high priest Ananias came down with some elders and an attorney named Tertullus. They presented their charges against Paul to the governor.
v02: When Paul was summoned, Tertullus began accusing him:
“Because of you, most excellent Felix, we enjoy much peace, and through your foresight reforms are being made for this nation.
v03: In every way and everywhere we receive these benefits with deep gratitude.
v04: But so that I do not detain you any longer, I ask you to hear us briefly in your kindness.
v05: We have found this man to be a plague, stirring up riots among Jewish people throughout the inhabited world and serving as a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes.
v06: He even tried to profane the temple, so we seized him.
v08: By examining him yourself, you will be able to learn the truth about all the charges we bring against him.”
v09: The Jewish leaders joined in the accusation, insisting that these things were true.
</p>

## Paul’s Defense

<p>
v10: When the governor motioned for Paul to speak, he answered:
“I gladly make my defense because I know you have been a judge over this nation for many years.
v11: You can verify that it has been no more than twelve days since I went up to Jerusalem to worship.
v12: They did not find me arguing with anyone or stirring up a crowd, either in the temple, the synagogues, or the city.
v13: Nor can they prove to you the charges they are now making against me.
v14: But I do admit this to you: according to the Way, which they call a sect, I worship the God of our ancestors, trusting everything in accordance with the Law and written in the Prophets.
v15: I have hope in God—a hope these men themselves also accept—that there will be a resurrection of both the righteous and the unrighteous.
v16: Because of this, I always make every effort to maintain a blameless conscience before God and people.
v17: After many years away, I came to bring gifts for the poor to my nation and to present offerings.
v18: They found me doing this in the temple, purified, without a crowd or disturbance. But there were some Jewish people from Asia
v19: who should be here before you to accuse me if they have anything against me.
v20: Or let these men themselves say what wrongdoing they found when I stood before the council,
v21: other than this one statement I shouted while standing among them: ‘I am on trial before you today concerning the resurrection of the dead.’”
</p>

## Felix Delays the Case

<p>
v22: Felix, who had a more accurate knowledge of the Way, postponed the case and said, “When Commander Lysias comes down, I will decide your case.”
v23: He ordered the centurion to keep Paul in custody but allow him some freedom and not prevent his friends from caring for him.
v24: Some days later Felix came with his wife Drusilla, who was Jewish. He sent for Paul and listened to him about trust in Christ Jesus.
v25: As Paul reasoned about righteousness, self-control, and the coming judgment, Felix became afraid and said, “Go away for now. When I have an opportunity, I will send for you.”
v26: At the same time Felix was hoping Paul would give him money, so he sent for him frequently and talked with him.
v27: After two years had passed, Porcius Festus succeeded Felix. Wanting to gain favor with the Jewish leaders, Felix left Paul imprisoned.
</p>

## Notes

v02–04: Tertullus opens with conventional flattery designed to secure Felix’s goodwill before the accusation.

v05: The formal charges are political, social, and religious: worldwide unrest, leadership of a “sect,” and temple profanation.

v06–08: Public verse 7 and related expansion around vv06–08 are absent from SBLGNT’s main text. Later manuscripts add a longer account of Lysias forcibly taking Paul away and ordering the accusers to come.

v09: The accusers are the delegation that came with Ananias; Fluent does not broaden their agreement to Jewish people generally.

v11–13: Paul’s defense begins with verifiable chronology and the absence of evidence for public disorder.

v14: Paul explicitly says the Way is his manner of worshiping “the God of our ancestors” and affirms the Law and Prophets. He presents continuity, not a new deity.

v15: Resurrection of both righteous and unrighteous is presented as a hope shared with at least some of Paul’s Jewish accusers.

v16: Conscience before both God and people governs Paul’s ethical self-description.

v17: Paul says he came to Jerusalem with alms and offerings, directly countering the charge that his purpose was sedition.

v18–19: Paul points out the absence of the Asian accusers who supposedly witnessed the temple event.

v24–25: Felix and Drusilla hear about trust in Christ, but Paul’s reasoning immediately includes righteousness, self-control, and coming judgment.

v26: Luke explicitly exposes corruption in Felix’s repeated interviews: he hopes for a bribe.

v27: Political favor, not legal guilt, keeps Paul imprisoned for two years.
