## Timothy Joins Paul and Silas

<p>
v01: Paul also came to Derbe and Lystra. A disciple named Timothy was there, the son of a Jewish woman who was a believer and a Greek father.
v02: The brothers and sisters in Lystra and Iconium spoke well of him.
v03: Paul wanted Timothy to travel with him, so he took him and circumcised him because of the Jewish people in those places, since they all knew his father was Greek.
v04: As they traveled through the cities, they delivered the decisions reached by the apostles and elders in Jerusalem for the believers to observe.
v05: So the churches were strengthened in the faith and grew in number every day.
</p>

## The Spirit Redirects the Mission

<p>
v06: They traveled through the region of Phrygia and Galatia, having been prevented by the Holy Spirit from speaking the word in Asia.
v07: When they came near Mysia, they tried to enter Bithynia, but the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them.
v08: So they passed by Mysia and went down to Troas.
v09: During the night Paul saw a vision: a Macedonian man was standing and pleading with him, “Cross over to Macedonia and help us.”
v10: After Paul saw the vision, we immediately tried to leave for Macedonia, concluding that God had called us to proclaim the good news to them.
</p>

## Lydia’s Heart Is Opened

<p>
v11: We sailed from Troas straight to Samothrace, and the next day to Neapolis.
v12: From there we went to Philippi, a leading city of that district of Macedonia and a Roman colony. We stayed in that city for several days.
v13: On the Sabbath we went outside the city gate beside a river, where we expected to find a place of prayer. We sat down and spoke with the women who had gathered there.
v14: One of those listening was a woman named Lydia, a seller of purple cloth from Thyatira who worshiped God. The Lord opened her heart to respond to what Paul was saying.
v15: After she and her household were baptized, she urged us, “If you consider me faithful to the Lord, come and stay at my house.” And she persuaded us.
</p>

## A Slave Girl Is Freed and Paul and Silas Are Beaten

<p>
v16: Once, as we were going to the place of prayer, a slave girl who had a spirit of divination met us. By fortune-telling she earned a great deal of money for her owners.
v17: She followed Paul and us, crying out, “These people are servants of the Most High God, who proclaim to you a way of salvation.”
v18: She kept doing this for many days. Finally Paul became deeply troubled, turned to the spirit, and said, “I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her.” And it came out that very hour.
v19: When her owners realized that their hope of profit was gone, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace before the authorities.
v20: They brought them to the magistrates and said, “These men are Jews and are throwing our city into turmoil.
v21: They advocate customs that we Romans are not permitted to accept or practice.”
v22: The crowd joined in attacking them, and the magistrates tore the clothes off them and ordered them beaten with rods.
v23: After striking them many times, they threw them into prison and ordered the jailer to guard them securely.
v24: Receiving this order, he put them in the inner prison and fastened their feet in the stocks.
</p>

## The Philippian Jailer

<p>
v25: Around midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the other prisoners were listening to them.
v26: Suddenly there was such a violent earthquake that the prison’s foundations shook. At once all the doors opened and everyone’s chains came loose.
v27: When the jailer woke and saw the prison doors open, he drew his sword and was about to kill himself because he thought the prisoners had escaped.
v28: But Paul shouted loudly, “Do not harm yourself! We are all here.”
v29: The jailer called for lights, rushed in, and fell trembling before Paul and Silas.
v30: He brought them outside and asked, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?”
v31: They replied, “Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved—you and your household.”
v32: Then they spoke the Lord’s word to him and to everyone in his house.
v33: At that hour of the night, the jailer took them and washed their wounds. Immediately he and everyone in his household were baptized.
v34: He brought them into his home and set food before them. He and his whole household rejoiced because he had come to believe in God.
</p>

## Paul Asserts His Roman Citizenship

<p>
v35: When morning came, the magistrates sent officers with the order, “Release those men.”
v36: The jailer reported these words to Paul: “The magistrates have sent orders for you to be released. So come out now and go in peace.”
v37: But Paul told the officers, “They beat us publicly without a trial, even though we are Roman citizens, and threw us into prison. Now they want to send us away secretly? Absolutely not! They must come themselves and escort us out.”
v38: The officers reported these words to the magistrates, who became afraid when they heard that Paul and Silas were Roman citizens.
v39: They came and apologized to them, escorted them out, and asked them to leave the city.
v40: After leaving the prison, Paul and Silas went to Lydia’s house. They saw and encouraged the brothers and sisters, then departed.
</p>

## Notes

v16–19: The girl is enslaved, exploited for profit, and then disappears from the account after liberation from the spirit. The translation preserves both spiritual and economic harm.

v37: Paul and Silas invoke Roman citizenship after being beaten publicly without trial.

## Vocabulary

v12: **κολωνία (kolōnia)**: A Roman colony.

v31: **πιστεύω (pisteuō)**: Believe or trust.
