## Paul Travels through Macedonia and Greece

<p>
v01: After the uproar ended, Paul sent for the disciples and encouraged them. Then he said farewell and left to go to Macedonia.
v02: He traveled through those regions, giving the believers much encouragement, and then came to Greece.
v03: After spending three months there, he was about to sail for Syria when a plot was made against him by some Jewish opponents, so he decided to return through Macedonia.
v04: He was accompanied by Sopater son of Pyrrhus from Berea; Aristarchus and Secundus from Thessalonica; Gaius from Derbe; Timothy; and Tychicus and Trophimus from Asia.
v05: These men went on ahead and waited for us in Troas.
v06: After the days of Unleavened Bread, we sailed from Philippi and joined them at Troas five days later. We stayed there seven days.
</p>

## Eutychus Falls and Is Restored

<p>
v07: On the first day of the week, when we gathered to break bread, Paul spoke with them. Because he intended to leave the next day, he continued speaking until midnight.
v08: There were many lamps in the upstairs room where we were gathered.
v09: A young man named Eutychus was sitting in the window. As Paul spoke at length, Eutychus sank into a deep sleep. Overcome by sleep, he fell from the third floor and was picked up dead.
v10: Paul went down, bent over him, and took him in his arms. “Do not be alarmed,” he said, “for his life is in him.”
v11: Then Paul went back upstairs, broke the bread and ate. He spoke with them for a long time, until dawn, and then left.
v12: They took the young man home alive and were greatly comforted.
</p>

## Paul Hurries toward Jerusalem

<p>
v13: We went on ahead to the ship and sailed for Assos, intending to take Paul aboard there. He had arranged it this way because he intended to travel there on foot.
v14: When he met us at Assos, we took him aboard and went to Mitylene.
v15: Sailing from there, we arrived opposite Chios the next day. On the following day we reached Samos, and the day after that we came to Miletus.
v16: Paul had decided to sail past Ephesus so that he would not lose time in Asia, for he was hurrying to be in Jerusalem on the day of Pentecost, if possible.
</p>

## Paul Calls the Ephesian Elders

<p>
v17: From Miletus he sent to Ephesus and summoned the elders of the church.
v18: When they came to him, he said, “You yourselves know how I lived among you the whole time, from the first day I set foot in Asia,
v19: serving the Lord with all humility, with tears, and amid the trials that came upon me through the plots of Jewish opponents.
v20: You know that I held back nothing beneficial, but proclaimed it to you and taught you publicly and from house to house.
v21: I testified to both Jewish and Greek people about repentance toward God and faith in our Lord Jesus.
v22: And now, compelled by the Spirit, I am going to Jerusalem without knowing what will happen to me there,
v23: except that in city after city the Holy Spirit warns me that chains and afflictions await me.
v24: But I do not consider my life worth anything to myself, if only I may finish my course and the ministry I received from the Lord Jesus: to testify to the good news of God's grace.
</p>

## Shepherd God’s Flock

<p>
v25: “And now I know that none of you among whom I went about proclaiming the kingdom will ever see my face again.
v26: Therefore I testify to you today that I am innocent of the blood of everyone,
v27: for I did not shrink from declaring to you the whole purpose of God.
v28: Watch over yourselves and the whole flock, in which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers, to shepherd the church of God, which he obtained through the blood of his own Son.
v29: I know that after my departure, fierce wolves will come in among you and will not spare the flock.
v30: Even from among your own number, men will rise up speaking distorted things to draw the disciples away after themselves.
v31: Therefore stay alert, remembering that for three years, night and day, I never stopped warning each one of you with tears.
v32: And now I entrust you to God and to the word of his grace, which is able to build you up and give you an inheritance among all who are sanctified.
</p>

## Work, Help the Weak, and Give

<p>
v33: I coveted no one's silver, gold, or clothing.
v34: You yourselves know that these hands provided for my needs and for those who were with me.
v35: In everything I showed you that by working hard in this way, we must help the weak and remember the words of the Lord Jesus, who himself said, ‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’”
v36: After saying this, he knelt down with all of them and prayed.
v37: They all wept greatly, embraced Paul, and kissed him tenderly.
v38: They were grieving most of all because he had said that they would never see his face again. Then they accompanied him to the ship.
</p>

## Notes

v07: ‘The first day of the week’ names the gathering’s day; breaking bread can carry both meal and communal worship associations.

v28: The phrase translated ‘the blood of his own Son’ is literally ‘the blood of his own’ and is grammatically difficult.

v35: Paul preserves a saying of Jesus not recorded in the four Gospels.

## Vocabulary

v24: **διακονία (diakonia)**: Service or ministry.

v28: **ποιμαίνω (poimainō)**: Shepherd or tend a flock.
