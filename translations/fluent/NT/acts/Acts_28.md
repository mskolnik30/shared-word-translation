## Malta: Hospitality, Misjudgment, and Healing

<p>
v01: Once we had reached safety, we learned that the island was called Malta.
v02: The local people showed us extraordinary kindness. Because of the rain and cold, they kindled a fire and welcomed all of us.
v03: Paul gathered a bundle of brushwood and put it on the fire. A viper, driven out by the heat, fastened onto his hand.
v04: When the local people saw the creature hanging from his hand, they said to one another, “This man must be a murderer. Though he escaped from the sea, Justice has not allowed him to live.”
v05: But Paul shook the creature off into the fire and suffered no harm.
v06: They expected him to swell up or suddenly fall down dead. But after waiting a long time and seeing nothing unusual happen to him, they changed their minds and began saying he was a god.
v07: Near that place were lands belonging to the island's leading man, whose name was Publius. He welcomed us and showed us hospitality for three days.
v08: Publius's father was lying sick with fever and dysentery. Paul went in to him, prayed, laid his hands on him, and healed him.
v09: After this happened, the rest of the sick people on the island also came and were healed.
v10: They honored us in many ways, and when we were preparing to sail, they supplied everything we needed.
</p>

## Paul Arrives in Rome

<p>
v11: After three months, we set sail on an Alexandrian ship that had wintered at the island and bore the Twin Gods as its figurehead.
v12: We put in at Syracuse and stayed there three days.
v13: From there we weighed anchor and came to Rhegium. A day later a south wind sprang up, and on the second day we came to Puteoli.
v14: There we found brothers and sisters who invited us to stay with them for seven days. And in this way we came to Rome.
v15: The brothers and sisters there heard about us and came as far as the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns to meet us. When Paul saw them, he thanked God and took courage.
v16: When we entered Rome, Paul was allowed to live by himself with the soldier guarding him.
</p>

## Paul Meets the Jewish Leaders in Rome

<p>
v17: Three days later, Paul called together the local leaders of the Jewish community. When they assembled, he said, “Brothers, though I had done nothing against our people or our ancestral customs, I was handed over as a prisoner from Jerusalem into the hands of the Romans.
v18: After examining me, they wanted to release me because there was no basis for a death sentence against me.
v19: But when the Jewish leaders objected, I was compelled to appeal to Caesar—not that I had any accusation to bring against my nation.
v20: For this reason I asked to see you and speak with you, because it is for the hope of Israel that I am wearing this chain.”
v21: They said to him, “We have received no letters from Judea about you, and none of the brothers arriving here has reported or spoken anything evil about you.
v22: But we want to hear from you what you think, for we know that people everywhere speak against this sect.”
</p>

## Paul Testifies from Moses and the Prophets

<p>
v23: They set a day to meet with Paul, and even more people came to his lodging. From morning until evening he explained the kingdom of God, solemnly testifying and trying to persuade them about Jesus from both the Law of Moses and the Prophets.
v24: Some were persuaded by what he said, but others refused to believe.
v25: They disagreed with one another and began to leave after Paul made this one statement: “The Holy Spirit rightly spoke through the prophet Isaiah to your ancestors,
v26: saying, ‘Go to this people and say: You will indeed hear but never understand; you will indeed look but never perceive.
v27: For this people's heart has grown dull; they hear with difficulty, and they have closed their eyes. Otherwise they might see with their eyes, hear with their ears, understand with their heart, turn back, and I would heal them.’
v28: Therefore let it be known to you that this salvation from God has been sent to the Gentiles, and they will listen.”
</p>

## The Kingdom Proclaimed without Hindrance

<p>
v30: Paul stayed for two full years in his own rented lodging and welcomed everyone who came to him.
v31: He proclaimed the kingdom of God and taught about the Lord Jesus Christ with complete boldness and without hindrance.
</p>

## Notes

v02: The Greek calls the islanders ‘barbarians,’ meaning non-Greek-speaking people; the main text uses the neutral description ‘local people.’

v11: The Twin Gods are Castor and Pollux, the ship’s pagan emblem.

v29: Public verse 29 is absent from the pinned Greek text, so the numbering moves from 28 to 30.

## Vocabulary

v15: **παράκλησις (paraklēsis)**: Encouragement, comfort, or appeal.

v31: **ἀκωλύτως (akōlytōs)**: Without hindrance.
