## Saul Encounters Jesus

<p>
v01: Saul was still breathing threats and murder against the Lord’s disciples. He went to the high priest
v02: and asked for letters to the synagogues in Damascus so that if he found anyone belonging to the Way, men or women, he could bring them bound to Jerusalem.
v03: As he traveled and came near Damascus, suddenly a light from heaven flashed around him.
v04: He fell to the ground and heard a voice saying, “Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?”
v05: He asked, “Who are you, Lord?” The voice said, “I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.
v06: But get up and enter the city, and you will be told what you must do.”
v07: The men traveling with Saul stood speechless. They heard the sound of the voice but saw no one.
v08: Saul got up from the ground, but when he opened his eyes he could see nothing. So they led him by the hand into Damascus.
v09: For three days he could not see, and he neither ate nor drank.
</p>

## Ananias Is Sent to Saul

<p>
v10: There was a disciple in Damascus named Ananias. The Lord said to him in a vision, “Ananias.” He answered, “Here I am, Lord.”
v11: The Lord said, “Get up and go to the street called Straight. At Judas’ house ask for a man from Tarsus named Saul. Look—he is praying,
v12: and in a vision he has seen a man named Ananias come in and lay hands on him so he may regain his sight.”
v13: Ananias answered, “Lord, I have heard from many people about this man and how much evil he has done to your holy ones in Jerusalem.
v14: And here he has authority from the chief priests to bind everyone who calls on your name.”
v15: But the Lord told him, “Go, because this man is my chosen instrument to carry my name before Gentiles, kings, and the people of Israel.
v16: I will show him how much he must suffer for my name.”
v17: Ananias went and entered the house. He laid his hands on Saul and said, “Brother Saul, the Lord Jesus, who appeared to you on the road as you came here, has sent me so that you may regain your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit.”
v18: Immediately something like scales fell from Saul’s eyes, and he regained his sight. He got up and was baptized,
v19: and after taking food, he regained his strength.
</p>

## Saul Proclaims Jesus

<p>
For several days Saul stayed with the disciples in Damascus.
v20: Immediately he began proclaiming Jesus in the synagogues: “He is God’s Son.”
v21: Everyone who heard him was amazed and said, “Is this not the man who was destroying in Jerusalem those who call on this name? And did he not come here to bring them bound before the chief priests?”
v22: But Saul grew stronger and kept confounding the Jewish people living in Damascus by demonstrating that Jesus is the Christ.
v23: After many days had passed, some Jewish opponents plotted together to kill him,
v24: but Saul learned about their plot. They were watching the city gates day and night so they could kill him.
v25: But his disciples took him by night and lowered him through an opening in the wall in a basket.
</p>

## Barnabas Brings Saul to the Apostles

<p>
v26: When Saul came to Jerusalem, he tried to join the disciples, but they were all afraid of him because they did not trust that he really was a disciple.
v27: Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles. He explained how Saul had seen the Lord on the road, that the Lord had spoken to him, and how Saul had spoken boldly in Jesus’ name in Damascus.
v28: So Saul moved freely among them in Jerusalem, speaking boldly in the Lord’s name.
v29: He also spoke and argued with the Hellenists, but they tried to kill him.
v30: When the brothers and sisters learned about this, they brought him down to Caesarea and sent him away to Tarsus.
v31: Then the church throughout Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and was being built up. Walking in the fear of the Lord and in the encouragement of the Holy Spirit, it continued to grow.
</p>

## Aeneas Is Healed

<p>
v32: As Peter traveled throughout the region, he also went down to the holy ones living in Lydda.
v33: There he found a man named Aeneas who had been confined to a mat for eight years because he was paralyzed.
v34: Peter said to him, “Aeneas, Jesus Christ heals you. Get up and make your bed.” Immediately he got up.
v35: Everyone living in Lydda and Sharon saw him, and they turned to the Lord.
</p>

## Tabitha Is Restored to Life

<p>
v36: In Joppa there was a disciple named Tabitha, which translated is Dorcas. She was full of good works and acts of mercy for the poor.
v37: In those days she became sick and died. After washing her body, they laid her in an upstairs room.
v38: Since Lydda was near Joppa, the disciples heard Peter was there and sent two men to urge him, “Please come to us without delay.”
v39: Peter got up and went with them. When he arrived, they brought him to the upstairs room. All the widows stood beside him weeping and showing him tunics and other clothing Dorcas had made while she was with them.
v40: Peter sent everyone outside, knelt down, and prayed. Turning toward the body, he said, “Tabitha, get up.” She opened her eyes, saw Peter, and sat up.
v41: Peter gave her his hand and raised her up. Then he called the holy ones and the widows and presented her alive.
v42: This became known throughout Joppa, and many trusted in the Lord.
v43: Peter stayed in Joppa for many days with a man named Simon, a tanner.
</p>

## Notes

v01–02: Saul’s persecution is organized and authorized, and again explicitly targets both men and women who belong to “the Way.”

v04–05: Jesus identifies persecution of his disciples as persecution of himself.

v07: Acts 9 says Saul’s companions hear the voice or sound but see no one. Later retellings describe the experience with different grammatical emphases; Fluent does not harmonize the accounts.

v09: Saul’s blindness, fasting, and dependence reverse his position as the powerful agent entering Damascus with authority.

v10–17: Ananias is allowed to voice realistic fear. The Lord does not deny Saul’s past violence but gives Ananias a new description of Saul’s calling.

v15–16: Saul’s vocation includes Gentiles, kings, Israel, and suffering for Jesus’ name from the beginning.

v17: Ananias addresses his former persecutor as “Brother Saul” before Saul’s sight is restored.

v18–19: Restored sight, baptism, food, and strength form one compressed transition into Saul’s new life.

v20: Saul’s first summarized proclamation in Acts is that Jesus is God’s Son.

v22: “Demonstrating” does not imply coercive proof; Saul brings scriptural and christological claims together in argument.

v23: The opponents here are a particular group of Jewish people in Damascus who plot against Saul. Fluent avoids turning this into a generalized statement about Jews.

v27: Barnabas becomes the mediator who makes Saul’s testimony socially credible to the frightened Jerusalem disciples.

v31: Peace, being built up, fear of the Lord, encouragement of the Spirit, and numerical growth are held together.

v34: Peter explicitly names Jesus Christ as the healer.

v36: Tabitha is explicitly called a female disciple, `mathētria`, a rare feminine form.

v39: The widows’ garments make Tabitha’s ministry materially visible; her good works are not abstract.

v40: Peter prays before speaking to Tabitha, and the scene intentionally echoes resurrection stories in Luke and the Elijah/Elisha traditions.

v43: Peter’s staying with a tanner places him in a household associated with ritually problematic animal-death work immediately before the Cornelius boundary-crossing narrative.
