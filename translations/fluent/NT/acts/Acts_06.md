## Seven Are Chosen for the Daily Distribution

<p>
v01: In those days, as the number of disciples was increasing, a complaint arose from the Hellenists against the Hebrews because their widows were being overlooked in the daily distribution.
v02: The twelve called together the whole community of disciples and said, “It would not be right for us to neglect God’s word in order to serve tables.
v03: Therefore, brothers and sisters, select from among you seven men with good reputations, full of the Spirit and wisdom, whom we can appoint over this responsibility.
v04: But we will devote ourselves to prayer and to the ministry of the word.”
v05: This proposal pleased the whole community. They chose Stephen, a man full of faith and the Holy Spirit; Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus, a convert to Judaism from Antioch.
v06: They presented these men to the apostles, who prayed and laid their hands on them.
v07: God’s word continued to spread, the number of disciples in Jerusalem increased greatly, and a large number of priests became obedient to the faith.
</p>

## Stephen Is Seized

<p>
v08: Stephen, full of grace and power, was doing great wonders and signs among the people.
v09: But some people from the synagogue called the Synagogue of the Freedmen, along with Cyrenians, Alexandrians, and people from Cilicia and Asia, rose up and argued with Stephen.
v10: They could not withstand the wisdom and the Spirit with which he was speaking.
v11: Then they secretly prompted men to say, “We have heard him speaking blasphemous words against Moses and God.”
v12: They stirred up the people, the elders, and the scribes. They came upon Stephen, seized him, and brought him before the council.
v13: They set up false witnesses who said, “This man never stops speaking against this holy place and the Law.
v14: We have heard him say that this Jesus the Nazarene will destroy this place and change the customs Moses handed down to us.”
v15: Everyone sitting in the council looked intently at Stephen and saw that his face was like the face of an angel.
</p>

## Notes

v01: The dispute is internal to the Jesus-community and follows a cultural-linguistic fault line between Greek-speaking and Hebrew/Aramaic-speaking Jewish believers. The problem is concrete: widows are being overlooked.

v02–04: Acts distinguishes responsibilities without devaluing either. The same `diakon-` word-family can describe serving tables and ministry of the word.

v03: The seven are selected for character, public reputation, Spirit-fullness, and wisdom rather than logistical skill alone.

v05–06: The community selects the seven, while the apostles pray and commission them by laying on hands.

v07: Luke marks the resolution not by institutional description but by renewed growth of God’s word and disciples.

v07: The obedience of many priests prevents Acts’ conflict narratives from becoming a blanket opposition between the Jesus movement and Jewish priesthood.

v08: Stephen’s ministry immediately extends beyond food distribution into signs and public witness.

v10: Wisdom and Spirit are joined in Stephen’s speech, fulfilling the qualification named in v03.

v11–14: Opposition escalates from failed argument to secretly induced accusation and false testimony. Fluent does not treat the accusations as proven descriptions of Stephen’s teaching.

v15: The angel-like face signals divine presence and prepares for Stephen’s long defense and vision.
