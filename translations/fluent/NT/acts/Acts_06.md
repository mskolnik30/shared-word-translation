## Seven Are Chosen for the Daily Distribution

<p>
v01: In those days, as the number of disciples increased, the Greek-speaking Jewish believers began complaining against the Hebrew-speaking believers because their widows were being overlooked in the daily distribution.
v02: So the Twelve called the whole community of disciples together and said, “It would not be right for us to neglect God’s word in order to serve tables.
v03: Brothers and sisters, select from among you seven men with good reputations, full of the Spirit and wisdom, whom we will appoint over this responsibility.
v04: But we will devote ourselves to prayer and to the ministry of the word.”
v05: This proposal pleased the whole community. They chose Stephen, a man full of faith and the Holy Spirit, along with Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus, a convert to Judaism from Antioch.
v06: They presented these men to the apostles, who prayed and laid their hands on them.
v07: God’s word continued to spread, and the number of disciples in Jerusalem increased greatly. Even a large group of priests became obedient to the faith.
</p>

## Stephen Is Seized

<p>
v08: Stephen, full of grace and power, was performing great wonders and signs among the people.
v09: But some members of the synagogue called the Synagogue of the Freedmen—including people from Cyrene, Alexandria, Cilicia, and Asia—rose up and argued with Stephen.
v10: Yet they could not withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by which he spoke.
v11: Then they secretly induced men to say, “We heard him speak blasphemous words against Moses and God.”
v12: They stirred up the people, elders, and scribes, then came upon Stephen, seized him, and brought him before the council.
v13: They set up false witnesses who said, “This man never stops speaking against this holy place and the law.
v14: For we heard him say that this Jesus the Nazarene will destroy this place and change the customs Moses handed down to us.”
v15: Everyone sitting in the council stared at Stephen and saw that his face was like the face of an angel.
</p>

## Notes

v01: The Hellenists and Hebrews are Greek-speaking and Hebrew- or Aramaic-speaking Jewish believers. The conflict concerns unequal daily care for their widows, not a division between Jewish and non-Jewish Christians.

v02–04: Service at tables and service of the word use related ministry language. The apostles distinguish responsibilities without declaring material care unspiritual.

v03: The selected seven are men in this narrated appointment. The wider address ‘brothers and sisters’ includes the whole community making the selection.

v05: Nicolaus is identified as a convert to Judaism from Antioch, preserving both his adopted religious community and geographic origin.

v09: ‘Freedmen’ likely refers to formerly enslaved people and their descendants associated with the named synagogue.

v11–14: The men are secretly induced, and the narrative calls the resulting witnesses false. Their accusations are reported as accusations, not accepted as fact.

v15: Stephen’s face is compared to an angel’s face. The image describes what the council sees without removing the danger of the hearing.

## Vocabulary

v01: **διακονία (diakonia)**: Service, ministry, or practical provision.

v09: **Λιβερτῖνος (Libertinos)**: A freed person or member of a freedmen’s community.

v13: **μάρτυς (martys)**: Witness; here modified as false.
