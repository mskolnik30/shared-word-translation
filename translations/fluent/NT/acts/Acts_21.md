## Paul Continues toward Jerusalem

<p>
v01: After tearing ourselves away from them, we set sail on a straight course to Cos, the next day to Rhodes, and from there to Patara.
v02: Finding a ship crossing to Phoenicia, we went aboard and set sail.
v03: When Cyprus came into view, we left it on our left, sailed toward Syria, and landed at Tyre, where the ship was to unload its cargo.
v04: We sought out the disciples and stayed there seven days. Through the Spirit they kept telling Paul not to set foot in Jerusalem.
v05: When our days there were over, we left and continued our journey. All of them, including women and children, escorted us outside the city. We knelt on the beach and prayed.
v06: After saying farewell to one another, we boarded the ship, and they returned home.
v07: We completed the voyage from Tyre and arrived at Ptolemais. We greeted the brothers and sisters and stayed with them for one day.
v08: The next day we left and came to Caesarea. We entered the house of Philip the evangelist, one of the Seven, and stayed with him.
v09: He had four unmarried daughters who prophesied.
v10: After we had stayed for several days, a prophet named Agabus came down from Judea.
v11: He came to us, took Paul's belt, bound his own feet and hands, and said, “This is what the Holy Spirit says: ‘In this way the Jewish leaders in Jerusalem will bind the man who owns this belt and hand him over to Gentiles.’”
v12: When we heard this, both we and the local believers pleaded with Paul not to go up to Jerusalem.
v13: Then Paul answered, “What are you doing, weeping and breaking my heart? I am ready not only to be bound but even to die in Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus.”
v14: Since he would not be persuaded, we became quiet and said, “Let the Lord's will be done.”
</p>

## Paul Meets James and the Elders

<p>
v15: After those days, we made our preparations and went up to Jerusalem.
v16: Some disciples from Caesarea went with us, bringing us to Mnason of Cyprus, an early disciple with whom we were to stay.
v17: When we arrived in Jerusalem, the brothers and sisters welcomed us gladly.
v18: The next day Paul went with us to see James, and all the elders were present.
v19: After greeting them, Paul recounted one by one the things God had done among the Gentiles through his ministry.
v20: When they heard this, they glorified God. Then they said to Paul, “You see, brother, how many tens of thousands of believers there are among the Jewish people, and they are all zealous for the Law.
v21: But they have been informed that you teach all the Jewish people living among the Gentiles to abandon Moses, telling them not to circumcise their children or follow the customs.
v22: What then should be done? They will certainly hear that you have come.
v23: So do what we tell you. We have four men who are under a vow.
v24: Take these men with you, purify yourself along with them, and pay their expenses so they may shave their heads. Then everyone will know that there is nothing to the reports they have heard about you, but that you yourself live in obedience to the Law.
v25: As for the Gentiles who have believed, we sent them our decision that they should keep away from food sacrificed to idols, from blood, from what is strangled, and from sexual immorality.”
v26: Then Paul took the men, and the next day he purified himself with them and entered the temple. He announced when the days of purification would be completed and when the offering would be made for each of them.
</p>

## Paul Is Seized in the Temple

<p>
v27: When the seven days were nearly over, some Jewish people from Asia saw Paul in the temple, stirred up the whole crowd, and seized him,
v28: shouting, “People of Israel, help us! This is the man who teaches everyone everywhere against our people, the Law, and this place. What is more, he has brought Greeks into the temple and defiled this holy place.”
v29: For they had previously seen Trophimus the Ephesian with Paul in the city and assumed that Paul had brought him into the temple.
v30: The whole city was stirred up, and the people came running together. They seized Paul, dragged him out of the temple, and immediately the doors were shut.
v31: While they were trying to kill him, word reached the commander of the cohort that all Jerusalem was in turmoil.
v32: He immediately took soldiers and centurions and ran down to them. When they saw the commander and the soldiers, they stopped beating Paul.
v33: Then the commander came near, arrested Paul, and ordered him to be bound with two chains. He began asking who he was and what he had done.
v34: Some in the crowd shouted one thing and some another. Since the commander could not learn the truth because of the uproar, he ordered Paul to be taken into the barracks.
v35: When Paul reached the steps, the soldiers had to carry him because of the crowd's violence,
v36: for the multitude of people followed, shouting, “Away with him!”
</p>

## Paul Requests Permission to Speak

<p>

v39: Paul said, “I am a Jewish man from Tarsus in Cilicia, a citizen of no insignificant city. I ask you, permit me to speak to the people.”

## Notes

v04, 11–14: The Spirit-given warnings name the suffering ahead; the narrative also records Paul’s resolve and the community’s submission to the Lord’s will.

v09: Philip’s four unmarried daughters are explicitly prophets.

v25: The pinned text contains all four requirements, including abstention from food offered to idols.

## Vocabulary

v08: **εὐαγγελιστής (euangelistēs)**: Evangelist or proclaimer of good news.

v24: **ἁγνίζω (hagnizō)**: Purify ceremonially.
