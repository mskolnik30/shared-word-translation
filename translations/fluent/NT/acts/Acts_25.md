## Paul Appeals to Caesar

<p>
v01: Three days after Festus arrived in the province, he went up from Caesarea to Jerusalem.
v02: The chief priests and leading men among the Jewish people presented their case against Paul and urged Festus,
v03: asking as a favor that he summon Paul to Jerusalem. They were planning an ambush to kill him along the way.
v04: Festus answered that Paul was being kept in Caesarea and that he himself would return there soon.
v05: “Therefore,” he said, “let your leaders come down with me and accuse the man if he has done something wrong.”
v06: After spending no more than eight or ten days among them, Festus went down to Caesarea. The next day he sat on the judgment seat and ordered Paul to be brought in.
v07: When Paul appeared, the Jewish leaders who had come down from Jerusalem stood around him and brought many serious charges they could not prove.
v08: Paul defended himself: “I have committed no offense against the Law of the Jewish people, against the temple, or against Caesar.”
v09: But Festus, wanting to gain favor with the Jewish leaders, asked Paul, “Do you want to go up to Jerusalem and be tried before me there concerning these charges?”
v10: Paul said, “I am standing before Caesar’s judgment seat, where I ought to be tried. I have done nothing wrong to the Jewish people, as you yourself know very well.
v11: If I am guilty of wrongdoing and have done something deserving death, I do not refuse to die. But if there is nothing to these charges against me, no one can hand me over to them as a favor. I appeal to Caesar.”
v12: Then Festus conferred with his council and answered, “You have appealed to Caesar. To Caesar you will go.”
</p>

## Festus Discusses Paul with Agrippa

<p>
v13: After several days, King Agrippa and Bernice arrived in Caesarea to pay their respects to Festus.
v14: Since they stayed there many days, Festus presented Paul’s case to the king: “There is a man Felix left here as a prisoner.
v15: When I was in Jerusalem, the chief priests and elders of the Jewish people brought charges against him and asked me for a sentence against him.
v16: I answered that it is not Roman custom to hand over an accused person before they meet their accusers face to face and have an opportunity to defend themselves against the charge.
v17: So when they came here, I did not delay. The next day I sat on the judgment seat and ordered the man to be brought in.
v18: When his accusers stood up, they did not charge him with any of the crimes I expected.
v19: Instead, they had certain disputes with him about their own religion and about a man named Jesus who had died but whom Paul claimed was alive.
v20: Since I was at a loss about how to investigate these matters, I asked whether he wanted to go to Jerusalem and be tried there.
v21: But Paul appealed to be kept for the Emperor’s decision, so I ordered him held until I can send him to Caesar.”
v22: Agrippa said to Festus, “I would like to hear the man myself.” Festus replied, “Tomorrow you will hear him.”
</p>

## Paul Is Brought Before Agrippa

<p>
v23: The next day Agrippa and Bernice came with great ceremony and entered the audience hall with the military commanders and prominent men of the city. At Festus’ command Paul was brought in.
v24: Festus said, “King Agrippa and everyone present with us, you see this man about whom a large number of Jewish people appealed to me both in Jerusalem and here, shouting that he should no longer live.
v25: But I found that he had done nothing deserving death. Since he himself appealed to the Emperor, I decided to send him.
v26: Yet I have nothing definite to write to my lord about him. Therefore I have brought him before all of you, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that after this examination I may have something to write.
v27: It seems unreasonable to me to send a prisoner without also indicating the charges against him.”
</p>

## Notes

v02–03: A formal legal request conceals an assassination plan. Acts continues to distinguish judicial procedure from extra-legal violence.

v07: Festus himself says the many serious charges cannot be proved.

v08: Paul claims innocence across three spheres: Jewish Law, temple, and Roman imperial law.

v09: Festus’ proposal to move the trial is explicitly motivated by desire to gain favor with the Jewish leadership.

v10–11: Paul does not deny the legitimacy of lawful capital punishment in principle; he denies that the accusations against him justify it.

v11: Paul’s appeal to Caesar is both a legal right and the narrative mechanism moving him toward the Rome mission promised in 23:11.

v16: Festus articulates a Roman procedural principle: the accused should face accusers and receive opportunity for defense before being handed over.

v19: Festus reduces the central theological dispute to “a certain Jesus who had died, whom Paul claimed was alive.” Luke lets the outsider’s summary expose resurrection as the core issue.

v24: Festus reports widespread pressure but then states his own legal finding of no capital crime.

v25–27: The absurdity of Paul’s legal situation becomes explicit: Festus plans to send a prisoner to the emperor without a clear charge to write.
