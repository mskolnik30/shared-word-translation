## Paul Appeals to Caesar

<p>
v01: Three days after Festus arrived in the province, he went up from Caesarea to Jerusalem.
v02: The chief priests and leaders of the Jewish people presented their case against Paul to him and pleaded with him,
v03: asking as a favor that he summon Paul to Jerusalem, because they were arranging an ambush to kill him along the way.
v04: Festus answered that Paul was being held in Caesarea and that he himself would be returning there soon.
v05: “Therefore,” he said, “let your influential men come down with me and accuse him if the man has done anything wrong.”
v06: After staying among them no more than eight or ten days, Festus went down to Caesarea. The next day he sat on the judgment seat and ordered Paul to be brought in.
v07: When Paul appeared, the Jewish leaders who had come down from Jerusalem surrounded him and brought many serious charges they could not prove.
v08: Paul defended himself: “I have committed no offense against the Law of the Jewish people, the temple, or Caesar.”
v09: But Festus, wanting to gain favor with the Jewish leaders, asked Paul, “Are you willing to go up to Jerusalem and be tried there before me on these charges?”
v10: Paul said, “I am standing before Caesar's judgment seat, where I ought to be tried. I have done no wrong to the Jewish people, as you yourself know very well.
v11: If I am guilty of wrongdoing and have done anything deserving death, I do not refuse to die. But if there is nothing to their charges against me, no one can hand me over to them as a favor. I appeal to Caesar.”
v12: After Festus conferred with his council, he answered, “You have appealed to Caesar; to Caesar you will go.”
</p>

## Festus Discusses Paul with Agrippa

<p>
v13: Several days later, King Agrippa and Bernice arrived in Caesarea to pay their respects to Festus.
v14: Since they were staying there for many days, Festus presented Paul's case to the king: “There is a man Felix left here as a prisoner.
v15: When I was in Jerusalem, the chief priests and elders of the Jewish people brought charges against him, asking for a sentence of condemnation.
v16: I answered that it is not Roman practice to hand anyone over before the accused meets the accusers face to face and has an opportunity to answer the charge.
v17: So when they assembled here, I made no delay. The next day I sat on the judgment seat and ordered the man to be brought in.
v18: When his accusers stood up, they brought none of the crimes I had expected against him.
v19: Instead, they had certain disputes with him about their own religion and about a dead man named Jesus whom Paul claimed was alive.
v20: At a loss about how to investigate these matters, I asked whether he wished to go to Jerusalem and be tried there concerning them.
v21: But when Paul appealed to be held for the emperor's decision, I ordered him kept in custody until I could send him to Caesar.”
v22: Agrippa said to Festus, “I would like to hear the man myself.” Festus replied, “Tomorrow you will hear him.”
</p>

## Paul Is Brought Before Agrippa

<p>
v23: So the next day Agrippa and Bernice came with great ceremony and entered the audience hall with the commanders and prominent men of the city. At Festus's command, Paul was brought in.
v24: Festus said, “King Agrippa and all you men present with us, you see this man. The whole Jewish leadership has petitioned me about him, both in Jerusalem and here, shouting that he should no longer live.
v25: But I found that he had done nothing deserving death. Since he himself appealed to the emperor, I decided to send him.
v26: Yet I have nothing definite to write to my lord about him. Therefore I have brought him before all of you, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that after this examination I may have something to write.
v27: For it seems unreasonable to me to send a prisoner without also indicating the charges against him.”
</p>

## Notes

v11: Paul accepts death if a capital offense is proved but refuses political surrender and appeals to Caesar.

v19: Festus describes the dispute from his own outsider perspective; ‘a dead man named Jesus’ is his characterization, not the narrator’s conclusion.

## Vocabulary

v10: **βῆμα (bēma)**: Judgment seat or tribunal.

v21: **Σεβαστός (Sebastos)**: Augustus or emperor, an imperial title.
