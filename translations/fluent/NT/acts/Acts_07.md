## Stephen’s Defense: Abraham and the Promise

<p>
v01: The high priest asked, “Are these things true?”
v02: Stephen replied, “Brothers and fathers, listen. The God of glory appeared to our ancestor Abraham while he was in Mesopotamia, before he lived in Haran,
v03: and said to him, ‘Leave your land and your relatives, and go to the land I will show you.’
v04: Then Abraham left the land of the Chaldeans and lived in Haran. After his father died, God moved him from there to this land where you now live.
v05: God gave him no inheritance in it—not even enough ground to set his foot on—but promised to give it to him as a possession and to his descendants after him, though Abraham had no child.
v06: God spoke of it this way: ‘His descendants will live as strangers in a foreign land, and its people will enslave and mistreat them for four hundred years.
v07: But I will judge the nation they serve,’ God said, ‘and afterward they will come out and worship me in this place.’
v08: Then God gave Abraham the covenant of circumcision. So Abraham fathered Isaac and circumcised him on the eighth day; Isaac fathered Jacob, and Jacob the twelve patriarchs.
</p>

## Joseph in Egypt

<p>
v09: “The patriarchs were jealous of Joseph and sold him into Egypt. But God was with him
v10: and rescued him from all his troubles. God gave him favor and wisdom before Pharaoh, king of Egypt, who appointed him ruler over Egypt and his whole household.
v11: Then a famine came over all Egypt and Canaan, bringing great suffering, and our ancestors could find no food.
v12: When Jacob heard that there was grain in Egypt, he sent our ancestors there on their first visit.
v13: On their second visit, Joseph made himself known to his brothers, and Pharaoh learned about Joseph’s family.
v14: Then Joseph sent for his father Jacob and his whole family, seventy-five people in all.
v15: So Jacob went down to Egypt, where he and our ancestors died.
v16: Their bodies were brought back to Shechem and placed in the tomb Abraham had bought for a sum of silver from the sons of Hamor in Shechem.
</p>

## Moses Is Born and Rejected

<p>
v17: “As the time approached for God to fulfill the promise he had made to Abraham, the people grew and multiplied in Egypt.
v18: Then another king rose to power over Egypt who knew nothing about Joseph.
v19: He dealt treacherously with our people and mistreated our ancestors, forcing them to abandon their infants so they would not survive.
v20: At that time Moses was born, and he was beautiful in God’s sight. For three months he was cared for in his father’s house.
v21: When he was abandoned, Pharaoh’s daughter took him in and raised him as her own son.
v22: Moses was educated in all the wisdom of the Egyptians and was powerful in his words and actions.
v23: When he was forty years old, it came into his heart to visit his own people, the Israelites.
v24: When he saw one of them being wronged, he defended the oppressed man and avenged him by striking down the Egyptian.
v25: He thought his own people would understand that God was giving them deliverance through him, but they did not understand.
v26: The next day he came upon two Israelites fighting and tried to reconcile them. He said, ‘Men, you are brothers. Why are you wronging each other?’
v27: But the man who was wronging his neighbor pushed Moses away and said, ‘Who made you a ruler and judge over us?
v28: Do you want to kill me as you killed the Egyptian yesterday?’
v29: At this remark Moses fled and lived as a foreigner in the land of Midian, where he became the father of two sons.
</p>

## God Sends Moses Back

<p>
v30: “After forty years had passed, an angel appeared to him in the wilderness near Mount Sinai, in the flame of a burning bush.
v31: When Moses saw it, he marveled at the sight. As he approached for a closer look, the Lord’s voice came:
v32: ‘I am the God of your ancestors—the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.’ Moses began to tremble and did not dare look.
v33: Then the Lord told him, ‘Take the sandals off your feet, because the place where you are standing is holy ground.
v34: I have surely seen the mistreatment of my people in Egypt. I have heard their groaning, and I have come down to rescue them. Now come—I will send you to Egypt.’
v35: “This Moses, whom they rejected by saying, ‘Who made you a ruler and judge?’ is the very one God sent as both ruler and redeemer through the angel who appeared to him in the bush.
v36: He led them out, performing wonders and signs in Egypt, at the Red Sea, and in the wilderness for forty years.
v37: This is the Moses who told the Israelites, ‘God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among your own people.’
v38: He is the one who was in the assembly in the wilderness with the angel who spoke to him at Mount Sinai and with our ancestors. He received living words to give to us.
</p>

## Israel Turns Back in Heart

<p>
v39: But our ancestors refused to obey him. They rejected him and in their hearts turned back to Egypt.
v40: They told Aaron, ‘Make gods for us who will go before us. As for this Moses who led us out of the land of Egypt, we do not know what has happened to him.’
v41: In those days they made a calf, offered sacrifice to the idol, and celebrated what their own hands had made.
v42: So God turned away and gave them over to worship the host of heaven, as it is written in the book of the prophets: ‘House of Israel, was it to me that you offered slaughtered animals and sacrifices for forty years in the wilderness?
v43: You carried the tent of Moloch and the star of your god Rephan, the images you made to worship. Therefore I will send you into exile beyond Babylon.’
</p>

## Tent, Temple, and the Most High

<p>
v44: “Our ancestors had the tent of testimony in the wilderness. It was made as God directed Moses, according to the pattern he had seen.
v45: Our ancestors received it and brought it in with Joshua when they took possession of the nations God drove out before them. It remained there until the days of David.
v46: David found favor before God and asked to find a dwelling place for the God of Jacob.
v47: But Solomon built him a house.
v48: Yet the Most High does not live in houses made by human hands. As the prophet says:
v49: ‘Heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool. What kind of house will you build for me, says the Lord, or where will my resting place be?
v50: Did not my hand make all these things?’
</p>

## Stephen Confronts the Council

<p>
v51: “You stiff-necked people, uncircumcised in heart and ears! You always resist the Holy Spirit. You are just like your ancestors.
v52: Which of the prophets did your ancestors not persecute? They even killed those who announced beforehand the coming of the Righteous One, whom you have now betrayed and murdered.
v53: You received the law as ordained through angels, yet you did not keep it.”
</p>

## Stephen Is Killed

<p>
v54: When they heard these things, they were cut to the heart and ground their teeth at him.
v55: But Stephen, full of the Holy Spirit, looked intently into heaven and saw God’s glory and Jesus standing at God’s right hand.
v56: He said, “Look! I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at God’s right hand.”
v57: But they cried out with a loud voice, covered their ears, and rushed at him together.
v58: They drove him out of the city and began to stone him. The witnesses laid their cloaks at the feet of a young man named Saul.
v59: As they were stoning Stephen, he called out, “Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.”
v60: Then he fell to his knees and cried out in a loud voice, “Lord, do not hold this sin against them.” After saying this, he fell asleep.
</p>

## Notes

v14: Stephen gives the family total as seventy-five, following the Greek form of the ancestral story. The translation does not harmonize this with the Hebrew total of seventy.

v16: Stephen compresses details associated with the ancestral burials and the purchase at Shechem. The translation follows his speech rather than silently reconciling it with Genesis.

v38: The word translated ‘assembly’ is the same term often rendered ‘church’; here it refers to Israel gathered in the wilderness.

v43: Moloch and Rephan are names in Stephen’s quotation of the prophets. The exact ancient identifications are not explained in the main text.

v51: ‘Uncircumcised in heart and ears’ is a prophetic image for stubborn resistance, not a statement about physical disability.

## Vocabulary

v08: **διαθήκη (diathēkē)**: Covenant, an established binding relationship or arrangement.

v35: **λυτρωτής (lytrōtēs)**: Redeemer or liberator, one who brings release.

v38: **λόγια (logia)**: Divine sayings or oracles.
