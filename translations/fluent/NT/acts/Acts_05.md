## Ananias and Sapphira

<p>
v01: But a man named Ananias, together with his wife Sapphira, sold a piece of property.
v02: With his wife’s knowledge, he kept back some of the price for himself. Then he brought only part and laid it at the apostles’ feet.
v03: Peter asked, “Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart so that you lied to the Holy Spirit and kept back some of the field’s price?
v04: While it remained unsold, didn’t it remain yours? And after it was sold, wasn’t the money under your authority? Why did you conceive this deed in your heart? You have not lied to people but to God.”
v05: When Ananias heard these words, he fell down and died. Great fear came upon everyone who heard about it.
v06: The young men got up, wrapped his body, carried him out, and buried him.
v07: About three hours later his wife came in, unaware of what had happened.
v08: Peter asked her, “Tell me, did you sell the field for this amount?” She answered, “Yes, for that amount.”
v09: Peter said to her, “Why did the two of you agree to test the Spirit of the Lord? Look, the feet of those who buried your husband are at the door, and they will carry you out too.”
v10: Immediately she fell down at his feet and died. When the young men came in, they found her dead, carried her out, and buried her beside her husband.
v11: Great fear came upon the whole church and everyone who heard about these things.
</p>

## Signs, Wonders, and Growing Crowds

<p>
v12: Through the apostles’ hands many signs and wonders were done among the people. All the believers were together in Solomon’s Portico.
v13: None of the others dared join them, though the people spoke highly of them.
v14: Yet more and more believers in the Lord—large numbers of both men and women—were added to them.
v15: As a result, people carried the sick into the streets and laid them on small beds and mats so that when Peter came by, at least his shadow might fall on some of them.
v16: Crowds also gathered from the towns around Jerusalem, bringing sick people and those tormented by unclean spirits, and all of them were healed.
</p>

## The Apostles Are Arrested and Freed

<p>
v17: Then the high priest and all his associates, who belonged to the party of the Sadducees, rose up, filled with jealousy.
v18: They arrested the apostles and put them in the public prison.
v19: But during the night an angel of the Lord opened the prison doors, brought them out, and said,
v20: “Go, stand in the temple courts, and tell the people all the words of this life.”
v21: When they heard this, they entered the temple courts at daybreak and began teaching. When the high priest and his associates arrived, they called together the council and the entire senate of Israel’s people. Then they sent to the prison to have the apostles brought in.
v22: But when the officers arrived, they did not find them in the prison, so they returned and reported,
v23: “We found the prison securely locked and the guards standing at the doors, but when we opened them, we found no one inside.”
v24: When the temple captain and the chief priests heard these words, they were perplexed about what this might lead to.
v25: Then someone came and reported, “Look, the men you put in prison are standing in the temple courts teaching the people.”
v26: Then the captain went with the officers and brought them back, but without violence, because they feared the people might stone them.
</p>

## We Must Obey God Rather Than People

<p>
v27: They brought the apostles in and had them stand before the council. The high priest questioned them,
v28: saying, “We strictly ordered you not to teach in this name, yet look—you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching and intend to bring this man’s blood upon us.”
v29: Peter and the apostles answered, “We must obey God rather than people.
v30: The God of our ancestors raised Jesus, whom you killed by hanging him on a tree.
v31: God exalted him to his right hand as Leader and Savior, to give repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sins.
v32: We are witnesses of these things, and so is the Holy Spirit, whom God has given to those who obey him.”
v33: When they heard this, they were enraged and wanted to kill the apostles.
</p>

## Gamaliel Advises Restraint

<p>
v34: But a Pharisee named Gamaliel, a teacher of the law honored by all the people, stood in the council and ordered the apostles put outside for a little while.
v35: Then he told the council, “People of Israel, be careful what you intend to do to these men.
v36: Some time ago Theudas rose up, claiming to be somebody, and about four hundred men joined him. He was killed, and all who followed him were scattered and came to nothing.
v37: After him Judas the Galilean rose up in the days of the census and drew people after him. He also perished, and all who followed him were scattered.
v38: So in the present case I tell you: stay away from these men and leave them alone. If this plan or work is of human origin, it will be overthrown.
v39: But if it is from God, you will not be able to overthrow them. You may even be found fighting against God.” They were persuaded by him.
v40: They called the apostles in, had them beaten, ordered them not to speak in the name of Jesus, and released them.
</p>

## Rejoicing and Continuing to Teach

<p>
v41: So the apostles left the council rejoicing that they had been considered worthy to suffer dishonor for the Name.
v42: Every day, in the temple courts and from house to house, they never stopped teaching and proclaiming the good news that Jesus is the Messiah.
</p>

## Notes

v01–11: Ananias and Sapphira retain control of the property and its proceeds; Peter identifies their sin as a coordinated lie about the gift. The deaths remain disturbing and are not softened into metaphor.

v14: The growing community explicitly includes large numbers of both men and women.

v15: People hope Peter’s shadow will fall on the sick. The narrative reports their action without stating that the shadow itself heals.

v17: The word can mean zeal or jealousy. The leaders’ subsequent imprisonment of the apostles favors the hostile sense ‘jealousy.’

v26: The officers avoid violence because they fear being stoned by the crowd. The threat of collective violence is explicit.

v30: ‘Tree’ is the ordinary word for wood and evokes scriptural language about a person hung on a tree.

v31: ‘Leader’ can also mean pioneer or originator. It is paired here with Savior.

v36–37: Gamaliel refers to Theudas and Judas the Galilean as past examples. The translation preserves Luke’s sequence without attempting to solve external chronological questions inside the text.

v40–41: The council has the apostles beaten and shamed. Their joy does not make the abuse good; it concerns being counted worthy for the Name.

## Vocabulary

v11: **ἐκκλησία (ekklēsia)**: Assembly, congregation, or church.

v29: **πειθαρχέω (peitharcheō)**: Obey one in authority.

v41: **ὄνομα (onoma)**: The Name, a reverent way of referring to Jesus’ identity and authority.
