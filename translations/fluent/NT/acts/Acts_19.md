## Disciples of John Receive the Spirit

<p>
v01: While Apollos was in Corinth, Paul traveled through the inland regions and came to Ephesus. There he found some disciples
v02: and asked them, “Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you came to believe?” They answered, “No, we have not even heard that there is a Holy Spirit.”
v03: He asked, “Then into what were you baptized?” They said, “Into John's baptism.”
v04: Paul said, “John baptized with a baptism of repentance, telling the people to believe in the one coming after him—that is, in Jesus.”
v05: When they heard this, they were baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus.
v06: When Paul laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they began speaking in tongues and prophesying.
v07: There were about twelve men in all.
</p>

## The Word Spreads Throughout Asia

<p>
v08: Paul entered the synagogue and spoke boldly for three months, reasoning with the people and persuading them about the kingdom of God.
v09: But when some became stubborn and refused to believe, speaking evil of the Way before the crowd, Paul withdrew from them. He took the disciples with him and reasoned daily in the lecture hall of Tyrannus.
v10: This continued for two years, so that everyone living in Asia heard the word of the Lord—both Jewish and Greek people.
</p>

## The Name of Jesus Is Not a Technique

<p>
v11: God was doing extraordinary miracles through Paul's hands,
v12: so that even cloths and work aprons that had touched his skin were carried to the sick. Their diseases left them, and the evil spirits came out.
v13: Some itinerant Jewish exorcists also tried invoking the name of the Lord Jesus over those who had evil spirits. They would say, “I command you by the Jesus whom Paul proclaims.”
v14: Seven sons of a Jewish chief priest named Sceva were doing this.
v15: But the evil spirit answered them, “Jesus I know, and Paul I recognize—but who are you?”
v16: Then the man in whom the evil spirit lived leaped on them, overpowered them, and prevailed against them. They fled from that house naked and wounded.
v17: This became known to everyone living in Ephesus, both Jewish and Greek people. Fear fell on them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus was magnified.
v18: Many who had believed came confessing and openly reporting their practices.
v19: A considerable number who had practiced magic brought their books together and burned them in front of everyone. They calculated their value and found it came to fifty thousand pieces of silver.
v20: In this powerful way, the word of the Lord kept growing and prevailing.
</p>

## Paul Sets His Direction toward Jerusalem and Rome

<p>
v21: After these things were completed, Paul resolved in the Spirit to pass through Macedonia and Achaia and go to Jerusalem. He said, “After I have been there, I must also see Rome.”
v22: He sent two of his assistants, Timothy and Erastus, into Macedonia, while he stayed in Asia for a time.
</p>

## The Riot over Artemis

<p>
v23: About that time, a serious disturbance arose concerning the Way.
v24: A silversmith named Demetrius made silver shrines of Artemis and brought the craftsmen a great deal of business.
v25: He gathered them together, along with workers in related trades, and said, “Men, you know that our prosperity comes from this business.
v26: You see and hear that not only in Ephesus but throughout almost all of Asia, this Paul has persuaded and turned away a large crowd by saying that gods made by hands are not gods.
v27: There is danger not only that our trade will fall into disrepute, but also that the temple of the great goddess Artemis will be regarded as nothing and that she herself—whom all Asia and the inhabited world worships—will be stripped of her majesty.”
v28: When they heard this, they became furious and began shouting, “Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!”
v29: The city was filled with confusion, and the people rushed together into the theater, dragging along Gaius and Aristarchus, Macedonian traveling companions of Paul.
v30: Paul wanted to enter the assembly, but the disciples would not let him.
v31: Even some provincial officials who were his friends sent word urging him not to venture into the theater.
v32: Some were shouting one thing and some another, for the assembly was in confusion, and most of them did not know why they had gathered.
v33: Some in the crowd prompted Alexander after the Jewish people put him forward. Alexander motioned with his hand and wanted to make a defense before the people.
v34: But when they recognized that he was Jewish, they all shouted with one voice for about two hours, “Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!”
v35: After the city clerk quieted the crowd, he said, “People of Ephesus, who does not know that the city of the Ephesians is guardian of the temple of great Artemis and of her image that fell from heaven?
v36: Since these facts cannot be denied, you must remain calm and do nothing rash.
v37: For you have brought these men here even though they have neither robbed temples nor blasphemed our goddess.
v38: If Demetrius and the craftsmen with him have a complaint against anyone, the courts are open and there are proconsuls. Let them bring charges against one another.
v39: But if you seek anything beyond that, it will be settled in the lawful assembly.
v40: As it is, we are in danger of being charged with rioting because of today's events, since there is no cause we can give to account for this disorderly gathering.” After saying this, he dismissed the assembly.
</p>

## Notes

v12: The narrative reports extraordinary healings associated with cloths and work aprons; it does not establish a commodity or technique.

v16: The men flee naked and wounded after severe physical violence.

v35: The object ‘fallen from heaven’ is left as the city clerk’s conventional claim about Artemis’s image.

v41: Public verse 41 is absent from the pinned Greek numbering; its dismissal sentence is included at the end of verse 40.

## Vocabulary

v09: **ὁδός (hodos)**: Way; here a name for the Jesus movement.

v24: **ναός (naos)**: Shrine or temple image.
