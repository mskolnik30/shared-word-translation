## Peter and John Before the Council

<p>
v01: While Peter and John were speaking to the people, the priests, the captain of the temple guard, and the Sadducees confronted them.
v02: They were greatly disturbed because the apostles were teaching the people and proclaiming in Jesus the resurrection from the dead.
v03: They seized them and put them in custody until the next day, because it was already evening.
v04: But many who heard the message believed, and the number of men grew to about five thousand.
v05: The next day their rulers, elders, and scribes gathered in Jerusalem,
v06: along with Annas the high priest, Caiaphas, John, Alexander, and all who belonged to the high-priestly family.
v07: They had Peter and John stand in the middle and began questioning them: “By what power or by what name did you do this?”
v08: Then Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, told them, “Rulers of the people and elders,
v09: if we are being examined today for a good deed done to a man who was weak and asked how he was healed,
v10: then let all of you and all the people of Israel know that this man stands before you healthy in the name of Jesus the Messiah, the Nazarene—whom you crucified and whom God raised from the dead.
v11: This Jesus is ‘the stone rejected by you builders that has become the cornerstone.’
v12: Salvation is found in no one else, for no other name under heaven has been given among humanity by which we must be saved.”
</p>

## They Cannot Stop Speaking

<p>
v13: When they saw Peter’s and John’s boldness and realized they were uneducated, ordinary men, they were amazed. They also recognized that these men had been with Jesus.
v14: And because they saw the man who had been healed standing with them, they had nothing they could say in opposition.
v15: So they ordered them to leave the council and began conferring with one another,
v16: saying, “What should we do with these men? For it is obvious to everyone living in Jerusalem that a remarkable sign has happened through them, and we cannot deny it.
v17: But to keep this from spreading any further among the people, let us threaten them never again to speak to anyone in this name.”
v18: They called Peter and John back and ordered them not to speak or teach at all in the name of Jesus.
v19: But Peter and John answered them, “Judge for yourselves whether it is right before God to listen to you rather than to God.
v20: For we cannot stop speaking about what we have seen and heard.”
v21: After threatening them further, the leaders released them. They could find no way to punish them because of the people, since everyone was glorifying God for what had happened.
v22: For the man on whom this sign of healing had been performed was more than forty years old.
</p>

## The Community Prays for Boldness

<p>
v23: After being released, Peter and John went to their own people and reported everything the chief priests and elders had told them.
v24: When they heard it, they raised their voices together to God and said, “Sovereign Lord, you made heaven, earth, sea, and everything in them.
v25: You spoke by the Holy Spirit through the mouth of our ancestor David, your servant: ‘Why did the nations rage and the peoples plot in vain?
v26: The kings of the earth took their stand, and the rulers gathered together against the Lord and against his Messiah.’
v27: For truly, in this city Herod and Pontius Pilate, together with the Gentiles and the peoples of Israel, gathered against your holy servant Jesus, whom you anointed.
v28: They did whatever your hand and your plan had determined beforehand would happen.
v29: And now, Lord, pay attention to their threats and grant your servants complete boldness to speak your word,
v30: while you stretch out your hand to heal, and signs and wonders happen through the name of your holy servant Jesus.”
v31: After they prayed, the place where they were gathered was shaken. They were all filled with the Holy Spirit and spoke God’s word with boldness.
</p>

## No One Among Them Was in Need

<p>
v32: The whole community of believers was one in heart and soul. No one claimed that any possession belonged privately to them; instead, they held everything in common.
v33: With great power the apostles gave their testimony to the resurrection of the Lord Jesus, and great grace was upon them all.
v34: There was not a needy person among them, because those who owned fields or houses sold them and brought the proceeds
v35: and laid them at the apostles’ feet. The money was then distributed to each person according to need.
v36: Joseph, whom the apostles called Barnabas—which means Son of Encouragement—was a Levite from Cyprus.
v37: He sold a field he owned, brought the money, and laid it at the apostles’ feet.
</p>

## Notes

v04: The count specifies about five thousand men; it does not provide a total for women and children who may also have belonged to the community.

v09–10: The verb can mean healed or saved. The man’s bodily restoration and the sermon’s saving claim deliberately share language.

v11: The rejected stone becomes the head of the corner, either a cornerstone or capstone. ‘Cornerstone’ preserves the familiar building image.

v12: Peter’s claim about salvation in Jesus’ name is translated directly without expanding it into a judgment about people or circumstances not under discussion.

v13: ‘Uneducated and ordinary’ describes the council’s social assessment: men without scribal training or official standing, not people lacking intelligence.

v24: ‘Sovereign Lord’ renders a title emphasizing ownership and authority; the prayer immediately names God as Creator.

v25: The pinned text includes variant-marked language identifying the Holy Spirit as speaking through David’s mouth. The words are present and translated.

v27–28: The prayer names Herod, Pilate, Gentiles, and Israel’s peoples as agents while also confessing God’s prior purpose. Neither agency is erased.

v32–35: Unity of heart and soul takes material form: possessions are not treated as exclusively private, and distribution follows actual need.

## Vocabulary

v13: **παρρησία (parrēsia)**: Boldness, openness, or freedom of speech.

v24: **δεσπότης (despotēs)**: Master or sovereign lord.

v36: **παράκλησις (paraklēsis)**: Encouragement, comfort, or exhortation.
