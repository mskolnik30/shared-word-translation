## Stand Firm in the Lord

<p>
v01: So, my beloved brothers and sisters whom I long for, my joy and crown, stand firm in the Lord in this way, my beloved.
</p>

## Seek the Same Mind in the Lord

<p>
v02: I appeal to Euodia, and I appeal to Syntyche: be of one mind in the Lord.
v03: Yes, I ask you too, my true companion, help these women. They struggled beside me for the good news, along with Clement and the rest of my fellow workers, whose names are in the book of life.
</p>

## Rejoicing, Prayer, and Peace

<p>
v04: Rejoice in the Lord always. I will say it again: rejoice!
v05: Let everyone know your gentleness. The Lord is near.
v06: Do not be anxious about anything. Instead, in everything, with prayer, petition and thanksgiving, make your requests known to God.
v07: And God's peace, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.
</p>

## What to Think About and Practice

<p>
v08: Finally, brothers and sisters, whatever is true, honorable, just, pure, lovely or commendable—if anything is excellent, if anything deserves praise—give these things your attention.
v09: Put into practice what you learned and received and heard and saw in me, and the God of peace will be with you.
</p>

## Gratitude for the Philippians’ Partnership

<p>
v10: I rejoiced greatly in the Lord that your concern for me has blossomed again. You were concerned before, but had no opportunity to act.
v11: I am not saying this because I am in need, for I have learned to be content in whatever circumstances I face.
v12: I know how to live with little, and I know how to have plenty. In every situation I have learned the secret: being well fed or hungry, having plenty or going without.
v13: I have strength for all things through the one who strengthens me.
</p>

<p>
v14: Still, you did well to share in my distress.
v15: You Philippians know that in the early days of the good news, when I left Macedonia, no church shared with me in the account of giving and receiving except you alone.
v16: Even in Thessalonica, you sent help for my needs more than once.
v17: It is not the gift I seek, but the fruit that grows to your account.
</p>

<p>
v18: I have received everything in full and have more than enough. I am well supplied now that I have received your gifts from Epaphroditus—a fragrant aroma, an acceptable sacrifice, pleasing to God.
v19: And my God will meet every need of yours according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus.
</p>

## Doxology

<p>
v20: To our God and Father be glory forever and ever. Amen.
</p>

## Final Greetings

<p>
v21: Greet every saint in Christ Jesus. The brothers and sisters with me send you greetings.
v22: All the saints send you greetings, especially those of Caesar's household.
v23: The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit.
</p>

## Notes

v02–03: The appeal is repeated equally for Euodia and Syntyche. Both women are described as sharing in the work of the good news. The ‘true companion’ is not named unambiguously; the Greek can also be read as a personal name, Syzygos.

v05: ‘Near’ may refer to presence, time or both.

v13: Literally, ‘I have strength for all things in the one strengthening me.’ The preceding verse names plenty, hunger and need. The pinned Greek does not add the name Christ in this verse.

v15–18: Giving, receiving, account and receipt language forms a connected accounting image. Verse 17 combines it with the image of fruit.

v23: ‘Your’ addresses the community; ‘spirit’ is singular. The pinned text ends without an added Amen.

## Vocabulary

v05: **ἐπιεικής (epieikēs)**: Gentle, considerate or forbearing.

v07: **φρουρέω (phroureō)**: Guard or keep watch over.

v12: **μυέω (mueō)**: Be initiated or learn a secret.
