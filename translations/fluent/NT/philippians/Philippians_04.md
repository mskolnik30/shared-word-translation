## Stand Firm in the Lord

<p>
v01: Therefore, my brothers and sisters, whom I love and long for, my joy and crown, stand firm in the Lord in this way, my beloved.
</p>

## Seek the Same Mind in the Lord

<p>
v02: I urge Euodia, and I urge Syntyche, to have the same mind in the Lord.
v03: Yes, and I ask you also, my true companion, help these women. They struggled alongside me in the gospel, together with Clement and the rest of my coworkers, whose names are in the book of life.
</p>

## Rejoicing, Prayer, and Peace

<p>
v04: Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say: rejoice.
v05: Let your gentleness be known to everyone. The Lord is near.
v06: Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything, by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God.
v07: And the peace of God, which surpasses every understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.
</p>

## What to Think About and Practice

<p>
v08: Finally, brothers and sisters, whatever is true, whatever is honorable, whatever is just, whatever is pure, whatever is pleasing, whatever is commendable—if there is any excellence and if there is anything worthy of praise—think about these things.
v09: Keep practicing what you learned and received and heard and saw in me, and the God of peace will be with you.
</p>

## Gratitude for the Philippians’ Partnership

<p>
v10: I rejoiced greatly in the Lord that now at last your concern for me has flourished again. You were indeed concerned for me, but you had no opportunity.
v11: I do not say this because I am in need, for I have learned to be content in whatever circumstances I am.
v12: I know how to be brought low, and I know how to abound. In any and every circumstance I have learned the secret—being well fed and being hungry, having abundance and being in need.
v13: I can do all things through the one who strengthens me.
</p>

<p>
v14: Still, you did well to share in my trouble.
v15: And you Philippians know that in the beginning of the gospel, when I left Macedonia, no church shared with me in the matter of giving and receiving except you alone.
v16: Even when I was in Thessalonica, you sent help for my needs more than once.
v17: Not that I seek the gift itself; I seek the fruit that increases to your account.
</p>

<p>
v18: I have received everything in full and have more than enough. I am well supplied, having received from Epaphroditus the gifts you sent—a fragrant offering, an acceptable sacrifice, pleasing to God.
v19: And my God will supply every need of yours according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus.
</p>

## Doxology

<p>
v20: To our God and Father be glory forever and ever. Amen.
</p>

## Final Greetings

<p>
v21: Greet every saint in Christ Jesus. The brothers and sisters who are with me greet you.
v22: All the saints greet you, especially those of Caesar's household.
v23: The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit.
</p>

## Notes

v01: “Stand firm” resumes the language of 1:27 and draws the argument of chapter 3 into a direct communal exhortation.

v02: The repeated verb “urge” gives Euodia and Syntyche equal treatment. Paul calls both women toward the same *phroneō* mindset in the Lord rather than assigning blame to one of them.

v03: The women are described as people who “struggled alongside” Paul in the gospel. The verse treats them as coworkers in mission, not merely as a conflict requiring management.

v04: Rejoicing is intentionally repetitive and remains “in the Lord.”

v05: *Epieikēs* can carry senses such as gentleness, graciousness, reasonableness, or yielding restraint. “Gentleness” is retained without reducing it to passivity.

v05: “The Lord is near” may emphasize the Lord's presence, the nearness of his coming, or both. The translation leaves the statement open.

v06–07: The passage does not deny the reality of anxiety; it directs the community toward prayer, thanksgiving, and God's guarding peace.

v07: *Phroureō* is a guarding verb with military resonance. God's peace is portrayed as actively guarding hearts and minds.

v08–09: Deliberate thought and embodied practice belong together. Paul directs attention toward what is worthy and then calls the community to practice what it has received and observed.

v10: The flowering image in “flourished again” is retained from the Greek rather than reduced to a simple statement that concern resumed.

v11–13: “All things” in v13 is controlled by Paul's immediate discussion of being brought low, abounding, hunger, plenty, abundance, and need. The translation retains Paul's broad wording rather than replacing it with an explanatory paraphrase.

v12: The verb *mueō* means to be initiated or to learn a secret through experience. Paul's contentment is learned in changing circumstances.

v14–18: The repeated *koinōnia* word-family presents financial support as partnership. Paul's accounting language of giving, receiving, account, and full payment is deliberate and should remain visible.

v18: The Philippians' material gift is interpreted with sacrificial language: fragrant offering, acceptable sacrifice, pleasing to God.

v19: The promise concerns God's supply of need in the context of a community that has itself practiced generous partnership; it is not presented as a guarantee of unlimited wealth.

v22: “Caesar's household” refers to people associated with the imperial household or administration; it does not require that members of Caesar's immediate family are meant.

## Vocabulary

v01: στήκω (stēkō)
“To stand firm.” To remain stable or steadfast under pressure.

v02: φρονέω (phroneō)
“To think,” “set one's mind,” or “adopt a mindset.” The same thematic verb used elsewhere in Philippians.

v03: συνεργός (synergos)
“Coworker.” A person engaged in shared labor.

v05: ἐπιεικής (epieikēs)
“Gentle,” “gracious,” “reasonable,” or “forbearing.” A posture of restrained consideration toward others.

v06: προσευχή (proseuchē)
“Prayer.” A general term for addressing God.

v07: εἰρήνη (eirēnē)
“Peace,” well-being, or wholeness. Here God's peace actively guards the community's inner life.

v07: φρουρέω (phroureō)
“To guard.” A protective verb that can carry military resonance.

v08: λογίζομαι (logizomai)
“To think about,” “consider,” or “reckon.” Deliberate, sustained attention.

v11: αὐτάρκης (autarkēs)
“Content” or sufficient. Paul describes a learned stability not controlled by material circumstances.

v12: μυέω (mueō)
“To initiate” or “teach a secret.” Here: to learn through experience how to live in changing circumstances.

v13: ἐνδυναμόω (endynamoō)
“To strengthen” or empower. The strength comes from another rather than from autonomous self-sufficiency.

v14: συγκοινωνέω (synkoinōneō)
“To share with” or “participate together in.” Here the Philippians share in Paul's trouble.

v15: κοινωνέω (koinōneō)
“To share” or “enter into partnership.” Used here of the Philippians' financial partnership with Paul.

v17: καρπός (karpos)
“Fruit.” A result or outcome; here pictured as increasing to the Philippians' account.

v18: ὀσμή (osmē)
“Fragrance” or “aroma.” Part of sacrificial language for an offering pleasing to God.

v19: πληρόω (plēroō)
“To fill” or “supply fully.” God is named as the source of provision.

v23: χάρις (charis)
“Grace.” Gifted favor that closes the letter as it began.
