## Unity and Humility

<p>
v01: So if there is any encouragement in Christ, any comfort from love, any sharing in the Spirit, any affection and compassion,
v02: make my joy complete by being of the same mind, having the same love, united in spirit, thinking as one.
v03: Do nothing from selfish ambition or empty conceit, but in humility consider others as more important than yourselves.
v04: Let each of you look not only to your own interests, but also to the interests of others.
</p>

## Work Out What God Works In

<p>
v12: Therefore, my beloved, just as you have always obeyed—not only when I was present, but now much more in my absence—work out your own salvation with fear and trembling,
v13: for God is the one working in you, both to desire and to work for his good pleasure.
v14: Do everything without grumbling and arguing,
v15: so that you may be blameless and innocent, children of God without blemish in the midst of a crooked and twisted generation, among whom you shine like lights in the world,
v16: holding firmly to the word of life, so that on the day of Christ I may boast that I did not run in vain or labor in vain.
</p>

## Shared Joy in Sacrifice

<p>
v17: But even if I am being poured out as a drink offering over the sacrifice and service of your faith, I rejoice and rejoice together with all of you.
v18: In the same way, you also should rejoice and rejoice together with me.
</p>

## Timothy

<p>
v19: I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you soon, so that I too may be encouraged when I hear how you are doing.
v20: I have no one else like him who will genuinely care about your welfare,
v21: because everyone else seeks their own interests, not those of Jesus Christ.
v22: But you know Timothy's proven character, how, like a child with a parent, he has served with me in the work of the gospel.
v23: So I hope to send him as soon as I see how things go with me,
v24: and I trust in the Lord that I myself will also come soon.
</p>

## Epaphroditus

<p>
v25: I considered it necessary to send Epaphroditus back to you—my brother, coworker, and fellow soldier, and your messenger and minister to my need.
v26: He has been longing for all of you and has been distressed because you heard that he was ill.
v27: He was indeed ill and nearly died. But God had mercy on him, and not only on him but also on me, so that I would not have sorrow upon sorrow.
v28: So I am all the more eager to send him, so that when you see him again you may rejoice and I may be less anxious.
v29: Welcome him in the Lord with all joy, and honor people like him,
v30: because he came close to death for the work of Christ, risking his life to complete what was lacking in your service to me.
</p>

## Notes

v01–04: Paul's appeal to unity does not erase difference. It directs the community away from selfish ambition and toward active regard for one another.

v05: The verb *phroneō* means to think, set one's mind, or adopt a mindset. It recurs throughout Philippians and links Christ's pattern with the community's way of life.

v06: *Harpagmos* is difficult. It can involve grasping, exploiting, or treating something as an advantage. “Something to be exploited” preserves the contrast with Christ's self-emptying without implying that equality with God was absent from him.

v06–08: The passage moves downward through form, self-emptying, servanthood, human likeness, humility, obedience, and death. The translation preserves that movement without explaining a theory of Christ's incarnation inside the text.

v07: “Emptied himself” translates *ekenōsen*. The verse immediately explains the self-emptying through taking the form of a servant and becoming like human beings; the translation does not specify that Christ surrendered divine attributes.

v09–11: Exaltation follows the cross. The language of every knee bending and every tongue confessing evokes universal acknowledgment of Jesus Christ as Lord.

v12–13: Human responsibility and divine agency remain side by side: the community works out its salvation because God is actively working within it.

v12: “Your own salvation” is plural in context and is addressed to the community. It should not automatically be reduced to an individual technique of personal salvation.

v16: *Epechō* can mean “hold fast to” or “hold out.” “Holding firmly to the word of life” is selected for the main text; the alternative remains possible.

v17–18: Paul uses sacrificial language for his own suffering and joins it to repeated joy shared with the Philippians.

v20–22: Timothy's genuine concern for the Philippians embodies the others-oriented mindset urged earlier in the chapter.

v25: The Philippians had sent Epaphroditus as their *apostolos*—their commissioned messenger. The word here does not by itself assign him the technical role later associated with “the apostles.”

v30: The verb can mean to risk or expose oneself to danger. Epaphroditus's near death is interpreted as costly service rather than failure.

## Vocabulary

v01: παράκλησις (paraklēsis)
“Encouragement,” “comfort,” or “appeal.” A relational word that can involve strengthening or coming alongside another.

v03: κενοδοξία (kenodoxia)
“Empty conceit” or “empty glory.” Status-seeking without substance.

v05: φρονέω (phroneō)
“To think,” “set one's mind,” or “adopt a mindset.” A major thematic verb in Philippians.

v06: μορφή (morphē)
“Form.” The term is used both of the form of God and the form of a servant.

v06: ἁρπαγμός (harpagmos)
“Something grasped, seized, exploited, or treated as an advantage.” The precise nuance is debated.

v07: κενόω (kenoō)
“To empty.” Here used reflexively: Christ “emptied himself,” with the following phrases describing the direction of that self-giving.

v08: ταπεινόω (tapeinoō)
“To humble” or “lower.” Here Christ humbles himself in obedience.

v09: ὑπερυψόω (hyperypsoō)
“To highly exalt.” An intensified verb emphasizing God's action in exalting Christ.

v10: κάμπτω (kamptō)
“To bend,” especially to bend the knee in recognition or submission.

v12: κατεργάζομαι (katergazomai)
“To work out,” “bring about,” or “carry through.” The command concerns bringing salvation to expression within the community's life.

v13: ἐνεργέω (energeō)
“To work” or “be active in.” God is described as actively producing both willing and working.

v14: γογγυσμός (gongysmos)
“Grumbling” or “murmuring.” Dissatisfied complaint that can erode communal unity.

v16: ἐπέχω (epechō)
“To hold fast to” or “hold out.” Both senses are possible in context.

v17: σπένδομαι (spendomai)
“To be poured out as a drink offering.” Sacrificial imagery for Paul's own service and suffering.

v25: ἀπόστολος (apostolos)
“Messenger,” “envoy,” or “apostle.” Here Epaphroditus is the Philippians' commissioned representative to Paul.

v30: παραβολεύομαι (paraboleuomai)
“To risk” or expose oneself to danger.
