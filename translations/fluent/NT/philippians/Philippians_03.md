## Rejoice in the Lord

<p>
v01: Finally, my brothers and sisters, rejoice in the Lord. It is no trouble for me to write the same things to you, and for you it is a safeguard.
</p>

## Warning Against Confidence in the Flesh

<p>
v02: Watch out for the dogs. Watch out for the evil workers. Watch out for those who mutilate the flesh.
v03: For we are the circumcision—we who worship by the Spirit of God, boast in Christ Jesus, and put no confidence in the flesh,
v04: though I myself have reasons for confidence even in the flesh. If anyone else thinks they have reasons for confidence in the flesh, I have more:
</p>

<p>
v05: circumcised on the eighth day, from the people of Israel, from the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews; as to the law, a Pharisee;
v06: as to zeal, a persecutor of the church; as to righteousness under the law, blameless.
</p>

## Loss and Gain in Christ

<p>
v07: But whatever gains I had, I have come to regard these as loss because of Christ.
v08: More than that, I regard everything as loss because of the surpassing value of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord. Because of him I have lost all things, and I regard them as rubbish, so that I may gain Christ
v09: and be found in him—not having my own righteousness from the law, but the righteousness that comes through the faithfulness of Christ, the righteousness from God grounded in trust.
</p>

<p>
v10: I want to know him—the power of his resurrection and the sharing of his sufferings—becoming conformed to his death,
v11: if somehow I may attain the resurrection from the dead.
</p>

## Pressing On Toward the Goal

<p>
v12: Not that I have already received this or have already been made complete, but I press on to take hold of it because Christ Jesus has taken hold of me.
v13: Brothers and sisters, I do not consider myself to have taken hold of it. But one thing I do: forgetting what lies behind and reaching forward to what lies ahead,
v14: I press on toward the goal for the prize of God's upward call in Christ Jesus.
</p>

<p>
v15: So let those of us who are mature think this way. And if you think differently about anything, God will reveal that to you as well.
v16: Only let us keep living in line with what we have already attained.
</p>

## Our Citizenship Is in Heaven

<p>
v17: Brothers and sisters, join together in imitating me, and pay attention to those who live according to the pattern you have in us.
v18: For many live as enemies of the cross of Christ. I have often told you about them, and now I tell you even with tears.
v19: Their end is destruction, their god is their belly, and their glory is in their shame. Their minds are set on earthly things.
</p>

<p>
v20: But our citizenship is in heaven, and from there we eagerly await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ.
v21: He will transform our humiliated body to be conformed to his glorious body, by the power that enables him even to bring all things under his rule.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Repetition is presented as protection. The call to rejoice returns throughout Philippians and is rooted “in the Lord,” not in favorable circumstances alone.

v02: Paul's language is intentionally harsh and polemical. The heading avoids identifying the target more narrowly than the text itself does.

v02–03: *Katatomē* (“mutilation”) and *peritomē* (“circumcision”) form a deliberate wordplay. Paul contrasts reliance on bodily marking with worship by the Spirit and boasting in Christ.

v04–06: Paul does not deny the reality of his inherited and achieved credentials; he lists them before radically revaluing them in light of Christ.

v07–08: The gain/loss language is accounting language and connects with the “gain” language of 1:21 and the giving/receiving account language in chapter 4.

v08: *Skybala* is a strong term for refuse or waste. “Rubbish” preserves its dismissive force without requiring a vulgar English equivalent.

v09: The Greek phrase *pisteōs Christou* can mean “trust in Christ” or “the faithfulness of Christ.” Fluent follows the project decision already established in Romans 3 by rendering Christ's faithfulness as the ground and human trust as the response. Current TSW Philippians uses “trust in Christ”; that difference is recorded for later TSW/Core reconciliation rather than silently altered here.

v10: Knowing Christ is participatory: resurrection power and sharing in suffering are held together rather than separated.

v11: Paul's “if somehow” expresses humility and forward orientation; it should not be turned into a denial of Christian hope.

v12: The repeated verb *katalambanō* allows Paul's pursuit and Christ's prior action to mirror each other: Paul seeks to take hold because Christ has already taken hold of him.

v15: *Teleioi* can mean mature or complete. Paul has just denied that he is already “made complete” in v12, so “mature” preserves the rhetorical tension without contradiction.

v16: The command is to continue in line with the point already reached, not to abandon progress until every disagreement is solved.

v20: *Politeuma* means citizenship or commonwealth. It deliberately connects with the civic verb in 1:27. The verse says that citizenship is in heaven; it does not say that the Christian hope is escape from the earth.

v21: “Our humiliated body” preserves the contrast with Christ's glorious body. The phrase concerns bodily transformation rather than rejection of embodiment.

## Vocabulary

v01: χαίρω (chairō)
“To rejoice.” A recurring command and posture throughout Philippians.

v02: κατατομή (katatomē)
“Mutilation.” A polemical wordplay against *peritomē*, “circumcision.”

v03: περιτομή (peritomē)
“Circumcision.” Here Paul redefines covenant identity around worship by the Spirit, boasting in Christ, and refusal of fleshly confidence.

v03: καυχάομαι (kauchaomai)
“To boast” or “take pride in.” In Philippians, legitimate boasting is redirected toward Christ.

v04: πεποίθησις (pepoithēsis)
“Confidence” or reliance. Here used for confidence in the flesh.

v07: ζημία (zēmia)
“Loss.” An accounting term for disadvantage, forfeiture, or damage.

v08: σκύβαλα (skybala)
“Rubbish,” “refuse,” or waste. Strong dismissive language for what Paul once counted as gain.

v09: πίστις Χριστοῦ (pistis Christou)
“Faithfulness of Christ” or “trust in Christ.” The genitive construction permits both readings; Fluent follows the project's established Christ-faithfulness rendering while preserving the alternative in the note.

v09: δικαιοσύνη (dikaiosynē)
“Righteousness.” Right standing from God, contrasted here with righteousness derived from the law.

v10: κοινωνία (koinōnia)
“Sharing” or “participation.” Here participation in Christ's sufferings.

v12: καταλαμβάνω (katalambanō)
“To take hold of,” “grasp,” or “make one's own.” Used both for Paul's pursuit and Christ's prior grasp of Paul.

v14: σκοπός (skopos)
“Goal” or fixed point of focus.

v20: πολίτευμα (politeuma)
“Citizenship” or “commonwealth.” Corporate identity belonging to a polity; here located in heaven.

v21: μετασχηματίζω (metaschēmatizō)
“To transform” or change in form.
