## The Word of Life

<p>
v01: What was from the beginning, what we have heard, what we have seen with our eyes, what we looked at and our hands touched, concerning the word of life—
v02: the life was revealed, and we have seen it and testify to it and proclaim to you the eternal life that was with the Father and was revealed to us—
v03: what we have seen and heard we also proclaim to you, so that you too may have fellowship with us. And our fellowship is with the Father and with his Son, Jesus Christ.
v04: We are writing these things so that our joy may be complete.
</p>

## Walking in the Light

<p>
v05: This is the message we have heard from him and proclaim to you: God is light, and in him there is no darkness at all.
v06: If we say that we have fellowship with him while we walk in darkness, we lie and do not practice the truth.
v07: But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin.
v08: If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.
v09: If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive our sins and cleanse us from all unrighteousness.
v10: If we say that we have not sinned, we make him a liar, and his word is not in us.
</p>

## Notes

v01–03: The opening delays naming its subject directly. Hearing, seeing, looking, and touching accumulate before the proclamation of the “word of life.”

v01: “Word of life” is retained rather than expanded into an identification with Jesus inside the verse. The surrounding lines connect the proclamation with the manifested eternal life while preserving the opening's grammar.

v02: “Was with the Father” preserves the relational preposition rather than paraphrasing it as origin or location.

v03: `koinōnia` is rendered “fellowship.” The term means shared participation or communion, not merely social companionship.

v04: SBLGNT reads “our joy.” Some manuscripts read “your joy.” The critical-text reading is retained.

v05: “God is light” remains a direct predication. The translation does not reduce it to “God gives light” or another explanatory paraphrase.

v06–07: “Walk” remains visible as the letter's metaphor for lived conduct.

v07: The present tense of “cleanses” naturally presents cleansing as ongoing within the sentence's continuing walk-in-the-light condition.

v08–10: The sequence distinguishes having sin, confessing sins, and claiming not to have sinned. The translation preserves the distinctions rather than systematizing them.

v09: “Faithful and just” is retained without explaining how forgiveness relates to divine justice.

## Vocabulary

v01: ἀρχή (archē)
“Beginning, origin, starting point.”

v01: λόγος τῆς ζωῆς (logos tēs zōēs)
“Word of life.” A compact phrase whose relation to the manifested life unfolds through the opening.

v02: φανερόω (phaneroō)
“To reveal, make manifest, make visible.”

v03: κοινωνία (koinōnia)
“Fellowship, participation, communion.”

v05: φῶς (phōs)
“Light.”

v06: περιπατέω (peripateō)
“To walk,” used metaphorically for how one lives.

v07: καθαρίζω (katharizō)
“To cleanse, make clean.”

v08: ἁμαρτία (hamartia)
“Sin.”

v09: ὁμολογέω (homologeō)
“To confess, acknowledge openly.”

v09: πιστός (pistos)
“Faithful, trustworthy.”

v10: ψεύστης (pseustēs)
“Liar.”
