## Test the Spirits

<p>
v01: Beloved, do not trust every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world.
v02: By this you know the Spirit of God: every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is from God,
v03: and every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not from God. This is the spirit of the antichrist, which you heard is coming and now is already in the world.
v04: Little children, you are from God and have overcome them, because the one who is in you is greater than the one who is in the world.
v05: They are from the world; therefore they speak from the world's perspective, and the world listens to them.
v06: We are from God. Whoever knows God listens to us; whoever is not from God does not listen to us. By this we know the Spirit of truth and the spirit of error.
</p>

## God Is Love

<p>
v07: Beloved, let us love one another, because love is from God. Everyone who loves has been born of God and knows God.
v08: Whoever does not love does not know God, because God is love.
v09: By this God's love was revealed among us: God sent his one and only Son into the world so that we might live through him.
v10: This is love: not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son as an atoning sacrifice for our sins.
v11: Beloved, if God loved us in this way, we also ought to love one another.
v12: No one has ever seen God. If we love one another, God remains in us, and his love has been brought to completion in us.
</p>

## God Remains in Us

<p>
v13: By this we know that we remain in him and he in us: he has given us from his Spirit.
v14: We have seen and testify that the Father has sent the Son as Savior of the world.
v15: Whoever confesses that Jesus is the Son of God, God remains in them, and they in God.
v16: And we have come to know and trust the love that God has for us.
God is love, and the one who remains in love remains in God, and God remains in them.
</p>

## Love and Confidence

<p>
v17: By this love has been brought to completion among us, so that we may have confidence on the day of judgment, because as he is, so also are we in this world.
v18: There is no fear in love, but complete love drives out fear, because fear involves punishment. The one who fears has not been brought to completion in love.
</p>

## Loving God and One Another

<p>
v19: We love because he first loved us.
v20: If someone says, “I love God,” and hates a brother or sister, that person is a liar. For the one who does not love a brother or sister whom they have seen cannot love God whom they have not seen.
v21: And we have this commandment from him: whoever loves God must also love a brother or sister.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Testing is required precisely because spiritual claims are not self-authenticating.

v02: The perfect form “has come in the flesh” holds the incarnation as an event with continuing significance.

v03: SBLGNT retains the shorter “does not confess Jesus.” Later manuscripts expand the wording. Fluent follows the critical text.

v03: “Antichrist” describes opposition to Christ in the immediate letter and is not expanded into an identification with one later historical figure.

v06: `planē` can mean error, deception, or wandering. “Spirit of error” keeps the traditional contrast while apparatus preserves the broader range.

v07–10: “God is love” is interpreted by the surrounding sending-of-the-Son narrative, not by an abstract definition imported into the sentence.

v09: `monogenēs` is rendered “one and only,” emphasizing the Son's unique relation to the Father without importing the older English “only begotten” as a lexical claim.

v10: `hilasmos` is again rendered “atoning sacrifice,” matching 2:2 and avoiding a forced single-mechanism theory of atonement.

v12: “No one has ever seen God” is retained even as the verse immediately speaks of God's remaining presence through communal love.

v13: The Greek says God has given “from” his Spirit. The wording suggests participation/gift without defining the metaphysics of that giving.

v14: “Savior of the world” retains the same `kosmos` word that elsewhere can name the sphere opposed to God. The letter itself controls the differing contextual senses.

v16: Knowledge and trust are both retained: the community knows and relies upon God's love.

v17: “As he is, so also are we in this world” remains unexplained in the main text. The comparison should not be narrowed to a single attribute without textual warrant.

v18: The fear in view is explicitly connected with punishment. The verse should not be generalized into a claim that every kind of fear is incompatible with faith.
