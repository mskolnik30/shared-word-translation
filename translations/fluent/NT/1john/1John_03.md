## Children of God

<p>
v01: See what kind of love the Father has given us, that we should be called children of God—and we are. This is why the world does not know us: it did not know him.
v02: Beloved, we are God's children now, and what we will be has not yet been revealed. We know that when he is revealed, we will be like him, because we will see him as he is.
v03: Everyone who has this hope in him purifies themselves, just as he is pure.
</p>

## Sin and Righteousness

<p>
v04: Everyone who practices sin also practices lawlessness; sin is lawlessness.
v05: You know that he was revealed to take away sins, and in him there is no sin.
v06: Everyone who remains in him does not sin; everyone who sins has neither seen him nor known him.
</p>

<p>
v07: Little children, let no one deceive you. Whoever practices righteousness is righteous, just as he is righteous.
v08: Whoever practices sin is of the devil, because the devil has sinned from the beginning. The Son of God was revealed for this purpose: to destroy the works of the devil.
v09: Everyone who has been born of God does not practice sin, because God's seed remains in them; and they are not able to sin, because they have been born of God.
v10: By this the children of God and the children of the devil are evident: whoever does not practice righteousness is not of God, nor is the one who does not love a brother or sister.
</p>

## Love One Another

<p>
v11: This is the message you heard from the beginning: that we should love one another.
v12: We should not be like Cain, who was of the evil one and killed his brother. And why did he kill him? Because his own deeds were evil, while his brother's were righteous.
v13: Do not be surprised, brothers and sisters, if the world hates you.
v14: We know that we have passed from death into life because we love our brothers and sisters. Whoever does not love remains in death.
v15: Everyone who hates a brother or sister is a murderer, and you know that no murderer has eternal life remaining in them.
</p>

## Love in Deed and Truth

<p>
v16: By this we have come to know love: he laid down his life for us, and we ought to lay down our lives for our brothers and sisters.
v17: But if anyone has the world's goods and sees a brother or sister in need and closes their heart against them, how can the love of God remain in that person?
v18: Little children, let us not love in word or speech, but in deed and truth.
</p>

## Confidence Before God

<p>
v19: By this we will know that we are of the truth and will reassure our hearts before him,
v20: whenever our heart condemns us, because God is greater than our heart and knows all things.
v21: Beloved, if our heart does not condemn us, we have confidence before God,
v22: and whatever we ask we receive from him, because we keep his commandments and do what is pleasing before him.
</p>

<p>
v23: And this is his commandment: that we trust in the name of his Son Jesus Christ and love one another, just as he commanded us.
v24: The one who keeps his commandments remains in him, and he in them. By this we know that he remains in us: by the Spirit whom he gave us.
</p>

## Notes

v01: “And we are” is strongly supported in the critical text and emphatically confirms the identity just named.

v02: The future remains deliberately undefined. Likeness to him is connected with seeing him as he is, but the verse does not describe the extent or mechanism of that likeness.

v04: “Sin is lawlessness” is concise. Fluent retains the equation without expanding it into a complete doctrine of sin.

v05: “Was revealed” continues the letter's manifestation language.

v06–09: These are deliberately strong present-tense statements. Fluent does not weaken them to “rarely sins” or explain them away in the main text. The distinction between simple sinning language and “practicing sin” is preserved where the Greek itself shifts expressions.

v09: “God's seed” remains an unexplained metaphor. The translation does not identify it with the Spirit, word, or another referent inside the verse.

v12: The verb for Cain's killing is forceful and can evoke slaughter. “Killed” keeps the Genesis reference recognizable without overstating the lexical effect.

v14: The perfect tense presents passing from death to life as an accomplished transition with continuing result.

v16–18: The letter defines love through self-giving and material action, not sentiment alone.

v17: “Closes their heart” reflects an idiom of shutting inward compassion against visible need.

v19–20: The syntax is compressed and punctuation is debated. The main text keeps the reassurance/condemnation/God-knows relationship without claiming one disputed syntactical reconstruction is certain.

v21: `parrēsia` is confidence or openness in God's presence, not presumption.

v23: One singular “commandment” joins trust in Jesus Christ with love for one another.
