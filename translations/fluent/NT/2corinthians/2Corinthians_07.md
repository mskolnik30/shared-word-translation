## Cleansing and Mutual Belonging

<p>
v01: Therefore, beloved, since we have these promises, let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of body and spirit, bringing holiness to completion in the fear of God.
</p>

<p>
v02: Make room for us. We wronged no one, ruined no one, exploited no one.
v03: I do not say this to condemn you, for I have said before that you are in our hearts, to die together and live together.
</p>

<p>
v04: I have great confidence in you and great pride in you. I am filled with comfort; I overflow with joy in all our affliction.
</p>

## Comfort Through Titus

<p>
v05: For even when we came to Macedonia, our bodies had no rest; we were afflicted in every way—conflicts outside, fears within.
v06: But God, who comforts the downcast, comforted us by Titus's arrival,
v07: and not only by his arrival, but also by the comfort he received from you. He told us of your longing, your mourning, and your zeal for me, so that I rejoiced even more.
</p>

## Godly Grief and Repentance

<p>
v08: Even if I caused you sorrow by my letter, I do not regret it. Though I did regret it—for I see that the letter caused you sorrow, even if only for a while—
v09: now I rejoice, not because you were made sorrowful, but because your sorrow led to repentance. For you felt sorrow in accordance with God, so that you suffered no loss through us.
</p>

<p>
v10: For godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation, which brings no regret; but worldly sorrow produces death.
v11: Just look at what this very godly sorrow produced in you: what earnestness, what defense, what indignation, what fear, what longing, what zeal, what readiness to see justice done! In every way you showed yourselves innocent in the matter.
</p>

<p>
v12: So although I wrote to you, it was not for the sake of the one who did wrong, nor for the sake of the one wronged, but so that your earnest concern for us might be revealed to you before God.
</p>

<p>
v13: Because of this we have been comforted. And beyond our own comfort, we rejoiced even more at Titus's joy, because all of you refreshed his spirit.
v14: For if I boasted to him about you in any way, I was not put to shame. Just as everything we said to you was true, so our boasting before Titus also proved true.
</p>

<p>
v15: His affection for you grows even greater as he remembers the obedience of all of you and how you received him with fear and trembling.
v16: I rejoice because I have complete confidence in you.
</p>

## Notes

v08–10: Paul distinguishes sorrow that produces repentance and salvation from sorrow that produces death; he does not celebrate pain for its own sake.

v11: The response includes a desire to see justice done, not only private emotion.

v12: The text names both a wrongdoer and a person wronged even while stating another purpose for the letter.

## Vocabulary

v09: **μετάνοια (metanoia)**: Repentance, a change of mind and direction.

v11: **ἐκδίκησις (ekdikēsis)**: Vindication, justice, or punishment of wrong.
