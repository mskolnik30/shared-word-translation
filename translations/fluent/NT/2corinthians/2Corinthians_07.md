## Cleansing and Mutual Belonging

<p>
v01: So, since we have these promises, beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of body and spirit, bringing holiness to completion in the fear of God.
</p>

<p>
v02: Make room for us; we have wronged no one, we have corrupted no one, we have taken advantage of no one.

v03: I do not say this to condemn you, for I have said before that you are in our hearts, to die together and to live together.
</p>

<p>
v04: I have great boldness toward you; I have great boasting on your behalf; I am filled with comfort; in all our affliction I overflow with joy.
</p>

## Comfort Through Titus

<p>
v05: For even when we came into Macedonia, our flesh had no rest, but we were afflicted in every way—conflicts without, fears within.

v06: Yet God, who comforts the lowly, comforted us by the coming of Titus,

v07: and not only by his coming but also by the comfort with which he was comforted by you, as he reported to us your longing, your mourning, your zeal for me, so that I rejoiced still more.
</p>

## Godly Grief and Repentance

<p>
v08: For even if I caused you sorrow by my letter, I do not regret it—though I did regret it—for I see that the letter caused you sorrow, though only for a time.

v09: Now I rejoice, not because you were grieved, but because you were grieved into repentance; for you experienced a grief according to God, so that you suffered no loss through us.
</p>

<p>
v10: For grief according to God produces a repentance leading to salvation without regret, but the grief of the world produces death.

v11: For see what earnestness this very grief according to God has produced in you—what eagerness to clear yourselves, what indignation, what fear, what longing, what zeal, what readiness to see justice done. In every way you have proved yourselves to be pure in this matter.
</p>

<p>
v12: So although I wrote to you, it was not for the sake of the one who did the wrong, nor for the sake of the one who suffered the wrong, but so that your earnestness for us might be revealed to you in the sight of God.
</p>

<p>
v13: So we are comforted. And in addition to our comfort, we rejoiced still more at the joy of Titus, because his spirit has been refreshed by all of you.

v14: For whatever I boasted to him about you, I was not put to shame; but just as everything we said to you was true, so also our boasting before Titus has proved true.
</p>

<p>
v15: And his affection for you is even greater, as he remembers the obedience of all of you, how you received him with fear and trembling.

v16: I rejoice, because in everything I have confidence in you.
</p>

## Notes

v01: “Cleanse ourselves” introduces active participation without displacing divine initiative. Holiness is both given and pursued.

v01: “Body and spirit” functions as a comprehensive expression. The concern is not dualistic but total.

v02: Paul’s threefold denial (wronged, corrupted, exploited) addresses possible accusations directly and without elaboration.

v03: “To die together and to live together” signals relational solidarity that persists across outcomes.

v04: Joy and affliction coexist. Paul does not sequence them but holds them simultaneously.

v05: The pairing “conflicts without, fears within” maps external and internal pressure without privileging one over the other.

v06: God is named as “the one who comforts the lowly,” locating divine action specifically among the brought-low.

v07: The report from Titus mediates reconciliation. Presence and testimony carry relational weight.

v08: Paul acknowledges ambivalence (“I did regret it”) without retracting the action. The effect of the letter reinterprets the regret.

v09: Grief is not avoided but redirected. Its value is determined by its movement toward repentance.

v10: Two kinds of grief are distinguished by outcome, not by intensity. One leads to life; the other to death.

v11: The sequence of responses (earnestness, indignation, fear, longing, zeal, justice) traces the communal effect of repentance without systematizing it.

v12: The letter’s purpose is reframed: not adjudication of individuals, but revelation of the community’s stance before God.

v13–14: Paul’s prior boasting becomes a test of truthfulness. The community’s response confirms the reliability of his claim.

v15: “Fear and trembling” marks seriousness of reception rather than terror alone.

v16: Confidence is restored but not idealized. It emerges from tested relationship.

## Vocabulary

v01: μολυσμός (molysmos)
“Defilement.” Suggests contamination affecting purity or integrity.

v01: ἁγιωσύνη (hagiōsynē)
“Holiness.” A state or condition brought toward completion.

v02: φθείρω (phtheirō)
“To corrupt.” Can imply moral or relational damage.

v05: σάρξ (sarx)
“Flesh.” Here indicating embodied existence under strain.

v06: ταπεινός (tapeinos)
“Lowly” or “brought low.” Not merely humble but pressed down.

v09: μετάνοια (metanoia)
“Repentance.” A change of mind and orientation.

v10: ἀμεταμέλητος (ametamelētos)
“Without regret.” Not subject to reversal.

v11: σπουδή (spoudē)
“Earnestness.” Diligent seriousness or urgency.

v11: ἐκδίκησις (ekdikēsis)
“Justice” or “vindication.” The restoration of what is right.

v15: φόβος καὶ τρόμος (phobos kai tromos)
“Fear and trembling.” A formula expressing seriousness, reverence, or weight of response.
