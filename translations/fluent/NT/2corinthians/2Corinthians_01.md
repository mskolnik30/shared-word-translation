## Greeting

<p>
v01: Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus by God's will, and Timothy our brother, to God's church in Corinth, together with all the saints throughout Achaia:
</p>

<p>
v02: Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.
</p>

## The God of All Comfort

<p>
v03: Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of compassion and God of all comfort.
v04: He comforts us in all our affliction so that we can comfort those in any affliction with the comfort we ourselves receive from God.
</p>

<p>
v05: For just as Christ's sufferings overflow to us, so through Christ our comfort also overflows.
v06: If we are afflicted, it is for your comfort and salvation; if we are comforted, it is for your comfort, which takes effect as you patiently endure the same sufferings we also suffer.
v07: Our hope for you is firm, because we know that as you share in the sufferings, so you also share in the comfort.
</p>

<p>
v08: Brothers and sisters, we do not want you to be unaware of the affliction we experienced in Asia. We were burdened far beyond our strength, so much that we despaired even of living.
v09: Indeed, we felt within ourselves that we had received a sentence of death. This happened so that we would not trust in ourselves but in God, who raises the dead.
v10: He rescued us from such great danger of death and will rescue us. We have set our hope on him that he will rescue us again,
v11: as you also help us by your prayer, so that many may give thanks on our behalf for the gracious gift given to us through the prayers of many.
</p>

## Paul’s Integrity and Change of Plans

<p>
v12: For this is our reason for boasting: the testimony of our conscience that we conducted ourselves in the world—and especially toward you—with God's holiness and sincerity, not with fleshly wisdom but with God's grace.
v13: For we are not writing you anything other than what you can read and understand. I hope you will understand fully,
v14: just as you have understood us in part, that on the day of our Lord Jesus we are your reason for boasting, just as you are ours.
</p>

<p>
v15: Because I was confident of this, I intended to come to you first, so that you might receive grace a second time.
v16: I intended to travel through you to Macedonia, then come back to you from Macedonia and have you send me on my way to Judea.
v17: When I intended this, was I being fickle? Or do I make my plans according to the flesh, so that with me it is both “Yes, yes” and “No, no”?
</p>

<p>
v18: But as God is faithful, our word to you is not both “Yes” and “No.”
v19: For the Son of God, Jesus Christ, whom we proclaimed among you—through me, Silvanus, and Timothy—did not become “Yes” and “No,” but in him it has become “Yes.”
v20: For however many God's promises are, in him they are “Yes.” Therefore through him we also say the “Amen” to God for his glory.
</p>

<p>
v21: The one who establishes us with you in Christ and anointed us is God.
v22: He also sealed us and gave the Spirit in our hearts as a down payment.
</p>

<p>
v23: But I call God as witness against my own life: it was to spare you that I did not come again to Corinth.
v24: Not that we rule over your faith; rather, we work with you for your joy, because you stand firm by faith.
</p>

## Notes

v22: The Spirit is called a down payment or first installment, a commercial image of a promised completion.

v24: Paul explicitly denies ruling over the Corinthians' faith and describes the workers as coworkers for their joy.

## Vocabulary

v03: **παράκλησις (paraklēsis)**: Comfort, encouragement, or strengthening appeal.

v22: **ἀρραβών (arrabōn)**: Down payment, deposit, or first installment.
