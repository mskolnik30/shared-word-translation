## Paul and the “Super-Apostles”

<p>
v01: I wish you would put up with a little foolishness from me. Indeed, do put up with me!
v02: For I am jealous for you with God's jealousy, because I promised you to one husband, to present you to Christ as a pure virgin.
</p>

<p>
v03: But I fear that, just as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, your minds may be corrupted from sincere and pure devotion to Christ.
v04: For if someone comes proclaiming another Jesus whom we did not proclaim, or you receive a different spirit you did not receive, or a different gospel you did not accept, you put up with it well enough!
</p>

<p>
v05: For I consider myself in no way inferior to those “super-apostles.”
v06: Even if I am untrained in speech, I am not untrained in knowledge. In every way we have made this clear to you in all things.
</p>

## Paul’s Refusal of Support

<p>
v07: Or did I commit a sin by humbling myself so that you might be exalted, because I proclaimed God's gospel to you without charge?
v08: I robbed other churches by accepting support in order to serve you.
</p>

<p>
v09: And when I was with you and in need, I did not burden anyone, for the brothers who came from Macedonia supplied what I lacked. In everything I kept myself from being a burden to you, and I will continue to do so.
v10: As surely as Christ's truth is in me, this boasting of mine will not be silenced in the regions of Achaia.
v11: Why? Because I do not love you? God knows I do.
</p>

<p>
v12: But what I do, I will continue to do, in order to cut off the opportunity from those who want an opportunity to be regarded as our equals in what they boast about.
</p>

## False Apostles

<p>
v13: For such people are false apostles, deceitful workers, disguising themselves as apostles of Christ.
v14: And no wonder, for Satan himself disguises himself as an angel of light.
v15: So it is no great surprise if his servants also disguise themselves as servants of righteousness. Their end will correspond to their works.
</p>

## Paul’s Boasting in Weakness

<p>
v16: I say again: no one should think I am a fool. But if you do, then receive me even as a fool so that I too may boast a little.
v17: What I am saying, I am not saying according to the Lord, but as in foolishness, with this confidence in boasting.
v18: Since many boast according to the flesh, I too will boast.
</p>

<p>
v19: For you gladly put up with fools, since you yourselves are so wise!
v20: Indeed, you put up with it if someone enslaves you, devours you, takes from you, exalts himself, or strikes you in the face.
v21: To our shame, I admit that we were too weak for that! But whatever anyone dares to boast about—I speak in foolishness—I also dare.
</p>

<p>
But whatever anyone else dares to boast of—I am speaking in foolishness—I also dare.

v22: Are they Hebrews? So am I. Are they Israelites? So am I. Are they Abraham's offspring? So am I.
v23: Are they servants of Christ? I am speaking like a madman: I am far more—in far more labors, far more imprisonments, countless beatings, and often near death.
</p>

<p>
v24: Five times I received from the Jews forty lashes minus one.
v25: Three times I was beaten with rods, once I was stoned, three times I was shipwrecked; I spent a night and a day in the open sea.
v26: I have often been on journeys, in danger from rivers, danger from robbers, danger from my own people, danger from Gentiles, danger in the city, danger in the wilderness, danger at sea, danger among false brothers,
</p>

<p>
v27: in toil and hardship, through many sleepless nights, in hunger and thirst, often without food, in cold and without enough clothing.
v28: Besides everything else, there is the daily pressure on me: my concern for all the churches.
</p>

<p>
v29: Who is weak without my sharing their weakness? Who is made to stumble without my burning with distress?
v30: If I must boast, I will boast about the things that show my weakness.
v31: The God and Father of the Lord Jesus—the one blessed forever—knows that I am not lying.
</p>

<p>
v32: In Damascus, the governor under King Aretas guarded the city of the Damascenes in order to seize me,
v33: but I was lowered in a basket through a window in the wall and escaped his hands.
</p>

## Notes

v08: Paul deliberately says he ‘robbed’ other churches by accepting their support; the harsh irony highlights Corinth's cost-free benefit.

v20: Paul names enslavement, economic predation, arrogance, and a blow to the face as abuse the Corinthians wrongly tolerate.

v23–27: The catalogue preserves imprisonment, beating, stoning, shipwreck, danger, hunger, cold, and inadequate clothing as real bodily harm.

## Vocabulary

v05: **ὑπερλίαν ἀπόστολοι (hyperlian apostoloi)**: An ironic expression often rendered ‘super-apostles.’

v33: **σαργάνη (sarganē)**: A woven basket or hamper.
