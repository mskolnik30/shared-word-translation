## Difficult Times

<p>
v01: Know this: difficult times will come in the last days.
v02: For people will be lovers of themselves, lovers of money, boastful, arrogant, slanderous, disobedient to parents, ungrateful, unholy,
v03: without natural affection, irreconcilable, malicious gossips, without self-control, brutal, not loving what is good,
v04: traitors, reckless, conceited, lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God,
v05: holding to an appearance of godliness but denying its power. Turn away from such people.
v06: For among them are those who slip into households and captivate vulnerable women burdened with sins and driven by various desires,
v07: always learning yet never able to come to a knowledge of the truth.
v08: Just as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses, so these people also oppose the truth—people corrupted in mind and rejected concerning the faith.
v09: But they will not advance much further, because their foolishness will become clear to everyone, just as happened with those men.
</p>

## Continue in What You Learned

<p>
v10: But you have followed my teaching, way of life, purpose, trust, patience, love, endurance,
v11: persecutions, and sufferings—what happened to me at Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra, what persecutions I endured. Yet the Lord rescued me from them all.
v12: Indeed, everyone who wants to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted.
v13: But evil people and impostors will go from bad to worse, deceiving and being deceived.
v14: But you, continue in the things you learned and became convinced of, knowing from whom you learned them,
v15: and how from infancy you have known the sacred writings, which are able to make you wise for salvation through trust in Christ Jesus.
v16: All Scripture is God-breathed and useful for teaching, for correction, for setting things right, and for training in righteousness,
v17: so that the person of God may be complete, equipped for every good work.
</p>

## Notes

v01: “Last days” in New Testament usage can describe the eschatological era already underway, not only a short period immediately before the end.

v02–05: The vice list culminates in religious appearance without power, making hypocrisy within professing religion the central danger.

v06: The verse uses polemical language about vulnerable women targeted by exploitative teachers; it should not be generalized into a claim that women are inherently gullible.

v08: Jannes and Jambres are traditional names for Pharaoh’s magicians, not supplied by Exodus itself. The letter draws on a known Jewish interpretive tradition.

v10–11: Timothy has not followed doctrine alone but an embodied pattern including suffering and endurance.

v12: Persecution is stated broadly as an expectation for godly life, not a promise that every faithful believer will experience identical forms of hostility.

v15: “Sacred writings” refers first to the Jewish Scriptures Timothy knew from childhood. They are described as able to make wise for salvation through trust in Christ.

v16: `theopneustos` means God-breathed. Fluent retains the image rather than turning it into a technical theory of inspiration not stated in the adjective itself.

v16: “All Scripture” can grammatically mean every Scripture or all Scripture; Fluent uses the traditional collective sense while preserving the practical emphasis on usefulness.

v17: Scripture equips the person of God for “every good work,” linking doctrine directly with practiced life.
