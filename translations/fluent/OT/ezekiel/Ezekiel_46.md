## The gate and the offerings

<p>
v01: “This is what the Lord GOD says: The east-facing gate of the inner court must remain shut during the six working days. It must be opened on the Sabbath and on the new moon.
v02: The prince must enter from outside through the gate’s vestibule and stand beside the gatepost. The priests will offer his burnt offering and peace offerings. He must bow at the threshold and then leave, but the gate must remain open until evening.
v03: On Sabbaths and new moons the people of the land must bow before the LORD at that gate’s entrance.
v04: The burnt offering the prince presents to the LORD on the Sabbath must be six unblemished lambs and one unblemished ram.
v05: The grain offering will be an ephah for the ram, and for the lambs whatever he gives, with a hin of oil for each ephah.
v06: On the new moon he must offer an unblemished young bull, six lambs, and a ram; they must be unblemished.
v07: He must provide an ephah as a grain offering for the bull and an ephah for the ram. For the lambs he may give as much as he can, with a hin of oil for each ephah.
v08: When the prince enters, he must come through the gate’s vestibule and leave the same way.
v09: When the people of the land come before the LORD at the appointed festivals, whoever enters by the north gate to worship must leave by the south gate, and whoever enters by the south must leave by the north. No one may return by the gate they entered; they must leave by the opposite gate.
v10: The prince will be among them, entering when they enter; when they leave, they will go out together.
v11: At the feasts and appointed festivals, the grain offering will be an ephah for each bull and an ephah for each ram, and for the lambs whatever he gives, with a hin of oil for each ephah.
v12: When the prince makes a freewill offering to the LORD, a burnt offering or peace offerings, the east-facing gate must be opened for him. He will offer his burnt offering and peace offerings as on the Sabbath. Then he must leave, and the gate must be shut after him.
v13: “Provide an unblemished year-old lamb each day as a burnt offering to the LORD. Offer it morning by morning.
v14: With it, morning by morning, provide a grain offering: one-sixth of an ephah and one-third of a hin of oil to moisten the fine flour. This is a grain offering to the LORD, a lasting statute, continually.
v15: They must offer the lamb, grain offering, and oil morning by morning as a continual burnt offering.
</p>

## The prince’s inheritance

<p>
v16: “This is what the Lord GOD says: If the prince gives a gift from his inheritance to one of his sons, it will belong to his sons as their inherited possession.
v17: But if he gives a gift from his inheritance to one of his servants, it will belong to that servant until the year of release, then return to the prince. His inheritance belongs permanently to his sons.
v18: The prince must not take any of the people’s inheritance, forcing them from their possessions. He must give his sons an inheritance from his own possession, so none of my people is scattered from their property.”
</p>

## The kitchens

<p>
v19: He brought me through the entrance beside the gate into the priests’ holy chambers facing north. At their far western end was a place
v20: where, he told me, “The priests will boil the guilt offering and sin offering and bake the grain offering, so they do not carry them into the outer court and transmit holiness to the people.”
v21: He led me out into the outer court and around its four corners. In each corner was another court.
v22: These four corner courts were enclosed, each forty long and thirty wide. All four had the same dimensions.
v23: A row of masonry ran around each of the four, with cooking hearths built beneath the rows all around.
v24: He told me, “These are the kitchens where the temple’s servants will boil the people’s sacrifices.”
</p>

## Notes

v1: This is the inner court’s east gate. The closed outer east gate in 44:1–2 is a different gate.

v13–15: The daily offering described here is a morning offering. An evening offering is not added to the text.

v17: The year of release marks the return of a grant made to a servant. This passage does not state the number of years in the cycle.

v22: The word describing the corner courts is uncertain and has been understood as enclosed, open, or related to smoke. The source also marks a word about the corner courts with extraordinary dots; it is retained.

## Vocabulary

v17: **Year of release**: The appointed year when a servant’s grant from the prince returns to the prince’s property.

v20: **Guilt offering**: An offering associated with liability and reparation; its preparation is restricted to the priestly area here.
