## A lament for Israel’s princes

<p>
v01: “Take up a lament for the princes of Israel,
v02: and say:
What was your mother? A lioness!
She lay among lions
and raised her cubs among young lions.
v03: She brought up one of her cubs;
he became a young lion.
He learned to tear prey
and devoured people.
v04: The nations heard about him;
he was caught in their pit.
They brought him with hooks
to the land of Egypt.
v05: She saw that her waiting was hopeless,
that her hope had perished.
She took another of her cubs
and made him a young lion.
v06: He prowled among lions
and became a young lion.
He learned to tear prey
and devoured people.
v07: He knew his widows
and laid waste their cities.
The land and all it held were appalled
at the sound of his roar.
v08: Nations from the surrounding provinces
set themselves against him.
They spread their net over him;
he was caught in their pit.
v09: They put him in a cage with hooks
and brought him to the king of Babylon.
They brought him into strongholds,
so his voice would no longer be heard
on the mountains of Israel.
</p>

## The uprooted vine

<p>
v10: Your mother was like a vine in your blood,
planted beside water,
fruitful and full of branches
because of abundant water.
v11: She had strong branches
fit for rulers’ scepters.
It grew tall above the thick foliage,
conspicuous in its height
and its many branches.
v12: But she was uprooted in fury
and thrown to the ground.
The east wind dried her fruit.
Her strong branches were torn off and withered;
fire consumed them.
v13: Now she is planted in the wilderness,
in a dry and thirsty land.
v14: Fire went out from one of her branches
and consumed her fruit.
No strong branch remained in her,
no scepter for ruling.”
This is a lament, and it is used as a lament.
</p>

## Notes

v07: The pinned Hebrew has “he knew his widows,” an obscure expression. The translation retains his widows and the verb “knew”; other readings understand sexual violation or propose “ravaged their strongholds.” The precise meaning remains unresolved.

v10: “In your blood” is the wording of the pinned Hebrew and is obscure here. Readings such as “in your vineyard” require a textual change. The surrounding description of a well-watered vine is clear.

## Vocabulary

v01,14: **Lament**: Hebrew qinah identifies the poem’s mourning form. The lion cubs and vine branches portray rulers without supplying their personal names.
