## Gold and clay

<p>
v01: How the gold has grown dim,
the fine gold changed!
The sacred stones lie scattered
at every street corner.
v02: Zion's precious sons,
worth their weight in pure gold—
how they are treated as clay jars,
the work of a potter's hands!
v03: Even jackals offer the breast
and nurse their young.
But my people have become cruel,
like ostriches in the wilderness.
v04: The nursing infant's tongue sticks
to the roof of its mouth from thirst.
Children ask for bread,
but no one breaks it for them.
v05: Those who ate delicacies
lie desolate in the streets.
Those raised in scarlet
embrace refuse heaps.
v06: My people's punishment is greater
than Sodom's punishment.
Sodom was overthrown in a moment,
with no hands turned against her.
v07: Her consecrated ones were purer than snow,
whiter than milk.
Their bodies were ruddier than coral,
their form like sapphire.
v08: Now their appearance is darker than soot;
no one recognizes them in the streets.
Their skin clings to their bones,
dry as wood.
</p>

## Hunger and bloodshed

<p>
v09: Better off are those killed by the sword
than those killed by hunger.
They waste away, pierced through
for lack of the field's produce.
v10: The hands of compassionate women
have cooked their own children.
They became their food
when my people were shattered.
v11: The **LORD** has spent his wrath,
poured out his burning anger.
He kindled a fire in Zion
that consumed her foundations.
v12: The kings of the earth did not believe,
nor did anyone living in the world,
that foe and enemy could enter
Jerusalem's gates.
v13: It was because of her prophets' sins
and her priests' guilt:
they shed the blood of the righteous
within her.
v14: They staggered blindly through the streets,
polluted with blood,
so that no one could touch their clothes.
v15: “Away! Unclean!” people cried at them.
“Away, away! Do not touch!”
When they fled and wandered,
people among the nations said,
“They may stay here no longer.”
v16: The **LORD**'s presence scattered them;
he will look on them no longer.
Priests received no honor;
elders received no favor.
</p>

## The help that did not come

<p>
v17: Still our eyes failed,
watching in vain for help.
From our lookout we kept watching
for a nation that could not save.
v18: They hunted our steps,
so we could not walk in our squares.
Our end drew near; our days were full.
Our end had come.
v19: Our pursuers were swifter
than eagles in the sky.
They chased us on the mountains
and lay in wait for us in the wilderness.
v20: The breath of our nostrils,
the **LORD**'s anointed, was caught in their pits—
the one of whom we said,
“In his shade we will live among the nations.”
v21: Rejoice and be glad, daughter Edom,
you who dwell in the land of Uz!
The cup will pass to you too;
you will become drunk and strip yourself bare.
v22: Your punishment is complete, daughter Zion;
he will not send you into exile again.
But he will call your guilt to account, daughter Edom;
he will expose your sins.
</p>

## Notes

v3,6–7: The animal comparisons reflect the poem's ancient imagery. “Punishment” in verse 6 can also be “guilt”; the final hands clause may concern attack or help. “Consecrated ones” in verse 7 may mean Nazirites or nobles. The precise minerals represented by coral and sapphire are uncertain.

v10,13: The mothers are called compassionate even as famine leads to cannibalism. The next movement specifically names bloodshed by prophets and priests. Neither feature has been removed.

v20–22: “The LORD's anointed” refers in this setting to the captured king. The promise to Zion and warning to Edom are retained alongside the book's later unresolved ending. This chapter, like chapters 2 and 3, places pe before ayin in its alphabetic order.

## Vocabulary

v21: **Cup**: The allotted experience of judgment, pictured as intoxicating drink.

v5: **Scarlet**: Costly colored clothing or cloth, marking the comfort in which these people were raised.
