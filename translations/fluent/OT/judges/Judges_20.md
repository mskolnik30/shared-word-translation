## Israel gathers at Mizpah

<p>
v01: All the Israelites came out, from Dan to Beer-sheba and from the land of Gilead. The congregation assembled as one before the LORD at Mizpah.
v02: The leaders of all the people, all Israel’s tribes, took their places in the assembly of God’s people: four hundred thousand foot soldiers armed with swords.
v03: The Benjaminites heard that the Israelites had gone up to Mizpah. The Israelites said, “Tell us how this evil happened.”
</p>

<p>
v04: The Levite, the husband of the murdered woman, answered, “My concubine and I came to Gibeah in Benjamin to spend the night.
v05: The men of Gibeah rose against me and surrounded the house at night. They meant to kill me. They violated my concubine, and she died.
v06: I took my concubine, cut her into pieces, and sent her throughout the land of Israel’s inheritance, because they had committed depravity and outrage in Israel.
v07: You are all Israelites. Give your judgment and counsel here.”
</p>

## Israel demands the offenders

<p>
v08: All the people rose as one and said, “None of us will go to his tent or turn aside to his house.
v09: This is what we will do to Gibeah: we will proceed against it by lot.
v10: We will take ten men from every hundred, a hundred from every thousand, and a thousand from every ten thousand throughout Israel’s tribes to get supplies for the army. Then, when the army reaches Geba in Benjamin, it can repay all the outrage committed in Israel.”
v11: So all Israel’s men gathered against the town, united as one.
</p>

<p>
v12: Israel’s tribes sent men through all the clans of Benjamin, asking, “What is this evil that has happened among you?
v13: Hand over those worthless men in Gibeah so we can put them to death and purge evil from Israel.” But the Benjaminites would not listen to their fellow Israelites.
</p>

## Benjamin prepares for war

<p>
v14: The Benjaminites gathered from their towns at Gibeah to go out to war against the Israelites.
v15: That day Benjamin mustered twenty-six thousand swordsmen from the towns, besides seven hundred chosen men from Gibeah.
</p>

<p>
v16: Among all these troops were seven hundred chosen left-handed men. Every one could sling a stone at a hair and not miss.
v17: Israel, apart from Benjamin, mustered four hundred thousand swordsmen, all fighting men.
</p>

## Two defeats for Israel

<p>
v18: The Israelites went up to Bethel and asked God, “Who should go up first for us to fight the Benjaminites?” The LORD answered, “Judah first.”
v19: The Israelites rose in the morning and camped against Gibeah.
v20: Israel’s men went out to fight Benjamin and drew up their battle line against Gibeah.
</p>

<p>
v21: The Benjaminites came out from Gibeah and struck down twenty-two thousand Israelites that day.
v22: But Israel’s men took courage and again formed their battle line where they had stood on the first day.
</p>

<p>
v23: The Israelites went up and wept before the LORD until evening. They asked him, “Should I again go into battle against my brother Benjamin?” The LORD answered, “Go up against him.”
v24: On the second day the Israelites advanced against the Benjaminites.
</p>

<p>
v25: Benjamin came out from Gibeah to meet them on the second day and struck down another eighteen thousand Israelites, all swordsmen.
</p>

## The third inquiry

<p>
v26: Then all the Israelites, the whole army, went up to Bethel. They wept and sat there before the LORD, fasting until evening that day. They offered burnt offerings and well-being offerings before the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v27: The Israelites inquired of the LORD. The ark of God’s covenant was there in those days,
v28: and Phinehas son of Eleazar, son of Aaron, ministered before it. They asked, “Should I go out once more to fight my brother Benjamin, or stop?” The LORD answered, “Go up, for tomorrow I will give him into your hand.”
</p>

## The battle turns

<p>
v29: Israel placed men in ambush around Gibeah.
v30: On the third day the Israelites went up against Benjamin and formed their battle line against Gibeah as before.
</p>

<p>
v31: The Benjaminites came out to meet the army and were drawn away from the town. As before, they began killing Israelites along the roads—one leading to Bethel, the other toward Gibeah—and in the open country. About thirty Israelites fell.
v32: The Benjaminites said, “They are being defeated before us as before.” But the Israelites had said, “Let us retreat and draw them away from the town onto the roads.”
</p>

<p>
v33: All Israel’s men rose from their positions and formed their line at Baal-tamar. Meanwhile, Israel’s ambush force burst from its position in the open ground near Geba.
v34: Ten thousand chosen men from all Israel came against Gibeah. The battle was fierce, and the Benjaminites did not know disaster was closing in on them.
</p>

<p>
v35: The LORD defeated Benjamin before Israel. That day the Israelites struck down twenty-five thousand one hundred men of Benjamin, all swordsmen.
</p>

## How Gibeah fell

<p>
v36: The Benjaminites saw that they were defeated. Israel’s men had given ground to Benjamin because they trusted the ambush they had placed against Gibeah.
v37: The ambush force rushed into Gibeah, spread through it, and struck the whole town with the sword.
</p>

<p>
v38: The agreed signal between Israel’s men and the ambush force was to send up a great cloud of smoke from the town.
v39: Israel’s men had turned back in the battle, and Benjamin had begun to kill about thirty of them. “They are certainly being defeated before us, just as in the first battle,” Benjamin said.
</p>

<p>
v40: Then the cloud began rising from the town, a pillar of smoke. Benjamin looked back: the whole town was going up toward heaven.
v41: Israel’s men turned around, and the Benjaminites were terrified. They saw that disaster had reached them.
</p>

## Benjamin’s defeat

<p>
v42: They turned away from Israel’s men toward the wilderness, but the battle overtook them. Those coming from the towns struck them down in their midst.
v43: They surrounded Benjamin, pursued him, and trampled him at his resting place, as far as the area east of Gibeah.
</p>

<p>
v44: Eighteen thousand men of Benjamin fell, all capable warriors.
v45: The survivors turned and fled toward the wilderness, to the rock of Rimmon. Israel gleaned five thousand men along the roads, then pursued them as far as Gidom and struck down two thousand more.
v46: In all, twenty-five thousand swordsmen of Benjamin fell that day, all capable warriors.
</p>

<p>
v47: Six hundred men turned and fled into the wilderness to the rock of Rimmon. They stayed there four months.
</p>

<p>
v48: Israel’s men turned back against the Benjaminites and struck them with the sword—whole towns, livestock, everything they found. They also set fire to every town they came upon.
</p>

## Notes

v04–06: The Levite says the men intended to kill him and that his concubine died from their abuse. He does not report handing her over. His retelling is left distinct from chapter 19.

v09–12: Verse 9 mentions a lot without explaining its exact use. Verse 10 spells the town Geba rather than Gibeah. Clans in verse 12 renders tribes used for divisions within Benjamin.

v16: Left-handed renders an expression literally involving restriction of the right hand, also used of Ehud in 3:15. The text does not explain its cause.

v18–28: The first inquiry asks who goes first, the second whether to return, and the third whether to continue or stop. The explicit promise of victory tomorrow comes only with the third answer. Before it in verse 28 may also be before him, referring to God.

v31–33: The routes and terrain are uncertain. Verse 31 has Gibeah, though Geba is often proposed. Open ground near Geba in verse 33 may instead be a place name, Maareh-geba; other witnesses point to the west of Gibeah.

v35–46: The summary in verse 35 is followed by a fuller account of the same defeat. Turned in verse 39 is understood as the feigned retreat; verse 41 gives the counterattack. The totals of 25,100 in verse 35 and 25,000 in verse 46 are both retained.

v42–43: The Hebrew has towns in verse 42; some witnesses have town. The next clause may refer to pursuit at a resting place, pursuit without rest, or a place name such as Menuhah or Nohah. The geography is not certain.

v48: The compressed whole-town expression has textual uncertainty. The verse’s sweep includes livestock and everything found, extending the violence beyond the opposing soldiers.

## Vocabulary

v13: purge evil
Language of removing grave wrongdoing from the community; here the tribes use it in demanding the offenders.

v26: well-being offerings
Sacrifices also called peace offerings, associated with shared fellowship meals.

v45: gleaned
A harvest image used for killing those left along the roads.
