## The Levite and his concubine

<p>
v01: In those days, when Israel had no king, a Levite was living as a sojourner in the remote hill country of Ephraim. He took a concubine from Bethlehem in Judah.
v02: His concubine was unfaithful to him and left him for her father’s house in Bethlehem in Judah. She stayed there four months.
</p>

<p>
v03: Her husband went after her to speak to her heart and bring her back. He had his servant and a pair of donkeys with him. She brought him into her father’s house, and her father was glad to meet him.
v04: His father-in-law, the woman’s father, persuaded him to stay. He remained with him three days, and they ate, drank, and spent the nights there.
</p>

<p>
v05: On the fourth day they rose early, and he got ready to leave. But the woman’s father told his son-in-law, “Strengthen yourself with a little bread; then you can go.”
v06: The two men sat down to eat and drink together. Then the woman’s father said, “Please stay the night and enjoy yourself.”
</p>

<p>
v07: The man got up to leave, but his father-in-law urged him to stay, so he spent another night there.
</p>

<p>
v08: On the fifth day he rose early to leave. The woman’s father said, “Please strengthen yourself.” They lingered until the day began to decline, and the two men ate.
v09: The man got up to leave with his concubine and servant. His father-in-law, the woman’s father, said, “Look, the day is sinking toward evening. Please spend the night. The day is nearly over. Stay here and enjoy yourself. Tomorrow you can rise early for your journey and go home.”
</p>

## The journey to Gibeah

<p>
v10: But the man refused to stay the night. He set out and came near Jebus, that is, Jerusalem. He had his pair of saddled donkeys and his concubine with him.
</p>

<p>
v11: They were near Jebus, and daylight was fading fast. The servant told his master, “Come, let us turn into this Jebusite town and spend the night.”
v12: His master answered, “We will not turn into a foreign town whose people are not Israelites. We will go on to Gibeah.”
v13: He told his servant, “Come, let us reach one of those places and spend the night in Gibeah or Ramah.”
</p>

<p>
v14: They continued on their way, and the sun set as they approached Gibeah in Benjamin.
v15: They turned in to spend the night at Gibeah. The man sat down in the town square, but no one took them into a house for the night.
</p>

## A stranger offers lodging

<p>
v16: That evening an old man came in from his work in the field. He was from the hill country of Ephraim and lived as a sojourner in Gibeah; the people of the town were Benjaminites.
v17: He looked up and saw the traveler in the town square. “Where are you going, and where have you come from?” the old man asked.
</p>

<p>
v18: The traveler answered, “We are passing from Bethlehem in Judah to the remote hill country of Ephraim, where I come from. I went to Bethlehem in Judah, and now I am going to the house of the LORD. But no one will take me in.
v19: We have straw and fodder for our donkeys, and bread and wine for me, your maidservant, and the young man with your servants. We lack nothing.”
</p>

<p>
v20: The old man said, “Peace to you. I will take care of whatever you need. Just do not spend the night in the square.”
v21: He brought him into his house and fed the donkeys. The travelers washed their feet, then ate and drank.
</p>

## The assault at the house

<p>
v22: While they were enjoying themselves, some worthless men of the town surrounded the house and pounded on the door. They called to the old man who owned the house, “Bring out the man who came to your house so we can have sex with him.”
</p>

<p>
v23: The owner went out to them and said, “No, my brothers! Please do not do this evil. This man has come into my house. Do not commit this outrage.
v24: Here are my virgin daughter and his concubine. Let me bring them out. Violate them and do to them what is good in your eyes. But do not commit this outrage against the man.”
</p>

<p>
v25: The men would not listen to him. Then the man seized his concubine and brought her out to them. They raped her and abused her all night until morning, and let her go as dawn was breaking.
</p>

## The woman at the threshold

<p>
v26: At daybreak the woman came and collapsed at the entrance to the house where her master was. She lay there until daylight.
v27: Her master got up in the morning, opened the doors, and went out to continue his journey. There was the woman, his concubine, fallen at the entrance, with her hands on the threshold.
</p>

<p>
v28: “Get up. Let us go,” he told her. There was no answer. He put her on the donkey and set off for his home.
</p>

<p>
v29: When he reached his house, he took a knife, seized his concubine, and cut her body into twelve pieces, dividing it at the bones. He sent her throughout Israel’s territory.
v30: Everyone who saw it said, “Nothing like this has happened or been seen from the day the Israelites came up from Egypt until today. Take this to heart. Consider it and speak!”
</p>

## Notes

v01–03: Concubine names a marital relationship of lower status than a principal wife. Verse 2 follows the Hebrew verb for sexual unfaithfulness; an important Greek reading says she became angry with him. Neither reading justifies the later violence.

v18: The Hebrew says house of the LORD. Some ancient witnesses instead have my house. The draft preserves the Hebrew destination without supplying an unmentioned sanctuary.

v22–25: Know has a sexual meaning here. The surrounding threat and assault make coercion unmistakable. The host offers his daughter and the guest’s concubine; the man then hands over his concubine. These are their acts and words.

v26–29: The narrator now calls the man her master. Her hands on the threshold and her lack of an answer remain explicit. The Hebrew does not state exactly when she dies; 20:4 calls her murdered, and 20:5 gives the husband’s report.

## Vocabulary

v03: speak to her heart
An expression for speaking persuasively or reassuringly.

v19: your maidservant
A deferential way of referring to the accompanying woman, not a claim that she belongs to the host.

v23: outrage
A grave violation of communal and moral obligations, repeated in the following chapter.
