## Midian strips the land

<p>
v01: The Israelites did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes, and he gave them into Midian’s hand for seven years.
v02: Midian’s hand bore down on Israel. To escape the Midianites, the Israelites made hiding places in the mountains, caves, and strongholds.
v03: Whenever Israel sowed its crops, Midian, Amalek, and the eastern peoples would come up against them.
v04: They camped against them and ruined the land’s produce as far as Gaza. They left Israel nothing to live on—not a sheep, an ox, or a donkey.
v05: They came up with their livestock and tents, swarming like locusts. They and their camels were beyond counting, and they entered the land to destroy it.
v06: Midian reduced Israel to deep poverty, and the Israelites cried out to the LORD.
</p>

## The LORD sends a prophet

<p>
v07: When the Israelites cried out to the LORD because of Midian,
v08: he sent them a prophet, who said, “This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: ‘I brought you up from Egypt. I brought you out of the house of slavery.
v09: I rescued you from Egypt’s hand and from the hand of everyone who oppressed you. I drove them out before you and gave you their land.
v10: I told you, “I am the LORD your God. You must not fear the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you live.” But you have not obeyed my voice.’”
</p>

## Gideon is called

<p>
v11: The angel of the LORD came and sat beneath the terebinth at Ophrah that belonged to Joash the Abiezrite. Joash’s son Gideon was beating out wheat in the winepress to keep it from Midian.
v12: The angel of the LORD appeared to him and said, “The LORD is with you, mighty warrior.”
v13: “Please, my lord,” Gideon replied, “if the LORD is with us, why has all this happened to us? Where are all his wonders our ancestors told us about when they said, ‘Did not the LORD bring us up from Egypt?’ Now the LORD has abandoned us and given us into Midian’s grasp.”
</p>

<p>
v14: The LORD turned to him and said, “Go with this strength of yours and rescue Israel from Midian’s grasp. Am I not sending you?”
v15: “Please, Lord,” he answered, “how can I rescue Israel? My clan is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the youngest in my father’s household.”
v16: The LORD told him, “I will be with you, and you will strike Midian down as if it were one man.”
</p>

## A sign at the rock

<p>
v17: Gideon said, “If I have found favor in your eyes, give me a sign that it is you speaking with me.
v18: Please do not leave until I return with my offering and set it before you.” He answered, “I will stay until you return.”
v19: Gideon went in and prepared a young goat and unleavened bread from an ephah of flour. He put the meat in a basket and the broth in a pot, brought them out beneath the terebinth, and presented them.
v20: The angel of God told him, “Take the meat and unleavened bread, place them on that rock, and pour out the broth.” Gideon did so.
v21: The angel of the LORD reached out the tip of the staff in his hand and touched the meat and unleavened bread. Fire rose from the rock and consumed them, and the angel of the LORD vanished from Gideon’s sight.
</p>

<p>
v22: Gideon realized that it was the angel of the LORD. “Ah, Lord GOD!” he said. “I have seen the angel of the LORD face to face!”
v23: The LORD told him, “Peace to you. Do not be afraid; you will not die.”
v24: Gideon built an altar to the LORD there and named it “The LORD Is Peace.” It is still at Ophrah of the Abiezrites to this day.
</p>

## Baal’s altar torn down

<p>
v25: That night the LORD told him, “Take your father’s bull and the second bull, seven years old. Tear down your father’s altar to Baal and cut down the Asherah beside it.
v26: Build a properly arranged altar to the LORD your God on top of this stronghold. Take the second bull and offer it as a burnt offering, using the wood of the Asherah you cut down.”
v27: Gideon took ten of his servants and did what the LORD had told him. He was afraid of his father’s household and the men of the town, so he did it at night instead of by day.
</p>

<p>
v28: When the men of the town got up early, they found Baal’s altar demolished, the Asherah beside it cut down, and the second bull offered on the newly built altar.
v29: “Who did this?” they asked one another. They investigated and searched until they said, “Gideon son of Joash did it.”
</p>

<p>
v30: The men of the town told Joash, “Bring your son out. He must die, because he demolished Baal’s altar and cut down the Asherah beside it.”
v31: Joash answered everyone confronting him, “Are you going to plead Baal’s case? Are you going to save him? Anyone who pleads for him must be put to death by morning! If he is a god, let him plead his own case, since someone has torn down his altar.”
v32: That day Gideon was named Jerubbaal, meaning, “Let Baal contend with him,” because he had torn down Baal’s altar.
</p>

## Gideon gathers the tribes

<p>
v33: All Midian, Amalek, and the eastern peoples gathered together, crossed over, and camped in the Jezreel Valley.
v34: The Spirit of the LORD clothed himself with Gideon. Gideon sounded the horn, and the Abiezrites rallied behind him.
v35: He sent messengers throughout Manasseh, and they too rallied behind him. He also sent messengers to Asher, Zebulun, and Naphtali, and they came up to meet them.
</p>

## The fleece

<p>
v36: Gideon said to God, “If you are going to rescue Israel through my hand as you have said,
v37: look, I am laying a wool fleece on the threshing floor. If dew falls only on the fleece while all the ground is dry, I will know that you will rescue Israel through my hand, as you have said.”
v38: That is what happened. Gideon got up early the next day, squeezed the fleece, and wrung out a bowlful of water.
v39: Then Gideon said to God, “Do not let your anger burn against me. Let me speak just once more. Please let me test the fleece just this once: let the fleece alone be dry, with dew on all the ground.”
v40: God did that very night as Gideon asked. Only the fleece was dry; dew covered all the ground.
</p>

## Notes

v11: Gideon beats wheat in a winepress to hide it from Midian. The unusual setting belongs to the account of hunger and fear.

v13–16: The account moves between the angel and the LORD as speaker. Gideon first says my lord, then Lord. Youngest in verse 15 can also mean least in standing.

v25–26: The Hebrew can describe two bulls, or identify the father’s bull as the second, seven-year-old bull. The wording about arranging the altar is also uncertain; it may concern laying its stones in order.

v31–32: The word for pleading or contending connects Joash’s challenge with Gideon’s new name, Jerubbaal.

v34: The Hebrew pictures the Spirit putting on Gideon as clothing. The image is retained rather than reduced to a statement about courage.

## Vocabulary

v11: terebinth
A tree that provides shade; the tree is a landmark in the encounter.

v19: ephah
An ancient measure of dry goods, used here for flour.

v25: Asherah
Here a wooden object associated with the worship of Asherah, cut down and burned.

v26: burnt offering
An offering burned on the altar.

v34: horn
Hebrew shofar: an animal horn used to sound a signal.

v37: fleece
The wool shorn from a sheep, kept together as a covering.
