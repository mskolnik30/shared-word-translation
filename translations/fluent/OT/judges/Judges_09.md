## Abimelech Seizes Power

<p>
v01: Abimelech son of Jerubbaal went to Shechem to his mother’s brothers and spoke to them and to the whole clan of his mother’s father’s house, saying,

v02: “Please speak in the hearing of all the leaders of Shechem: Which is better for you, that seventy men, all the sons of Jerubbaal, rule over you, or that one man rule over you? Remember also that I am your bone and your flesh.”

v03: His mother’s brothers spoke all these words about him in the hearing of all the leaders of Shechem, and their hearts inclined after Abimelech, for they said, “He is our brother.”

v04: They gave him seventy pieces of silver from the house of Baal-Berith, and Abimelech hired with them worthless and reckless men who followed him.

v05: He went to his father’s house at Ophrah and killed his brothers, the sons of Jerubbaal, seventy men, on one stone. But Jotham, the youngest son of Jerubbaal, was left, for he hid himself.

v06: Then all the leaders of Shechem and all Beth-Millo gathered together and went and made Abimelech king beside the oak of the pillar at Shechem.
</p>

## Jotham’s Fable

<p>
v07: When they told Jotham, he went and stood on the top of Mount Gerizim, lifted his voice, and called out:

“Listen to me, leaders of Shechem,
so that God may listen to you.

v08: The trees once went out
to anoint a king over themselves.
They said to the olive tree,
‘Reign over us.’

v09: But the olive tree said to them,
‘Should I stop producing my richness,
by which gods and people are honored,
and go wave over the trees?’

v10: Then the trees said to the fig tree,
‘You come and reign over us.’

v11: But the fig tree said to them,
‘Should I stop producing my sweetness
and my good fruit
and go wave over the trees?’

v12: Then the trees said to the vine,
‘You come and reign over us.’

v13: But the vine said to them,
‘Should I stop producing my new wine,
which gladdens gods and people,
and go wave over the trees?’

v14: Then all the trees said to the bramble,
‘You come and reign over us.’

v15: The bramble said to the trees,
‘If in truth you are anointing me king over you,
come, take refuge in my shade.
But if not,
let fire come out from the bramble
and consume the cedars of Lebanon.’
</p>

## Jotham Pronounces Judgment

<p>
v16: “Now therefore, if you have acted in truth and integrity in making Abimelech king, and if you have dealt well with Jerubbaal and his house, and have done to him according to the work of his hands—

v17: for my father fought for you, risked his life, and delivered you from the hand of Midian,

v18: but you have risen against my father’s house this day, killed his sons, seventy men on one stone, and made Abimelech, son of his female servant, king over the leaders of Shechem because he is your brother—

v19: if then you have acted in truth and integrity with Jerubbaal and his house this day, rejoice in Abimelech, and let him also rejoice in you.

v20: But if not, let fire come out from Abimelech and consume the leaders of Shechem and Beth-Millo, and let fire come out from the leaders of Shechem and Beth-Millo and consume Abimelech.”

v21: Then Jotham fled and escaped. He went to Beer and lived there because of Abimelech his brother.
</p>

## Division Between Abimelech and Shechem

<p>
v22: Abimelech ruled over Israel three years.

v23: Then God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and the leaders of Shechem, and the leaders of Shechem dealt treacherously with Abimelech,

v24: so that the violence done to the seventy sons of Jerubbaal might come back upon Abimelech their brother, who killed them, and upon the leaders of Shechem, who strengthened his hands to kill his brothers.

v25: The leaders of Shechem set men in ambush against him on the tops of the mountains, and they robbed everyone who passed by them on the road. And it was told to Abimelech.

v26: Gaal son of Ebed came with his brothers and crossed into Shechem, and the leaders of Shechem put their trust in him.

v27: They went out into the field, gathered the grapes from their vineyards, trod them, and held a festival. They went into the house of their god, ate and drank, and cursed Abimelech.

v28: Gaal son of Ebed said, “Who is Abimelech, and who is Shechem, that we should serve him? Is he not the son of Jerubbaal, and is not Zebul his officer? Serve the men of Hamor, father of Shechem. Why should we serve this one?

v29: If only this people were under my hand! Then I would remove Abimelech.” And he said to Abimelech, “Increase your army and come out!”
</p>

## Zebul Warns Abimelech

<p>
v30: When Zebul ruler of the city heard the words of Gaal son of Ebed, his anger burned.

v31: He sent messengers to Abimelech secretly, saying, “Look, Gaal son of Ebed and his brothers have come to Shechem, and look, they are stirring up the city against you.

v32: So now rise by night, you and the people who are with you, and lie in wait in the field.

v33: Then in the morning, as soon as the sun rises, rise early and rush upon the city. Look, when he and the people who are with him come out against you, do to him as your hand finds opportunity.”
</p>

## Abimelech Defeats Gaal

<p>
v34: So Abimelech and all the people who were with him rose by night and lay in wait against Shechem in four companies.

v35: Gaal son of Ebed went out and stood at the entrance of the city gate. Then Abimelech and the people who were with him rose from the ambush.

v36: When Gaal saw the people, he said to Zebul, “Look, people are coming down from the tops of the mountains.” Zebul said to him, “You see the shadows of the mountains as men.”

v37: Gaal spoke again and said, “Look, people are coming down from the center of the land, and one company is coming from the direction of the Diviners’ Oak.”

v38: Then Zebul said to him, “Where now is your mouth, with which you said, ‘Who is Abimelech, that we should serve him?’ Is this not the people you despised? Go out now and fight them.”

v39: So Gaal went out before the leaders of Shechem and fought Abimelech.

v40: Abimelech pursued him, and he fled before him. Many fell wounded as far as the entrance of the gate.

v41: Abimelech lived at Arumah, and Zebul drove out Gaal and his brothers so that they could not remain in Shechem.
</p>

## Shechem Is Destroyed

<p>
v42: The next day the people went out into the field, and it was told to Abimelech.

v43: He took the people, divided them into three companies, and lay in wait in the field. He looked, and there were the people coming out of the city. He rose against them and struck them.

v44: Abimelech and the company with him rushed forward and stood at the entrance of the city gate, while the other two companies rushed upon everyone who were in the field and struck them.

v45: Abimelech fought against the city all that day. He captured the city, killed the people who were in it, tore down the city, and sowed it with salt.

v46: When all the leaders of the Tower of Shechem heard of it, they entered the stronghold of the house of El-Berith.

v47: It was told to Abimelech that all the leaders of the Tower of Shechem had gathered together.

v48: Abimelech went up to Mount Zalmon, he and all the people who were with him. Abimelech took an axe in his hand, cut down a branch from the trees, lifted it, and put it on his shoulder. Then he said to the people who were with him, “What you have seen me do, hurry and do as I have done.”

v49: So all the people also cut down each his branch and followed Abimelech. They put the branches against the stronghold and set the stronghold on fire over them. So all the people of the Tower of Shechem died also, about one thousand men and women.
</p>

## Abimelech Dies at Thebez

<p>
v50: Then Abimelech went to Thebez, camped against Thebez, and captured it.

v51: But there was a strong tower in the middle of the city, and all the men and women and all the leaders of the city fled there. They shut themselves in and went up onto the roof of the tower.

v52: Abimelech came to the tower and fought against it. He approached the entrance of the tower to burn it with fire.

v53: But a certain woman threw an upper millstone on Abimelech’s head and crushed his skull.

v54: He quickly called to the young man who carried his weapons and said to him, “Draw your sword and kill me, lest they say of me, ‘A woman killed him.’” So his young man thrust him through, and he died.

v55: When the men of Israel saw that Abimelech was dead, they departed, each to his place.

v56: Thus God returned the evil of Abimelech that he had done to his father by killing his seventy brothers.

v57: God returned all the evil of the men of Shechem upon their heads, and the curse of Jotham son of Jerubbaal came upon them.
</p>

## Notes

v02: “your bone and your flesh” — Abimelech appeals to kinship with Shechem through his mother’s family, using family loyalty as the basis for political power.

v08–15: Jotham’s fable contrasts fruitful trees that refuse kingship with the bramble, which offers useless shade and threatens fire. The fable exposes Abimelech’s kingship as destructive rather than fruitful.

v23: “evil spirit” — The phrase describes a destructive spirit sent by God to produce division. The text presents the resulting conflict as judgment upon violence already committed.

v27: The political conspiracy unfolds during a cultic festival in the house of Shechem’s god, linking civic rebellion and religious allegiance.

v45: “sowed it with salt” — The act symbolizes devastation and perhaps permanent ruin. The text does not require that the salt have been intended literally to sterilize all soil.

v53–54: Abimelech’s concern is not merely death but the shame, in his cultural setting, of being remembered as killed by a woman. His attempt to control the story fails; the narrative preserves the cause of his mortal wound.

v56–57: The chapter explicitly interprets the destruction of both Abimelech and Shechem as corresponding judgment for their shared violence.

## Vocabulary

v02: עֶצֶם וּבָשָׂר (etsem u-basar)
“Bone and flesh.” An idiom of close kinship.

v04: רֵיק (req)
“Empty,” “worthless,” or “reckless.” Used for the men Abimelech hires.

v08: מָשַׁח (mashach)
“To anoint.” To set apart for an office, here kingship.

v15: אָטָד (atad)
“Bramble” or “thornbush.” The worthless plant that accepts kingship in Jotham’s fable.

v23: רוּחַ רָעָה (ruach ra'ah)
“Evil spirit.” A destructive spirit producing hostility between former allies.

v24: חָמָס (chamas)
“Violence.” Wrongful bloodshed brought back upon those responsible.

v45: מֶלַח (melach)
“Salt.” Scattered over conquered Shechem as an image of devastation.

v53: פֶּלַח רֶכֶב (pelach rekhev)
“Upper millstone.” A heavy rotating stone used for grinding grain.
