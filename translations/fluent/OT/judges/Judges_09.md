## Abimelech becomes king

<p>
v01: Abimelech son of Jerubbaal went to his mother’s brothers in Shechem. He spoke to them and to the whole clan of his mother’s father:
v02: “Ask all Shechem’s citizens this: Which is better for you—to be ruled by seventy men, all Jerubbaal’s sons, or by one man? Remember, I am your own bone and flesh.”
v03: His mother’s brothers repeated all this to Shechem’s citizens on his behalf. Their hearts turned toward Abimelech, for they said, “He is our brother.”
</p>

<p>
v04: They gave him seventy shekels of silver from the temple of Baal-berith. With it Abimelech hired worthless, reckless men, who followed him.
v05: He went to his father’s house at Ophrah and killed his brothers, Jerubbaal’s seventy sons, on one stone. But Jotham, Jerubbaal’s youngest son, survived because he hid.
v06: All Shechem’s citizens and all Beth-millo gathered and made Abimelech king beside the oak of the pillar at Shechem.
</p>

## Jotham’s trees

<p>
v07: When Jotham heard, he went and stood on top of Mount Gerizim. He raised his voice and called out, “Listen to me, citizens of Shechem, so that God may listen to you!
</p>

<p>
v08: The trees once went out to anoint a king over themselves. They said to the olive tree, ‘Reign over us.’
v09: But the olive tree answered, ‘Should I give up my rich oil, by which gods and people are honored, to go and sway over the trees?’
</p>

<p>
v10: Then the trees said to the fig tree, ‘Come, you reign over us.’
v11: The fig tree answered, ‘Should I give up my sweetness and my good fruit to go and sway over the trees?’
</p>

<p>
v12: Then the trees said to the vine, ‘Come, you reign over us.’
v13: The vine answered, ‘Should I give up my wine, which gladdens gods and people, to go and sway over the trees?’
</p>

<p>
v14: Then all the trees said to the bramble, ‘Come, you reign over us.’
v15: The bramble told the trees, ‘If you are truly anointing me king over you, come and take shelter in my shade. But if not, let fire come out of the bramble and consume the cedars of Lebanon.’
</p>

## Jotham’s accusation

<p>
v16: Now, have you acted faithfully and with integrity in making Abimelech king? Have you treated Jerubbaal and his household well and repaid him as his actions deserved?
v17: My father fought for you, threw his life into danger, and rescued you from Midian’s hand.
v18: But today you have risen against my father’s household. You killed his seventy sons on one stone and made Abimelech, the son of his slave woman, king over Shechem’s citizens because he is your brother.
</p>

<p>
v19: If you have acted faithfully and with integrity toward Jerubbaal and his household today, then rejoice in Abimelech, and let him rejoice in you.
v20: But if not, let fire come out of Abimelech and consume Shechem’s citizens and Beth-millo. Let fire come out of Shechem’s citizens and Beth-millo and consume Abimelech!”
v21: Jotham escaped and fled to Beer, where he lived out of reach of his brother Abimelech.
</p>

## Shechem turns against Abimelech

<p>
v22: Abimelech ruled over Israel for three years.
</p>

<p>
v23: God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and Shechem’s citizens, and they betrayed Abimelech.
v24: This brought the violence done to Jerubbaal’s seventy sons back upon those responsible: their blood fell on their brother Abimelech, who killed them, and on Shechem’s citizens, who strengthened his hand to kill his brothers.
v25: Shechem’s citizens set ambushes against him on the hilltops. They robbed everyone passing along the road, and Abimelech was told.
</p>

## Gaal challenges Abimelech

<p>
v26: Gaal son of Ebed came with his brothers and moved into Shechem. The citizens of Shechem put their trust in him.
v27: They went out to the fields, harvested their vineyards, trod the grapes, and held a festival. They entered their god’s temple, ate and drank, and cursed Abimelech.
</p>

<p>
v28: Gaal son of Ebed said, “Who is Abimelech, and who is Shechem, that we should serve him? Is he not Jerubbaal’s son, and is not Zebul his officer? Serve the men of Hamor, Shechem’s father! Why should we serve Abimelech?
v29: If only these people were in my hand, I would remove Abimelech!” And he challenged Abimelech, “Build up your army and come out!”
</p>

## Zebul sends a warning

<p>
v30: When Zebul, the town’s ruler, heard what Gaal son of Ebed had said, his anger burned.
v31: He secretly sent messengers to Abimelech: “Gaal son of Ebed and his brothers have come to Shechem. They are stirring up the town against you.
v32: Now get up by night, you and the people with you, and lie in ambush in the fields.
v33: At sunrise, rise early and rush the town. When he and his people come out against you, do whatever your hand finds to do.”
</p>

## Gaal driven out

<p>
v34: Abimelech and everyone with him got up by night and lay in ambush against Shechem in four companies.
v35: Gaal son of Ebed went out and stood at the town gate. Abimelech and his people rose from their ambush.
v36: Gaal saw the people and told Zebul, “Look! People are coming down from the hilltops.” Zebul replied, “You are seeing the shadows of the hills as men.”
</p>

<p>
v37: Gaal spoke again: “People are coming down from the Navel of the Land, and one company is coming by the Diviners’ Oak.”
v38: Zebul told him, “Where is that mouth of yours now—the one that said, ‘Who is Abimelech, that we should serve him?’ Are these not the people you despised? Go out now and fight them!”
v39: Gaal went out at the head of Shechem’s citizens and fought Abimelech.
v40: Abimelech pursued him, and Gaal fled. Many fell slain, all the way to the gate entrance.
v41: Abimelech stayed at Arumah, and Zebul drove Gaal and his brothers out of Shechem.
</p>

## Shechem destroyed

<p>
v42: The next day the people went out into the fields, and Abimelech was told.
v43: He took his men, divided them into three companies, and hid them in the fields. When he saw the people coming out of the town, he rose against them and struck them down.
v44: Abimelech and the companies with him rushed forward and took their stand at the town gate. The other two companies rushed everyone in the fields and struck them down.
v45: Abimelech fought against the town all that day. He captured it, killed the people in it, demolished the town, and sowed it with salt.
</p>

<p>
v46: When all the citizens of the Tower of Shechem heard, they entered the stronghold of the temple of El-berith.
v47: Abimelech was told that all the citizens of the Tower of Shechem had gathered there.
</p>

<p>
v48: He and everyone with him went up Mount Zalmon. Abimelech took an ax in his hand, cut a branch from the trees, lifted it onto his shoulder, and told his people, “You have seen what I did. Hurry and do the same.”
v49: Each man cut a branch and followed Abimelech. They piled the branches against the stronghold and set it on fire over the people inside. All the people of the Tower of Shechem died too—about a thousand men and women.
</p>

## Abimelech at Thebez

<p>
v50: Abimelech went to Thebez, encamped against it, and captured it.
v51: There was a strong tower inside the town. All the men and women and all the town’s citizens fled into it, shut themselves in, and climbed onto the tower’s roof.
v52: Abimelech came to the tower and attacked it. He approached its entrance to burn it down.
</p>

<p>
v53: But a woman threw an upper millstone onto Abimelech’s head and crushed his skull.
v54: He quickly called the young man carrying his weapons. “Draw your sword and kill me,” he said, “so they will not say of me, ‘A woman killed him.’” His attendant ran him through, and he died.
v55: When the Israelites saw that Abimelech was dead, each went home.
</p>

<p>
v56: So God returned upon Abimelech the evil he had done to his father by killing his seventy brothers.
v57: God also brought all the evil of Shechem’s men back on their own heads. The curse of Jotham son of Jerubbaal came upon them.
</p>

## Notes

v02–06: Citizens renders a word for possessors or leading members of a town. It does not imply modern equal citizenship. The text says seventy brothers were killed and also says Jotham survived; the count is retained.

v06: The oak’s designation is uncertain. Of the pillar is a traditional understanding of the landmark; the Hebrew form is related to standing or being stationed.

v09–15: The trees tell a fable about kingship. Gods can also be read as God in verses 9 and 13. The recurring sway, shade, and fire images are left within the story.

v18: Jotham calls Abimelech’s mother his father’s slave woman. The earlier narrator called her his concubine. These voices and terms are kept distinct.

v23: Evil spirit can also be understood as a spirit of hostility. God remains the sender in either reading.

v31: Secretly translates an uncertain expression that can also be read as a place-name, Tormah. Some translations read Arumah, the place named in verse 41. Stirring up interprets a verb associated with besieging or provoking.

v37: Navel of the Land is a landscape expression that can mean a central or prominent height. The other landmark is literally the oak of diviners.

v43–46: Verse 43 names three companies; verse 44 has a plural expression for those with Abimelech and then names the other two. The stronghold in verse 46 is an uncertain inner refuge within the temple.

v53–54: The woman remains unnamed. Abimelech’s attempt to prevent people saying that a woman killed him belongs to his own speech.

## Vocabulary

v06: Beth-millo
A name meaning house of the filling or terrace, associated here with Shechem.

v08: anoint
To apply oil as a sign of appointment; the trees seek a king.

v14: bramble
A thorny shrub, whose offered shade contrasts with the great trees in the fable.

v46: El-berith
God of the covenant; the name differs in form from Baal-berith.

v53: upper millstone
The movable upper stone used with a lower stone to grind grain.
