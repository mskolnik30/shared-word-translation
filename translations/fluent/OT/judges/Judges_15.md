## Retaliation spreads

<p>
v01: Some time later, during the wheat harvest, Samson visited his wife with a young goat. “I will go in to my wife in her room,” he said. But her father would not let him enter.
v02: Her father said, “I was sure you hated her, so I gave her to your companion. Is not her younger sister more attractive than she is? Please take her instead.”
</p>

<p>
v03: Samson told them, “This time I will be blameless when I harm the Philistines.”
v04: Samson went and caught three hundred foxes. He took torches, turned the animals tail to tail, and placed a torch between each pair of tails.
v05: He lit the torches and released the animals into the Philistines’ standing grain. He burned the stacked grain, the standing crops, and the olive orchards.
</p>

<p>
v06: The Philistines asked, “Who did this?” They were told, “Samson, the Timnite’s son-in-law, because his father-in-law took his wife and gave her to his companion.” The Philistines went up and burned her and her father to death.
v07: Samson told them, “Since you have done this, I will take revenge on you. Only then will I stop.”
v08: He struck them down, hip and thigh, in a great slaughter. Then he went down and stayed in a cleft of the rock of Etam.
</p>

## Judah hands Samson over

<p>
v09: The Philistines came up, camped in Judah, and spread out at Lehi.
</p>

<p>
v10: The men of Judah asked, “Why have you come against us?” They answered, “We have come to bind Samson and do to him what he did to us.”
v11: Three thousand men of Judah went down to the cleft in the rock of Etam. They told Samson, “Do you not know that the Philistines rule over us? What have you done to us?” He answered, “I did to them what they did to me.”
</p>

<p>
v12: “We have come to bind you and hand you over to the Philistines,” they said. Samson replied, “Swear to me that you yourselves will not strike me down.”
v13: They answered, “No. We will only bind you and give you into their hand. We certainly will not kill you.” They tied him with two new ropes and brought him up from the rock.
</p>

## The jawbone at Lehi

<p>
v14: When he reached Lehi, the Philistines came toward him shouting. The Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him. The ropes on his arms became like flax burned in fire, and his bonds melted away from his hands.
v15: He found a fresh donkey’s jawbone, reached out and took it, and killed a thousand men with it.
</p>

<p>
v16: Then Samson said,
“With a donkey’s jawbone,
heaps upon heaps!
With a donkey’s jawbone
I struck down a thousand men.”
v17: When he finished speaking, he threw the jawbone from his hand and called that place Ramath-lehi.
</p>

<p>
v18: He became very thirsty and called to the LORD: “You have given this great rescue through your servant’s hand. Must I now die of thirst and fall into the hand of the uncircumcised?”
v19: God split open the hollow at Lehi, and water came out. Samson drank, his spirit returned, and he revived. So he called it En-hakkore; it is at Lehi to this day.
v20: He judged Israel for twenty years in the days of the Philistines.
</p>

## Notes

v03–07: Both sides describe their violence as repayment. Samson’s claim to be blameless is his own.

v04: The animal name can denote foxes or jackals. The account gives the number and the method of setting the fire, without explaining how the animals were caught.

v05: The final Hebrew phrase is literally vineyard of olive, rendered olive orchards. Some translations distinguish vineyards and olive groves.

v08: Hip and thigh is an unusual bodily expression for a severe defeat; the precise picture is uncertain.

v16–19: The poem plays on similar Hebrew sounds for donkey and heap. Lehi also means jawbone. Verse 19 locates the water in a hollow at Lehi; it does not require a hollow inside the discarded bone.

## Vocabulary

v16: heaps upon heaps
Samson’s image for the people struck down, with wordplay that English cannot fully reproduce.

v17: Ramath-lehi
A name meaning Jawbone Height.

v19: En-hakkore
A name meaning Spring of the One Who Called.
