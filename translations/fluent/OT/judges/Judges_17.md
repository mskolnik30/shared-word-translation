## Micah’s shrine

<p>
v01: There was a man in the hill country of Ephraim named Micah.
v02: He told his mother, “The eleven hundred shekels of silver taken from you—the silver about which you spoke a curse in my hearing—is here with me. I took it.” His mother said, “May my son be blessed by the LORD!”
</p>

<p>
v03: He returned the eleven hundred shekels of silver to his mother. She said, “I solemnly dedicate this silver from my hand to the LORD for my son, to make a carved image and a cast image. Now I will give it back to you.”
v04: He returned the silver to his mother. She took two hundred shekels and gave them to a metalworker, who made a carved image and a cast image. They were placed in Micah’s house.
</p>

<p>
v05: This man Micah had a shrine. He made an ephod and teraphim, and installed one of his sons as his priest.
v06: In those days Israel had no king. Everyone did what was right in their own eyes.
</p>

## A Levite becomes Micah’s priest

<p>
v07: There was a young man from Bethlehem in Judah, of the clan of Judah. He was a Levite living there as a sojourner.
v08: He left the town of Bethlehem in Judah to find somewhere to live. On his travels he came to Micah’s house in the hill country of Ephraim.
</p>

<p>
v09: Micah asked him, “Where have you come from?” He answered, “I am a Levite from Bethlehem in Judah, looking for a place to live.”
v10: Micah said, “Stay with me and be a father and priest to me. I will give you ten shekels of silver a year, a set of clothes, and your food.” So the Levite went with him.
</p>

<p>
v11: The Levite agreed to stay with the man, and the young man became like one of his sons.
v12: Micah installed the Levite, and the young man became his priest and lived in his house.
v13: Then Micah said, “Now I know the LORD will treat me well, because a Levite has become my priest.”
</p>

## Notes

v01: The Hebrew uses the longer name Micayahu in verses 1 and 4, and Micah elsewhere in the account.

v02–04: Shekels supplies the weight unit. The eleven hundred returned and the two hundred used for images are both retained. Carved image and cast image may describe two objects or a carved object with a metal covering; verse 4 concludes with a singular pronoun.

v05,12: Installed renders the Hebrew expression filled the hand, used for appointment to priestly service.

v06: Everyone is the generalizing force of the Hebrew individual man. The same refrain closes the book in 21:25.

v07: The Levite is also described as of Judah’s clan. This may refer to his place within the community where he lived; the wording is not rewritten as a new genealogy.

v10–13: The Levite is called a father, becomes like a son, and serves as Micah’s priest. Micah’s confidence about the LORD’s favor is his own conclusion.

## Vocabulary

v05: ephod
An object associated with priestly worship. Its exact form in this household shrine is not specified.

v05: teraphim
Household sacred images, sometimes connected with divination.

v07: sojourner
Someone living outside their own place or kin group, dependent on the receiving community.
