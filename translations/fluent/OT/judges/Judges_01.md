## Judah and Simeon go up

<p>
v01: After Joshua died, the Israelites asked the LORD, “Who should go up first for us to fight the Canaanites?”
v02: The LORD answered, “Judah is to go up. I have given the land into his hand.”
v03: Judah said to his brother Simeon, “Come with me into my allotted territory, and we will fight the Canaanites. Then I will go with you into yours.” So Simeon went with him.
v04: Judah went up, and the LORD gave the Canaanites and Perizzites into their hand. At Bezek they struck down ten thousand men.
v05: They found Adoni-bezek at Bezek and fought him, defeating the Canaanites and Perizzites.
v06: Adoni-bezek fled, but they chased him down, captured him, and cut off his thumbs and big toes.
v07: Adoni-bezek said, “Seventy kings with their thumbs and big toes cut off used to gather scraps under my table. God has repaid me for what I did.” They brought him to Jerusalem, where he died.
</p>

## Jerusalem, Hebron, and Debir

<p>
v08: The people of Judah fought against Jerusalem and captured it. They put it to the sword and set the city on fire.
v09: Afterward Judah’s people went down to fight the Canaanites living in the hill country, the Negev, and the lowlands.
v10: Judah advanced against the Canaanites living in Hebron, formerly called Kiriath-arba. They struck down Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai.
v11: From there Judah advanced against the people of Debir, formerly called Kiriath-sepher.
</p>

<p>
v12: Caleb said, “I will give my daughter Achsah in marriage to the man who attacks Kiriath-sepher and captures it.”
v13: Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother, captured it, and Caleb gave him his daughter Achsah in marriage.
v14: When Achsah arrived, she urged Othniel to ask her father for the field. She got down from the donkey, and Caleb asked her, “What do you want?”
v15: “Give me a blessing,” she told him. “You have given me land in the Negev; give me springs of water too.” Caleb gave her the upper springs and the lower springs.
</p>

## Land taken and peoples remaining

<p>
v16: The descendants of the Kenite, Moses’ father-in-law, went up from the City of Palms with Judah’s people into the wilderness of Judah, in the Negev near Arad. They went and settled among the people.
v17: Judah went with his brother Simeon. They struck the Canaanites living in Zephath and devoted the city to destruction. So it was named Hormah.
</p>

<p>
v18: Judah captured Gaza and its territory, Ashkelon and its territory, and Ekron and its territory.
v19: The LORD was with Judah, and Judah took possession of the hill country. But they could not drive out the people of the valley, because they had iron chariots.
v20: Hebron was given to Caleb as Moses had promised, and he drove out the three sons of Anak from there.
</p>

<p>
v21: But Benjamin’s people did not drive out the Jebusites living in Jerusalem. The Jebusites have lived with Benjamin’s people in Jerusalem to this day.
</p>

## Joseph’s household at Bethel

<p>
v22: Joseph’s household also went up against Bethel, and the LORD was with them.
v23: Joseph’s household sent scouts to Bethel, the town formerly called Luz.
v24: The scouts saw a man coming out of the town. “Please show us the way in,” they told him, “and we will show you loyal kindness.”
v25: He showed them the way into the town, and they put the town to the sword. But they let the man and his whole clan go.
v26: The man went to the land of the Hittites, built a town, and named it Luz. That is its name to this day.
</p>

## Peoples Israel did not drive out

<p>
v27: Manasseh did not drive out the people of Beth-shean and its dependent towns, Taanach and its dependent towns, Dor and its dependent towns, Ibleam and its dependent towns, or Megiddo and its dependent towns. The Canaanites were determined to stay in that land.
v28: When Israel grew strong, they subjected the Canaanites to forced labor, but they still did not drive them out.
</p>

<p>
v29: Ephraim did not drive out the Canaanites living in Gezer. The Canaanites remained among them in Gezer.
v30: Zebulun did not drive out the people of Kitron or Nahalol. The Canaanites remained among them and were subjected to forced labor.
</p>

<p>
v31: Asher did not drive out the people of Acco, Sidon, Ahlab, Achzib, Helbah, Aphik, or Rehob.
v32: So Asher’s people lived among the Canaanites who inhabited the land, because they had not driven them out.
v33: Naphtali did not drive out the people of Beth-shemesh or Beth-anath. They lived among the Canaanite inhabitants of the land, but subjected the people of Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath to forced labor.
</p>

<p>
v34: The Amorites pressed Dan’s people back into the hill country and would not let them come down into the valley.
v35: The Amorites were determined to remain at Mount Heres, Aijalon, and Shaalbim. But Joseph’s household laid a heavy hand on them, and they were subjected to forced labor.
v36: The Amorite boundary ran from the Ascent of Akrabbim, from Sela and upward.
</p>

## Notes

v03: Judah and Simeon are spoken of as brothers. Here their names stand for their tribes and recall the sons of Jacob.

v13–15: The younger-brother phrase can attach to Othniel or to Kenaz. In verse 14, the unnamed man Achsah urges is understood here as Othniel. Compare the related account in Joshua 15:16–19.

v16: The Kenite is identified through marriage to Moses’ family but is not named here. The kinship word is rendered father-in-law; see also 4:11.

v18–21: The capture notices stand beside limits on possession. Verse 19’s compressed wording is understood as Judah’s inability to expel the valley’s inhabitants. Verse 21 names Benjamin; Joshua 15:63 names Judah.

v36: Sela can also be read as “the rock.” The boundary is called Amorite here.

## Vocabulary

v09: Negev
The dry southern region; the name also serves as a direction word for south.

v17: devoted to destruction
Set apart for destruction. Hormah echoes the Hebrew word for this devotion.

v24: loyal kindness
Hebrew hesed: here a pledge of protective kindness, carried out for the man and his clan.

v28: forced labor
Compulsory service imposed on a subjected population.
