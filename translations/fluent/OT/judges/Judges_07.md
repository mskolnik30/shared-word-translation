## Too many to fight

<p>
v01: Jerubbaal—that is, Gideon—and all the people with him rose early and camped by the spring of Harod. Midian’s camp lay north of them, in the valley by the hill of Moreh.
v02: The LORD told Gideon, “You have too many people for me to give Midian into their hand. Israel might boast against me and say, ‘My own hand saved me.’
v03: Now announce for everyone to hear: ‘Anyone who is afraid and trembling may turn back and leave Mount Gilead.’” Twenty-two thousand turned back, and ten thousand remained.
</p>

<p>
v04: The LORD told Gideon, “There are still too many. Bring them down to the water, and I will sift them for you there. Whoever I tell you is to go with you will go. Whoever I tell you is not to go will not go.”
v05: Gideon brought the people down to the water. The LORD said, “Set apart everyone who laps the water with his tongue as a dog laps, and everyone who kneels down to drink.”
v06: Three hundred men lapped with their hands to their mouths. All the rest knelt down to drink water.
</p>

<p>
v07: The LORD told Gideon, “With the three hundred men who lapped, I will rescue you and give Midian into your hand. Let everyone else go home.”
v08: The men took the people’s provisions and horns. Gideon sent the rest of Israel back to their tents but kept the three hundred. Midian’s camp was below him in the valley.
</p>

## A dream in the enemy camp

<p>
v09: That night the LORD told him, “Get up and go down against the camp, for I have given it into your hand.
v10: But if you are afraid to go down, take your servant Purah with you to the camp.
v11: Listen to what they say; then your hands will be strengthened, and you can go down against the camp.” So Gideon and his servant Purah went down to the edge of the armed men in the camp.
</p>

<p>
v12: Midian, Amalek, and all the eastern peoples lay spread across the valley like a swarm of locusts. Their camels were beyond counting, like sand on the seashore.
v13: Gideon arrived as a man was telling his companion a dream. “Listen to the dream I had,” he said. “A round loaf of barley bread came tumbling into Midian’s camp. It struck the tent so that it fell, overturned it, and left it flat.”
v14: His companion answered, “This can only be the sword of Gideon son of Joash, an Israelite. God has given Midian and the whole camp into his hand.”
</p>

<p>
v15: When Gideon heard the dream and its interpretation, he bowed in worship. He returned to Israel’s camp and said, “Get up! The LORD has given Midian’s camp into your hand.”
</p>

## Horns, jars, and torches

<p>
v16: He divided the three hundred men into three companies. He put a horn in each man’s hand, along with empty jars and torches inside the jars.
v17: “Watch me and do what I do,” he told them. “When I reach the edge of the camp, follow my example.
v18: When I and everyone with me blow our horns, you too must blow yours all around the camp and shout, ‘For the LORD and for Gideon!’”
</p>

<p>
v19: Gideon and the hundred men with him reached the edge of the camp at the start of the middle watch, just after the guards had been posted. They blew their horns and smashed the jars in their hands.
v20: All three companies blew their horns and shattered their jars. Holding torches in their left hands and horns to blow in their right, they shouted, “A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!”
v21: Each man stood in his place around the camp, while the whole camp ran, shouted, and fled.
v22: As the three hundred horns sounded, the LORD turned each man’s sword against his companion throughout the camp. The army fled as far as Beth-shittah toward Zererah, to the edge of Abel-meholah near Tabbath.
</p>

## The pursuit

<p>
v23: Israelite men were called out from Naphtali, Asher, and all Manasseh, and they pursued Midian.
v24: Gideon sent messengers throughout Ephraim’s hill country: “Come down to meet Midian and seize the waterways ahead of them as far as Beth-barah, and the Jordan.” All the men of Ephraim were called out, and they seized those waterways as far as Beth-barah, and the Jordan.
v25: They captured Midian’s two leaders, Oreb and Zeeb. They killed Oreb at the Rock of Oreb and Zeeb at the Winepress of Zeeb, and pursued Midian. They brought Oreb’s and Zeeb’s heads to Gideon from beyond the Jordan.
</p>

## Notes

v03: Mount Gilead is the name in the Hebrew, though its location is difficult in this setting. The departure verb is also unusual. The name has not been replaced to repair the geography.

v05–08: The text describes lapping like a dog, bringing hands to mouths, and kneeling. It does not explain the choice as a test of alertness or courage. Verse 8’s compressed wording is understood as the retained men taking the supplies and horns.

v13: The rare word describing the barley bread is read here as a round loaf; its exact sense is uncertain.

v18–20: The instructions give the cry without sword; the shouted cry includes it. Both forms are retained.

v25: The final phrase says from beyond the Jordan. Its relation to the arrival of the heads is compressed; the next chapter explicitly narrates Gideon’s crossing.

## Vocabulary

v19: middle watch
A division of the night during which a guard remained on duty.

v24: waterways
The water routes or crossing points ahead of the fleeing army.
