## A child loved and called

<p>
v01: “When Israel was a child, I loved him,
and out of Egypt I called my son.
v02: The more they called to them,
the more they went away from them.
They sacrificed to the Baals
and burned offerings to carved images.
v03: Yet I was the one who taught Ephraim to walk,
taking them by the arms.
But they did not know that I healed them.
v04: I drew them with human cords, with ropes of love.
I was to them like one who lifts a yoke from their jaws;
I bent toward him and fed him.
v05: He will not return to the land of Egypt;
Assyria will be his king,
because they refused to return.
v06: The sword will whirl through his cities,
consume his gate bars, and devour
because of their schemes.
v07: My people are bent on turning away from me.
Though they are called upward,
no one rises at all.
</p>

## How can I give you up?

<p>
v08: How can I give you up, Ephraim?
How can I hand you over, Israel?
How can I make you like Admah
or treat you like Zeboiim?
My heart turns within me;
all my compassion grows warm.
v09: I will not unleash my burning anger;
I will not turn to destroy Ephraim again.
For I am God, not a man,
the Holy One among you.
I will not enter a city.
v10: They will follow the LORD;
he will roar like a lion.
When he roars,
his children will come trembling from the west.
v11: They will come trembling like birds from Egypt,
like doves from the land of Assyria.
I will settle them in their homes,” declares the LORD.
v12: “Ephraim has surrounded me with lies,
and the house of Israel with deceit.
Judah still wanders with God,
with the faithful Holy One.
</p>

## Notes

v02-04: Verse 2 has plural callers, without naming them. The arms phrase in verse 3 is difficult; this draft relates the arms to Ephraim. Verse 4 follows the pointed “yoke,” not a repointed word for an infant.

v05,07,09,12: Several lines resist a single clear reading. The negative about Egypt is retained. “Upward” in verse 7 and “city” in verse 9 follow the transmitted wording; “wrath” is another proposed understanding of verse 9. Verse 12 can be read as criticizing Judah’s restlessness or contrasting Judah’s faithfulness; the draft’s reading remains provisional.

## Vocabulary

v08: **Admah; Zeboiim**: Cities remembered among those destroyed with Sodom and Gomorrah.

v10-11: **Trembling**: The repeated verb joins awe and the urgent movement home.
