## The vine and its altars

<p>
v01: Israel is a luxuriant vine
bearing fruit for itself.
As its fruit increased, its altars increased;
as its land prospered, its sacred pillars grew more splendid.
v02: Their hearts are false;
now they must bear their guilt.
He will break down their altars
and devastate their sacred pillars.
v03: Now they will say, ‘We have no king,
because we did not fear the LORD.
And what could a king do for us?’
v04: They speak empty words, swear false oaths,
and make covenants.
So judgment springs up like poison weed
in the furrows of a field.
v05: Samaria’s inhabitants tremble for the calves of Beth-aven.
Its people mourn over it,
and its priests who rejoiced over it mourn for its glory,
for that glory has gone into exile.
v06: It too will be carried to Assyria
as tribute to the contending king.
Ephraim will receive shame;
Israel will be ashamed of its counsel.
v07: Samaria’s king will be swept away
like a twig on the surface of water.
v08: The high places of Aven, Israel’s sin, will be destroyed.
Thorns and thistles will grow over their altars.
They will say to the mountains, ‘Cover us!’
and to the hills, ‘Fall on us!’
</p>

## Plowing and reaping

<p>
v09: “Since the days of Gibeah you have sinned, Israel.
There they have remained.
Will not war overtake them in Gibeah,
war against the children of wrongdoing?
v10: When I wish, I will discipline them.
Peoples will gather against them
when they are bound for their two offenses.
v11: Ephraim was a trained heifer that loved to thresh,
but I passed over her beautiful neck.
I will harness Ephraim; Judah will plow;
Jacob will harrow for himself.
v12: Sow righteousness for yourselves;
reap a harvest of faithful love.
Break up your unplowed ground.
It is time to seek the LORD
until he comes and rains righteousness on you.
v13: You have plowed wickedness and reaped injustice;
you have eaten the fruit of lies.
You trusted in your own way,
in your many warriors.
v14: The uproar of battle will rise among your people,
and all your fortresses will be devastated,
as Shalman devastated Beth-arbel on the day of battle,
when mothers were dashed to pieces with their children.
v15: This is what Bethel has done to you
because of your immense wickedness.
At dawn Israel’s king will be utterly cut off.”
</p>

## Notes

v02,05-07: The heart can be described as false or divided. Verse 5 moves from plural calves to a singular image. The floating object in verse 7 may be a twig or foam.

v09-12: The Gibeah clause is difficult; this draft reads a rhetorical question. Verse 10 follows the traditional read form “offenses.” The neck clause in verse 11 is not fully explained. The final verb of verse 12 may mean rain or teach righteousness.

v14-15: Shalman and Beth-arbel are retained without a firm identification. The family killings and the source’s naming of Bethel as responsible remain explicit.

## Vocabulary

v01: **Sacred pillars**: Standing stones used in worship.

v12: **Unplowed ground**: Land that must be broken up before sowing; the farming image describes a needed change in conduct.
