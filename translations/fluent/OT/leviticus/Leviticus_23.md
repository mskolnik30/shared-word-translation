## The LORD’s appointed times

<p>
v01: The LORD said to Moses,
v02: “Tell the Israelites: These are the LORD’s appointed times, which you are to announce as sacred assemblies. These are my appointed times.
</p>

## The weekly Sabbath

<p>
v03: You may work for six days. The seventh day is a Sabbath of complete rest, a sacred assembly. Do no work at all. It is a Sabbath to the LORD wherever you live.
</p>

## Passover and Unleavened Bread

<p>
v04: These are the LORD’s appointed times, the sacred assemblies you are to announce at their appointed times.
v05: In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month at twilight, comes the LORD’s Passover.
v06: On the fifteenth day of that month comes the LORD’s Festival of Unleavened Bread. Eat unleavened bread for seven days.
v07: Hold a sacred assembly on the first day. Do none of your ordinary work.
v08: For seven days, bring offerings by fire to the LORD. On the seventh day, hold a sacred assembly and do none of your ordinary work.”
</p>

## The first sheaf

<p>
v09: The LORD said to Moses,
v10: “Tell the Israelites: When you enter the land I am giving you and harvest its crops, bring the priest a sheaf from the first of your harvest.
v11: The priest is to wave the sheaf before the LORD so it will be accepted on your behalf. He is to wave it on the day after the Sabbath.
</p>

<p>
v12: On the day you wave the sheaf, offer the LORD a year-old male lamb without blemish as a burnt offering.
v13: Its grain offering is to be two-tenths of an ephah of fine flour mixed with oil, an offering by fire to the LORD, a pleasing aroma. Its drink offering is to be a quarter of a hin of wine.
v14: Eat no bread, roasted grain or fresh grain until that very day, when you bring your God’s offering. This is a lasting rule for your generations, wherever you live.
</p>

## Counting to the new grain offering

<p>
v15: Starting with the day after the Sabbath, the day you bring the sheaf as a wave offering, count seven Sabbaths. The count must cover seven full weeks.
v16: Count fifty days, up to the day after the seventh Sabbath. Then bring the LORD an offering of new grain.
</p>

<p>
v17: Bring two loaves from where you live as a wave offering. Make them with two-tenths of an ephah of fine flour and bake them with leaven, as firstfruits for the LORD.
v18: With the bread, bring seven year-old male lambs without blemish, one young bull and two rams. They are to be a burnt offering to the LORD, with their grain offering and drink offerings—an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.
v19: Offer one male goat as a sin offering and two year-old male lambs as a peace sacrifice.
v20: The priest is to wave them before the LORD with the firstfruits bread, together with the two lambs, as a wave offering. They are holy to the LORD and belong to the priest.
</p>

<p>
v21: On that very day, announce a sacred assembly for yourselves. Do none of your ordinary work. This is a lasting rule for your generations, wherever you live.
</p>

<p>
v22: When you harvest your land, do not reap right to the edges of your field or gather what the harvesters leave behind. Leave it for the poor and the foreigner living among you. I am the LORD your God.”
</p>

## A day of remembrance

<p>
v23: The LORD said to Moses,
v24: “Tell the Israelites: On the first day of the seventh month, you are to rest and hold a sacred assembly, a remembrance marked by blasts of sound.
v25: Do none of your ordinary work, and bring an offering by fire to the LORD.”
</p>

## The Day of Atonement

<p>
v26: The LORD said to Moses,
v27: “The tenth day of this seventh month is the Day of Atonement. Hold a sacred assembly, deny yourselves and bring an offering by fire to the LORD.
v28: Do no work at all on that very day. It is a day of atonement, when atonement is made for you before the LORD your God.
</p>

<p>
v29: Anyone who does not deny themselves on that very day will be cut off from their people.
v30: And anyone who does any work on that very day—I will destroy that person from among their people.
v31: Do no work at all. This is a lasting rule for your generations, wherever you live.
v32: It is a Sabbath of complete rest for you, and you must deny yourselves. Observe your Sabbath from the evening of the ninth day of the month until the next evening.”
</p>

## The Festival of Booths

<p>
v33: The LORD said to Moses,
v34: “Tell the Israelites: Starting on the fifteenth day of this seventh month, celebrate the Festival of Booths to the LORD for seven days.
v35: Hold a sacred assembly on the first day. Do none of your ordinary work.
v36: For seven days, bring offerings by fire to the LORD. On the eighth day, hold a sacred assembly and bring an offering by fire to the LORD. It is a solemn gathering; do none of your ordinary work.
</p>

<p>
v37: These are the LORD’s appointed times, which you are to announce as sacred assemblies. Bring offerings by fire to the LORD: burnt offerings, grain offerings, sacrifices and drink offerings, each on its proper day.
v38: These are in addition to the LORD’s Sabbaths, in addition to your gifts, in addition to all your vow offerings, and in addition to all your freewill offerings that you give the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v39: On the fifteenth day of the seventh month, after you gather the land’s produce, celebrate the LORD’s festival for seven days. The first day is a day of rest, and so is the eighth.
v40: On the first day, take fruit from splendid trees, palm fronds, branches of leafy trees and willows from the streams. Rejoice before the LORD your God for seven days.
v41: Celebrate this festival to the LORD for seven days each year. It is a lasting rule for your generations. Celebrate it in the seventh month.
</p>

<p>
v42: Live in booths for seven days. Everyone native-born in Israel is to live in booths,
v43: so your generations will know that I made the Israelites live in booths when I brought them out of Egypt. I am the LORD your God.”
</p>

<p>
v44: Moses told the Israelites about the LORD’s appointed times.
</p>

## Notes

v05: “At twilight” renders “between the evenings,” an expression also used in Exodus 12:6. Its precise span is disputed; the translation does not assign a modern clock time.

v07: “Ordinary work” represents a longer Hebrew expression than “work” alone. This distinction recurs through the calendar; the Sabbath and Day of Atonement prohibit all work (verses 3, 28–32).

v10–16: Omer can name a sheaf or a measured portion of grain. “Sheaf” is used here. The identity of “the Sabbath” from which the count begins is disputed: readers have understood the weekly Sabbath or a festival rest day. The wording keeps that question open.

v17: These loaves are explicitly baked with leaven. The instruction should not be changed to match the unleavened bread in verse 6 or the altar restriction in 2:11.

v20: The Hebrew repeats the two lambs after the pronoun “them.” The precise range of that pronoun and the manner of presenting bread and animals together are not fully explained.

v24: Teruah denotes a loud signal, such as a shout or an instrumental blast. This verse does not name the instrument; 25:9 explicitly names a shofar, or horn.

v27–32: “Deny yourselves” retains the expression used in 16:29–31. It is associated with fasting, but the Hebrew does not simply use the verb “fast.” Verse 29 says “cut off”; verse 30 separately says that God will “destroy” the person. The evening of the ninth begins the observance of the tenth day.

v40: The first item is literally “fruit of a tree of splendor.” The exact species is not named. This verse directs the people to take these plants and rejoice; it does not explicitly say to build the booths from them.

## Vocabulary

v02: moed (מוֹעֵד)
An appointed time or meeting. The repeated expression organizes the chapter around times set apart for Israel to gather.

v11: tenufah (תְּנוּפָה)
A wave offering: something presented before the LORD. Keep the term connected with its uses in Leviticus 7–10.

v13: ephah / hin (אֵיפָה / הִין)
Measures for dry goods and liquids. The verse names two tenth-portions of flour and a quarter-hin of wine; “of an ephah” supplies the understood dry measure.

v34: sukkot (סֻכּוֹת)
Booths or temporary shelters. The festival’s name connects the act of living in booths with remembering the departure from Egypt.
