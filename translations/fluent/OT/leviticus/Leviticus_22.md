## Holy food and uncleanness

<p>
v01: The LORD said to Moses,
v02: “Tell Aaron and his sons to keep away from the holy offerings the Israelites consecrate to me, so that they do not profane my holy name. I am the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v03: Tell them: ‘Throughout your generations, any man among your descendants who approaches the holy offerings the Israelites consecrate to the LORD while he is unclean will be cut off from my presence. I am the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v04: No descendant of Aaron with defiling skin disease or a discharge may eat the holy offerings until he is clean. If a man touches anything unclean through contact with the dead, or has an emission of semen,
v05: or touches a swarming creature that makes him unclean, or a person who makes him unclean, whatever that person's uncleanness,
v06: he is unclean until evening and must not eat the holy offerings unless he bathes his body in water.
v07: When the sun sets, he will be clean. Then he may eat the holy offerings, because they are his food.
</p>

<p>
v08: He must not eat an animal that died on its own or was torn by wild animals, making himself unclean through it. I am the LORD.
v09: The priests must keep the charge I have given them, so that they do not bear sin and die for profaning the holy food. I am the LORD who makes them holy.
</p>

## Who may eat holy food

<p>
v10: No one outside the priestly household may eat holy food. Neither a lodger staying with a priest nor a hired worker may eat it.
v11: But a person the priest buys as a slave with his own money may eat it. Those born in his household may also eat his food.
</p>

<p>
v12: If a priest's daughter marries a man outside the priestly household, she may not eat the contributions from the holy offerings.
v13: But if she is widowed or divorced, has no offspring, and returns to her father's house as in her youth, she may eat her father's food. No one outside the priestly household may eat it.
</p>

<p>
v14: Anyone who eats holy food by mistake must repay it to the priest, adding one-fifth of its value.
</p>

<p>
v15: The priests must not profane the holy offerings the Israelites contribute to the LORD,
v16: and so cause the people to bear guilt and its penalty when they eat their holy offerings, for I am the LORD who makes them holy.’”
</p>

## Acceptable offerings

<p>
v17: The LORD said to Moses,
v18: “Speak to Aaron, his sons, and all the Israelites. Tell them: ‘When anyone from the house of Israel or any foreigner residing in Israel presents a burnt offering to the LORD, whether to fulfill a vow or as a freewill offering,
v19: it must be a male without blemish from the cattle, sheep, or goats to be accepted on your behalf.
</p>

<p>
v20: Do not present an animal with a blemish, because it will not be accepted on your behalf.
</p>

<p>
v21: When someone offers a peace sacrifice to the LORD from the herd or flock, in fulfillment of a vow or as a freewill offering, it must be without blemish to be accepted. It must have no blemish at all.
</p>

<p>
v22: Do not present to the LORD an animal that is blind, has a broken bone or a mutilated part, or has a growth, itching sores, or a skin eruption. Do not put any of these on the altar as an offering by fire to the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v23: You may offer an animal from the cattle, sheep, or goats with an overlong or stunted limb as a freewill offering, but it will not be accepted in fulfillment of a vow.
</p>

<p>
v24: Do not offer to the LORD an animal whose testicles are bruised, crushed, torn away, or cut off. You must not do this in your land.
</p>

<p>
v25: Do not accept any such animals from a foreigner to present as the food of your God. They are damaged and have a blemish; they will not be accepted on your behalf.’”
</p>

## Mothers, young animals, and thanksgiving

<p>
v26: The LORD said to Moses,
v27: “When a calf, lamb, or kid is born, it must stay with its mother for seven days. From the eighth day onward, it may be accepted as an offering by fire to the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v28: Whether from the cattle, sheep, or goats, do not slaughter an animal and its young on the same day.
</p>

<p>
v29: When you offer a thanksgiving sacrifice to the LORD, offer it in a way that will be accepted on your behalf.
v30: Eat it that same day. Do not leave any of it until morning. I am the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v31: Keep my commandments and carry them out. I am the LORD.
v32: Do not profane my holy name. I am to be held holy among the Israelites. I am the LORD who makes you holy,
v33: who brought you out of Egypt to be your God. I am the LORD.”
</p>

## Notes

v2–7: The instruction to keep away from the offerings is explained by the following rules about uncleanness, washing, sunset and permission to eat.

v4–6: The list is compressed. This rendering includes a man who has an emission of semen; another reading includes someone who touches that man. The closing verse resumes the language of touching.

v9: The Hebrew uses singular “it” after “bear sin” and “profane.” Holy food is understood here; the preceding charge is another possible referent.

v10–13: “Outside the priestly household” describes eligibility for this food, not foreign nationality. “Lodger” may describe a resident guest or a dependent worker; the purchased person in verse 11 is a separate category.

v15–16: The Hebrew does not name the subject. This rendering takes the priests as responsible for others' eating; it can also be read as warning the eaters themselves. The final “them” may refer to people or holy offerings.

v21, 23: The vow expression may concern making or fulfilling a special vow. Verse 23 explicitly allows two limb conditions for a freewill offering while excluding them for a vow.

v22–24: The bodily terms describe conditions without providing exact diagnoses. The four injuries in verse 24 concern the testicles, understood from the wording and context.

v25: “Such animals” limits the prohibition: it does not reject all offerings obtained from foreigners.

v27–30: Seven days with the mother precede eligibility from the eighth day onward. The next rule forbids slaughtering parent and young on one day. Thanksgiving meat must be eaten that day, unlike the two-day allowance in 19:6.

## Vocabulary

v3, 9, 32–33: קָדַשׁ (qadash)
Consecrate or make holy. People consecrate offerings; the LORD makes the people holy and is to be held holy among them.

v4: צָרַעַת (tsaraat)
The ritual category rendered “defiling skin disease” in chapter 13. It is not a diagnosis of one modern disease.

v10, 12–13, 18, 25: זָר / גֵּר / בֶּן־נֵכָר (zar / ger / ben-nekhar)
Outsider / resident foreigner / foreigner. Context determines each term's scope; the first concerns priestly food eligibility here.

v19–25: תָּמִים / מוּם (tamim / mum)
Whole or without blemish / blemish. These terms recur in the conditions for acceptable animals.

v18, 21, 23: נֶדֶר / נְדָבָה (neder / nedavah)
Vow / freewill offering. The passage distinguishes an offering tied to a vow from one given freely.
