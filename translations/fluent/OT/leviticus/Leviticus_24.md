## Oil for the lamps

<p>
v01: The LORD said to Moses,
v02: “Command the Israelites to bring you pure oil from beaten olives for the light, so the lamp can be kept burning regularly.
v03: Aaron is to tend it before the LORD from evening until morning, outside the curtain of the testimony in the tent of meeting. This is a lasting rule for your generations.
v04: He is to tend the lamps on the pure lampstand before the LORD regularly.
</p>

## Bread before the LORD

<p>
v05: Take fine flour and bake twelve loaves, using two-tenths of an ephah for each loaf.
v06: Set them in two arrangements, six in each, on the pure table before the LORD.
v07: Put pure frankincense with each arrangement. It will serve as the bread’s memorial portion, an offering by fire to the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v08: Sabbath after Sabbath, Aaron is to arrange the bread before the LORD regularly. It comes from the Israelites as an everlasting covenant.
v09: The bread belongs to Aaron and his sons. They are to eat it in a holy place, for it is most holy, his lasting share of the LORD’s offerings by fire.”
</p>

## A curse in the camp

<p>
v10: A man whose mother was an Israelite and whose father was an Egyptian went out among the Israelites. In the camp, he and an Israelite man began fighting.
v11: The Israelite woman’s son spoke the Name in blasphemy and cursed. They brought him to Moses. His mother was Shelomith, daughter of Dibri, from the tribe of Dan.
v12: They kept him in custody until the LORD’s own word would make the decision clear to them.
</p>

<p>
v13: The LORD said to Moses,
v14: “Take the man who cursed outside the camp. Everyone who heard him is to lay their hands on his head. Then the whole assembly is to stone him.
</p>

<p>
v15: Tell the Israelites: Anyone who curses his God will bear the guilt of his sin.
v16: Anyone who blasphemes the LORD’s name must be put to death. The whole assembly must stone him. Foreigner and native-born alike must be put to death for blaspheming the Name.
</p>

## Life and injury

<p>
v17: Anyone who strikes a human being dead must be put to death.
v18: Anyone who strikes an animal dead must replace it, life for life.
v19: If someone causes a bodily injury to another person, what he has done must be done to him.
v20: Fracture for fracture, eye for eye, tooth for tooth: the injury he caused another person must be inflicted on him.
</p>

<p>
v21: Whoever strikes an animal dead must replace it; whoever strikes a human being dead must be put to death.
v22: You are to have one rule for foreigner and native-born alike. I am the LORD your God.”
</p>

<p>
v23: Moses told the Israelites, and they took the man who had cursed outside the camp and stoned him. The Israelites did as the LORD had commanded Moses.
</p>

## Notes

v03–04: The testimony recalls the covenant tablets behind the curtain (Exodus 25:16, 21). “Pure” describes the lampstand here and the table in verse 6; the Hebrew does not add “gold” in these verses.

v06–07: The bread is placed in two arrangements; the term does not settle whether they are rows or stacks. The frankincense serves as the memorial portion, while the bread is eaten by the priests (verse 9).

v11, 16: The verb naqav can refer to naming or pronouncing, and its force here is disputed. “Spoke the Name in blasphemy” reads it in connection with the accompanying curse. The wording does not specify whether he cursed God directly or invoked the divine name in a curse.

v15: The Hebrew says “his God,” which may also be understood as “his god.” Verse 16 explicitly names the LORD. The possessive is retained rather than silently making the two formulations identical.

v19–22: The text states matching bodily injury and then repeats the distinction between replacing an animal and the death penalty for killing a human. The translation retains those formulations rather than replacing them with a later interpretation of how restitution was administered. “One rule” concerns the foreigner and native-born within this legal setting.

## Vocabulary

v07: azkarah (אַזְכָּרָה)
Memorial portion. In the grain-offering instructions it is the portion offered by fire; here frankincense represents the bread before the LORD.

v08: berit (בְּרִית)
Covenant: a binding relationship or commitment. Here the regularly presented bread is linked with an everlasting covenant from Israel.

v11: ha-shem (הַשֵּׁם)
The Name. In this account it refers to the divine name, made explicit as the LORD’s name in verse 16.

v19: mum (מוּם)
Bodily defect or injury. The same word translated “physical blemish” in chapter 21 here describes harm inflicted on another person.
