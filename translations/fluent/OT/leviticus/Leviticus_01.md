## The LORD speaks from the tent

<p>
v01: The LORD called to Moses and spoke to him from the tent of meeting:
v02: “Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When any of you brings an offering to the LORD from your livestock, bring your offering from the herd or the flock.
</p>

## A burnt offering from the herd

<p>
v03: If the offering is a burnt offering from the herd, the person bringing it must present a male without defect. They must present it at the entrance to the tent of meeting, for acceptance before the LORD.
v04: They are to lay a hand on the burnt offering’s head, and it will be accepted on their behalf to make atonement for them.
</p>

<p>
v05: They must slaughter the bull before the LORD. Aaron’s sons, the priests, are to present the blood and splash it against all sides of the altar at the entrance to the tent of meeting.
v06: The person bringing the burnt offering is to skin it and cut it into pieces.
v07: The sons of Aaron the priest are to put fire on the altar and arrange wood on the fire.
v08: Aaron’s sons, the priests, are to arrange the pieces, the head, and the fat on the wood over the fire on the altar.
v09: The person bringing the offering must wash its entrails and legs with water. The priest is to burn all of it on the altar as a burnt offering, an offering by fire with a pleasing aroma to the LORD.
</p>

## A burnt offering from the flock

<p>
v10: If the burnt offering comes from the flock, from sheep or goats, the person bringing it must present a male without defect.
v11: They must slaughter it on the north side of the altar before the LORD. Aaron’s sons, the priests, are to splash its blood against all sides of the altar.
v12: The person bringing the offering is to cut it into pieces. The priest must arrange these, together with the head and fat, on the wood over the fire on the altar.
v13: The person bringing the offering must wash the entrails and legs with water. The priest is to present all of it and burn it on the altar. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire with a pleasing aroma to the LORD.
</p>

## A burnt offering of birds

<p>
v14: If the burnt offering brought to the LORD is a bird, the offering must be a turtledove or a young pigeon.
v15: The priest is to bring it to the altar, wring off its head, and burn the head on the altar. Its blood is to be drained against the side of the altar.
v16: He is to remove its crop with its contents and throw it beside the altar on the east side, where the ashes are placed.
v17: The priest is to tear the bird open by its wings without separating it completely. Then the priest must burn it on the altar, on the wood over the fire. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire with a pleasing aroma to the LORD.”
</p>

## Notes

v1–2: The LORD speaks from the tent that was filled by his glory at the end of Exodus. The instructions continue through chapter 3; chapter divisions and quotation marks are reading aids.

v3–4: The Hebrew phrase in verse 3 concerns acceptance; its pronoun may refer to the offering or the person presenting it. Verse 4 links the accepted offering with atonement on that person’s behalf.

v5–9: The Hebrew distinguishes the person preparing the animal from the priests handling blood and the altar. Some ancient versions use plural verbs for preparation. This draft follows the Hebrew text’s singular verbs.

v15–16: The head is burned before the bird’s body is prepared. The anatomy in verse 16 is uncertain: crop with contents, crop with feathers, or entrails removed at the tail feathers. See the [NET translator notes](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+1&version=NET).

## Vocabulary

v2: qorbān — offering
Something brought forward as a gift; the related verb recurs in the instructions for presenting it.

v3: ʿōlāh — burnt offering
An offering that goes up in smoke on the altar. The preparations differ for cattle, sheep or goats, and birds.

v4: kipper — make atonement
Ritual action addressing a barrier in the relationship with God; the passage does not give a full theory of how it works.

v9: ʾiššeh — offering by fire
A traditional rendering; also understood as a food gift. The distinct food image is explicit in 3:11,16.

v9: nîḥōaḥ — pleasing
Describes the offering’s aroma. The repeated phrase helps mark the close of each animal category.
