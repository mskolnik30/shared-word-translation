## Bringing slaughtered animals to the LORD

<p>
v01: The LORD spoke to Moses:
v02: “Speak to Aaron, his sons, and all the Israelites. Tell them: This is what the LORD has commanded:
v03: If anyone of the house of Israel slaughters an ox, a sheep, or a goat, either inside or outside the camp,
v04: but does not bring it to the entrance to the tent of meeting to present it as an offering to the LORD before his tabernacle, that person is guilty of bloodshed. They have shed blood and must be cut off from their people.
</p>

<p>
v05: This is so the Israelites will bring the sacrifices they offer in the open country to the LORD, to the priest at the entrance to the tent of meeting, and offer them there as peace offerings to the LORD.
v06: The priest must splash the blood against the LORD's altar at the entrance to the tent of meeting and burn the fat as a pleasing aroma to the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v07: They must no longer offer their sacrifices to goat demons, prostituting themselves by going after them. This is a lasting statute for them throughout their generations.
</p>

## Offerings at the tent of meeting

<p>
v08: Tell them also: If anyone of the house of Israel, or any foreigner living among them, offers a burnt offering or a sacrifice,
v09: but does not bring it to the entrance to the tent of meeting to offer it to the LORD, that person must be cut off from their people.
</p>

## Blood and life

<p>
v10: If anyone of the house of Israel, or any foreigner living among them, eats any blood, I will set my face against that person who eats blood and cut them off from their people.
v11: For the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I myself have given it to you on the altar to make atonement for your lives. It is the blood that makes atonement through the life it carries.
</p>

<p>
v12: That is why I have told the Israelites, ‘None of you may eat blood, and no foreigner living among you may eat blood.’
</p>

<p>
v13: If any Israelite or any foreigner living among them hunts a wild animal or bird that may be eaten, they must pour out its blood and cover it with earth.
v14: For the life of every creature is its blood, which carries its life. So I have told the Israelites, ‘You must not eat the blood of any creature, because the life of every creature is its blood. Anyone who eats it must be cut off.’
</p>

## Eating an animal found dead

<p>
v15: Anyone, whether native-born or a foreigner, who eats an animal that died on its own or was torn by wild animals must wash their clothes and bathe in water. They remain unclean until evening; then they will be clean.
v16: But if they do not wash their clothes and do not bathe their body, they must bear their guilt.”
</p>

## Notes

v3–5: The wording names the slaughter of oxen, sheep, and goats, then requires bringing them as offerings. Its scope is debated: all slaughter of these animals or specifically sacrificial slaughter. This translation leaves the opening wording broad.

v4, 10: “Cut off” is a severe penalty whose precise form is not specified here. Verse 10 explicitly names the LORD as its agent.

v7: The word for “goat demons” is also used for male goats. Here it names recipients of forbidden sacrifice. Prostitution is the passage’s metaphor for pursuing them.

v11: “Through the life it carries” takes the final phrase as explaining the means of atonement. Another reading understands blood as the price or exchange for a life. The Hebrew does not settle a complete theory of atonement here.

v14: The opening Hebrew clause repeats “life” in a compressed construction. Both this clause and the following prohibition retain the close identification of blood and life.

v15–16: These verses state the consequences of eating the two kinds of carcass and of failing to wash. “Bear their guilt” includes responsibility for its consequences, as in 5:1, 17.

## Vocabulary

v5: שְׁלָמִים (shelamim)
Peace offerings, also called well-being offerings; see chapter 3.

v10–15: נֶפֶשׁ (nefesh)
Life or person. The same Hebrew word refers to the eater, the animal’s life, and the human lives for which atonement is made.

v11, 14: דָּם (dam)
Blood. The repeated blood-and-life language also recalls Genesis 9:4–6.

v15: נְבֵלָה / טְרֵפָה (nevelah / terefah)
An animal that died on its own / an animal torn by predators. The two categories remain distinct.
