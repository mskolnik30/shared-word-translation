## Job states his case

<p>
v01: I made a covenant with my eyes;
how, then, could I gaze at a young virgin?
v02: What portion would God above allot me,
what inheritance would the Almighty send from on high?
v03: Is not disaster for the unjust,
and calamity for those who do wrong?
v04: Does he not see my ways
and count every step I take?
v05: If I have walked with falsehood
and my feet have hurried toward deceit—
v06: let God weigh me on honest scales
and recognize my integrity—
v07: if my steps have strayed from the path,
if my heart has followed my eyes,
or any stain has clung to my hands,
v08: then let another eat what I sow,
and let what grows for me be uprooted.
</p>

<p>
v09: If my heart has been enticed by a woman
and I have lurked at my neighbor’s door,
v10: then let my wife grind for another man,
and let other men bend over her.
v11: For that would be an outrage,
an offense calling for judgment.
v12: It would be a fire consuming as far as Abaddon,
uprooting everything I produce.
</p>

## One maker for servant and master

<p>
v13: If I rejected the just claim of my male or female slave
when they brought a case against me,
v14: what would I do when God rose up?
What answer could I give when he called me to account?
v15: Did not the one who made me in the womb make him too?
Did not the same one form us in the womb?
</p>

<p>
v16: If I withheld what the poor desired
or let a widow’s eyes fail,
v17: if I ate my bread alone
and did not share it with the orphan—
v18: though from my youth I raised him like a father,
and from my mother’s womb I guided the widow—
v19: if I saw someone perishing for lack of clothing,
a needy person with nothing to cover him,
v20: if his body did not bless me
for warming him with wool from my sheep,
v21: if I raised my hand against an orphan
because I knew I had support at the gate,
v22: then let my shoulder fall from its socket
and my arm break from its joint!
v23: For God’s disaster terrified me;
I could not stand before his majesty.
</p>

## If I put my trust in wealth

<p>
v24: If I made gold my confidence
or said to fine gold, ‘You are my security,’
v25: if I rejoiced because my wealth was great,
because my hands had gained so much,
v26: if I looked at the shining sun
or the moon moving in its splendor,
v27: and my heart was secretly enticed,
and my mouth kissed my hand in homage,
v28: that too would be an offense calling for judgment,
for I would have denied God above.
</p>

<p>
v29: If I rejoiced at my enemy’s ruin
or exulted when evil overtook him—
v30: I have not let my mouth sin
by calling down a curse on his life—
v31: if the people of my tent did not say,
‘Who has not had his fill of Job’s meat?’—
v32: no stranger spent the night outside;
I opened my doors to the traveler—
v33: if I concealed my rebellion as people do,
hiding my wrongdoing in my bosom,
v34: because I feared the crowd
and the clans’ contempt terrified me,
so that I kept silent and did not go out the door—
</p>

## Let the Almighty answer me

<p>
v35: if only someone would hear me!
Here is my signature; let the Almighty answer me!
Let my accuser put his indictment in writing!
v36: I would carry it on my shoulder
and bind it on myself like a crown.
v37: I would account for every step I have taken;
I would approach him like a prince.
</p>

<p>
v38: If my land cried out against me
and its furrows wept together,
v39: if I ate its yield without paying
or caused its owners to breathe their last,
v40: then let thorns grow instead of wheat
and foul weeds instead of barley.”
The words of Job were ended.
</p>

## Notes

v08: Job’s “growth” may include his crops or his descendants.

v10: Grinding for another man evokes servitude; the men bending over Job’s wife also carry a sexual threat. The curse’s harm to her is not softened.

v18: The Hebrew shifts between a male and a female object, understood as the orphan and widow. Caring for them from the womb is Job’s expansive claim, not a literal chronology.

v27: The Hebrew literally says that Job’s hand kissed his mouth. The draft describes the same gesture as kissing his hand in homage toward the heavenly lights.

v33: “Like other people” can also be read “like Adam.”

v35: The word for signature is a mark, taw. The passage does not give it a later religious symbolism.

v39: The final expression can mean causing the land’s owners to die or causing them anguish.

## Vocabulary

v13: Slave
Job explicitly names both a male slave and a female slave as people with a claim against him.

v15: One maker
Job grounds his obligation in their shared creation in the womb.
