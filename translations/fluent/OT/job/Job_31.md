## I Made a Covenant with My Eyes

<p>
v01: “I made a covenant with my eyes;
how then could I gaze upon a young woman?

v02: For what is the portion from God above,
or the inheritance from the Almighty on high?

v03: Is not calamity for the unjust
and disaster for workers of harm?

v04: Does he not see my ways
and count all my steps?
</p>

## Let God Weigh Me in Honest Scales

<p>
v05: If I have walked with falsehood
or my foot hurried toward deceit,

v06: let him weigh me in honest scales,
and let God know my integrity.

v07: If my step has turned from the way,
if my heart has gone after my eyes,
or if any stain has clung to my hands,

v08: then let me sow and another eat,
and let my crops be uprooted.
</p>

## If My Heart Was Enticed by a Woman

<p>
v09: If my heart was enticed by a woman,
or I lay in wait at my neighbor’s door,

v10: then let my wife grind for another,
and let others bow down over her.

v11: For that would be a disgraceful thing;
it would be guilt for the judges.

v12: For it is a fire that consumes as far as Abaddon,
and it would uproot all my produce.
</p>

## If I Rejected the Case of My Servants

<p>
v13: If I rejected the case of my male servant or my female servant
when they contended with me,

v14: what then would I do when God rises?
When he visits, what would I answer him?

v15: Did not the one who made me in the womb make him?
Did not one form us both in the womb?
</p>

## If I Withheld from the Poor

<p>
v16: If I withheld the poor from their desire,
or caused the eyes of the widow to fail,

v17: or ate my morsel alone
and the fatherless did not eat from it—

v18: for from my youth he grew up with me as with a father,
and from my mother’s womb I guided the widow—

v19: if I saw anyone perishing for lack of clothing,
or a needy person without covering,

v20: if his loins did not bless me,
and he was not warmed with the fleece of my sheep,

v21: if I raised my hand against the fatherless
because I saw my help in the gate,

v22: then let my shoulder fall from its socket,
and let my arm be broken from its joint.

v23: For calamity from God was terror to me,
and because of his majesty I could not do such things.
</p>

## If I Made Gold My Confidence

<p>
v24: If I made gold my confidence
or said to fine gold, ‘You are my security,’

v25: if I rejoiced because my wealth was great
and because my hand had found much,

v26: if I saw the sun when it shone
or the moon walking in splendor,

v27: and my heart was secretly enticed
and my hand kissed my mouth,

v28: this also would be guilt for the judges,
for I would have denied God above.
</p>

## If I Rejoiced at My Enemy’s Ruin

<p>
v29: If I rejoiced at the ruin of the one who hated me
or exulted when evil found him—

v30: I have not allowed my mouth to sin
by asking for his life with a curse—

v31: if the men of my tent have not said,
‘Who has not been satisfied with his meat?’

v32: The resident alien did not spend the night outside;
I opened my doors to the traveler.
</p>

## If I Covered My Transgressions

<p>
v33: If I covered my transgressions like Adam,
hiding my guilt in my bosom,

v34: because I feared a great crowd
and the contempt of clans terrified me,
so that I kept silent
and did not go outside—
</p>

## Let the Almighty Answer Me

<p>
v35: If only I had someone to hear me!
Look, here is my mark.
Let the Almighty answer me!
Let my accuser write the indictment!

v36: Surely I would carry it on my shoulder;
I would bind it upon myself like a crown.

v37: I would declare to him the number of my steps;
like a prince I would approach him.
</p>

## If My Land Has Cried Out Against Me

<p>
v38: If my land has cried out against me
and its furrows have wept together,

v39: if I have eaten its strength without payment
or caused the life of its owners to breathe out,

v40: then let thorns grow instead of wheat,
and stinkweed instead of barley.”

The words of Job are ended.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Job’s “covenant with my eyes” is a self-imposed discipline concerning sexual desire. His oath moves from inward gaze to outward conduct.

v05–06: Job invites divine examination in “honest scales,” reversing the friends’ claim that his suffering already proves guilt.

v13–15: Job grounds fair treatment of servants in shared creation: master and servant were both formed by the same God in the womb. This is a striking equality claim within an ancient household structure.

v16–23: Job directly answers the social accusations of Eliphaz. He claims sustained care for poor, widow, fatherless, and inadequately clothed people.

v26–28: Gesturing worshipfully toward sun or moon is treated as denial of God. Job denies hidden astral worship as well as visible idolatry.

v29–30: Job denies rejoicing over enemies or cursing them to death, broadening integrity beyond public justice into response toward adversaries.

v33: The phrase “like Adam” may mean “like a human” or refer specifically to Adam. TSW retains the traditional personal name while acknowledging the lexical possibility.

v35: “my mark” — Hebrew *tav* can mean a mark or signature. Job effectively signs his legal deposition and demands an answering indictment.

v38–40: Even land and labor relations are included in Job’s oath. Exploiting landholders or workers would cause the earth itself to testify against him.

## Vocabulary

v01: בְּרִית (berit)
“Covenant.” Job describes a binding commitment made with his eyes.

v06: מֹאזְנֵי־צֶדֶק (mozenei-tsedeq)
“Honest scales,” literally “scales of righteousness.” The standard Job asks God to use.

v11: זִמָּה (zimmah)
“Disgraceful thing” or “wicked plan.” Used of adultery.

v15: כּוּן (kun)
“To form” or “establish.” The same God forms master and servant in the womb.

v24: כֶּסֶל (kesel)
“Confidence” or “security.” Job denies placing ultimate trust in gold.

v35: תָּו (tav)
“Mark” or “signature.” Job’s legal sign demanding an answer.
