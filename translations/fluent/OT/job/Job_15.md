## Eliphaz: Your own mouth condemns you

<p>
v01: Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered:
v02: “Should a wise man answer with windy knowledge
and fill his belly with the east wind?
v03: Should he argue with useless talk,
with words that do no good?
v04: You are undermining reverence
and diminishing meditation before God.
v05: Your wrongdoing teaches your mouth what to say;
you choose the tongue of the shrewd.
v06: Your own mouth condemns you, not I;
your lips testify against you.
</p>

<p>
v07: Were you the first human born?
Were you brought forth before the hills?
v08: Have you listened in on God’s council?
Have you kept wisdom for yourself?
v09: What do you know that we do not know?
What do you understand that we do not?
v10: Among us are the gray-haired and the aged,
older in days than your father.
v11: Are God’s consolations too little for you,
and words spoken gently to you?
v12: Why does your heart carry you away?
Why do your eyes flash,
v13: that you turn your spirit against God
and let such words out of your mouth?
</p>

<p>
v14: What is a mortal, that he could be pure,
one born of woman, that he could be righteous?
v15: God does not trust even his holy ones;
the heavens are not pure in his eyes.
v16: How much less one who is detestable and corrupt,
a man who drinks injustice like water!
</p>

## The fate of the wicked

<p>
v17: I will tell you; listen to me.
I will recount what I have seen,
v18: what the wise have handed down
without hiding what came from their ancestors,
v19: to whom alone the land was given,
with no foreigner passing among them.
</p>

<p>
v20: The wicked man writhes all his days,
through all the years stored up for the ruthless.
v21: Terrifying sounds fill his ears;
in a time of peace a destroyer comes upon him.
v22: He does not believe he will return from darkness;
he is marked out for the sword.
v23: He wanders in search of bread: ‘Where is it?’
He knows a day of darkness is close at hand.
v24: Distress and anguish terrify him;
they overpower him like a king ready for battle,
v25: because he stretched out his hand against God
and defied the Almighty,
v26: charging against him with a stiff neck,
with the thick bosses of his shields.
v27: Though he has covered his face with fat
and gathered fat around his waist,
v28: he lives in destroyed cities,
in houses no one should inhabit,
places destined to become rubble.
v29: He will not remain rich; his wealth will not endure,
and his possessions will not spread over the land.
v30: He will not escape the darkness.
A flame will dry up his shoots,
and at the breath of God’s mouth he will depart.
v31: Let him not deceive himself by trusting emptiness,
for emptiness will be his reward.
v32: It will be paid in full before his time,
and his palm branch will not be green.
v33: He will shed his unripe grapes like a vine
and cast off his blossom like an olive tree.
v34: For the company of the godless is barren,
and fire consumes the tents of bribery.
v35: They conceive trouble and give birth to wrongdoing;
their womb prepares deceit.”
</p>

## Notes

v12: The verb describing the eyes is rare; flashing or signaling with them are possible senses.

v19: Eliphaz invokes ancestral authority and the absence of foreigners. The claim belongs to his argument.

v27: The thickened body pictures wealth and security in the description of the wicked man.

v29: The last phrase concerns possessions or their spread; its precise wording is uncertain.

## Vocabulary

v05: Crafty
Eliphaz accuses Job of using calculated, evasive speech.
