## Job: How long will you crush me?

<p>
v01: Then Job answered:
v02: “How long will you torment me
and crush me with words?
v03: Ten times now you have shamed me;
you are not ashamed to mistreat me.
v04: Even if I really have gone wrong,
my error remains with me.
v05: If you really exalt yourselves over me
and use my disgrace as evidence against me,
v06: then know that God has wronged me
and drawn his net around me.
</p>

## God has closed my way

<p>
v07: I cry out, ‘Violence!’ but receive no answer.
I call for help, but there is no justice.
v08: He has walled up my way so I cannot pass
and put darkness over my paths.
v09: He has stripped away my honor
and removed the crown from my head.
v10: He tears me down on every side, and I am gone;
he uproots my hope like a tree.
v11: His anger burns against me;
he counts me among his enemies.
v12: His troops advance together,
build their siege road against me,
and camp around my tent.
</p>

<p>
v13: He has put my brothers far from me;
my acquaintances have become strangers to me.
v14: My relatives have failed me;
my close friends have forgotten me.
v15: Those staying in my house and my female slaves count me a stranger;
in their eyes I am a foreigner.
v16: I call my servant, but he does not answer,
though I plead with him with my own mouth.
v17: My breath is repulsive to my wife;
I am loathsome to my own family.
v18: Even little children despise me;
when I rise, they speak against me.
v19: All my intimate friends abhor me;
those I loved have turned against me.
v20: My bones cling to my skin and flesh;
I have escaped by the skin of my teeth.
</p>

<p>
v21: Have pity on me, have pity on me, my friends!
The hand of God has touched me.
v22: Why do you pursue me as God does?
Why are you not satisfied with my flesh?
</p>

## I know that my redeemer lives

<p>
v23: If only my words were written down!
If only they were inscribed in a record,
v24: with an iron stylus and lead,
carved in rock forever!
v25: But I know that my redeemer lives,
and at the last he will stand upon the dust.
v26: After my skin has been destroyed like this,
from my flesh I will see God,
v27: whom I myself will see on my behalf;
my own eyes will see, not a stranger’s.
My heart is spent with longing within me!
v28: If you say, ‘How we will pursue him!’
and the root of the matter is found in me,
v29: then fear the sword yourselves,
for wrath brings punishments by the sword,
so that you may know there is judgment.”
</p>

## Notes

v17: “My own family” renders “sons of my womb,” an unusual expression for close kin. It can also be understood as Job’s own children, which creates a tension with the opening narrative.

v20: The expression “skin of my teeth” is obscure. Its vivid image of barely surviving is preserved.

v25–27: The redeemer is a defender or vindicator, but the poem does not name this figure. Standing at last upon the dust and seeing God “from my flesh” permit different understandings of timing and bodily life. “From” can be understood as from within or apart from the flesh. The draft does not settle these questions through later doctrine.

v28: The pinned Hebrew says “in me,” not “in him.” The change of person is retained in Job’s report of his opponents’ accusation.

v27: “Not a stranger” can contrast Job’s own seeing with another person’s, or describe seeing God no longer as a stranger. The draft follows the first reading.

v29: The final expression is difficult but concerns judgment. The warning retains the relation between wrath, offenses, and the sword.

## Vocabulary

v25: Redeemer
A person who acts to reclaim, protect, or vindicate another; here a hoped-for defender of Job’s case.

v27: Heart
The Hebrew names the kidneys, a bodily image for deep feeling and longing.
