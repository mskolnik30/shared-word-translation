## Job: Listen to what I am saying

<p>
v01: Then Job answered:
v02: “Listen closely to my words;
let that be the comfort you give me.
v03: Bear with me while I speak;
after I have spoken, you may mock.
v04: Is my complaint directed at a human?
Why should I not lose patience?
v05: Look at me and be appalled;
put your hand over your mouth.
v06: When I remember, I am dismayed,
and trembling seizes my flesh.
</p>

## Why do the wicked live on?

<p>
v07: Why do the wicked live on,
grow old, and become mighty in wealth?
v08: Their descendants are established in their presence,
their offspring before their eyes.
v09: Their houses are safe from fear;
God’s rod is not upon them.
v10: Their bull breeds without fail;
their cow gives birth and does not miscarry.
v11: They send their little ones out like a flock,
and their children dance.
v12: They sing to tambourine and lyre
and rejoice at the sound of the pipe.
v13: They finish their days in prosperity
and go down to Sheol in a moment.
v14: Yet they say to God, ‘Leave us alone!
We do not want to know your ways.
v15: What is the Almighty, that we should serve him?
What do we gain by appealing to him?’
v16: Their prosperity is not in their own hands.
The counsel of the wicked is far from me.
</p>

## How often does disaster reach them?

<p>
v17: How often is the lamp of the wicked put out?
How often does disaster come upon them,
or does God allot them suffering in his anger?
v18: How often are they like straw before the wind,
like chaff a storm carries away?
v19: ‘God stores up a man’s punishment for his children,’ you say.
Let him repay the man himself, so he knows it!
v20: Let his own eyes see his destruction;
let him drink the Almighty’s wrath.
v21: What does he care for his household after him,
when the number of his months has been cut off?
</p>

<p>
v22: Can anyone teach God knowledge,
when he judges even those on high?
v23: One person dies in full strength,
entirely at ease and secure,
v24: his vessels full of milk,
his bones moist with marrow.
v25: another dies with a bitter soul,
never having tasted good.
v26: Together they lie in the dust,
and worms cover them both.
</p>

## Your answers are empty

<p>
v27: I know your thoughts
and the schemes by which you wrong me.
v28: You ask, ‘Where is the nobleman’s house?
Where is the tent in which the wicked lived?’
v29: Have you not asked those who travel the roads?
Do you fail to recognize their testimony,
v30: that the wicked person is spared on the day of disaster,
led away on the day of wrath?
v31: Who tells him to his face how he has lived?
Who repays him for what he has done?
v32: He is carried to the grave,
and a watch is kept over his burial mound.
v33: The clods of the valley are sweet to him.
Everyone follows after him,
and those before him are beyond counting.
v34: How, then, can you comfort me with emptiness?
All that remains of your answers is falsehood.”
</p>

## Notes

v17–18: The questions continue across both verses. Job challenges the claim that the wicked regularly meet these disasters.

v19: “You say” makes an inferred objection explicit; the Hebrew does not mark the quotation with those words. Job answers that the offender himself should experience the consequences.

v24: The word rendered “vessels” is uncertain; proposed senses include milk pails or parts of the body such as breasts or loins. The parallel line describes well-nourished bones.

v30: The wording can mean being spared on the day of disaster or reserved for that day. The draft follows the former in keeping with Job’s argument that the wicked escape the fate his friends predict.

v32: The grave is watched, but the Hebrew does not specify who keeps watch.

## Vocabulary

v13: Sheol
The realm of the dead; Job contrasts an easy descent there with a life of suffering.
