## The LORD answers Job

<p>
v01: Then the LORD answered Job out of the storm:
v02: “Who is this who darkens counsel
with words without knowledge?
v03: Brace yourself like a man;
I will question you, and you must answer me.
</p>

## Where were you?

<p>
v04: Where were you when I laid the earth’s foundations?
Tell me, if you have understanding.
v05: Who determined its dimensions—surely you know!
Who stretched a measuring line across it?
v06: On what were its bases sunk?
Who laid its cornerstone,
v07: while the morning stars sang together
and all the sons of God shouted for joy?
</p>

<p>
v08: Who shut the sea behind doors
when it burst out from the womb,
v09: when I clothed it with clouds
and wrapped it in thick darkness,
v10: when I marked out its boundary
and set its bars and doors,
v11: and said, ‘You may come this far, but no farther;
here your proud waves must stop’?
v12: In all your days, have you commanded the morning
or shown the dawn its place,
v13: so that it takes hold of the earth’s edges
and shakes the wicked out of it?
v14: The earth changes like clay under a seal;
its features stand out like a garment.
v15: The wicked are deprived of their light,
and the uplifted arm is broken.
</p>

## Have you entered the depths?

<p>
v16: Have you entered the springs of the sea
or walked through the recesses of the deep?
v17: Have death’s gates been revealed to you?
Have you seen the gates of deep shadow?
v18: Have you comprehended the earth’s breadth?
Tell me, if you know it all.
v19: Where is the path to light’s dwelling?
Where is darkness’s home,
v20: so that you can lead each to its boundary
and understand the paths to its house?
v21: You must know—you were born then,
and the number of your days is so great!
</p>

<p>
v22: Have you entered the storehouses of snow
or seen the storehouses of hail,
v23: which I have kept for a time of distress,
for a day of battle and war?
v24: What is the way to where light is divided,
where the east wind spreads across the earth?
v25: Who cuts a channel for the downpour
and a path for the thunderbolt,
v26: to send rain on land where no one lives,
on a wilderness without a human being,
v27: to satisfy waste and desolation
and make fresh grass sprout?
v28: Does the rain have a father?
Who gave birth to the drops of dew?
v29: From whose womb did the ice come?
Who gave birth to heaven’s frost,
v30: when water becomes hard as stone
and the face of the deep freezes fast?
</p>

## Can you bind the stars?

<p>
v31: Can you bind the cluster of the Pleiades
or loosen Orion’s cords?
v32: Can you bring out the constellations in their season
or guide the Bear with her young?
v33: Do you know the laws of the heavens?
Can you establish their rule on earth?
v34: Can you raise your voice to the clouds
so that a flood of water covers you?
v35: Can you send lightning bolts on their way,
so they say to you, ‘Here we are’?
v36: Who put wisdom in the inward parts
or gave understanding to the mind?
v37: Who has wisdom to count the clouds?
Who tips the water jars of heaven,
v38: when dust hardens into a mass
and clods stick together?
v39: Can you hunt prey for the lioness
or satisfy the young lions’ hunger,
v40: as they crouch in their dens
and lie in wait in the thicket?
v41: Who provides the raven’s food
when its young cry out to God
and wander about with nothing to eat?
</p>

## Notes

v14: The earth takes shape like clay under a seal; the following garment image is compressed and its plural subject uncertain.

v21: The claim that Job knows because he was born then is pointed irony.

v31–32: The identifications Pleiades, Orion, and Bear are conventional and not certain in every detail. “Constellations” renders the obscure Mazzaroth.

v36: The paired words can concern inward parts and the mind, or particular creatures such as the ibis and rooster. The draft follows the inward-wisdom reading.

## Vocabulary

v03: Brace yourself
Literally, gird your loins: secure clothing for demanding action.

v07: Sons of God
The heavenly assembly, also named in the opening chapters.

v26–27: Land without people
The rain nourishes places beyond human settlement.
