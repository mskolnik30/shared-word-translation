## Job and his household

<p>
v01: There was a man in the land of Uz named Job. He was a man of integrity, upright, fearing God and turning away from evil.
v02: He had seven sons and three daughters.
v03: He owned seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred pairs of oxen, and five hundred female donkeys, with a very large household workforce. He was the greatest of all the people of the east.
v04: His sons would take turns holding banquets in their homes, each on his own day. They would invite their three sisters to eat and drink with them.
v05: When a round of banquets was over, Job would send for them and consecrate them. He rose early in the morning and offered burnt offerings for each of them, thinking, “Perhaps my children have sinned and cursed God in their hearts.” Job did this regularly.
</p>

## The accuser’s challenge

<p>
v06: One day the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and the accuser came among them.
v07: The LORD asked the accuser, “Where have you come from?” The accuser answered, “From roaming the earth and walking about on it.”
v08: The LORD said to the accuser, “Have you considered my servant Job? There is no one like him on earth: a man of integrity, upright, fearing God and turning away from evil.”
v09: The accuser answered the LORD, “Does Job fear God for nothing?
v10: Have you not put a hedge around him, his household, and everything he owns? You have blessed the work of his hands, and his livestock have spread across the land.
v11: But stretch out your hand and touch everything he has. He will surely curse you to your face!”
v12: The LORD said to the accuser, “Everything he has is in your hands. But do not lay a hand on the man himself.” Then the accuser left the LORD’s presence.
</p>

## Disaster reaches Job

<p>
v13: One day Job’s sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in their oldest brother’s house.
v14: A messenger came to Job and said, “The oxen were plowing and the female donkeys were grazing nearby,
v15: when the Sabeans attacked and carried them off. They killed the workers with the sword. I alone escaped to tell you.”
v16: While he was still speaking, another messenger came and said, “Fire from God fell from heaven, burning up the sheep and the workers. I alone escaped to tell you.”
v17: While he was still speaking, another messenger came and said, “The Chaldeans formed three raiding bands, swept down on the camels, and carried them off. They killed the workers with the sword. I alone escaped to tell you.”
v18: While he was still speaking, another messenger came and said, “Your sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in their oldest brother’s house,
v19: when a great wind came across the wilderness and struck the house’s four corners. It collapsed on the young people, and they died. I alone escaped to tell you.”
</p>

<p>
v20: Job rose, tore his robe, and shaved his head. Then he fell to the ground and worshiped.
v21: He said, “Naked I came from my mother’s womb,
and naked I will return there.
The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away.
Blessed be the name of the LORD.”
v22: Through all this, Job did not sin or charge God with wrongdoing.
</p>

## Notes

v01: “A man of integrity” describes Job’s soundness of character. It does not claim that he has never done anything wrong.

v04: “His day” may identify a customary feast day or a birthday; the Hebrew does not settle this.

v05: The Hebrew uses “bless” here as a euphemism for cursing, as also in 1:11 and 2:5, 9. Verse 21 uses blessing in its ordinary sense.

v06: “The accuser” translates a title with the definite article. The scene does not itself supply the later biography associated with Satan. “Sons of God” names the heavenly assembly.

v16: “Fire of God” is the messenger’s description of the disaster.

v21: “Return there” places the mother’s womb beside death and the earth. The image is retained without making its physical geography explicit.

## Vocabulary

v01: Fear God
To regard God with reverence and live accordingly.

v10: Hedge
An image of protective enclosure; Job later uses enclosure as an image of confinement.
