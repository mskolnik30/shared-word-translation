## Elihu: There is more to say for God

<p>
v01: Elihu went on:
v02: “Bear with me a little, and I will show you;
there is still more to say on God’s behalf.
v03: I will bring my knowledge from afar
and ascribe righteousness to my Maker.
v04: Truly, my words are not false;
one complete in knowledge is with you.
v05: God is mighty, yet despises no one;
he is mighty in strength of understanding.
v06: He does not keep the wicked alive;
he grants justice to the afflicted.
v07: He does not take his eyes off the righteous;
he seats them with kings on thrones forever,
and they are exalted.
</p>

## God opens ears through suffering

<p>
v08: But if they are bound in chains,
caught in the cords of affliction,
v09: he tells them what they have done,
their rebellions and their arrogance.
v10: He opens their ears to correction
and tells them to turn from wrongdoing.
v11: If they listen and serve him,
they finish their days in prosperity
and their years in pleasantness.
v12: But if they do not listen, they pass away by the weapon
and die without knowledge.
v13: The godless at heart store up anger;
they do not cry for help when he binds them.
v14: They die in their youth;
their life ends among the male shrine attendants.
v15: He rescues the afflicted through their affliction
and opens their ears through oppression.
</p>

<p>
v16: He is drawing you too from distress’s jaws
into a broad place with no confinement,
to a table set with rich food.
v17: But you are full of the judgment due the wicked;
judgment and justice take hold of you.
v18: Beware that anger does not lure you into mockery;
do not let a great ransom turn you aside.
v19: Can your cry keep you out of distress,
or all the force of your strength?
v20: Do not long for the night
when peoples are swept from their places.
v21: Take care; do not turn to wrongdoing,
for you have chosen it instead of affliction.
</p>

## Who teaches like him?

<p>
v22: God is exalted in power.
Who is a teacher like him?
v23: Who has prescribed his course for him?
Who has said, ‘You have done wrong’?
v24: Remember to magnify his work,
which people have celebrated in song.
v25: All humanity has seen it;
mortals gaze on it from afar.
v26: God is great beyond our knowing;
the number of his years cannot be searched out.
</p>

<p>
v27: He draws up drops of water;
they distill his mist into rain,
v28: which the clouds pour down
and shower abundantly on people.
v29: Can anyone understand the spreading clouds,
the thunder from his shelter?
v30: He spreads his light around him
and covers the roots of the sea.
v31: By these he judges peoples
and gives food in abundance.
v32: He covers his hands with lightning
and commands it to strike its target.
v33: His thunder announces him;
even the cattle tell of the storm’s approach.
</p>

## Notes

v04: Elihu claims that one complete in knowledge is present with Job. Similar language in 37:16 refers to God.

v12: The term rendered “weapon” has also been related to a channel or river, as in 33:18.

v14: The term for male shrine attendants is traditionally associated with cult prostitution, but the precise role is uncertain. The draft does not assert a sexual occupation as settled.

v18–19: These lines are difficult. They can concern anger, mockery, ransom, and an appeal for help, or the danger and inability of wealth to deliver.

v27: The relation of water drops, rain, and mist is compressed; the draft retains the imagery without turning it into a technical account.

v33: The final line about cattle and what is coming is especially obscure. The draft follows the reading that cattle announce the storm’s approach.

## Vocabulary

v16: A broad place
An image of relief from constriction and distress.

v30: Roots of the sea
The deep reaches of the sea in the poem’s spatial imagery.
