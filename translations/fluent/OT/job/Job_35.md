## Elihu: What do your actions give God?

<p>
v01: Elihu continued:
v02: “Do you think this is just?
Do you say, ‘I am more righteous than God,’
v03: when you ask, ‘What use is it to you?
What do I gain by not sinning?’
v04: I will answer you
and your friends along with you.
v05: Look up at the heavens and see;
look at the clouds, high above you.
v06: If you sin, what do you do to him?
If your rebellions multiply, how do they affect him?
v07: If you are righteous, what do you give him?
What does he receive from your hand?
v08: Your wickedness affects a person like yourself,
and your righteousness another human being.
</p>

## Cries without an answer

<p>
v09: People cry out under great oppression;
they call for help beneath the arm of the powerful.
v10: But no one asks, ‘Where is God my Maker,
who gives songs in the night,
v11: who teaches us more than the earth’s animals
and makes us wiser than the birds of the heavens?’
v12: There they cry out, but he does not answer,
because of the arrogance of evil people.
v13: Surely God does not listen to emptiness;
the Almighty pays it no attention.
v14: How much less when you say you cannot see him!
Your case is before him; wait for him.
v15: But now, because his anger has not punished
and he has not taken much notice of transgression,
v16: Job opens his mouth in emptiness
and multiplies words without knowledge.”
</p>

## Notes

v02: The comparison can mean being more righteous than God or being in the right before God.

v03: The wording shifts between “you” and “I” in the reported question. The draft keeps that movement.

v11: God may teach people more than the animals, or teach people through the animals.

v15: The rare term rendered “transgression” is uncertain. The verse remains part of Elihu’s explanation for Job’s speech.

## Vocabulary

v06–08: Your wrongdoing and righteousness
Elihu contrasts their effect on God with their effect on other people.
