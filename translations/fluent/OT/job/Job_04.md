## Eliphaz: Can anyone be right before God?

<p>
v01: Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered:
v02: “If someone ventures a word with you, will you grow weary?
Yet who can hold back from speaking?
v03: You have instructed many
and strengthened feeble hands.
v04: Your words have raised up those who stumbled;
you have steadied buckling knees.
v05: But now trouble comes to you, and you grow weary;
it touches you, and you are dismayed.
v06: Is your reverence not your confidence,
your upright way of life your hope?
</p>

<p>
v07: Think now: who ever perished while innocent?
Where were the upright destroyed?
v08: As I have seen, those who plow wrongdoing
and sow trouble reap it.
v09: At God’s breath they perish;
at the blast of his anger they come to an end.
v10: The lion’s roar and the fierce lion’s voice fall silent;
the young lions’ teeth are broken.
v11: The strong lion dies for lack of prey,
and the lioness’s cubs are scattered.
</p>

## A voice in the night

<p>
v12: A word came stealing to me;
my ear caught a whisper of it.
v13: Amid troubling thoughts from visions in the night,
when deep sleep falls on people,
v14: fear and trembling came over me,
shaking all my bones.
v15: A spirit passed before my face;
the hair on my body stood on end.
v16: It stood still, but I could not make out its appearance.
A shape was before my eyes.
There was silence, and then I heard a voice:
v17: ‘Can a mortal be righteous before God?
Can a man be pure before his Maker?
v18: He does not trust even his servants;
he finds fault with his messengers.
v19: How much more those who live in houses of clay,
whose foundations are in dust,
who are crushed more easily than a moth!
v20: Between morning and evening they are shattered;
unnoticed, they perish forever.
v21: Is not their tent cord pulled up?
They die without wisdom.’
</p>

## Notes

v10: The first line names the lion’s roar without a complete verb; the draft supplies “falls silent” from the surrounding image of broken power.

v15: The word for “spirit” can also mean wind or breath. The account remains Eliphaz’s report of his experience.

v17: The question can ask whether a mortal is righteous before God or more righteous than God.

v19: The comparison with a moth is compressed. Being crushed more easily than a moth is one reading; other readings picture destruction by or like a moth.

## Vocabulary

v19: Houses of clay
Human bodies pictured as fragile dwellings founded in dust.
