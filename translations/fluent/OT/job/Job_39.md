## The lives of wild creatures

<p>
v01: Do you know when the mountain goats give birth?
Do you watch the deer in labor?
v02: Can you count the months they carry their young?
Do you know the time they give birth?
v03: They crouch and deliver their young;
they bring forth their offspring.
v04: Their young grow strong in the open country;
they leave and do not return to them.
</p>

<p>
v05: Who set the wild donkey free?
Who loosened the onager’s bonds?
v06: I gave it the wilderness for a home,
the salt land for its dwelling.
v07: It laughs at the city’s clamor
and hears no driver’s shouting.
v08: It ranges over the mountains for pasture,
searching for every green thing.
</p>

## Will the wild ox serve you?

<p>
v09: Will the wild ox agree to serve you?
Will it spend the night at your feeding trough?
v10: Can you bind it with ropes to the furrow?
Will it harrow the valleys behind you?
v11: Will you trust it because its strength is great
and leave your hard work in its care?
v12: Can you rely on it to bring in your grain
and gather it at your threshing floor?
</p>

<p>
v13: The ostrich’s wings flap joyfully,
but are her pinions and feathers like the stork’s?
v14: She leaves her eggs on the ground
and warms them in the dust,
v15: forgetting that a foot may crush them
or a wild animal trample them.
v16: She treats her young harshly, as though they were not hers;
she does not fear that her labor may be wasted,
v17: because God has withheld wisdom from her
and given her no share in understanding.
v18: Yet when she rises to run,
she laughs at horse and rider.
</p>

## The horse in battle

<p>
v19: Do you give the horse its strength
or clothe its neck with a mane?
v20: Do you make it leap like a locust?
Its majestic snorting strikes terror.
v21: It paws the valley floor and rejoices in its strength,
going out to meet the weapons.
v22: It laughs at fear and is not dismayed;
it does not turn back before the sword.
v23: The quiver rattles against it,
the flashing spear and javelin.
v24: Quivering with excitement, it swallows the ground;
it cannot stand still at the trumpet’s sound.
v25: At each trumpet blast it says, ‘Aha!’
It smells battle from afar,
the thunder of commanders and the battle cry.
</p>

## Hawk and eagle

<p>
v26: Is it by your understanding that the hawk soars
and spreads its wings toward the south?
v27: Does the eagle rise at your command
and build its nest on high?
v28: It lives on the rock and spends the night there,
on a rocky crag, its stronghold.
v29: From there it searches out food;
its eyes spot it far away.
v30: Its young gulp blood;
where the slain lie, there it is.”
</p>

## Notes

v13: The ostrich’s wing and its comparison with the stork are described with rare, difficult words.

v16–17: The poem’s portrayal of the ostrich is retained as poetry; it is not rewritten as a modern zoological description.

v19: The word rendered “mane” is uncertain and has also been related to thunder.

v24: The last clause can describe not standing still at the trumpet or not believing the trumpet’s signal.

## Vocabulary

v05: Wild donkey
The paired Hebrew names evoke the free-ranging donkey or onager.

v30: Blood
The eagle’s young gulp blood; the image’s violence remains explicit.
