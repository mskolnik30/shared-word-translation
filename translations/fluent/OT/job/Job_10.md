## Job: Tell me why you accuse me

<p>
v01: I loathe my life.
I will give my complaint free rein;
I will speak from my soul’s bitterness.
v02: I will say to God, ‘Do not condemn me.
Tell me why you bring a case against me.
v03: Does it please you to oppress,
to reject the work of your own hands,
while you smile on the schemes of the wicked?
v04: Do you have eyes of flesh?
Do you see as a mortal sees?
v05: Are your days like a mortal’s days,
your years like a man’s lifetime,
v06: that you search out my wrongdoing
and investigate my sin,
v07: though you know that I am not wicked
and that no one can rescue me from your hand?
</p>

## You shaped me

<p>
v08: Your hands shaped me and made me, every part of me;
yet now you destroy me.
v09: Remember, please, that you made me like clay.
Will you turn me back to dust?
v10: Did you not pour me out like milk
and curdle me like cheese?
v11: You clothed me with skin and flesh
and knit me together with bones and sinews.
v12: You gave me life and faithful love;
your care guarded my spirit.
</p>

<p>
v13: Yet these things you hid in your heart;
I know this was your intent:
v14: if I sinned, you would watch me
and would not acquit me of wrongdoing.
v15: If I am wicked, woe to me!
Even if I am righteous, I cannot lift my head.
I am full of disgrace—look at my affliction!
v16: If my head rises, you hunt me like a lion
and again display your wondrous power against me.
v17: You renew your witnesses against me
and increase your anger toward me;
wave after wave of troops comes against me.
</p>

## Why did you bring me out of the womb?

<p>
v18: Why did you bring me out of the womb?
If only I had died before any eye saw me!
v19: I would be as though I had never existed,
carried straight from the womb to the grave.
v20: Are not my days few? Stop, then;
leave me alone, so I may find a little cheer,
v21: before I go, never to return,
to the land of darkness and deep shadow,
v22: a land of gloom like thick darkness,
of deep shadow without order,
where even its light is like darkness.’”
</p>

## Notes

v10: Milk curdling into cheese is an ancient bodily image of formation in the womb, retained as poetry.

v15: The final words are read as an appeal, “look at my affliction,” rather than absorbed into a description of misery.

v20: The read forms address God with imperatives; the written forms can continue speaking of him in the third person.

## Vocabulary

v12: Steadfast care
God’s attention to Job’s life becomes the basis of Job’s bewilderment at his suffering.
