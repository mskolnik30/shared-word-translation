## Will the critic answer?

<p>
v01: The LORD said to Job:
v02: “Will a faultfinder contend with the Almighty?
Let the one who argues with God answer!”
</p>

## Job puts his hand over his mouth

<p>
v03: Job answered the LORD:
v04: “I am of little account. What can I answer you?
I put my hand over my mouth.
v05: I have spoken once and will not answer;
twice, and I will add no more.”
</p>

## The LORD speaks again

<p>
v06: Then the LORD answered Job out of the storm:
v07: “Brace yourself like a man;
I will question you, and you must answer me.
v08: Would you overturn my justice?
Would you condemn me so that you could be right?
v09: Do you have an arm like God’s?
Can your voice thunder like his?
v10: Then adorn yourself with majesty and grandeur;
clothe yourself in glory and splendor.
v11: Let loose the fury of your anger;
look at every proud person and bring him low.
v12: Look at every proud person and humble him;
crush the wicked where they stand.
v13: Hide them together in the dust;
bind their faces in the hidden place.
v14: Then I too will acknowledge
that your own right hand can save you.
</p>

## Consider Behemoth

<p>
v15: Look at Behemoth, which I made as I made you;
it eats grass like an ox.
v16: Look at the strength in its loins,
the power in its belly’s muscles.
v17: It stiffens its tail like a cedar;
the sinews of its thighs are tightly woven.
v18: Its bones are tubes of bronze,
its limbs like bars of iron.
v19: It is foremost among God’s works;
its Maker can bring his sword near.
v20: The mountains yield food for it,
where all the wild animals play.
v21: It lies beneath the lotus plants,
hidden among reeds in the marsh.
v22: The lotus plants cover it with shade;
the willows of the stream surround it.
v23: If a river surges, it does not panic;
it stays confident even if the Jordan rushes against its mouth.
v24: Can anyone seize it while it watches
or pierce its nose with a snare?
</p>

## Notes

v04: Job calls himself small or of little account; the word does not by itself mean morally vile.

v15: Behemoth names a formidable creature. Identifying it with one particular animal, extinct or living, goes beyond what the passage settles.

v17: The cedar-like tail and tightly woven thigh sinews are vivid but difficult; the description may also carry sexual associations.

v19: The maker and sword clause can picture the maker bringing a sword near the creature or furnishing it with a sword.

v24: The first question can concern capturing it while it watches or taking it by the eyes.

v24: The Hebrew chapter continues for eight more verses. They appear here under the established public labels 41:1–8; every original record is retained.

## Vocabulary

v07: Brace yourself
The same summons to prepare for an encounter as in 38:3.

v18: Bronze and iron
Metal supplies images for the creature’s powerful bones and limbs.
