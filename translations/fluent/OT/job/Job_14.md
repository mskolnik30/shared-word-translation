## Job: A mortal’s few days

<p>
v01: A human being, born of woman,
has few days and is full of turmoil.
v02: He comes out like a flower and withers;
he flees like a shadow and does not last.
v03: Do you fix your eyes even on such a one
and bring me into judgment with you?
v04: Who can bring something clean from what is unclean?
No one.
v05: His days are determined;
the number of his months is with you.
You have set limits he cannot cross.
v06: Look away from him and let him rest,
until he enjoys his day like a hired worker.
</p>

## A tree has hope

<p>
v07: There is hope for a tree:
if it is cut down, it can sprout again,
and its shoots need not cease.
v08: Though its roots grow old in the earth
and its stump dies in the dust,
v09: at the scent of water it will bud
and put out branches like a young plant.
v10: But a man dies and lies powerless;
a human breathes his last, and where is he?
v11: Water drains away from a lake;
a river runs dry and withers.
v12: So a person lies down and does not rise.
Until the heavens are no more, they will not awake
or be roused from their sleep.
</p>

## If only you would hide me

<p>
v13: If only you would hide me in Sheol,
conceal me until your anger passes,
set a time for me and remember me!
v14: If a man dies, will he live again?
All the days of my hard service I would wait
until my relief came.
v15: You would call, and I would answer;
you would long for the work of your hands.
v16: Then you would count my steps
but would not keep watch over my sin.
v17: My rebellion would be sealed in a bag;
you would cover over my wrongdoing.
</p>

<p>
v18: But a mountain falls and crumbles,
and a rock moves from its place.
v19: Water wears away stones;
its torrents wash away the soil of the earth.
So you destroy a mortal’s hope.
v20: You overpower him forever, and he goes away;
you change his face and send him off.
v21: His children gain honor, but he does not know it;
they are brought low, but he does not perceive it.
v22: He feels only the pain of his own flesh,
and his soul mourns for himself.”
</p>

## Notes

v04: The question concerns cleanness arising from uncleanness. No developed account of inherited guilt is supplied.

v14: The question about living again remains a question within Job’s wish; it is not turned into a settled declaration.

v16–17: The draft continues the hoped-for situation: God would count Job’s steps without keeping watch over sin. These lines can instead be heard as a return to present scrutiny and guilt being preserved in a sealed bag.

v22: Pain and mourning continue in the closing image. The poem does not resolve exactly how this relates to death or the dead.

## Vocabulary

v14: Service
The appointed period of waiting is pictured as compulsory service.
