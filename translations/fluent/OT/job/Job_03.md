## Job curses the day of his birth

<p>
v01: After this, Job opened his mouth and cursed the day of his birth.
v02: Job said:
</p>

<p>
v03: “Let the day I was born perish,
and the night that said, ‘A boy is conceived!’
v04: Let that day become darkness.
Let God above take no notice of it;
let no light shine on it.
v05: Let darkness and deep shadow reclaim it.
Let a cloud settle over it;
let the day’s blackness terrify it.
v06: Let thick darkness seize that night.
Let it find no joy among the days of the year;
let it never enter the count of the months.
v07: Let that night be barren;
let no shout of joy enter it.
v08: Let those who curse days curse it,
those skilled at rousing Leviathan.
v09: Let its twilight stars go dark.
Let it wait for light that never comes,
and never see the eyelids of dawn,
v10: because it did not shut the doors of the womb that bore me
and hide trouble from my eyes.
</p>

## Why was I born?

<p>
v11: Why did I not die at birth,
come out of the womb and breathe my last?
v12: Why were there knees to receive me,
and breasts for me to nurse?
v13: Then I would be lying down in quiet.
I would be asleep and at rest,
v14: with kings and counselors of the earth
who built ruined places for themselves,
v15: or with princes who possessed gold
and filled their houses with silver.
v16: Or I would be like a buried stillborn child,
like infants who never saw the light.
v17: There the wicked stop their raging,
and there the exhausted find rest.
v18: Prisoners are at ease together;
they no longer hear the taskmaster’s voice.
v19: Small and great are there alike,
and the slave is free from his master.
</p>

## Why give light to the suffering?

<p>
v20: Why is light given to those in misery,
and life to those whose souls are bitter,
v21: who long for death that does not come
and dig for it more eagerly than for buried treasure,
v22: who rejoice with great joy
and are glad when they find a grave?
v23: Why is life given to a man whose way is hidden,
whom God has hedged in?
v24: My sighing comes in place of bread,
and my groans pour out like water.
v25: What I dreaded has overtaken me;
what I feared has come upon me.
v26: I have no ease, no quiet, no rest—
only turmoil comes.”
</p>

## Notes

v05: The Hebrew expression rendered “deep shadow” can also evoke the shadow of death.

v06: The pointed Hebrew can mean finding no joy among the days; another reading concerns not being joined to their number.

v08: Leviathan evokes a formidable creature of chaos. Job calls on imagined powers capable of undoing the day of his birth.

v14: The “ruined places” are difficult: rebuilding ruins or constructing monuments that become ruins are possible senses.

v24: The phrase can mean sighing in place of bread or sighing before eating.

## Vocabulary

v09: Eyelids of dawn
The first opening of morning light.

v23: Hedged in
The protection pictured in 1:10 becomes confinement in Job’s lament.
