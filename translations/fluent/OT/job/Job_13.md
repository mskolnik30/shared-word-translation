## I Would Speak to the Almighty

<p>
v01: “Look, my eye has seen all this;
my ear has heard and understood it.

v02: What you know, I also know;
I am not inferior to you.

v03: Yet I would speak to the Almighty,
and I desire to argue my case with God.

v04: But you smear with lies;
you are all worthless physicians.

v05: If only you would be completely silent!
That would become your wisdom.

v06: Hear now my argument,
and listen to the contentions of my lips.
</p>

## Will You Speak Unjustly for God?

<p>
v07: Will you speak injustice for God?
Will you speak deceitfully for him?

v08: Will you show partiality toward him?
Will you contend for God?

v09: Would it be good if he examined you?
Can you deceive him as one deceives a human?

v10: He would surely correct you
if in secret you show partiality.

v11: Would not his majesty terrify you,
and dread of him fall upon you?

v12: Your remembered sayings are proverbs of ash;
your defenses are defenses of clay.
</p>

## Though He Kill Me, I Will Hope in Him

<p>
v13: Be silent before me,
and let me speak;
then let come upon me what may.

v14: Why should I take my flesh in my teeth
and put my life in my hand?

v15: Though he kill me, I will hope in him;
yet I will argue my ways before his face.

v16: This also will be my salvation,
for no godless person can come before him.

v17: Listen carefully to my words,
and let my declaration be in your ears.

v18: Look now, I have arranged my case;
I know that I am in the right.

v19: Who is there who will contend with me?
For then I would be silent and expire.
</p>

## Withdraw Your Hand from Me

<p>
v20: Only two things do not do to me;
then I will not hide from your face:

v21: withdraw your hand far from me,
and do not let your terror frighten me.

v22: Then call, and I will answer;
or let me speak, and you answer me.
</p>

## How Many Are My Sins?

<p>
v23: How many are my iniquities and sins?
Make me know my transgression and my sin.

v24: Why do you hide your face
and consider me your enemy?

v25: Will you terrify a driven leaf
and pursue dry chaff?

v26: For you write bitter things against me
and make me inherit the iniquities of my youth.

v27: You put my feet in the stocks
and watch all my paths;
you mark the soles of my feet.

v28: And he wastes away like something rotten,
like a garment eaten by moths.”
</p>

## Notes

v03: Job explicitly asks to argue his case with God rather than continue debating the friends. Legal language increasingly structures his prayer.

v04: “worthless physicians” — The friends claim to diagnose Job’s condition but their treatment is based on what Job calls lies.

v07–10: Job argues that defending God through falsehood is itself an offense against God. Divine honor does not require dishonest advocacy.

v15: The Hebrew consonantal text can be read “Though he kill me, I will hope in him,” while the traditional marginal reading yields “I have no hope.” Both fit aspects of Job’s speech. TSW follows the well-known hopeful reading while noting the textual ambiguity.

v16: Job’s confidence is paradoxical: the very willingness to approach God with his case becomes evidence that he is not godless.

v20–22: Job asks for two conditions for a fair hearing: relief from divine force and relief from terror. Only then can speech occur without coercion.

v23: Rather than denying the possibility of all sin, Job asks for specific charges. His complaint is against disproportionate or unexplained punishment.

## Vocabulary

v03: יָכַח (yakhach)
“To argue,” “prove,” or “contend.” Job desires a reasoned case with God.

v04: רֹפְאֵי אֱלִל (rof'ei elil)
“Worthless physicians.” Literally healers of worthlessness.

v08: נָשָׂא פָנִים (nasa panim)
“To show partiality.” Literally to lift a face in favor of one side.

v15: יָחַל (yachal)
“To hope” or “wait.” The reading followed by TSW in Job’s declaration toward God.

v18: מִשְׁפָּט (mishpat)
“Case,” “judgment,” or “justice.” Job says he has arranged his legal case.

v27: סַד (sad)
“Stocks.” A device restraining the feet, used as an image of divine confinement.
