## Job: I want to argue my case with God

<p>
v01: My eyes have seen all this;
my ears have heard and understood it.
v02: What you know, I know too.
I am no less than you.
v03: But I want to speak to the Almighty;
I desire to argue my case with God.
v04: You smear things over with lies;
all of you are worthless physicians.
v05: If only you would be completely silent!
That would be wisdom for you.
v06: Listen, please, to my argument;
attend to the case my lips present.
v07: Will you speak injustice on God’s behalf
and tell lies for him?
v08: Will you show partiality toward him?
Will you plead God’s case?
v09: Will it go well when he examines you?
Can you deceive him as you deceive a mortal?
v10: He will surely rebuke you
if you secretly show partiality.
v11: Will not his majesty terrify you,
and dread of him fall upon you?
v12: Your memorable sayings are proverbs of ashes;
your defenses are defenses of clay.
</p>

## Let me speak

<p>
v13: Be silent and let me speak;
then let whatever comes come upon me.
v14: Why should I carry my flesh in my teeth
and put my life in my hands?
v15: He may kill me; still I will wait for him.
Yet I will argue my ways to his face.
v16: This too will be my deliverance,
for no godless person would come before him.
v17: Listen closely to my words;
let my explanation reach your ears.
v18: I have now arranged my case;
I know that I will be vindicated.
v19: Who is there to dispute with me?
Then I would be silent and breathe my last.
</p>

## Only grant me two things

<p>
v20: Only grant me these two things;
then I will not hide from your face:
v21: withdraw your hand from me,
and do not let your terror frighten me.
v22: Then call, and I will answer;
or let me speak, and you answer me.
v23: How many are my wrongs and sins?
Make my rebellion and my sin known to me.
v24: Why do you hide your face
and count me as your enemy?
v25: Will you terrify a windblown leaf
and pursue dry straw?
v26: You write bitter charges against me
and make me inherit the wrongs of my youth.
v27: You put my feet in the stocks
and watch all my paths;
you mark the soles of my feet.
v28: A person wastes away like something rotten,
like a garment eaten by moths.
</p>

## Notes

v14: Taking flesh in the teeth and life in the hand pictures mortal risk. The question is retained rather than converted into a calm statement.

v15: The read form means “for him”; the written form means “not.” The draft follows the read form: Job waits for God even under threat of death. Following the written form yields “I have no hope.” In either case Job insists on arguing his conduct to God’s face.

v19: The relation between a successful challenge, silence, and death is compressed. The draft keeps the conditional force.

v27: Marking or tracing the soles of Job’s feet may picture surveillance or the marks of confinement.

v28: The Hebrew shifts to “he,” understood here as a wasting human being, not as God decaying.

## Vocabulary

v07: Speak injustice for God
Job challenges the use of dishonest advocacy in God’s defense.

v18: Case
Job frames his appeal in the language of a legal hearing.
