## Can you draw out Leviathan?

<p>
v01: Can you pull Leviathan out with a fishhook
or press down its tongue with a cord?
v02: Can you put a rush cord through its nose
or pierce its jaw with a hook?
v03: Will it plead with you at length
or speak gentle words to you?
v04: Will it make a covenant with you,
so that you can take it as your slave forever?
v05: Will you play with it like a bird
or tie it up for your girls?
v06: Will partners bargain over it
and divide it among merchants?
v07: Can you fill its hide with harpoons
or its head with fishing spears?
v08: Lay your hand on it!
Remember the battle—you will not do it again.
</p>

<p>
v09: Any hope of capturing it proves false;
is not a person thrown down at the very sight?
v10: No one is fierce enough to rouse it.
Who, then, can stand before me?
v11: Who has given to me first, so that I must repay?
Everything under heaven is mine.
</p>

## Its mighty frame

<p>
v12: I will not be silent about its limbs,
its mighty strength and graceful form.
v13: Who can strip off its outer covering?
Who can enter between its double jaws?
v14: Who can open the doors of its face?
Terror surrounds its teeth.
v15: Its pride is its rows of shields,
closed tight as a seal.
v16: Each fits so closely to the next
that no air can pass between them.
v17: They cling to one another,
locked together, unable to separate.
</p>

<p>
v18: Its sneezes flash with light;
its eyes are like the eyelids of dawn.
v19: Flaming torches pour from its mouth;
sparks of fire leap out.
v20: Smoke rises from its nostrils
like a boiling pot over burning reeds.
v21: Its breath sets coals ablaze;
a flame comes from its mouth.
v22: Strength dwells in its neck;
dismay dances before it.
v23: The folds of its flesh cling together,
firmly cast upon it, immovable.
v24: Its heart is cast hard as stone,
hard as a lower millstone.
</p>

## Nothing on earth is its equal

<p>
v25: When it rises, the mighty are afraid;
at its crashing they lose their bearings.
v26: A sword that reaches it cannot stand,
nor spear, dart, or javelin.
v27: It counts iron as straw
and bronze as rotten wood.
v28: An arrow cannot make it flee;
slingstones become chaff to it.
v29: Clubs are counted as chaff;
it laughs at a javelin’s rattling.
v30: Its underside is sharp as broken pottery;
it spreads a threshing sledge across the mud.
v31: It makes the deep boil like a pot,
the sea like a vessel of ointment.
v32: It leaves a shining path behind it;
one would think the deep had turned gray-haired.
v33: Nothing on earth is its equal,
a creature made without fear.
v34: It looks down on all that is lofty;
it is king over all the proud beasts.”
</p>

## Notes

v01–34: English verses 1–8 correspond to Hebrew 40:25–32; English verses 9–34 correspond to Hebrew 41:1–26.

v06: The partners may bargain over Leviathan or feast upon it.

v12: The draft follows the written negative, “I will not,” rather than the read form “to it.”

v13: The “double bridle” may picture the creature’s two jaws.

v20: The smoking pot and burning reeds form a compressed comparison.

v25: The final expression can mean being beside oneself with terror or being purified; the fear reading fits the surrounding response.

## Vocabulary

v01: Leviathan
A formidable creature associated elsewhere with chaotic power; the poem does not give a secure zoological classification.

v32: Gray hair
The foaming deep is pictured as gray-haired.
