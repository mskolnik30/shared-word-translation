## Can You Draw Out Leviathan?

<p>
v01: “Can you draw out Leviathan with a hook
or press down its tongue with a cord?

v02: Can you put a rope through its nose
or pierce its jaw with a hook?

v03: Will it multiply pleas to you?
Will it speak soft words to you?

v04: Will it make a covenant with you
so that you take it as a servant forever?

v05: Will you play with it as with a bird
or tie it up for your girls?

v06: Will traders bargain over it?
Will they divide it among merchants?

v07: Can you fill its skin with harpoons
or its head with fishing spears?

v08: Put your hand upon it;
remember the battle—
you will not do it again!
</p>

## No One Is Fierce Enough to Stir It

<p>
v09: Look, one’s hope concerning it is false;
is one not cast down even at the sight of it?

v10: No one is so fierce that he dares stir it up.
Who then can stand before me?

v11: Who has first given to me, that I should repay him?
Whatever is under all the heavens is mine.
</p>

## I Will Not Be Silent About Its Limbs

<p>
v12: I will not be silent about its limbs,
or the word of its might,
or the grace of its form.

v13: Who can uncover the face of its garment?
Who can enter its double row of teeth?

v14: Who can open the doors of its face?
Around its teeth is terror.

v15: Its back is rows of shields,
shut up tightly with a seal.

v16: One is so near another
that no air can come between them.

v17: They cling to one another;
they hold together and cannot be separated.
</p>

## Its Sneezings Flash Light

<p>
v18: Its sneezings flash light,
and its eyes are like the eyelids of dawn.

v19: From its mouth go burning torches;
sparks of fire leap out.

v20: From its nostrils smoke goes out,
as from a boiling pot over reeds.

v21: Its breath kindles coals,
and flame goes out from its mouth.

v22: Strength dwells in its neck,
and terror dances before it.

v23: The folds of its flesh cling together;
they are firm upon it and cannot be moved.

v24: Its heart is hard as stone,
hard as the lower millstone.
</p>

## The Mighty Are Afraid

<p>
v25: When it rises, mighty ones are afraid;
because of the crashing they lose themselves.

v26: The sword that reaches it does not stand,
nor spear, dart, or javelin.

v27: It regards iron as straw
and bronze as rotten wood.

v28: An arrow does not make it flee;
sling stones become stubble to it.

v29: Clubs are regarded as stubble,
and it laughs at the rattling of the javelin.
</p>

## It Makes the Deep Boil Like a Pot

<p>
v30: Its undersides are like sharp potsherds;
it spreads a threshing sledge upon the mud.

v31: It makes the deep boil like a pot;
it makes the sea like a pot of ointment.

v32: Behind it, it makes a path shine;
one would think the deep had white hair.

v33: On earth there is nothing like it,
a creature made without fear.

v34: It looks upon everything high;
it is king over all the children of pride.”
</p>

## Notes

v01: “Leviathan” is a great sea or chaos creature known elsewhere in biblical poetry. The LORD does not ask Job to explain it but to consider a creature beyond human mastery.

v01–08: The opening questions turn ordinary fishing and domestication practices into absurd possibilities when applied to Leviathan.

v01–08: Public English numbering differs from the Masoretic Text here. English Job 41:1–8 corresponds to Hebrew Job 40:25–32; Hebrew Job 41:1 begins at English Job 41:9. TSW follows standard public English verse numbering.

v10–11: The argument shifts from creature to Creator: if no human can safely confront Leviathan, who can stand before the one to whom everything under heaven belongs?

v18–21: Fire and smoke imagery enlarges Leviathan beyond ordinary zoological description. TSW preserves the poetic force rather than reducing the creature to a modern species.

v24: The heart “hard as the lower millstone” portrays unyielding physical and psychological fearlessness, not moral hardness in the usual biblical sense.

v31–32: Leviathan’s movement transforms the sea itself, making the deep boil and leaving a luminous wake.

v34: Leviathan is “king over all the children of pride,” yet the entire speech presents even this untamable king as a creature within God’s creation.

## Vocabulary

v01: לִוְיָתָן (Livyatan)
“Leviathan.” A great sea or chaos creature in biblical poetic imagery.

v01: חַכָּה (chakkah)
“Hook” or “fishhook.” An ordinary fishing tool made absurdly inadequate for Leviathan.

v04: בְּרִית (berit)
“Covenant.” The LORD asks whether Leviathan could be bound into permanent service by agreement.

v15: מָגֵן (magen)
“Shield.” Leviathan’s scales or back are compared with tightly joined shields.

v24: פֶּלַח תַּחְתִּית (pelach tachti)
“Lower millstone.” A heavy stone used as an image of Leviathan’s hardness.

v31: תְּהוֹם (tehom)
“Deep.” The great waters Leviathan makes boil.
