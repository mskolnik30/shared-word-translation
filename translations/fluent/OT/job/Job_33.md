## Elihu: Listen to my words

<p>
v01: But now, Job, listen to my words;
pay attention to everything I say.
v02: I have opened my mouth;
my tongue is speaking within it.
v03: My words come from an upright heart;
my lips speak sincerely what they know.
v04: God’s spirit made me;
the Almighty’s breath gives me life.
v05: Answer me if you can;
set out your case before me and take your stand.
v06: Before God I am just like you;
I too was shaped from clay.
v07: No terror of me need frighten you;
my pressure will not weigh heavily on you.
</p>

## You say you are clean

<p>
v08: You said it in my hearing;
I heard the sound of your words:
v09: ‘I am pure, without rebellion;
I am clean, with no wrongdoing in me.
v10: Yet God finds grounds to oppose me;
he counts me as his enemy.
v11: He puts my feet in the stocks
and watches every path I take.’
v12: Here, I answer you, you are not right,
for God is greater than a mortal.
v13: Why do you dispute with him
for not accounting for everything he does?
</p>

## God speaks in more than one way

<p>
v14: God speaks in one way, then another,
though people do not perceive it.
v15: In a dream, a vision of the night,
when deep sleep falls on people
as they slumber in their beds,
v16: then he opens their ears
and seals his warning upon them,
v17: to turn a person from his course
and keep pride from a man,
v18: to hold his life back from the pit,
to keep him from crossing into death by the weapon.
v19: Or a person is corrected by pain on his bed,
with unceasing anguish in all his bones,
v20: until his life loathes bread
and his appetite rejects even desirable food.
v21: His flesh wastes from sight;
his bones, once unseen, protrude.
v22: His life draws near the pit,
his existence to those who bring death.
</p>

## A messenger and a ransom

<p>
v23: But if a messenger stands beside him,
an interpreter, one among a thousand,
to tell a person what is right for him,
v24: then he shows him mercy and says,
‘Spare him from going down to the pit;
I have found a ransom,’
v25: his flesh grows fresh as a child’s;
he returns to the days of his youthful strength.
v26: He prays to God, and God accepts him;
he sees God’s face with a shout of joy,
and God restores his right standing.
v27: He sings before others and says,
‘I sinned and twisted what was right,
but I was not repaid as I deserved.’
v28: God has redeemed his life from crossing into the pit,
and he will live to see the light.
</p>

<p>
v29: God does all these things
twice, three times, with a person,
v30: to bring his life back from the pit,
to shine on him with the light of the living.
v31: Pay attention, Job; listen to me.
Be silent, and I will speak.
v32: If you have something to say, answer me.
Speak, for I want to find you in the right.
v33: If not, listen to me;
be silent, and I will teach you wisdom.”
</p>

## Notes

v06–07: Elihu stresses that he too was formed from clay. The rare word for pressure or burden accompanies his claim that Job need not fear him.

v13: The line may concern God not giving an account of his deeds or not answering a person’s words.

v18: The word rendered “weapon” can also be understood in relation to a channel or river, here associated with death.

v19,21: The draft follows the read forms for the whole frame of bones and for bones protruding. The original written and read forms are both preserved in the source binding.

v23–24: The messenger or interpreter is not named. The subject who shows mercy and speaks of a ransom is also not explicitly identified; the draft does not assign a later religious identity.

v27–28: “Sings” can also be understood as looking toward people. Verse 28 follows the read third-person forms; the written forms are first person.

## Vocabulary

v24: Ransom
A price or means of release from death.

v30: Light of life
Living again in contrast to descending into the pit.
