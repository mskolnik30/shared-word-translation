## Two responses to Israel

<p>
v01: The kings west of the Jordan heard what had happened—the Hittite, Amorite, Canaanite, Perizzite, Hivite, and Jebusite kings in the hill country, the lowlands, and along the whole coast of the Great Sea toward Lebanon.
v02: They joined forces with one purpose: to fight Joshua and Israel.
v03: But the people of Gibeon, hearing what Joshua had done to Jericho and Ai,
v04: devised a cunning plan of their own. They set out posing as envoys, with worn-out sacks on their donkeys and old wineskins that were split and tied up.
v05: They wore patched, worn-out sandals and old clothes. All the bread they took for provisions was dry and crumbling.
</p>

## A covenant made at Gilgal

<p>
v06: They came to Joshua at the camp in Gilgal and told him and the men of Israel, “We have come from a distant land. Now make a covenant with us.”
v07: The men of Israel said to the Hivites, “Perhaps you live among us. How could we make a covenant with you then?”
v08: “We are your servants,” they told Joshua. “Who are you, and where do you come from?” Joshua asked.
</p>

<p>
v09: They answered, “Your servants have come from a very distant land because of the name of the LORD your God. We have heard of his fame and everything he did in Egypt,
v10: and everything he did to the two Amorite kings east of the Jordan: Sihon king of Heshbon and Og king of Bashan, who was in Ashtaroth.
v11: Our elders and everyone in our land told us, ‘Take provisions for the journey and go to meet them. Tell them you are their servants and ask them to make a covenant with you.’
v12: This bread was still warm when we took it from our homes on the day we set out to meet you. Look at it now—dry and crumbling!
v13: These wineskins were new when we filled them, but now they are split. And these clothes and sandals have worn out on the very long journey.”
v14: The men of Israel took some of their provisions, but they did not ask for the LORD’s counsel.
v15: Joshua made peace with them and made a covenant to let them live. The leaders of the assembly swore an oath to them.
</p>

## The deception is discovered

<p>
v16: Three days after making the covenant, the Israelites learned that these people were their neighbors and lived among them.
v17: The Israelites set out and reached their towns on the third day: Gibeon, Chephirah, Beeroth, and Kiriath-jearim.
v18: They did not attack them, because the leaders of the assembly had sworn to them by the LORD, the God of Israel. The whole assembly grumbled against the leaders.
</p>

<p>
v19: All the leaders told the assembly, “We have sworn to them by the LORD, the God of Israel. We cannot touch them now.
v20: This is what we must do: let them live, so that wrath does not fall on us because of the oath we swore to them.”
v21: “Let them live,” the leaders said. So they became woodcutters and water carriers for the whole assembly, as the leaders had said.
</p>

## Lives spared, labor imposed

<p>
v22: Joshua summoned the Gibeonites and asked, “Why did you deceive us by saying, ‘We live very far from you,’ when you actually live among us?
v23: Now you are cursed. Some of you will always be slaves, cutting wood and carrying water for the house of my God.”
</p>

<p>
v24: They answered Joshua, “Your servants were told plainly that the LORD your God had commanded his servant Moses to give you the whole land and destroy all its inhabitants before you. We feared greatly for our lives because of you, so we did this.
v25: Now we are in your hand. Do to us whatever seems good and right to you.”
v26: Joshua dealt with them in this way: he rescued them from the Israelites, who did not kill them.
v27: That day he made them woodcutters and water carriers for the assembly and for the LORD’s altar, at the place the LORD would choose. They remain so to this day.
</p>

## Notes

v04: The Hebrew verb is uncertain. “Posing as envoys” keeps a traditional understanding of the written form. Some Hebrew manuscripts and ancient versions have a closely spelled verb meaning prepare provisions.

v05,12: “Crumbling” can also be understood as moldy. The bread is presented as evidence of the travelers’ claimed long journey.

v14: The men “took” provisions; whether they tasted or inspected them is not specified. Asking for the LORD’s counsel is literally asking his mouth.

v23–27: The same Hebrew word can mean servant or slave. The imposed, continuing labor in verse 23 is rendered slaves; the visitors’ formal address remains “your servants.” “To this day” is the narrator’s time of telling.

## Vocabulary

v06: בְּרִית (berith)
Covenant. A binding relationship; here the covenant and oath protect lives even after the deception is discovered.

v18: עֵדָה (edah)
Assembly or congregation. The community and its leaders have distinct voices in the dispute.

v23: עֶבֶד (eved)
Servant or slave, depending on the relationship. The passage moves from an offer of service to imposed labor.
