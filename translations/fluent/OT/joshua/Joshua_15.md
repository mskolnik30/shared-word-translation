## Judah’s boundaries

<p>
v01: The lot for the tribe of Judah, clan by clan, reached the border of Edom, to the wilderness of Zin at the far southern end.
v02: Their southern boundary began at the end of the Salt Sea, at the tongue of water pointing south.
v03: It ran south of the Ascent of Akrabbim, crossed to Zin, went up south of Kadesh-barnea, passed Hezron, went up to Addar, and turned toward Karka.
v04: It passed Azmon, reached the Wadi of Egypt, and ended at the sea. That is to be your southern boundary.
</p>

<p>
v05: The eastern boundary was the Salt Sea as far as the mouth of the Jordan. The northern boundary began at the tongue of the sea, at the mouth of the Jordan.
v06: The boundary went up to Beth-hoglah, passed north of Beth-arabah, and went up to the Stone of Bohan son of Reuben.
v07: From the Valley of Achor it went up to Debir, then turned north toward Gilgal, opposite the Ascent of Adummim south of the wadi. It passed the waters of En-shemesh and reached En-rogel.
v08: It went up the Valley of Ben-hinnom to the southern flank of the Jebusite city, that is, Jerusalem. Then it went up to the hilltop west of the Valley of Hinnom, at the northern end of the Valley of Rephaim.
v09: From the hilltop it curved to the spring of the waters of Nephtoah, continued to the towns of Mount Ephron, and curved toward Baalah, that is, Kiriath-jearim.
v10: From Baalah it turned west to Mount Seir, passed the northern flank of Mount Jearim, that is, Chesalon, went down to Beth-shemesh, and passed Timnah.
v11: The boundary reached the northern flank of Ekron, curved toward Shikkeron, passed Mount Baalah, and continued to Jabneel, ending at the sea.
</p>

<p>
v12: The western boundary was the Great Sea and its shoreline. These were the boundaries around Judah’s clans.
</p>

## Caleb, Othniel, and Achsah

<p>
v13: As the LORD had commanded Joshua, he gave Caleb son of Jephunneh a share among the people of Judah: Kiriath-arba, that is, Hebron. Arba was the father of Anak.
v14: Caleb drove out from there the three sons of Anak—Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai, descendants of Anak.
v15: From there he went up against the inhabitants of Debir, formerly called Kiriath-sepher.
</p>

<p>
v16: Caleb said, “I will give my daughter Achsah in marriage to the man who attacks Kiriath-sepher and captures it.”
v17: Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s brother, captured it, and Caleb gave him his daughter Achsah in marriage.
</p>

<p>
v18: When Achsah arrived, she urged Othniel to ask her father for a field. She got down from the donkey, and Caleb asked her, “What do you want?”
v19: “Give me a blessing,” she said. “You have given me land in the Negev; give me springs of water too.” So he gave her the upper springs and the lower springs.
</p>

## Notes

v02,05: The Hebrew calls the projecting end or bay of the sea a tongue. The image is retained.

v17: “Caleb’s brother” may identify Othniel or Kenaz. The latter reading makes Othniel Caleb’s nephew.

v18–19: “She urged him” is kept with Achsah as subject and Othniel as the implied person urged. Another understanding takes Caleb as that person. Achsah herself speaks the request in verse 19. “Blessing” here is a tangible gift; the Negev is the southern dry region.

v32,36: With the compound and alternate names grouped as shown, the first list contains thirty-six towns but states twenty-nine; the next contains fifteen but states fourteen. Both the names and stated totals are preserved.

v40,49: The pinned Hebrew has Lahmas in verse 40 and Kiriath-sannah in verse 49. The latter is another name for Debir here; Kiriath-sepher occurs in verse 15.

v47,53: The main text follows the Hebrew reading tradition: Great Sea in verse 47 and Janum in verse 53. The written forms are “boundary” and Janim, respectively.

## Vocabulary

v01: גּוֹרָל (goral)
Lot or allotted share. Land is distributed by clans through an allotment.

v04: נַחַל (nachal)
Stream valley or wadi. The term can name both a watercourse and the valley it follows.

v45: בָּנוֹת (banoth)
Daughters. Here dependent towns belonging to a principal town, distinguished from villages.
