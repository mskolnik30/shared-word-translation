## Simeon’s inheritance

<p>
v01: The second lot came out for Simeon’s clans. Their inheritance lay within Judah’s inheritance.
v02: Their inheritance included Beer-sheba, Sheba, Moladah,
v03: Hazar-shual, Balah, Ezem,
v04: Eltolad, Bethul, Hormah,
v05: Ziklag, Beth-marcaboth, Hazar-susah,
v06: Beth-lebaoth, and Sharuhen—thirteen towns, with their villages;
v07: Ain, Rimmon, Ether, and Ashan—four towns, with their villages;
v08: and all the villages around these towns as far as Baalath-beer, Ramath of the Negev. This was the inheritance of the tribe of Simeon, clan by clan.
</p>

<p>
v09: Simeon’s inheritance came from Judah’s portion, because Judah’s share was too large for them. So Simeon’s descendants received an inheritance within Judah’s.
</p>

## Zebulun’s inheritance

<p>
v10: The third lot came up for Zebulun’s clans. The boundary of their inheritance reached Sarid.
v11: Their boundary went up westward to Maralah, reached Dabbesheth, and reached the wadi opposite Jokneam.
v12: From Sarid it turned east, toward the sunrise, to the territory of Chisloth-tabor. It continued to Daberath and went up to Japhia.
v13: From there it passed eastward to Gath-hepher and Eth-kazin, and continued to Rimmon, curving toward Neah.
v14: The boundary curved around on the north to Hannathon and ended at the Valley of Iphtah-el.
v15: It included Kattath, Nahalal, Shimron, Idalah, and Bethlehem—twelve towns, with their villages.
v16: These towns and their villages were the inheritance of Zebulun’s descendants, clan by clan.
</p>

## Issachar’s inheritance

<p>
v17: The fourth lot came out for Issachar, for Issachar’s descendants by their clans.
v18: Their territory included Jezreel, Chesulloth, Shunem,
v19: Hapharaim, Shion, Anaharath,
v20: Rabbith, Kishion, Ebez,
v21: Remeth, En-gannim, En-haddah, and Beth-pazzez.
v22: The boundary reached Tabor, Shahazimah, and Beth-shemesh, and ended at the Jordan—sixteen towns, with their villages.
v23: These towns and their villages were the inheritance of the tribe of Issachar, clan by clan.
</p>

## Asher’s inheritance

<p>
v24: The fifth lot came out for the tribe of Asher, clan by clan.
v25: Their territory included Helkath, Hali, Beten, Achshaph,
v26: Allammelech, Amad, and Mishal. The boundary reached Carmel on the west and Shihor-libnath.
v27: It turned east to Beth-dagon and reached Zebulun and the Valley of Iphtah-el on the north, Beth-emek and Neiel. It continued to Cabul on the left,
v28: and to Ebron, Rehob, Hammon, and Kanah, as far as Great Sidon.
v29: The boundary turned toward Ramah and the fortified city of Tyre. It turned toward Hosah and ended at the sea, in the region of Achzib.
v30: It included Ummah, Aphek, and Rehob—twenty-two towns, with their villages.
v31: These towns and their villages were the inheritance of the tribe of Asher, clan by clan.
</p>

## Naphtali’s inheritance

<p>
v32: The sixth lot came out for Naphtali, for Naphtali’s descendants by their clans.
v33: Their boundary ran from Heleph, from the oak at Zaanannim, through Adami-nekeb and Jabneel to Lakkum, and ended at the Jordan.
v34: It turned west to Aznoth-tabor and continued from there to Hukkok. It reached Zebulun on the south, Asher on the west, and Judah at the Jordan toward the sunrise.
</p>

<p>
v35: The fortified towns were Ziddim, Zer, Hammath, Rakkath, Chinnereth,
v36: Adamah, Ramah, Hazor,
v37: Kedesh, Edrei, En-hazor,
v38: Yiron, Migdal-el, Horem, Beth-anath, and Beth-shemesh—nineteen towns, with their villages.
v39: These towns and their villages were the inheritance of the tribe of Naphtali, clan by clan.
</p>

## Dan’s inheritance

<p>
v40: The seventh lot came out for the tribe of Dan, clan by clan.
v41: The territory of their inheritance included Zorah, Eshtaol, Ir-shemesh,
v42: Shaalabbin, Aijalon, Ithlah,
v43: Elon, Timnah, Ekron,
v44: Eltekeh, Gibbethon, Baalath,
v45: Jehud, Bene-berak, Gath-rimmon,

</p>

<p>
v47: But Dan’s territory slipped from their grasp. So the Danites went up and attacked Leshem, captured it, put it to the sword, took possession of it, and settled there. They renamed Leshem Dan, after their ancestor Dan.

</p>

## Joshua’s inheritance

<p>
v49: When the Israelites finished assigning the land within its boundaries, they gave Joshua son of Nun an inheritance among them.
v50: At the LORD’s command they gave him the town he requested, Timnath-serah in the hill country of Ephraim. He built up the town and lived there.
</p>

<p>
v51: These were the inheritances assigned by lot by Eleazar the priest, Joshua son of Nun, and the heads of the ancestral houses of Israel’s tribes, at Shiloh before the LORD, at the entrance to the tent of meeting. So they finished dividing the land.
</p>

## Notes

v02,06: The source lists Beer-sheba and Sheba, giving fourteen names with the stated total thirteen. Both entries and the total are retained; some understand Sheba as another name for Beer-sheba.

v15,38: The town totals are retained even where they do not directly match the adjacent name lists. Verse 15 ends with five names and states twelve; verses 35–38 list sixteen names and state nineteen.

v22: Shahazimah follows the Hebrew reading tradition; the written form is commonly rendered Shahazumah.

v27,29: “On the left” is the Hebrew directional expression at Cabul. The ending of verse 29 is difficult: hevel can mean region, while some treat it as a place-name. The translation keeps the region-of-Achzib reading.

v34: The pinned Hebrew names Judah by the Jordan, an unexpected location for Judah in Naphtali’s boundary. The name remains rather than being replaced by a reconstructed reading.

v47: The Hebrew says Dan’s territory “went out from them.” The translation understands a loss of control; another reading takes it as extension beyond the original territory. The ensuing capture and killing at Leshem remain explicit.

## Vocabulary

v01: נַחֲלָה (nachalah)
Inheritance. Simeon’s share lies within Judah’s because Judah’s portion is described as too large.

v33: אֵלוֹן (elon)
Oak or other large tree. Here a landmark at Zaanannim, not an added modern town identification.

v51: חָלַק (chalaq)
Divide or distribute. The repeated division language closes the tribal allotments.
