## The kings hear of the crossing

<p>
v01: All the Amorite kings west of the Jordan and all the Canaanite kings along the sea heard that the LORD had dried up the Jordan’s water before the Israelites until they crossed. Their hearts melted, and they had no spirit left to face the Israelites.
</p>

## Circumcision at Gilgal

<p>
v02: At that time the LORD told Joshua, “Make flint knives and circumcise the Israelites again, a second time.”
v03: Joshua made flint knives and circumcised the Israelites at the Hill of the Foreskins.
v04: This is why Joshua circumcised them: all the males who had left Egypt—the fighting men—had died in the wilderness on the journey after leaving Egypt.
v05: All the people who left Egypt had been circumcised, but none of those born in the wilderness on the journey after leaving Egypt had been circumcised.
v06: The Israelites traveled in the wilderness for forty years, until the entire nation of fighting men who had left Egypt had died out. They had not obeyed the LORD’s voice, and the LORD had sworn that he would not let them see the land he had sworn to their ancestors to give us—a land flowing with milk and honey.
v07: The LORD raised up their sons in their place. These were the ones Joshua circumcised, for they were uncircumcised; no one had circumcised them on the journey.
v08: After the whole nation had been circumcised, they stayed where they were in the camp until they healed.
v09: The LORD told Joshua, “Today I have rolled away the disgrace of Egypt from you.” So that place has been called Gilgal to this day.
</p>

## Passover and the land’s produce

<p>
v10: While the Israelites were camped at Gilgal on the plains of Jericho, they kept the Passover on the evening of the fourteenth day of the month.
v11: The day after the Passover, on that very day, they ate some of the land’s produce: unleavened bread and roasted grain.
v12: The manna stopped the next day, after they had eaten some of the land’s produce. The Israelites had no more manna; that year they ate the crops of the land of Canaan.
</p>

## The commander of the LORD’s army

<p>
v13: When Joshua was near Jericho, he looked up and saw a man standing in front of him with a drawn sword in his hand. Joshua approached him and asked, “Are you for us or for our enemies?”
v14: “No,” he said. “I have now come as commander of the LORD’s army.” Joshua fell facedown to the ground and bowed. “What does my lord have to say to his servant?” he asked.
v15: The commander of the LORD’s army told Joshua, “Take your sandal off your foot, for the place where you are standing is holy.” And Joshua did so.
</p>

## Notes

v01: The Hebrew reading tradition has “until they crossed”; the written form has “until we crossed.” The main text follows the reading tradition here.

v02–07: “Again, a second time” concerns Israel’s circumcision as a people. The following explanation identifies the uncircumcised wilderness-born sons; it does not describe circumcising the same men twice.

v06: “Give us” retains the Hebrew narrator’s first-person wording.

v09: Gilgal echoes the verb “roll.” The text does not spell out precisely what “the disgrace of Egypt” refers to.

v14: The reply begins simply “No.” It does not directly choose either side in Joshua’s question. The figure identifies himself as commander of the LORD’s army; no further identity is supplied here.

## Vocabulary

v09: גָּלַל (galal)
Roll. The LORD’s “rolled away” saying is linked to the place-name Gilgal.

v10: פֶּסַח (pesach)
Passover. Its observance here connects the entry into the land with the departure from Egypt.

v15: קֹדֶשׁ (qodesh)
Holiness or what is holy. The command to remove a sandal recalls Moses at the burning bush in Exodus 3:5.
