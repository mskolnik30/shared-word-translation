## The plan against Ai

<p>
v01: The LORD told Joshua, “Do not be afraid or discouraged. Take all the fighting men with you and go up to Ai. Look! I have given Ai’s king, people, city, and land into your hand.
v02: Do to Ai and its king what you did to Jericho and its king. But this time you may take its plunder and livestock for yourselves. Set an ambush behind the city.”
v03: Joshua and all the fighting men set out to go up to Ai. Joshua chose thirty thousand mighty warriors and sent them out at night.
v04: He commanded them, “Listen. You are to lie in ambush behind the city. Do not go too far from it, and all of you be ready.
v05: I and all the people with me will approach the city. When they come out against us as they did before, we will flee from them.
v06: They will come after us until we have drawn them away from the city. They will say, ‘They are running from us just as before!’ As we flee from them,
v07: you are to rise from the ambush and seize the city. The LORD your God will give it into your hands.
v08: Once you have captured the city, set it on fire. Do as the LORD has said. See that you carry out my command.”
</p>

## The forces take their positions

<p>
v09: Joshua sent them out, and they took their ambush position between Bethel and Ai, west of Ai. Joshua spent that night among the people.
v10: Early the next morning Joshua mustered the people. He and the elders of Israel went ahead of them up to Ai.
v11: All the fighting men with him advanced and came in front of the city. They camped north of Ai, with the valley between Joshua and Ai.
v12: He took about five thousand men and placed them in ambush between Bethel and Ai, west of the city.
v13: The people positioned the whole camp north of the city and its rear detachment west of the city. That night Joshua went into the valley.
</p>

## Ai’s pursuit and the ambush

<p>
v14: When the king of Ai saw this, he and all his people hurried out early to fight Israel at the appointed place facing the Arabah. He did not know there was an ambush behind the city.
v15: Joshua and all Israel let themselves be driven back and fled along the wilderness road.
v16: All the people in Ai were called out to pursue them. They chased Joshua and were drawn away from the city.
v17: Not a man remained in Ai or Bethel who did not go out after Israel. They left the city open and pursued Israel.
</p>

<p>
v18: The LORD told Joshua, “Stretch out the javelin in your hand toward Ai, for I will give the city into your hand.” Joshua stretched out the javelin he held toward the city.
v19: The ambush force sprang from its position and ran as soon as he stretched out his hand. They entered the city, captured it, and quickly set it on fire.
v20: The men of Ai looked back and saw smoke from the city rising toward heaven. They had no power to flee this way or that, for the people who had been fleeing toward the wilderness turned on their pursuers.
v21: When Joshua and all Israel saw that the ambush force had captured the city and that its smoke was rising, they turned back and struck the men of Ai.
v22: The men from the city came out against them too, trapping Ai’s men between Israel’s forces on either side. They struck them down until no survivor or fugitive was left.
v23: They captured the king of Ai alive and brought him to Joshua.
</p>

## Ai is destroyed

<p>
v24: Israel finished killing all Ai’s inhabitants in the open country, in the wilderness where they had pursued Israel. Every one of them fell by the sword. Then all Israel returned to Ai and put the city to the sword.
v25: All who fell that day, men and women, numbered twelve thousand—all the people of Ai.
v26: Joshua did not draw back the hand holding his outstretched javelin until he had devoted all Ai’s inhabitants to destruction.
v27: Israel took only the livestock and the city’s plunder for themselves, just as the LORD had commanded Joshua.
v28: Joshua burned Ai and made it a permanent mound of ruins, a desolate place to this day.
v29: He hung the king of Ai on a tree until evening. At sunset Joshua ordered the body taken down from the tree. They threw it at the entrance to the city gate and raised a great heap of stones over it, which remains to this day.
</p>

## The instruction is written and read

<p>
v30: Then Joshua built an altar on Mount Ebal to the LORD, the God of Israel,
v31: as Moses, the LORD’s servant, had commanded the Israelites. As written in the scroll of Moses’ instruction, it was an altar of whole stones on which no iron tool had been used. They offered burnt offerings to the LORD on it and sacrificed peace offerings.
v32: There, in the sight of the Israelites, Joshua wrote on the stones a copy of Moses’ instruction.
v33: All Israel, with its elders, officers, and judges, stood on both sides of the ark, facing the Levitical priests who carried the ark of the LORD’s covenant. Resident foreigners and native-born Israelites stood together: half faced Mount Gerizim, and half faced Mount Ebal, as Moses, the LORD’s servant, had first commanded for blessing the people of Israel.
v34: Afterward Joshua read all the words of the instruction—the blessing and the curse—everything written in the scroll of instruction.
v35: Not one word of all Moses had commanded was left unread by Joshua before the whole assembly of Israel, including the women, the little ones, and the resident foreigners who lived among them.
</p>

## Notes

v03,12–13: The text names thirty thousand men sent at night, then about five thousand placed in ambush. Their relationship is not explained. The deployment and night notices remain in their given order; the translation does not supply an extra detachment or a reconstructed timeline.

v13–14: “Rear detachment” renders a word associated with the heel or rear. “At the appointed place” can also be understood as “at the appointed time.”

v17: The pinned Hebrew includes Bethel as well as Ai among the places whose men pursued Israel. The name is retained.

v20: “No power to flee” is literally “no hands to flee,” using hands for ability.

v29: The Hebrew says the king was hung on a tree and later calls what was taken down his body. It does not specify here whether hanging was the means of death or the display of a corpse. Removal at sunset recalls Deuteronomy 21:22–23.

v30–35: The altar, inscription, and assembly recall the instructions in Deuteronomy 27. The reading includes both blessing and curse, and the named listeners include women, little ones, and resident foreigners.

## Vocabulary

v18: כִּידוֹן (kidon)
Javelin. Joshua holds it out toward the city as the ambush begins.

v31: שְׁלָמִים (shelamim)
Peace offerings. A distinct sacrificial category alongside burnt offerings.

v32: מִשְׁנֶה (mishneh)
Copy or duplicate. Here a written copy of Moses’ instruction.

v33: גֵּר (ger)
Resident foreigner. Those living among Israel are explicitly included in the assembly.
