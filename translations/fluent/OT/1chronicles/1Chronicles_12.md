## Warriors join David

<p>
v01: These are the men who came to David at Ziklag while he was still kept away from Saul son of Kish. They were among the mighty warriors who helped him in battle.
v02: Armed with bows, they could sling stones and shoot arrows with either hand. They were Saul’s relatives from Benjamin:
v03: Ahiezer the chief, and Joash, sons of Shemaah the Gibeathite; Jeziel and Pelet, sons of Azmaveth; Beracah; Jehu the Anathothite;
v04: Ishmaiah the Gibeonite, a warrior among the Thirty and a leader over them; Jeremiah; Jahaziel; Johanan; Jozabad the Gederathite;
v05: Eluzai; Jerimoth; Bealiah; Shemariah; Shephatiah the Haruphite;
v06: Elkanah, Isshiah, Azarel, Joezer, and Jashobeam, the Korahites;
v07: and Joelah and Zebadiah, sons of Jeroham from Gedor.
</p>

<p>
v08: Some Gadites separated themselves to join David at the wilderness stronghold. They were mighty warriors, trained for battle with shield and spear, with faces like lions and speed like gazelles on the mountains.
v09: Ezer was chief, Obadiah second, Eliab third,
v10: Mishmannah fourth, Jeremiah fifth,
v11: Attai sixth, Eliel seventh,
v12: Johanan eighth, Elzabad ninth,
v13: Jeremiah tenth, and Machbannai eleventh.
v14: These Gadites were army commanders. The least was a match for a hundred, and the greatest for a thousand.
v15: They crossed the Jordan in the first month, when it overflowed all its banks, and drove away everyone in the valleys to the east and west.
</p>

<p>
v16: Some men from Benjamin and Judah came to David at the stronghold.
v17: David went out to meet them. “If you have come in peace to help me, my heart will be united with yours,” he said. “But if you have come to betray me to my enemies, though my hands have done no violence, may the God of our ancestors see and judge.”
v18: Then the Spirit clothed Amasai, chief of the Thirty:
“Yours, David! With you, son of Jesse!
Peace, peace to you,
and peace to those who help you,
for your God helps you.”
David welcomed them and made them leaders of his troops.
</p>

<p>
v19: Some from Manasseh defected to David when he went with the Philistines to fight Saul. But he did not help the Philistines, because their rulers conferred and sent him away: “He will defect to his master Saul at the cost of our heads.”
v20: As David went to Ziklag, these men from Manasseh joined him: Adnah, Jozabad, Jediael, Michael, Jozabad, Elihu, and Zillethai, commanders of thousands in Manasseh.
v21: They helped David against the raiding band. All were mighty warriors, and they became commanders in the army.
</p>

<p>
v22: Day after day men came to help David, until he had a great army, like an army of God.
</p>

## Israel gathers at Hebron

<p>
v23: These are the numbers of the armed troops who came to David at Hebron to turn Saul’s kingdom over to him, as the LORD had said:
v24: From Judah, carrying shield and spear: 6,800 equipped for war.
v25: From Simeon: 7,100 mighty warriors ready for service.
v26: From Levi: 4,600;
v27: also Jehoiada, leader of Aaron’s descendants, with 3,700;
v28: and Zadok, a young mighty warrior, with twenty-two leaders from his ancestral household.
v29: From Benjamin, Saul’s relatives: three thousand. Until then most of them had remained loyal to Saul’s house.
v30: From Ephraim: 20,800 mighty warriors, renowned men in their ancestral households.
v31: From the half-tribe of Manasseh: eighteen thousand, designated by name to come and make David king.
v32: From Issachar came men who understood the times and knew what Israel should do: two hundred heads, with all their relatives under their direction.
v33: From Zebulun: fifty thousand ready for service, prepared for battle with every kind of weapon, able to keep ranks with undivided hearts.
v34: From Naphtali: a thousand commanders, with thirty-seven thousand men carrying shield and spear.
v35: From Dan: 28,600 prepared for battle.
v36: From Asher: forty thousand ready for military service and battle.
v37: From across the Jordan, from Reuben, Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh: one hundred twenty thousand with every kind of weapon.
</p>

<p>
v38: All these fighting men, able to keep their ranks, came to Hebron wholeheartedly to make David king over all Israel. The rest of Israel too was of one heart in making David king.
v39: They stayed with David for three days, eating and drinking, because their relatives had prepared supplies for them.
v40: Their neighbors, even as far as Issachar, Zebulun, and Naphtali, brought food on donkeys, camels, mules, and oxen: flour, fig cakes, raisins, wine, oil, and cattle and flocks in abundance. There was joy in Israel.
</p>

## Notes

v04: This public verse combines Hebrew 12:4–5. Public 12:5–40 then corresponds to Hebrew 12:6–41.

v14: The Hebrew may mean each man was a match for a hundred or a thousand, or that they commanded units of those sizes.

v18: The Spirit “clothed” Amasai: the ancient image is retained. His title can also be understood as chief of officers.

v20: Jozabad occurs twice in the Hebrew list and is retained twice.

v22: “Like an army of God” preserves the Hebrew comparison, which conveys extraordinary size or strength.

## Vocabulary

v18: Peace
Hebrew shalom can include safety, well-being, and a sound relationship; the repeated peace belongs to Amasai’s pledge.

v38: Whole heart
Undivided commitment, connected here with the shared decision to make David king.
