## Willing gifts for the temple

<p>
v01: King David said to the whole assembly, “My son Solomon, the one God has chosen, is young and inexperienced, and the work is great. This palace is for the LORD God, not for a human being.
v02: With all my strength I have prepared for my God’s house: gold for the gold work, silver for the silver, bronze for the bronze, iron for the iron, and wood for the woodwork; onyx stones and stones for settings, dark stones and colored stones, every kind of precious stone, and marble in abundance.
v03: Besides this, because I delight in my God’s house, I am giving my own treasure of gold and silver to my God’s house, beyond everything I have prepared for the holy house:
v04: three thousand talents of gold from Ophir and seven thousand talents of refined silver to overlay the walls of the buildings,
v05: gold for the gold work and silver for the silver work, and for everything the craftsmen make. Who, then, is willing to consecrate himself today to the LORD?”
</p>

<p>
v06: Then the heads of ancestral houses, the tribal leaders of Israel, the commanders of thousands and hundreds, and the officials over the king’s work gave willingly.
v07: For the service of God’s house they gave five thousand talents and ten thousand darics of gold, ten thousand talents of silver, eighteen thousand talents of bronze, and a hundred thousand talents of iron.
v08: Those who had precious stones gave them to the treasury of the LORD’s house, under Jehiel the Gershonite.
v09: The people rejoiced over their willing gifts, for they had given willingly to the LORD with a whole heart. King David also rejoiced greatly.
</p>

## David’s thanksgiving

<p>
v10: David blessed the LORD before the whole assembly. He said, “Blessed are you, LORD, God of Israel our father, from everlasting to everlasting.
v11: Yours, LORD, are greatness, power, splendor, victory, and majesty, for everything in heaven and on earth is yours. Yours, LORD, is the kingdom, and you are exalted as head over all.
v12: Wealth and honor come from you, and you rule over all. Power and might are in your hand, and in your hand is the strength to make anyone great and strong.
v13: Now, our God, we give you thanks and praise your glorious name.
</p>

<p>
v14: But who am I, and who are my people, that we should have the strength to give so willingly? Everything comes from you, and we have given you only what comes from your hand.
v15: We are foreigners and temporary residents before you, like all our ancestors. Our days on earth are like a shadow, with no hope of lasting.
v16: LORD our God, all this abundance we have prepared to build you a house for your holy name comes from your hand, and it all belongs to you.
v17: I know, my God, that you test the heart and delight in uprightness. With an upright heart I have willingly given all these things, and now I have seen your people here giving willingly to you with joy.
</p>

<p>
v18: LORD, God of our ancestors Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, keep this forever in the purposes and thoughts of your people’s hearts. Direct their hearts toward you.
v19: Give my son Solomon a whole heart to keep your commands, testimonies, and statutes, to carry them all out, and to build the palace for which I have made preparations.”
</p>

## Solomon acknowledged as king

<p>
v20: Then David told the whole assembly, “Bless the LORD your God.” The whole assembly blessed the LORD, the God of their ancestors. They bowed low and paid homage to the LORD and to the king.
</p>

<p>
v21: The next day they offered sacrifices to the LORD and presented burnt offerings to him: a thousand bulls, a thousand rams, and a thousand lambs, with their drink offerings, and abundant sacrifices for all Israel.
v22: They ate and drank before the LORD that day with great joy. They made Solomon son of David king a second time, anointing him before the LORD as ruler and Zadok as priest.
</p>

<p>
v23: Solomon sat on the LORD’s throne as king in place of his father David. He prospered, and all Israel obeyed him.
v24: All the officials and warriors, and all King David’s sons as well, pledged their allegiance to King Solomon.
v25: The LORD made Solomon exceedingly great in the eyes of all Israel and gave him royal majesty such as no king over Israel before him had possessed.
</p>

## David’s reign ends

<p>
v26: David son of Jesse had reigned over all Israel.
v27: He reigned over Israel for forty years: seven years in Hebron and thirty-three in Jerusalem.
v28: He died at a good old age, full of days, wealth, and honor. His son Solomon became king in his place.
v29: The acts of King David, from first to last, are written in the records of Samuel the seer, Nathan the prophet, and Gad the seer,
v30: together with his whole reign, his mighty deeds, and the times that passed over him, over Israel, and over all the kingdoms of the lands.
</p>

## Notes

v02: Several ancient stone names are uncertain. Onyx, dark stones, colored stones, and marble are approximate identifications.

v07: Darics are Persian gold coins. The narrator uses a term from a later period; it is not replaced with a presumed coin of David’s time.

v10: “Our father” may attach to Israel, the ancestor Jacob, or be heard as an address to God. The draft keeps it beside Israel.

v15: The Hebrew says “there is no hope”; the shadow and temporary-resident images frame this as human life’s lack of permanence.

v20: The same bowing gesture is directed toward the LORD and the king. The verse does not make them equals.

v22: “A second time” is explicit; compare the earlier installation in 23:1.

## Vocabulary

v05: Consecrate
The Hebrew image is “fill the hand,” a phrase associated with committing someone to sacred service.

v19: Palace
Birah can describe a palace or citadel; here it names the temple as God’s royal dwelling.
