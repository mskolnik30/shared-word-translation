## Israel’s sons

<p>
v01: These were Israel’s sons: Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun,
v02: Dan, Joseph, Benjamin, Naphtali, Gad, and Asher.
</p>

## Judah’s family

<p>
v03: Judah’s sons were Er, Onan, and Shelah. These three were born to him by Shua’s daughter, a Canaanite woman. Judah’s firstborn Er was evil in the LORD’s eyes, and the LORD put him to death.
v04: His daughter-in-law Tamar bore him Perez and Zerah. Judah had five sons in all.
</p>

<p>
v05: Perez’s sons were Hezron and Hamul.
v06: Zerah’s sons were Zimri, Ethan, Heman, Calcol, and Dara—five in all.
</p>

<p>
v07: Carmi’s son was Achar, who brought trouble on Israel by being unfaithful with what was devoted to destruction.
v08: Ethan’s son was Azariah.
</p>

<p>
v09: The sons born to Hezron were Jerahmeel, Ram, and Chelubai.
</p>

## The line to David

<p>
v10: Ram fathered Amminadab, and Amminadab fathered Nahshon, leader of the people of Judah.
v11: Nahshon fathered Salma, and Salma fathered Boaz.
v12: Boaz fathered Obed, and Obed fathered Jesse.
</p>

<p>
v13: Jesse fathered Eliab his firstborn, Abinadab the second, Shimea the third,
v14: Nethanel the fourth, Raddai the fifth,
v15: Ozem the sixth, and David the seventh.
</p>

<p>
v16: Their sisters were Zeruiah and Abigail. Zeruiah’s three sons were Abishai, Joab, and Asahel.
v17: Abigail bore Amasa. Amasa’s father was Jether the Ishmaelite.
</p>

## Caleb and Hezron

<p>
v18: Caleb son of Hezron had children by his wife Azubah and by Jerioth. These were her sons: Jesher, Shobab, and Ardon.
v19: Azubah died, and Caleb married Ephrath, who bore him Hur.
v20: Hur fathered Uri, and Uri fathered Bezalel.
</p>

<p>
v21: Later Hezron went in to the daughter of Machir, Gilead’s father. He married her when he was sixty years old, and she bore him Segub.
v22: Segub fathered Jair, who had twenty-three towns in the land of Gilead.
v23: Geshur and Aram took Havvoth-jair from them, along with Kenath and its villages—sixty towns. All these were descendants of Machir, Gilead’s father.
</p>

<p>
v24: After Hezron died in Caleb-ephrathah, his wife Abijah bore him Ashhur, the father of Tekoa.
</p>

## Jerahmeel’s family

<p>
v25: The sons of Jerahmeel, Hezron’s firstborn, were Ram his firstborn, Bunah, Oren, Ozem, and Ahijah.
v26: Jerahmeel had another wife named Atarah. She was Onam’s mother.
v27: The sons of Ram, Jerahmeel’s firstborn, were Maaz, Jamin, and Eker.
</p>

<p>
v28: Onam’s sons were Shammai and Jada. Shammai’s sons were Nadab and Abishur.
v29: Abishur’s wife was named Abihail. She bore him Ahban and Molid.
</p>

<p>
v30: Nadab’s sons were Seled and Appaim. Seled died without sons.
v31: Appaim’s son was Ishi, Ishi’s son was Sheshan, and Sheshan’s descendant was Ahlai.
</p>

<p>
v32: The sons of Jada, Shammai’s brother, were Jether and Jonathan. Jether died without sons.
v33: Jonathan’s sons were Peleth and Zaza. These were Jerahmeel’s descendants.
</p>

<p>
v34: Sheshan had no sons, only daughters. He had an Egyptian slave named Jarha.
v35: Sheshan gave his daughter in marriage to his slave Jarha, and she bore him Attai.
</p>

<p>
v36: Attai fathered Nathan, and Nathan fathered Zabad.
v37: Zabad fathered Ephlal, and Ephlal fathered Obed.
v38: Obed fathered Jehu, and Jehu fathered Azariah.
v39: Azariah fathered Helez, and Helez fathered Eleasah.
v40: Eleasah fathered Sismai, and Sismai fathered Shallum.
v41: Shallum fathered Jekamiah, and Jekamiah fathered Elishama.
</p>

## Further families of Caleb

<p>
v42: The descendants of Caleb, Jerahmeel’s brother, included Mesha his firstborn, the father of Ziph, and the sons of Mareshah, the father of Hebron.
v43: Hebron’s sons were Korah, Tappuah, Rekem, and Shema.
v44: Shema fathered Raham, the father of Jorkeam, and Rekem fathered Shammai.
v45: Shammai’s son was Maon, and Maon was the father of Beth-zur.
</p>

<p>
v46: Caleb’s concubine Ephah bore Haran, Moza, and Gazez. Haran fathered Gazez.
v47: Jahdai’s sons were Regem, Jotham, Geshan, Pelet, Ephah, and Shaaph.
</p>

<p>
v48: Caleb’s concubine Maacah bore Sheber and Tirhanah.
v49: She also bore Shaaph, the father of Madmannah, and Sheva, the father of Machbenah and Gibea. Caleb’s daughter was Achsah.
</p>

<p>
v50: These were the descendants of Caleb son of Hur, Ephrathah’s firstborn: Shobal, the father of Kiriath-jearim,
v51: Salma, the father of Bethlehem, and Hareph, the father of Beth-gader.
</p>

<p>
v52: Shobal, the father of Kiriath-jearim, had these descendants: Haroeh and half the Menuhoth.
v53: The clans of Kiriath-jearim were the Ithrites, Puthites, Shumathites, and Mishraites. From these came the Zorathites and Eshtaolites.
</p>

<p>
v54: Salma’s descendants were Bethlehem, the Netophathites, Atroth-beth-joab, half the Manahathites, and the Zorites.
v55: The clans of scribes living at Jabez were the Tirathites, Shimeathites, and Sucathites. These were the Kenites who came from Hammath, the father of the house of Rechab.
</p>

## Notes

v07: Achar resembles the Hebrew word for bringing trouble. Joshua calls him Achan. His offense concerns property devoted to destruction.

v15: David is listed as Jesse’s seventh son here; Samuel’s account presents eight sons. The list is not expanded with an invented name.

v18: The Hebrew syntax around Azubah and Jerioth is difficult, and the referent of “her sons” is not explicit. Both names and the uncertainty are retained.

v24: The pinned wording can be read as the place Caleb-ephrathah. Other construals rearrange the relationship between Caleb and Ephrathah; the draft does not silently adopt them.

v31: Ahlai is introduced with the flexible term “sons/descendants.” Verse 34 explicitly says Sheshan had daughters but no sons.

v50: The Hebrew has singular “son of Hur,” which can attach to Caleb as rendered. Reading a new heading, “the sons of Hur,” would connect the names differently. The tension with Hur’s earlier descent from Caleb is not silently repaired.

## Vocabulary

v45: Father of a town
In these records, father can identify an ancestor, founder, or leading clan associated with a settlement, rather than the biological father of a place.

v55: Scribes
People trained in writing and the preservation or administration of records.
