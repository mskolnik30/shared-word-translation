## David established as king

<p>
v01: Huram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, along with cedar timber, stoneworkers, and woodworkers, to build him a house.
v02: David knew that the LORD had established him as king over Israel and had raised his kingdom high for the sake of his people Israel.
v03: In Jerusalem David took more wives, and he fathered more sons and daughters.
</p>

<p>
v04: These are the names of the children born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon,
v05: Ibhar, Elishua, Elpelet,
v06: Nogah, Nepheg, Japhia,
v07: Elishama, Beeliada, and Eliphelet.
</p>

## Battles with the Philistines

<p>
v08: When the Philistines heard that David had been anointed king over all Israel, they all went up to seek him out. David heard about it and went out to face them.
v09: The Philistines came and raided the Valley of Rephaim.
v10: David asked God, “Should I go up against the Philistines? Will you give them into my hand?” The LORD answered him, “Go up. I will give them into your hand.”
v11: They went up to Baal-perazim, and David defeated them there. David said, “God has broken through my enemies by my hand, like water bursting through.” That is why they named the place Baal-perazim.
v12: The Philistines left their gods there, and David ordered them burned.
</p>

<p>
v13: Once again the Philistines raided the valley.
v14: David asked God again, and God told him, “Do not go up after them. Circle around them and approach them opposite the balsam trees.
v15: When you hear the sound of marching in the treetops, go out to battle. God will have gone out ahead of you to strike the Philistine camp.”
v16: David did as God commanded. They struck down the Philistine army from Gibeon to Gezer.
v17: David’s fame spread through all the lands, and the LORD made all the nations fear him.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Huram follows the Hebrew read form; the written form is Hiram.

v05–07: Elpelet, Beeliada, and Eliphelet are retained as this list gives them.

v11: Baal-perazim is linked to “breaking through,” like the force of water bursting out.

v14: The tree called baka is not certainly identified; balsam is a traditional identification.

v16: This account reads Gibeon, whereas 2 Samuel 5:25 reads Geba.

## Vocabulary

v02: For the sake of his people
The verse states the benefit to Israel as the reason for the kingdom’s elevation.

v14: Seek guidance
David asks again rather than assuming the earlier battle instruction applies unchanged.
