## David orders a census

<p>
v01: Satan stood against Israel and incited David to count Israel.
v02: David told Joab and the leaders of the people, “Go, count Israel from Beersheba to Dan, and report back to me so I can know their number.”
v03: Joab said, “May the LORD multiply his people a hundred times over! My lord the king, aren’t they all your servants? Why does my lord want this? Why should it bring guilt on Israel?”
v04: But the king’s command prevailed over Joab. Joab left, traveled throughout Israel, and returned to Jerusalem.
v05: Joab gave David the total from the people’s registration. All Israel had 1,100,000 men who could draw a sword, and Judah had 470,000 men who could draw a sword.
v06: He did not count Levi and Benjamin among them, because Joab found the king’s command abhorrent.
</p>

<p>
v07: This matter was evil in God’s eyes, and he struck Israel.
v08: David said to God, “I have sinned greatly by doing this. Now, please take away your servant’s guilt, for I have acted very foolishly.”
</p>

## Three judgments

<p>
v09: The LORD spoke to Gad, David’s seer:
v10: “Go and tell David: This is what the LORD says: I am setting three things before you. Choose one of them, and I will do it to you.”
v11: Gad came to David and said, “This is what the LORD says: Take your choice:
v12: three years of famine; three months of being swept away before your foes, with your enemies’ sword overtaking you; or three days of the LORD’s sword—plague in the land, with the LORD’s angel destroying throughout Israel’s territory. Now decide what answer I should take to the one who sent me.”
v13: David told Gad, “I am in deep distress. Please let me fall into the LORD’s hand, for his compassion is very great. Do not let me fall into human hands.”
</p>

<p>
v14: So the LORD sent a plague on Israel, and seventy thousand Israelites fell.
v15: God sent an angel to destroy Jerusalem. As the angel was destroying it, the LORD saw and relented from the calamity. He told the destroying angel, “Enough! Now withdraw your hand.” The LORD’s angel was standing by the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite.
</p>

<p>
v16: David looked up and saw the LORD’s angel standing between earth and heaven, with a drawn sword in his hand stretched out over Jerusalem. David and the elders, dressed in sackcloth, fell facedown.
v17: David said to God, “Wasn’t I the one who ordered the people counted? I am the one who sinned and did such great wrong. But these sheep—what have they done? LORD my God, let your hand fall on me and my father’s house, but do not strike your people with a plague.”
</p>

## An altar at Ornan’s threshing floor

<p>
v18: The LORD’s angel told Gad to tell David to go up and build an altar to the LORD on the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite.
v19: David went up at Gad’s word, which Gad had spoken in the LORD’s name.
</p>

<p>
v20: Ornan turned and saw the angel. His four sons who were with him hid themselves. Ornan had been threshing wheat.
v21: David came to Ornan. When Ornan looked and saw David, he left the threshing floor and bowed before David with his face to the ground.
</p>

<p>
v22: David told Ornan, “Sell me the site of this threshing floor so I can build an altar to the LORD on it. Let me have it for the full price, so the plague on the people may stop.”
v23: Ornan said to David, “Take it! Let my lord the king do whatever seems good to him. Look, I give you the oxen for burnt offerings, the threshing sledges for wood, and the wheat for the grain offering. I give it all.”
v24: But King David told Ornan, “No. I will buy it for the full price. I will not take what belongs to you for the LORD or offer a burnt offering that costs me nothing.”
v25: David paid Ornan six hundred shekels of gold by weight for the site.
</p>

<p>
v26: David built an altar to the LORD there and presented burnt offerings and well-being offerings. He called to the LORD, who answered him with fire from heaven on the altar of burnt offering.
v27: Then the LORD commanded the angel, and he returned his sword to its sheath.
</p>

<p>
v28: At that time, when David saw that the LORD had answered him at Ornan the Jebusite’s threshing floor, he offered sacrifices there.
v29: The LORD’s tabernacle, which Moses had made in the wilderness, and the altar of burnt offering were then at the high place in Gibeon.
v30: But David could not go before it to seek God, because he was terrified of the sword of the LORD’s angel.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Satan can be read as a name or as “an adversary.” Chronicles’ wording differs from 2 Samuel 24:1, which attributes the incitement to the LORD.

v05: The totals differ from 2 Samuel 24:9; Chronicles also states that Levi and Benjamin were not counted.

v15: The narration retains both the beginning of destruction and the LORD’s relenting; it does not supply a timeline to remove the tension.

v25: The price here is six hundred shekels of gold for the site. Samuel gives fifty shekels of silver for the threshing floor and oxen; the difference remains visible.

## Vocabulary

v09: Seer
A person receiving divine revelation, a title related to prophetic sight.

v16: Sackcloth
Coarse cloth worn in mourning, distress, or repentance.
