## Issachar

<p>
v01: Issachar’s sons were Tola, Puah, Jashub, and Shimron—four.
v02: Tola’s sons were Uzzi, Rephaiah, Jeriel, Jahmai, Ibsam, and Shemuel. They headed Tola’s ancestral households, mighty warriors listed by their generations. In David’s days they numbered 22,600.
v03: Uzzi’s son was Izrahiah. Izrahiah’s sons were Michael, Obadiah, Joel, and Isshiah—five heads in all.
v04: With them, by generation and ancestral household, were 36,000 troops ready for war, for they had many wives and sons.
v05: Their relatives throughout Issachar’s clans were mighty warriors, 87,000 in all according to their genealogical records.
</p>

## Benjamin and Naphtali

<p>
v06: Benjamin had three sons: Bela, Becher, and Jediael.
v07: Bela’s sons were Ezbon, Uzzi, Uzziel, Jerimoth, and Iri—five ancestral household heads and mighty warriors. Their genealogical records listed 22,034.
v08: Becher’s sons were Zemirah, Joash, Eliezer, Elioenai, Omri, Jeremoth, Abijah, Anathoth, and Alemeth. All these were Becher’s sons.
v09: Their genealogical records, arranged by generations and ancestral household heads, listed 20,200 mighty warriors.
v10: Jediael’s son was Bilhan. Bilhan’s sons were Jeush, Benjamin, Ehud, Chenaanah, Zethan, Tarshish, and Ahishahar.
v11: All these were Jediael’s descendants, ancestral household heads and mighty warriors, with 17,200 men able to go out to war.
</p>

<p>
v12: Shuppim and Huppim were descendants of Ir; Hushim was a descendant of Aher.
</p>

<p>
v13: Naphtali’s sons were Jahziel, Guni, Jezer, and Shallum, descendants of Bilhah.
</p>

## Manasseh

<p>
v14: Manasseh’s descendants included Asriel, whom his Aramean concubine bore. She also bore Machir, Gilead’s father.
v15: Machir took a wife from Huppim and Shuppim. His sister’s name was Maacah. The second was named Zelophehad, and Zelophehad had daughters.
v16: Machir’s wife Maacah bore a son and named him Peresh. His brother was named Sheresh, whose sons were Ulam and Rakem.
v17: Ulam’s son was Bedan. These were descendants of Gilead son of Machir, son of Manasseh.
</p>

<p>
v18: His sister Hammolecheth bore Ishhod, Abiezer, and Mahlah.
v19: Shemida’s sons were Ahian, Shechem, Likhi, and Aniam.
</p>

## Ephraim

<p>
v20: Ephraim’s line continued through Shuthelah, then Bered his son, Tahath, Eleadah, and Tahath,
v21: then Zabad and Shuthelah. Ezer and Elead were killed by the men native to Gath, because they had gone down to take their livestock.
v22: Their father Ephraim mourned for many days, and his brothers came to comfort him.
</p>

<p>
v23: He slept with his wife, and she conceived and bore a son. He named him Beriah because disaster had come upon his house.
v24: His daughter Sheerah built Lower and Upper Beth-horon and Uzzen-sheerah.
</p>

<p>
v25: His son was Rephah; also Resheph, then Telah his son, and Tahan,
v26: then Ladan, Ammihud, Elishama,
v27: Nun, and Joshua.
</p>

<p>
v28: Their holdings and settlements included Bethel and its villages; Naaran to the east; Gezer and its villages to the west; and Shechem and its villages, as far as Ayyah and its villages.
v29: Along Manasseh’s territory were Beth-shean, Taanach, Megiddo, and Dor, each with its villages. The descendants of Joseph son of Israel lived in these places.
</p>

## Asher

<p>
v30: Asher’s sons were Imnah, Ishvah, Ishvi, and Beriah; Serah was their sister.
v31: Beriah’s sons were Heber and Malchiel, the father of Birzaith.
v32: Heber fathered Japhlet, Shomer, Hotham, and their sister Shua.
v33: Japhlet’s sons were Pasach, Bimhal, and Ashvath. These were Japhlet’s sons.
v34: Shemer’s sons were Ahi, Rohgah, Hubbah, and Aram.
</p>

<p>
v35: His brother Helem’s sons were Zophah, Imna, Shelesh, and Amal.
v36: Zophah’s sons were Suah, Harnepher, Shual, Beri, Imrah,
v37: Bezer, Hod, Shamma, Shilshah, Ithran, and Beera.
v38: Jether’s sons were Jephunneh, Pispah, and Ara.

</p>

## Notes

v03: Five leaders are stated, though four sons follow Izrahiah’s name. The total may include Izrahiah; the text does not explain it.

v14–15: The family connections in this passage are uncertain. The relationships involving Asriel, the Aramean concubine, Huppim, Shuppim, Maacah, and the “second” Zelophehad cannot all be settled from this wording.

v21: The pronouns do not clearly identify which group went down to seize whose livestock.

v23: Beriah sounds like the Hebrew phrase “in trouble,” linking the child’s name to the household’s calamity.

v24: Sheerah is explicitly a daughter and is credited with building the three named towns.

v25: The relationship of Resheph to the surrounding names is not fully stated.

## Vocabulary

v02: Ancestral house
A family group identified through a shared ancestor.

v13: Bilhah
The mother of Dan and Naphtali; see Genesis 30:3–8.
