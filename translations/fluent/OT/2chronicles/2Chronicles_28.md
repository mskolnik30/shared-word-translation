## Ahaz’s unfaithfulness

<p>
v01: Ahaz was twenty when he became king, and he ruled in Jerusalem for sixteen years. Unlike his ancestor David, he did not do what was right in the LORD's eyes.
v02: He followed the ways of Israel's kings and even made cast images for the Baals.
v03: He burned offerings in the Valley of Ben-hinnom and burned his sons in the fire, following the detestable practices of the nations the LORD had driven out before Israel.
v04: He sacrificed and burned offerings at the high places, on the hills, and under every flourishing tree.
</p>

## Defeat and captives

<p>
v05: The LORD his God handed him over to the king of Aram. The Arameans defeated him, took a great many captives, and brought them to Damascus. He was also handed over to Israel's king, who inflicted a devastating defeat on him.
v06: In a single day, Pekah son of Remaliah killed one hundred twenty thousand in Judah, all capable warriors, because they had abandoned the LORD, the God of their ancestors.
</p>

<p>
v07: Zichri, an Ephraimite warrior, killed Maaseiah the king's son, Azrikam the palace administrator, and Elkanah, second to the king.
v08: The Israelites took two hundred thousand captives from their own kin—women, sons, and daughters. They also seized a great deal of plunder and brought it to Samaria.
</p>

## A warning to the victors

<p>
v09: But a prophet of the LORD named Oded was there. He went out to meet the army returning to Samaria and said, “The LORD, the God of your ancestors, was angry with Judah and handed them over to you. Yet you have slaughtered them in a rage that has reached heaven.
v10: And now you intend to force the people of Judah and Jerusalem into slavery as your men and women slaves. Do you not have guilt of your own before the LORD your God?
v11: Listen to me now. Return the captives you have taken from your own kin, for the LORD's burning anger is against you.”
</p>

<p>
v12: Some of the Ephraimite leaders confronted those returning from the army: Azariah son of Jehohanan, Berechiah son of Meshillemoth, Jehizkiah son of Shallum, and Amasa son of Hadlai.
v13: They told them, “You must not bring the captives here. What you propose will make us guilty before the LORD, adding to our sins and guilt. Our guilt is already great, and burning anger hangs over Israel.”
</p>

<p>
v14: So the armed men left the captives and the plunder before the officials and the whole assembly.
</p>

<p>
v15: The men named above took charge of the captives. From the plunder they clothed everyone who was naked. They gave them clothes and sandals, food and drink, and rubbed them with oil. They put all who were weak on donkeys and brought them to their own kin at Jericho, the City of Palms. Then they returned to Samaria.
</p>

## Help that does not help

<p>
v16: At that time King Ahaz sent to the kings of Assyria for help.
v17: The Edomites had come again, attacked Judah, and taken captives.
</p>

<p>
v18: The Philistines had raided the cities of Judah's foothills and Negev. They captured Beth-shemesh, Aijalon, Gederoth, Soco and its villages, Timnah and its villages, and Gimzo and its villages, and settled there.
</p>

<p>
v19: The LORD brought Judah low because of Ahaz king of Israel, for he had let Judah run wild and had been utterly unfaithful to the LORD.
v20: Tilgath-pilneser king of Assyria came to him, but brought him distress instead of strength.
v21: Ahaz stripped resources from the LORD's house, the palace, and the officials and gave them to Assyria's king, but it did not help him.
</p>

<p>
v22: Even in his distress, this same King Ahaz became still more unfaithful to the LORD.
v23: He sacrificed to the gods of Damascus that had struck him, saying, “The gods of Aram's kings help them. I will sacrifice to those gods so they will help me.” But they brought about his downfall and that of all Israel.
</p>

<p>
v24: Ahaz gathered the utensils from God's house and cut them to pieces. He shut the doors of the LORD's house and made altars for himself on every corner of Jerusalem.
v25: In every city of Judah he made high places for burning offerings to other gods. He provoked the LORD, the God of his ancestors, to anger.
</p>

<p>
v26: The rest of his acts and all his ways, from first to last, are written in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel.
v27: Ahaz rested with his ancestors. They buried him in the city, in Jerusalem, but did not bring him into the tombs of Israel's kings. His son Hezekiah succeeded him as king.
</p>

## Notes

v03: This verse says that Ahaz burned his sons in the fire. The harm is explicit in the Hebrew.

v08–15: The captives are kin of their captors. Oded challenges the plan to enslave them, and the account lists concrete acts of care before their return.

v16: The Hebrew has kings of Assyria in the plural here, although one king is named in verse 20.

v19: The title king of Israel is retained as written; Ahaz ruled Judah.

v20: Tilgath-pilneser preserves the form of the Assyrian king’s name used here in Chronicles.

## Vocabulary

v02: Baals
Baal can be a divine title or name; the plural here refers to the objects of this worship.

v10: Men and women slaves
The Hebrew distinguishes male and female enslavement. Servants would obscure the coercion in this proposed action.
