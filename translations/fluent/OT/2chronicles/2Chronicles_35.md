## Josiah’s Passover

<p>
v01: Josiah celebrated a Passover to the LORD in Jerusalem. They slaughtered the Passover animals on the fourteenth day of the first month.
v02: He assigned the priests to their duties and encouraged them in the service of the LORD's house.
</p>

<p>
v03: He told the Levites who taught all Israel and were holy to the LORD, “Put the holy ark in the house built by Solomon son of David, king of Israel. You no longer have to carry it on your shoulders. Now serve the LORD your God and his people Israel.
</p>

<p>
v04: Prepare yourselves by ancestral families in your divisions, following the written directions of David king of Israel and of his son Solomon.
v05: Stand in the sanctuary by the ancestral family groups of your fellow people, with a division of Levite families for each.
v06: Slaughter the Passover animals, consecrate yourselves, and prepare for your fellow people, carrying out the LORD's word given through Moses.”
</p>

<p>
v07: Josiah contributed from his own possessions thirty thousand lambs and young goats for the people's Passover offerings, for everyone present, and three thousand cattle.
</p>

<p>
v08: His officials also gave freewill contributions to the people, priests, and Levites. Hilkiah, Zechariah, and Jehiel, officials of God's house, gave the priests two thousand six hundred Passover animals and three hundred cattle.
</p>

<p>
v09: Cananiah and his brothers Shemaiah and Nethanel, along with Hashabiah, Jeiel, and Jozabad, leaders of the Levites, contributed five thousand Passover animals and five hundred cattle for the Levites.
</p>

## The service and the meal

<p>
v10: The service was arranged. The priests took their places and the Levites their divisions, as the king had commanded.
v11: They slaughtered the Passover animals. The priests splashed the blood handed to them, while the Levites skinned the animals.
</p>

<p>
v12: They set aside the burnt offerings to distribute them to the ancestral family groups of the people, to offer to the LORD as written in the book of Moses. They did the same with the cattle.
</p>

<p>
v13: They cooked the Passover over the fire as prescribed. The sacred offerings they cooked in pots, kettles, and pans, and hurried them out to all the people.
</p>

<p>
v14: Afterward they prepared food for themselves and the priests, because the priests, Aaron's descendants, were offering the burnt offerings and fat until night. So the Levites prepared for themselves and for the priests, Aaron's descendants.
</p>

<p>
v15: The singers, Asaph's descendants, remained at their stations according to the command of David, Asaph, Heman, and Jeduthun the king's seer. The gatekeepers stayed at every gate. They did not need to leave their service, because their fellow Levites prepared food for them.
</p>

<p>
v16: All the LORD's service was arranged that day for celebrating the Passover and offering burnt offerings on the LORD's altar, as King Josiah commanded.
v17: The Israelites present celebrated the Passover at that time and the Festival of Unleavened Bread for seven days.
</p>

<p>
v18: No Passover like it had been celebrated in Israel since the days of Samuel the prophet. None of Israel's kings had celebrated a Passover like the one Josiah celebrated with the priests, Levites, all Judah and Israel who were present, and Jerusalem's inhabitants.
v19: This Passover was celebrated in the eighteenth year of Josiah's reign.
</p>

## Josiah’s final battle

<p>
v20: After all this, when Josiah had put the house in order, Neco king of Egypt marched up to fight at Carchemish on the Euphrates. Josiah went out to meet him.
</p>

<p>
v21: Neco sent messengers to him: “What is there between you and me, king of Judah? I have not come against you today, but against the house with which I am at war. God has told me to hurry. Stop opposing God, who is with me, or he will destroy you.”
</p>

<p>
v22: But Josiah would not turn away from him. He disguised himself to fight him and did not listen to Neco's words from God's mouth. He went to fight in the plain of Megiddo.
</p>

<p>
v23: The archers shot King Josiah. He told his servants, “Take me away. I am badly wounded.”
v24: His servants moved him out of his chariot, put him in his second chariot, and brought him to Jerusalem. He died and was buried in his ancestors' tombs. All Judah and Jerusalem mourned Josiah.
</p>

<p>
v25: Jeremiah composed a lament for Josiah. To this day all the male and female singers speak of Josiah in their laments. They made these a lasting practice in Israel, and they are written in the Laments.
</p>

<p>
v26: The rest of Josiah's acts, his deeds of loyal love in keeping with what is written in the LORD's law,
v27: and his acts from first to last are written in the Book of the Kings of Israel and Judah.
</p>

## Notes

v03: The command to place the ark in the house does not explain where it had been or why. No removal story is supplied.

v09: Cananiah follows the read form, consistently with 31:12–13.

v13: The Hebrew uses the same general cooking verb for the Passover over the fire and the other sacred offerings in vessels. The fire description is retained; compare Exodus 12:8–9.

v21–22: Neco first claims that God has sent him; verse 22 itself describes Neco’s words as from God’s mouth. The translation keeps both levels of attribution.

v24: This account places the journey to Jerusalem before the report of Josiah’s death. It is not rewritten to match the wording of 2 Kings 23:29–30.

v25: The Laments is the collection named by the text. It need not be identified with the biblical book now called Lamentations. Male and female singers are both explicitly present.

## Vocabulary

v07: Passover offerings
The lambs and young goats provided for the Passover meal; the cattle are separately counted.

v25: Lament
A formal expression of mourning. Here Josiah’s death becomes part of continuing communal remembrance.
