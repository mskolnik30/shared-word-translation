## Solomon seeks wisdom

<p>
v01: Solomon son of David strengthened his hold on his kingdom. The LORD his God was with him and made him exceedingly great.
v02: Solomon spoke to all Israel: the commanders of thousands and hundreds, the judges, and every leader in Israel, the heads of ancestral houses.
v03: Solomon and the whole assembly with him went to the high place at Gibeon. God’s tent of meeting was there, the one Moses the LORD’s servant had made in the wilderness.
v04: David, however, had brought God’s ark up from Kiriath-jearim to the place he had prepared for it, for he had pitched a tent for it in Jerusalem.
v05: The bronze altar made by Bezalel son of Uri, son of Hur, stood before the LORD’s tabernacle. Solomon and the assembly sought the LORD there.
v06: Solomon went up there to the bronze altar before the LORD at the tent of meeting and presented a thousand burnt offerings on it.
</p>

<p>
v07: That night God appeared to Solomon and said, “Ask what I should give you.”
v08: Solomon said to God, “You have shown great faithful love to my father David and have made me king in his place.
v09: Now, LORD God, let your word to my father David stand firm. You have made me king over a people as numerous as the dust of the earth.
v10: Give me wisdom and knowledge so I can go out and come in before this people. For who can govern this great people of yours?”
v11: God said to Solomon, “Because this was in your heart, and you did not ask for wealth, possessions, honor, the lives of those who hate you, or even many years of life, but asked for wisdom and knowledge to govern my people over whom I made you king,
v12: wisdom and knowledge are given to you. I will also give you wealth, possessions, and honor such as no king before you has had and none after you will have.”
v13: Solomon came from the high place at Gibeon, from before the tent of meeting, to Jerusalem, and he reigned over Israel.
</p>

## Chariots and trade

<p>
v14: Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen. He had fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen, which he stationed in the chariot towns and with the king in Jerusalem.
v15: The king made silver and gold as common in Jerusalem as stones, and cedar as plentiful as the sycamore figs in the foothills.
v16: Solomon’s horses were imported from Egypt and Kue. The king’s merchants bought them from Kue at the going price.
v17: They imported a chariot from Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver and a horse for a hundred fifty. Through these merchants they also exported them to all the Hittite and Aramean kings.
</p>

## Notes

v05: The pronoun after “sought” may refer to God or to the altar. The draft understands seeking God at the altar.

v13: The Hebrew preposition before “high place” is difficult in this departure notice. The draft follows the movement from Gibeon to Jerusalem.

v16: Kue is commonly understood as a region in Cilicia. The Hebrew term has also been understood in connection with a group or collection of horses.

## Vocabulary

v10: Go out and come in
A traditional image for leading the people in their public life.

v17: Shekel
A unit of weight for metal; the unit is implied rather than written in this price notice.
