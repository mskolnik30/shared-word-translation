## Uzziah seeks God

<p>
v01: All the people of Judah took Uzziah, who was sixteen years old, and made him king in place of his father Amaziah.
v02: He rebuilt Eloth and restored it to Judah after the king rested with his ancestors.
</p>

<p>
v03: Uzziah was sixteen when he became king, and he ruled in Jerusalem for fifty-two years. His mother's name was Jecoliah, from Jerusalem.
v04: He did what was right in the LORD's eyes, following all that his father Amaziah had done.
</p>

<p>
v05: He sought God during the lifetime of Zechariah, who understood visions of God. As long as he sought the LORD, God gave him success.
</p>

## Strength and building

<p>
v06: He went out to war against the Philistines and broke down the walls of Gath, Jabneh, and Ashdod. He built cities in the Ashdod region and elsewhere among the Philistines.
v07: God helped him against the Philistines, the Arabs living in Gur-baal, and the Meunites.
v08: The Ammonites paid tribute to Uzziah. His reputation reached as far as the entrance to Egypt, for he had become extraordinarily strong.
</p>

<p>
v09: Uzziah built towers in Jerusalem at the Corner Gate, the Valley Gate, and the angle of the wall, and fortified them.
v10: He built towers in the wilderness and cut many cisterns, because he had large herds, both in the foothills and on the plain. He had farmers and vinedressers in the hills and fertile fields, for he loved the soil.
</p>

<p>
v11: Uzziah had an army ready for war, going out in divisions according to the muster prepared by Jeiel the scribe and Maaseiah the officer, under Hananiah, one of the king's commanders.
v12: The total number of ancestral family heads among the capable warriors was two thousand six hundred.
v13: Under them was an army of three hundred seven thousand five hundred men, a powerful fighting force to help the king against the enemy.
</p>

<p>
v14: Uzziah supplied the whole army with shields, spears, helmets, armor, bows, and slingstones.
v15: In Jerusalem he made devices designed by skilled men for the towers and corners, to shoot arrows and large stones. His reputation spread far, for he was wonderfully helped until he became strong.
</p>

## Uzziah enters the sanctuary

<p>
v16: But once he became strong, his heart grew proud, to his ruin. He was unfaithful to the LORD his God and entered the LORD's temple to burn incense on the incense altar.
</p>

<p>
v17: Azariah the priest went in after him with eighty courageous priests of the LORD.
v18: They confronted King Uzziah: “It is not for you, Uzziah, to burn incense to the LORD. That belongs to the priests, Aaron's descendants, who are consecrated to burn incense. Leave the sanctuary! You have been unfaithful, and the LORD God will not honor you for this.”
</p>

<p>
v19: Uzziah became furious. He had a censer in his hand to burn incense, and while he raged at the priests, a skin disease broke out on his forehead before them in the LORD's house, beside the incense altar.
v20: Azariah the chief priest and all the priests turned toward him and saw the disease on his forehead. They hurried him out. He himself rushed to leave, because the LORD had struck him.
</p>

<p>
v21: King Uzziah had the skin disease until the day he died. He lived in a separate house because of it, cut off from the LORD's house. His son Jotham was in charge of the palace and governed the people of the land.
</p>

<p>
v22: Isaiah son of Amoz the prophet wrote the rest of Uzziah's acts, from first to last.
v23: Uzziah rested with his ancestors. They buried him beside them in the burial field belonging to the kings, for they said, “He had the skin disease.” His son Jotham succeeded him as king.
</p>

## Notes

v05: The pinned wording concerns seeing God and is rendered visions of God. A different reading yields instruction in the fear of God. The verse does not identify this Zechariah with a better-known prophet.

v11: Jeiel follows the source’s read form; the written name is Jeuel.

v15: The text describes designed devices for arrows and stones but does not give enough detail to identify their mechanism.

v19–21: The Hebrew tsaraat names a serious skin condition with ritual consequences. It is not a precise diagnosis of modern leprosy.

v21: Separate house interprets a difficult expression associated with freedom or exemption. The following clause makes exclusion from the LORD’s house explicit.

## Vocabulary

v16: Heart grew proud
Literally, his heart became high. Strength gives way to self-exaltation in the narrative.

v19: Censer
A vessel used for burning incense.
