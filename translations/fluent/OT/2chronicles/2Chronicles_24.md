## Joash restores the house

<p>
v01: Joash was seven when he became king, and he ruled in Jerusalem for forty years. His mother's name was Zibiah, from Beer-sheba.
v02: Joash did what was right in the LORD's eyes throughout the lifetime of Jehoiada the priest.
v03: Jehoiada obtained two wives for him, and Joash had sons and daughters.
</p>

<p>
v04: Some time later, Joash set his heart on restoring the LORD's house.
v05: He gathered the priests and Levites and told them, “Go out to Judah's cities and collect money from all Israel every year to repair your God's house. See to it quickly.” But the Levites did not act quickly.
</p>

<p>
v06: The king summoned Jehoiada the chief priest and asked, “Why have you not required the Levites to bring in from Judah and Jerusalem the levy of Moses, the LORD's servant, and Israel's assembly for the tent of the testimony?”
v07: For the sons of that wicked woman Athaliah had broken into God's house and even used all the sacred things from the LORD's house for the Baals.
</p>

<p>
v08: At the king's command, they made a chest and placed it outside the gate of the LORD's house.
v09: They issued a proclamation in Judah and Jerusalem to bring to the LORD the levy that Moses, God's servant, had imposed on Israel in the wilderness.
v10: All the officials and all the people were glad to bring their contributions and drop them into the chest until they had finished.
</p>

<p>
v11: Whenever the chest was brought by the Levites to the king's officers and they saw that it held a large sum, the king's scribe and the chief priest's officer came and emptied it. They carried it back to its place. They did this day after day and collected a great deal of money.
</p>

<p>
v12: The king and Jehoiada gave the money to those overseeing the work on the LORD's house. They hired stonecutters and carpenters to restore it, as well as workers in iron and bronze to repair it.
v13: The workers went about their tasks, and under their hands the repairs progressed. They restored God's house to its proper condition and made it strong.
v14: When they finished, they brought the remaining money to the king and Jehoiada. It was used to make utensils for the LORD's house: utensils for service and burnt offerings, dishes, and gold and silver vessels. Burnt offerings were offered regularly in the LORD's house throughout Jehoiada's lifetime.
</p>

## After Jehoiada’s death

<p>
v15: Jehoiada grew old, full of days, and died. He was one hundred thirty years old when he died.
v16: They buried him in the City of David with the kings, because he had done good in Israel, toward God and his house.
</p>

<p>
v17: After Jehoiada died, Judah's officials came and bowed before the king. Then the king listened to them.
v18: They abandoned the house of the LORD, the God of their ancestors, and served the Asherah poles and idols. Wrath came upon Judah and Jerusalem because of this guilt.
</p>

<p>
v19: He sent prophets among them to bring them back to the LORD. The prophets warned them, but they would not listen.
</p>

<p>
v20: Then God's Spirit clothed Zechariah son of Jehoiada the priest. He stood above the people and told them, “This is what God says: Why are you breaking the LORD's commands? You will not prosper. Because you have abandoned the LORD, he has abandoned you.”
v21: They conspired against him and, at the king's command, stoned him in the court of the LORD's house.
v22: King Joash did not remember the loyal love that Jehoiada, Zechariah's father, had shown him. Yet he killed Jehoiada's son. As Zechariah was dying, he said, “May the LORD see and call you to account!”
</p>

## Joash’s downfall

<p>
v23: At the turn of the year, the Aramean army marched against Joash. They entered Judah and Jerusalem, destroyed all the people's officials, and sent all their plunder to the king of Damascus.
v24: The Aramean army came with few men, yet the LORD handed over a very large army to them because Judah had abandoned the LORD, the God of their ancestors. They executed judgment on Joash.
</p>

<p>
v25: When they withdrew, they left him gravely wounded. His servants conspired against him because of the blood of Jehoiada the priest's sons. They killed him on his bed, and he died. They buried him in the City of David, but not in the royal tombs.
v26: Those who conspired against him were Zabad son of Shimeath the Ammonite woman and Jehozabad son of Shimrith the Moabite woman.
</p>

<p>
v27: The account of his sons, the many pronouncements against him, and the rebuilding of God's house is written in the exposition of the Book of the Kings. His son Amaziah succeeded him as king.
</p>

## Notes

v03: The sentence is understood as Jehoiada arranging two marriages for Joash.

v06: The levy recalls the contribution for the wilderness sanctuary; compare Exodus 30:11–16.

v20: Clothed preserves the Hebrew image of God’s Spirit coming upon Zechariah for his prophetic speech.

v25: The Hebrew says sons of Jehoiada, although this chapter has named one murdered son, Zechariah. The plural is retained.

v27: The difficult phrase rendered many pronouncements can also be understood in terms of a heavy burden or tribute. Exposition renders midrash, an account or interpretation, without identifying it with a later literary work.

## Vocabulary

v22: Loyal love
Hesed: committed kindness within a relationship. Joash’s failure to remember it drives the account’s accusation.

v22: Call to account
The dying appeal asks the LORD to see the killing and require an answer for it.
