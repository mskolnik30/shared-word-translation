## Fire and dedication

<p>
v01: When Solomon finished praying, fire came down from heaven and consumed the burnt offering and sacrifices. The LORD’s glory filled the house.
v02: The priests could not enter the LORD’s house because the LORD’s glory had filled it.
v03: All the Israelites saw the fire descend and the LORD’s glory over the house. They knelt with their faces to the ground on the pavement, bowed in worship, and thanked the LORD: “For he is good; his faithful love lasts forever.”
</p>

<p>
v04: The king and all the people offered sacrifices before the LORD.
v05: King Solomon sacrificed twenty-two thousand cattle and a hundred twenty thousand sheep and goats. In this way the king and all the people dedicated God’s house.
v06: The priests stood at their posts. The Levites had the musical instruments for the LORD that King David had made for giving thanks to the LORD—“His faithful love lasts forever”—as David offered praise through them. Opposite them the priests sounded trumpets, and all Israel stood.
v07: Solomon consecrated the middle of the courtyard in front of the LORD’s house. He offered the burnt offerings and the fat of the well-being offerings there, because the bronze altar he had made could not hold the burnt offering, grain offering, and fat.
</p>

<p>
v08: At that time Solomon kept the festival for seven days, with all Israel—a very large assembly from Lebo-hamath to the Wadi of Egypt.
v09: On the eighth day they held a solemn assembly. They had spent seven days dedicating the altar and seven days celebrating the festival.
v10: On the twenty-third day of the seventh month, he sent the people to their tents. They went with joyful, glad hearts because of the good the LORD had done for David, Solomon, and his people Israel.
</p>

## The LORD’s answer

<p>
v11: Solomon completed the LORD’s house and the royal house. Everything he had set his heart on doing in the LORD’s house and his own house succeeded.
v12: The LORD appeared to Solomon at night and said, “I have heard your prayer and chosen this place for myself as a house of sacrifice.
</p>

<p>
v13: When I shut the heavens so no rain falls, or command locusts to devour the land, or send plague among my people,
v14: and my people, who bear my name, humble themselves, pray, seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways, I will hear from heaven, forgive their sin, and heal their land.
v15: Now my eyes will be open and my ears attentive to prayer offered in this place.
v16: I have now chosen and consecrated this house for my name to be there forever. My eyes and my heart will be there always.
</p>

<p>
v17: “As for you, if you walk before me as your father David did, doing all I command you and keeping my statutes and judgments,
v18: I will establish your royal throne, as I covenanted with your father David: ‘You will never lack a man to rule in Israel.’
</p>

<p>
v19: But if you all turn away and abandon the statutes and commands I have set before you, and go off to serve other gods and bow to them,
v20: I will uproot them from my ground, which I gave them. This house I consecrated for my name I will throw out of my sight, and make it a proverb and a taunt among all peoples.
v21: This house, once so exalted, will leave every passerby appalled. They will ask, ‘Why has the LORD done this to this land and this house?’
v22: People will answer, ‘Because they abandoned the LORD, their ancestors’ God, who brought them out of Egypt. They clung to other gods, bowed to them, and served them. That is why he brought all this disaster on them.’”
</p>

## Notes

v9–10: The account distinguishes seven days for the altar dedication, seven for the festival, and the closing assembly. It dates the departure to the twenty-third day.

v13–14: The promise answers Solomon’s temple prayer and addresses the people who bear God’s name in their land.

v19–20: The Hebrew shifts from addressing you all to speaking of them. The draft preserves that shift.

## Vocabulary

v3: Faithful love
Hesed repeats the thanksgiving refrain from 5:13 and 1 Chronicles 16.

v14: Humble themselves
Kana describes yielding or lowering oneself; it recurs in Rehoboam’s response in chapter 12.
