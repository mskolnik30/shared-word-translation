## Asa’s alliance with Aram

<p>
v01: In the thirty-sixth year of Asa’s reign, Baasha king of Israel marched against Judah. He fortified Ramah to prevent anyone from going out or coming in to Asa king of Judah.
v02: Asa brought silver and gold out of the treasuries of the LORD’s house and the royal house and sent it to Ben-hadad king of Aram, who lived in Damascus, with this message:
</p>

<p>
v03: “Let there be a covenant between me and you, as between my father and yours. I am sending you silver and gold. Break your covenant with Baasha king of Israel so that he withdraws from me.”
</p>

<p>
v04: Ben-hadad listened to King Asa and sent his army commanders against Israel’s towns. They struck Ijon, Dan, Abel-maim, and all the storage places of Naphtali’s towns.
v05: When Baasha heard, he stopped fortifying Ramah and abandoned his work.
v06: King Asa brought all Judah to carry away the stones and timber Baasha had used at Ramah. With them he built Geba and Mizpah.
</p>

## Hanani’s rebuke

<p>
v07: At that time Hanani the seer came to Asa king of Judah and said, “Because you relied on the king of Aram instead of relying on the LORD your God, the army of the king of Aram has escaped your hand.
v08: Were not the Cushites and Libyans a vast army, with great numbers of chariots and horsemen? Yet when you relied on the LORD, he handed them over to you.
v09: The LORD’s eyes range throughout the earth to show his strength for those whose hearts are whole toward him. You have acted foolishly in this. From now on, you will have wars.”
</p>

<p>
v10: Asa was angry with the seer and put him in prison, in stocks, furious at what he had said. At that time Asa also brutally oppressed some of the people.
</p>

## Asa’s final years

<p>
v11: Asa’s acts, from first to last, are written in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel.
v12: In the thirty-ninth year of his reign, Asa became diseased in his feet, and his illness grew severe. Even in his illness he did not seek the LORD, but sought the physicians.
v13: Asa lay down with his ancestors. He died in the forty-first year of his reign.
v14: They buried him in the tomb he had cut for himself in David’s City. They laid him on a bier filled with spices and various preparations blended by a perfumer’s skill, and made an exceptionally great fire in his honor.
</p>

## Notes

v1: The Hebrew gives the thirty-sixth year of Asa. First Kings 16:8 places Baasha’s successor in Asa’s twenty-sixth year. The draft leaves the discrepancy visible.

v7: The army said to escape is Aram’s, not Israel’s.

v10: The place of imprisonment is literally the house of the stocks.

v12: The criticism concerns Asa’s failure to seek the LORD in this narrative; the verse does not state a general prohibition on physicians.

v14: A great burning in his honor follows the burial description. It need not mean that his body was cremated.

## Vocabulary

v9: A whole heart
Shalem conveys completeness or undivided commitment.

v12: Seek
Darash links inquiry, worship and reliance across these royal stories.
