## Josiah seeks and cleanses

<p>
v01: Josiah was eight when he became king, and he ruled in Jerusalem for thirty-one years.
v02: He did what was right in the LORD's eyes and followed the ways of his ancestor David, turning neither right nor left.
</p>

<p>
v03: In the eighth year of his reign, while he was still young, he began to seek the God of his ancestor David. In the twelfth year he began to cleanse Judah and Jerusalem of the high places, Asherah poles, carved images, and cast images.
</p>

<p>
v04: They demolished the Baals' altars in his presence. He cut down the incense altars above them, smashed the Asherah poles and the carved and cast images, ground them to powder, and scattered it over the graves of those who had sacrificed to them.
v05: He burned the priests' bones on their altars. In this way he cleansed Judah and Jerusalem.
</p>

<p>
v06: In the cities of Manasseh, Ephraim, and Simeon, as far as Naphtali, and in their surrounding ruins,
v07: he demolished the altars, crushed the Asherah poles and carved images to powder, and cut down all the incense altars throughout Israel's land. Then he returned to Jerusalem.
</p>

## Repairs and a discovered book

<p>
v08: In the eighteenth year of his reign, as he cleansed the land and the house, he sent Shaphan son of Azaliah, Maaseiah the city governor, and Joah son of Joahaz the recorder to repair the house of the LORD his God.
</p>

<p>
v09: They came to Hilkiah the high priest and handed over the money brought into God's house. The Levite gatekeepers had collected it from Manasseh and Ephraim, from all the remnant of Israel, and from all Judah and Benjamin. Then they returned to Jerusalem.
v10: They gave it to the supervisors of the work in the LORD's house, who passed it to the workers repairing and restoring the house.
v11: They gave it to carpenters and builders to buy quarried stone and timber for joints and beams for the buildings Judah's kings had ruined.
</p>

<p>
v12: The men worked faithfully. Their supervisors were Jahath and Obadiah, Levites descended from Merari, and Zechariah and Meshullam, descended from Kohath. The Levites skilled with musical instruments
v13: were also over the burden-bearers and supervised all the workers in every kind of service. Some Levites served as scribes, officers, and gatekeepers.
</p>

<p>
v14: While they were bringing out the money contributed to the LORD's house, Hilkiah the priest found the book of the LORD's law given through Moses.
v15: Hilkiah told Shaphan the scribe, “I have found the book of the law in the LORD's house.” Then he gave the book to Shaphan.
</p>

<p>
v16: Shaphan brought the book to the king and also reported, “Your servants are doing everything entrusted to them.
v17: They have poured out the money found in the LORD's house and handed it to the supervisors and workers.”
</p>

<p>
v18: Then Shaphan the scribe told the king, “Hilkiah the priest has given me a book.” And Shaphan read from it before the king.
v19: When the king heard the words of the law, he tore his clothes.
</p>

## Huldah’s answer

<p>
v20: The king gave orders to Hilkiah, Ahikam son of Shaphan, Abdon son of Micah, Shaphan the scribe, and Asaiah the king's servant:
v21: “Go and inquire of the LORD for me and for those left in Israel and Judah concerning the words of this book that has been found. Great is the LORD's wrath poured out on us, because our ancestors have not kept the LORD's word by doing everything written in this book.”
</p>

<p>
v22: Hilkiah and those the king had sent went to Huldah the prophet, the wife of Shallum son of Tokhath, son of Hasrah, keeper of the wardrobe. She lived in Jerusalem's Second Quarter. They spoke to her about this.
</p>

<p>
v23: She told them, “This is what the LORD, Israel's God, says: Tell the man who sent you to me,
v24: ‘This is what the LORD says: I am bringing disaster on this place and its inhabitants—all the curses written in the book read before Judah's king.
v25: They have abandoned me and burned offerings to other gods, provoking me with everything their hands have made. My wrath will pour out on this place and will not be quenched.’
</p>

<p>
v26: But say this to Judah's king, who sent you to inquire of the LORD: ‘This is what the LORD, Israel's God, says about the words you heard:
v27: Because your heart was tender and you humbled yourself before God when you heard his words against this place and its inhabitants—because you humbled yourself before me, tore your clothes, and wept before me—I too have heard you, declares the LORD.
v28: I will gather you to your ancestors, and you will be gathered to your grave in peace. Your eyes will not see all the disaster I am bringing on this place and its inhabitants.’” They brought this answer back to the king.
</p>

## The covenant is read and renewed

<p>
v29: The king sent word and gathered all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem.
</p>

<p>
v30: The king went up to the LORD's house with all the men of Judah, Jerusalem's inhabitants, the priests and Levites, and all the people, great and small. He read in their hearing every word of the book of the covenant found in the LORD's house.
</p>

<p>
v31: The king stood in his place and made the covenant before the LORD: to follow the LORD and keep his commands, testimonies, and statutes with all his heart and all his soul, carrying out the covenant's words written in this book.
v32: He made everyone present in Jerusalem and Benjamin stand with him in it. Jerusalem's inhabitants acted in accordance with the covenant of God, the God of their ancestors.
</p>

<p>
v33: Josiah removed all the detestable things from all the lands belonging to Israel. He made everyone present in Israel serve the LORD their God. Throughout his lifetime they did not turn away from following the LORD, the God of their ancestors.
</p>

## Notes

v06: Ruins follows the read form of a difficult Hebrew expression.

v09: The read form says they returned to Jerusalem; the written form can be read as and the inhabitants of Jerusalem. This draft follows the read form and retains the resulting repetition of location.

v17: Poured out refers to handling the collected silver or money. The verb can also be used for melting; this account does not describe minting coins.

v22: Huldah is a woman exercising prophetic authority. Tokhath represents the read form תָּקְהַת; the written form is תוקהת.

v28: The promise of burial in peace stands beside Josiah’s later fatal battle wound in 35:23–24. The accompanying explanation is that he will not see the coming disaster on this place; the tension is not removed.

## Vocabulary

v14: Law
Torah: instruction given through Moses. The discovered book is also called the book of the covenant in verse 30.

v27: Tender heart
A responsive heart, shown here in humility, torn clothes, and tears.

v31: Testimonies
Covenant instructions or stipulations, listed alongside commands and statutes.
