## An alliance and a campaign

<p>
v01: Jehoshaphat had great wealth and honor, and he formed a marriage alliance with Ahab.
v02: Some years later he went down to Ahab in Samaria. Ahab slaughtered many sheep, goats, and cattle for him and the people with him, and urged him to march against Ramoth-gilead.
v03: Ahab king of Israel asked Jehoshaphat king of Judah, “Will you go with me to Ramoth-gilead?” He answered, “I am as you are, and my people are as your people. I will be with you in the war.”
</p>

## Seeking a prophetic word

<p>
v04: But Jehoshaphat said to Israel’s king, “Please inquire first for the LORD’s word.”
v05: Israel’s king gathered four hundred prophets and asked, “Should we go to war against Ramoth-gilead, or should I hold back?” They answered, “Go up! God will hand it over to the king.”
</p>

<p>
v06: Jehoshaphat asked, “Is there not another prophet of the LORD here whom we can consult?”
v07: Israel’s king told him, “There is still one man through whom we can inquire of the LORD, but I hate him. He never prophesies good for me; all his days it is disaster. He is Micaiah son of Imlah.” Jehoshaphat replied, “The king should not say that.”
</p>

<p>
v08: Israel’s king called an officer and said, “Bring Micaiah son of Imlah quickly.”
</p>

<p>
v09: Israel’s king and Jehoshaphat king of Judah sat in their robes, each on his throne, at the threshing floor by Samaria’s gate. All the prophets were prophesying before them.
v10: Zedekiah son of Chenaanah made iron horns and said, “The LORD says: With these you will gore the Arameans until you destroy them.”
v11: All the prophets prophesied the same: “March against Ramoth-gilead and succeed! The LORD will hand it over to the king.”
</p>

## Micaiah before the kings

<p>
v12: The messenger sent to summon Micaiah told him, “Look, the prophets speak with one voice in the king’s favor. Please let your word agree with theirs. Speak favorably.”
v13: Micaiah answered, “As the LORD lives, I will speak whatever my God says.”
</p>

<p>
v14: When he came to the king, the king asked, “Micaiah, should we go to war against Ramoth-gilead, or should I hold back?” He replied, “Go up and succeed! They will be handed over to you.”
v15: The king said, “How many times must I put you under oath to tell me nothing but the truth in the LORD’s name?”
</p>

<p>
v16: Then Micaiah said, “I saw all Israel scattered over the mountains
like sheep without a shepherd.
The LORD said, ‘These have no master.
Let each go home in peace.’”
v17: Israel’s king said to Jehoshaphat, “Did I not tell you? He prophesies nothing good for me, only disaster.”
</p>

## The heavenly council

<p>
v18: Micaiah continued, “Then hear the LORD’s word. I saw the LORD seated on his throne, with all heaven’s army standing to his right and left.
v19: The LORD asked, ‘Who will entice Ahab king of Israel to march up and fall at Ramoth-gilead?’ One said this, another said that.
</p>

<p>
v20: Then the spirit came forward, stood before the LORD, and said, ‘I will entice him.’ The LORD asked, ‘How?’
v21: It replied, ‘I will go out and become a lying spirit in the mouths of all his prophets.’ The LORD said, ‘You will entice him and succeed. Go and do it.’
v22: So now the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouths of these prophets of yours, and the LORD has spoken disaster against you.”
</p>

<p>
v23: Zedekiah son of Chenaanah stepped forward and struck Micaiah on the cheek. “Which way did the LORD’s Spirit pass from me to speak to you?” he asked.
v24: Micaiah replied, “You will see on the day you go into an inner room to hide.”
</p>

<p>
v25: Israel’s king ordered, “Take Micaiah and return him to Amon, the city governor, and Joash, the king’s son.
v26: Tell them, ‘The king says: Put this man in prison and give him scant bread and scant water until I return in peace.’”
</p>

<p>
v27: Micaiah said, “If you return in peace at all, the LORD has not spoken through me.” Then he added, “Listen, all you peoples!”
</p>

## Ahab’s final battle

<p>
v28: Israel’s king and Jehoshaphat king of Judah marched against Ramoth-gilead.
v29: Israel’s king told Jehoshaphat, “I will disguise myself and enter the battle, but you wear your robes.” Israel’s king disguised himself, and they entered the battle.
</p>

<p>
v30: The king of Aram had ordered his chariot commanders, “Fight no one, small or great, except Israel’s king alone.”
v31: When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they said, “That is Israel’s king!” They surrounded him to fight. Jehoshaphat cried out, and the LORD helped him; God drew them away from him.
v32: When the chariot commanders realized he was not Israel’s king, they turned back from pursuing him.
</p>

<p>
v33: Someone drew a bow without knowing his target and struck Israel’s king between the armor’s joints and breastplate. The king told his chariot driver, “Turn around and take me out of the camp. I am wounded.”
v34: The battle intensified that day. Israel’s king kept himself upright in the chariot facing the Arameans until evening. At sunset he died.
</p>

## Notes

v8: The court title saris can mean an official or a eunuch.

v14–16: Micaiah’s first answer sounds like the other prophets’ assurance. The king’s demand for truth introduces the contrasting vision; the Hebrew supplies no explicit tone marker.

v18–22: The vision describes a lying spirit commissioned before the LORD. It does not identify the spirit by name or explain away the deception.

v26: Scant bread and scant water renders the repeated expression bread/water of distress.

v29: The first clause is compressed; the following action identifies Israel’s king as the one who disguises himself.

v33: The archer acts without knowing the significance of his target. The exact pieces of armor in the wound description are uncertain.

v34: The Hebrew portrays the king keeping himself upright; it does not name an attendant holding him.

## Vocabulary

v16: Shepherd
A common image for a ruler; the scattered sheep portray a people without one.

v19: Entice
Patah can mean persuade or deceive; the ensuing lying spirit makes deception explicit here.

v27: In peace
Shalom is the repeated test of the king’s expected safe return.
