## Assyria invades

<p>
v01: After these acts of faithfulness, Sennacherib king of Assyria came and invaded Judah. He encamped against the fortified cities, intending to break into them and take them for himself.
</p>

<p>
v02: Hezekiah saw that Sennacherib had come and was intent on making war against Jerusalem.
v03: He consulted his officials and warriors about stopping up the springs outside the city, and they helped him.
</p>

<p>
v04: A large crowd gathered and stopped up all the springs and the stream flowing through the land. They said, “Why should Assyria's kings come and find abundant water?”
</p>

<p>
v05: He acted resolutely. He rebuilt the whole broken wall and raised it to the towers, built another wall outside it, strengthened the Millo in the City of David, and made many weapons and shields.
</p>

<p>
v06: He appointed military commanders over the people and gathered them to him in the square at the city gate. He spoke to their hearts:
</p>

<p>
v07: “Be strong and courageous. Do not fear or lose heart before Assyria's king and the whole multitude with him. There is more with us than with him.
v08: With him is an arm of flesh; with us is the LORD our God, to help us and fight our battles.” The people leaned on the words of Hezekiah king of Judah.
</p>

## Sennacherib’s challenge

<p>
v09: After this, while Sennacherib king of Assyria was at Lachish with all his forces, he sent his servants to Jerusalem with a message for Hezekiah king of Judah and all Judah in Jerusalem:
</p>

<p>
v10: “This is what Sennacherib king of Assyria says: What are you trusting in, that you remain under siege in Jerusalem?
v11: Isn't Hezekiah misleading you, handing you over to die of hunger and thirst when he says, ‘The LORD our God will rescue us from Assyria's king’?
</p>

<p>
v12: Wasn't it Hezekiah himself who removed his high places and altars and told Judah and Jerusalem, ‘You must bow before one altar and burn offerings on it’?
</p>

<p>
v13: Do you not know what I and my ancestors have done to all the peoples of other lands? Were the gods of those nations able to rescue their lands from my hand?
v14: Which god among all those nations my ancestors devoted to destruction could rescue his people from my hand? How then could your God rescue you from my hand?
</p>

<p>
v15: Do not let Hezekiah deceive you or mislead you like this. Do not believe him. No god of any nation or kingdom has been able to rescue his people from my hand or my ancestors' hands. How much less will your God rescue you from my hand!”
</p>

<p>
v16: His servants said still more against the LORD God and against his servant Hezekiah.
</p>

<p>
v17: Sennacherib also wrote letters insulting the LORD, Israel's God: “Just as the gods of the nations could not rescue their people from my hand, Hezekiah's God will not rescue his people from my hand.”
</p>

<p>
v18: They shouted loudly in the language of Judah to Jerusalem's people on the wall, trying to frighten and terrify them so they could capture the city.
v19: They spoke of Jerusalem's God as though he were like the gods of the peoples of the earth, the work of human hands.
</p>

## Prayer and rescue

<p>
v20: Because of this, King Hezekiah and Isaiah son of Amoz the prophet prayed and cried out to heaven.
v21: The LORD sent an angel who wiped out every capable warrior, leader, and commander in Assyria's camp. Sennacherib returned to his land with shame on his face. When he entered his god's house, some of his own offspring struck him down there with the sword.
</p>

<p>
v22: So the LORD saved Hezekiah and Jerusalem's inhabitants from Sennacherib king of Assyria and from everyone else. He guided them on every side.
v23: Many brought offerings to the LORD in Jerusalem and precious gifts to Hezekiah king of Judah. From then on he was held high in the eyes of all the nations.
</p>

## Hezekiah’s later years

<p>
v24: In those days Hezekiah became mortally ill and prayed to the LORD. The LORD answered him and gave him a sign.
v25: But Hezekiah did not respond in keeping with the benefit he had received, because his heart grew proud. Wrath came upon him, Judah, and Jerusalem.
v26: Then Hezekiah humbled himself over his proud heart, and Jerusalem's inhabitants did too. The LORD's wrath did not come upon them during Hezekiah's lifetime.
</p>

<p>
v27: Hezekiah had very great wealth and honor. He made treasuries for silver, gold, precious stones, spices, shields, and every kind of valuable article,
v28: storehouses for the harvest of grain, new wine, and oil, stalls for every kind of livestock, and folds for flocks.
v29: He built cities for himself and acquired abundant flocks and herds, for God gave him very great possessions.
</p>

<p>
v30: It was Hezekiah who stopped up the upper outlet of Gihon's waters and directed them down westward to the City of David. Hezekiah prospered in all his work.
</p>

<p>
v31: But when the envoys of Babylon's rulers came to him to inquire about the sign that had happened in the land, God left him to test him and to know everything in his heart.
</p>

<p>
v32: The rest of Hezekiah's acts and his deeds of loyal love are written in the vision of Isaiah son of Amoz the prophet, in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel.
v33: Hezekiah rested with his ancestors. They buried him on the ascent to the tombs of David's descendants, and all Judah and Jerusalem's inhabitants honored him at his death. His son Manasseh succeeded him as king.
</p>

## Notes

v05: Raised it to the towers follows one understanding of a difficult construction phrase. Other readings describe raising or erecting towers on the wall.

v22: The pinned text says he guided them. A reading that gives them rest appears in some translations; it is not substituted here.

v24: The sign is mentioned without description. The fuller accounts in Kings and Isaiah supply details that this verse does not repeat.

v31: The statement that God left Hezekiah to test him and know his heart is retained without explaining away its wording.

v33: The burial location can be understood as the ascent to the tombs or their upper area.

## Vocabulary

v05: Millo
A named fortification or earthwork associated with the City of David.

v08: Arm of flesh
An image of mortal, human strength; it is contrasted with the LORD’s help.

v18: Language of Judah
The local language understood by the people on Jerusalem’s wall.
