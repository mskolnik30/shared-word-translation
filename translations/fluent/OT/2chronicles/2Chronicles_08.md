## Solomon’s building and labor

<p>
v01: At the end of the twenty years Solomon spent building the LORD’s house and his own house,
v02: Solomon rebuilt the towns Huram had given him and settled Israelites in them.
v03: Solomon marched against Hamath-zobah and took control of it.
v04: He built Tadmor in the wilderness and all the storage towns he built in Hamath.
v05: He built Upper Beth-horon and Lower Beth-horon as fortified towns with walls, gates, and bars.
v06: He also built Baalath, all his storage towns, the towns for chariots and horsemen, and everything else he wanted to build in Jerusalem, Lebanon, and throughout the land he ruled.
</p>

<p>
v07: There were people left from the Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites, who were not Israelites.
v08: Their descendants remained in the land because the Israelites had not destroyed them. Solomon conscripted them for forced labor, as they remain to this day.
v09: But Solomon did not make Israelites slaves for his work. They were fighting men, commanders of his officers, and commanders of his chariots and horsemen.
v10: King Solomon had two hundred fifty chief supervisors who exercised authority over the people.
</p>

<p>
v11: Solomon brought Pharaoh’s daughter up from David’s City to the house he had built for her. He said, “My wife must not live in the house of David king of Israel, because the places the LORD’s ark has entered are holy.”
</p>

## The ordered worship

<p>
v12: Then Solomon offered burnt offerings to the LORD on the LORD’s altar he had built in front of the entrance hall,
v13: according to each day’s requirement and Moses’ command: on Sabbaths, new moons, and the three annual festivals—the Festival of Unleavened Bread, the Festival of Weeks, and the Festival of Shelters.
v14: Following his father David’s arrangement, he appointed the priestly divisions to their service, the Levites to their duties of praise and assistance before the priests as each day required, and the gatekeepers in their divisions at each gate. This was the command of David, the man of God.
v15: They did not depart from the king’s command to the priests and Levites in any matter, including the treasuries.
v16: All Solomon’s work was arranged from the day the foundation of the LORD’s house was laid until its completion. The LORD’s house was finished.
</p>

## Ships and gold

<p>
v17: Then Solomon went to Ezion-geber and Eloth on the seacoast in Edom.
v18: Through his servants, Huram sent him ships and men who knew the sea. They went to Ophir with Solomon’s servants, brought back four hundred fifty talents of gold, and delivered it to King Solomon.
</p>

## Notes

v2: Here Huram gives towns to Solomon. The different direction of the gift in 1 Kings 9:11 is not imported into this account.

v8–9: The text distinguishes forced labor imposed on the remaining peoples from the Israelites’ military and command roles.

v11: The explanation for relocating Pharaoh’s daughter is Solomon’s own statement.

v18: Chronicles gives four hundred fifty talents here; the parallel account in 1 Kings 9:28 gives four hundred twenty.

## Vocabulary

v8: Forced labor
Mas names an imposed labor levy.

v13: Festival of Shelters
Sukkot recalls dwelling in temporary shelters; it is also called the Festival of Booths.

v18: Talent
A large ancient unit of weight, not a coin.
