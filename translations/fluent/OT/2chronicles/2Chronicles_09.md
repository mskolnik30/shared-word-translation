## The queen of Sheba

<p>
v01: The queen of Sheba heard of Solomon’s fame and came to Jerusalem to test him with riddles. She arrived with an immense retinue and camels carrying spices, great quantities of gold, and precious stones. When she met Solomon, she told him everything on her heart.
v02: Solomon answered all her questions. Nothing was hidden from him that he could not explain to her.
v03: When the queen of Sheba saw Solomon’s wisdom and the house he had built,
v04: the food on his table, the seating of his servants, the attendance and clothing of his ministers, his cupbearers and their clothing, and his ascent to the LORD’s house, she was left breathless.
</p>

<p>
v05: She said to the king, “The report I heard in my own land about your achievements and wisdom was true.
v06: I did not believe what people said until I came and saw with my own eyes. Yet I was not told even half the greatness of your wisdom! You surpass the report I heard.
v07: How fortunate your men are! How fortunate these servants of yours are, who stand before you continually and hear your wisdom!
v08: Blessed be the LORD your God, who delighted in you and placed you on his throne as king for the LORD your God! Because your God loves Israel and desires to establish it forever, he made you their king to uphold justice and righteousness.”
</p>

<p>
v09: She gave the king a hundred twenty talents of gold, immense quantities of spices, and precious stones. There had never been spices like those the queen of Sheba gave King Solomon.
</p>

<p>
v10: Huram’s servants and Solomon’s servants, who brought gold from Ophir, also brought algum timber and precious stones.
v11: The king used the algum timber to make steps for the LORD’s house and the royal house, and lyres and harps for the singers. Nothing like them had been seen before in Judah.
</p>

<p>
v12: King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba everything she desired and asked for, beyond what she had brought the king. Then she and her servants turned and went home to their own land.
</p>

## Solomon’s wealth

<p>
v13: The gold that came to Solomon in a single year weighed six hundred sixty-six talents,
v14: besides what the traders and merchants brought. All the kings of Arabia and the governors of the land brought Solomon gold and silver.
v15: King Solomon made two hundred large shields of beaten gold, using six hundred shekels of beaten gold for each shield.
v16: He also made three hundred smaller shields of beaten gold, using three hundred shekels for each. He placed them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.
</p>

<p>
v17: The king made a great ivory throne and covered it with pure gold.
v18: The throne had six steps and a gold footstool attached to it. Armrests stood on either side of the seat, with two lions beside them.
v19: Twelve lions stood on the six steps, one on either side of each step. Nothing like it had been made in any kingdom.
</p>

<p>
v20: All King Solomon’s drinking vessels were gold, and all the vessels in the House of the Forest of Lebanon were pure gold. Silver counted for nothing in Solomon’s day.
v21: The king’s ships sailed to Tarshish with Huram’s servants. Once every three years the Tarshish ships returned carrying gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks.
v22: King Solomon surpassed all the earth’s kings in wealth and wisdom.
v23: All the earth’s kings sought an audience with Solomon to hear the wisdom God had put in his heart.
v24: Year after year, each brought a gift: silver and gold vessels, clothing, weapons, spices, horses, and mules.
</p>

<p>
v25: Solomon had four thousand stalls for horses and chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen. He stationed them in the chariot towns and with the king in Jerusalem.
v26: He ruled over all the kings from the River to the land of the Philistines and the border of Egypt.
v27: The king made silver as common in Jerusalem as stones, and cedars as plentiful as sycamore-fig trees in the lowlands.
v28: Horses were imported for Solomon from Egypt and from all the lands.
</p>

## Solomon’s death

<p>
v29: The rest of Solomon’s acts, from first to last, are written in the records of Nathan the prophet, the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite, and the visions of Iddo the seer concerning Jeroboam son of Nebat.
v30: Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel for forty years.
v31: Solomon lay down with his ancestors and was buried in his father David’s City. His son Rehoboam became king in his place.
</p>

## Notes

v4: Ascent follows the Hebrew form here; some render it as an offering or the manner in which Solomon offered sacrifice.

v10–11: The kind of tree called algum is uncertain. Steps renders a term for raised ways; terraces or supports are also proposed.

v12: Beyond what she brought preserves the difficult comparison in Chronicles. It is not replaced with the wording of 1 Kings 10:13.

v15–16: Shekels supplies the conventional weight unit; the Hebrew gives the numbers without naming that unit.

v21: The last animal name is uncertain; peacocks is traditional, while baboons is another proposed identification.

v29: Iddo is the conventional English name here. The written Hebrew is יעדי; the read form is יֶעְדּוֹ (Yeddo).

## Vocabulary

v23: Sought an audience
The Hebrew says sought Solomon’s face.

v26: The River
The Euphrates.

v27: Sycamore-fig
A fig-bearing tree, distinct from the tree commonly called sycamore in North America.
