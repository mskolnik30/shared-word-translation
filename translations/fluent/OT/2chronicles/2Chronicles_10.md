## The people ask for relief

<p>
v01: Rehoboam went to Shechem, where all Israel had gathered to make him king.
v02: When Jeroboam son of Nebat heard, he was in Egypt, where he had fled from King Solomon. Jeroboam returned from Egypt.
v03: The people sent for him. Jeroboam and all Israel came to Rehoboam and said,
</p>

<p>
v04: “Your father made our yoke harsh. Now lighten your father’s harsh service and the heavy yoke he put on us, and we will serve you.”
v05: He told them, “Come back to me in three days.” So the people left.
</p>

## Two kinds of advice

<p>
v06: King Rehoboam consulted the elders who had served his father Solomon while he was alive. “What answer do you advise me to give these people?” he asked.
v07: They told him, “If you are good to these people, please them, and speak kindly to them, they will be your servants always.”
</p>

<p>
v08: But he abandoned the elders’ advice and consulted the young men who had grown up with him and now served him.
v09: He asked them, “What do you advise? How should we answer these people who have told me, ‘Lighten the yoke your father put on us’?”
</p>

<p>
v10: The young men who had grown up with him replied, “Tell these people who said, ‘Your father made our yoke heavy; lighten it for us’: ‘My little finger is thicker than my father’s waist!
v11: My father loaded you with a heavy yoke; I will add to your yoke. My father disciplined you with whips; I will do it with scorpions.’”
</p>

## The kingdom divides

<p>
v12: Jeroboam and all the people came to Rehoboam on the third day, as the king had told them: “Come back to me on the third day.”
v13: The king answered them harshly. King Rehoboam abandoned the elders’ advice
v14: and spoke to them as the young men advised: “I will make your yoke heavy and add to it. My father disciplined you with whips; I will do it with scorpions.”
</p>

<p>
v15: The king would not listen to the people. This turn of events came from God, so that the LORD would fulfill the word he had spoken through Ahijah the Shilonite to Jeroboam son of Nebat.
</p>

<p>
v16: When all Israel saw that the king would not listen, the people answered him:
“What share have we in David?
We have no inheritance in Jesse’s son!
To your tents, Israel!
Now look after your own house, David!”
So all Israel went to their tents.
v17: Rehoboam continued to reign over the Israelites living in Judah’s towns.
</p>

<p>
v18: King Rehoboam sent Hadoram, the man over the forced labor, but the Israelites stoned him to death. Rehoboam hurried to get into his chariot and flee to Jerusalem.
v19: Israel has rebelled against David’s house to this day.
</p>

## Notes

v10: Finger is supplied: the Hebrew says my little one. The boast contrasts it with his father’s waist.

v11,14: Scorpions retains the threatening image. It is often understood as a description of especially cruel whips.

v14: The pinned Hebrew has I will make your yoke heavy, not my father made it heavy. The actual answer differs from the earlier advice.

v15: The narrator attributes the turn of events to God while also describing Rehoboam’s refusal to listen.

## Vocabulary

v4: Yoke
A wooden bar used to harness animals; here an image for burdens imposed by the ruler.

v16: David’s house
The royal family or dynasty, not just a building.
