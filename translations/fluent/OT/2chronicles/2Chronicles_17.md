## Jehoshaphat strengthens Judah

<p>
v01: His son Jehoshaphat became king in his place and strengthened his position against Israel.
v02: He stationed troops in all Judah’s fortified towns and placed garrisons throughout Judah and in the towns of Ephraim his father Asa had captured.
</p>

<p>
v03: The LORD was with Jehoshaphat because he walked in the earlier ways of his ancestor David and did not seek the Baals.
v04: Instead, he sought his father’s God and followed his commands, unlike Israel’s practices.
v05: The LORD established the kingdom in his hand. All Judah brought tribute to Jehoshaphat, and he had great wealth and honor.
v06: His heart took courage in the LORD’s ways. He also removed the high places and Asherah poles from Judah.
</p>

## Teaching throughout the towns

<p>
v07: In the third year of his reign he sent his officials Ben-hail, Obadiah, Zechariah, Nethanel, and Micaiah to teach in Judah’s towns.
v08: With them went the Levites Shemaiah, Nethaniah, Zebadiah, Asahel, Shemiramoth, Jehonathan, Adonijah, Tobijah, and Tob-adonijah, and the priests Elishama and Jehoram.
v09: They taught throughout Judah, carrying the Book of the LORD’s Instruction. They traveled through every town in Judah and taught the people.
</p>

## Peace and military strength

<p>
v10: Dread of the LORD fell on all the kingdoms surrounding Judah, and they did not go to war with Jehoshaphat.
v11: Some Philistines brought Jehoshaphat gifts and silver as tribute. The Arabs also brought him flocks: seven thousand seven hundred rams and seven thousand seven hundred male goats.
</p>

<p>
v12: Jehoshaphat grew greater and greater. He built fortresses and storage towns in Judah.
v13: He had extensive work in Judah’s towns, and capable fighting men in Jerusalem.
</p>

<p>
v14: These were their enrolled numbers by ancestral house. From Judah, the commanders of thousands were: Adnah the commander, with three hundred thousand capable warriors;
v15: next to him, Jehohanan the commander, with two hundred eighty thousand;
v16: and next to him, Amasiah son of Zichri, who volunteered for the LORD, with two hundred thousand capable warriors.
v17: From Benjamin: Eliada, a capable warrior, with two hundred thousand men armed with bows and shields;
v18: and next to him, Jehozabad, with a hundred eighty thousand equipped for military service.
v19: These served the king, besides those he had stationed in fortified towns throughout Judah.
</p>

## Notes

v3: The Hebrew names David and qualifies his ways as earlier.

v6: The lifted-heart expression has a positive sense here, unlike its use for pride in other settings.

v8: Shemiramoth follows the read form of the name.

v13: Extensive work follows the ordinary sense of the Hebrew; some interpret it as supplies or stores in this setting.

## Vocabulary

v9: Instruction
Torah includes teaching and direction, not only individual laws.

v16: Volunteered
The verb describes willingly offering oneself.
