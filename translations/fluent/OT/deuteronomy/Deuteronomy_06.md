## Instruction for life in the land

<p>
v01: “This is the commandment, the statutes and rulings the LORD your God commanded me to teach you, so that you may practice them in the land you are crossing over to possess.
v02: Then you may fear the LORD your God, keeping all his statutes and commands that I give you—you, your son, and your grandson—all the days of your life, so that your days may be long.
v03: Hear, Israel, and be careful to do this, so that it may go well with you and you may become very numerous in a land flowing with milk and honey, as the LORD, the God of your fathers, promised you.
</p>

## Hear, Israel

<p>
v04: Hear, Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one.
v05: Love the LORD your God with all your heart, all your soul, and all your strength.
</p>

<p>
v06: These words that I command you today must be on your heart.
v07: Teach them to your children again and again. Talk about them when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up.
v08: Bind them as a sign on your hand, and let them be bands between your eyes.
v09: Write them on the doorposts of your house and on your gates.
</p>

## Remember amid abundance

<p>
v10: When the LORD your God brings you into the land he swore to your fathers, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, to give you—a land with large, good towns you did not build,
v11: with houses full of good things you did not fill, hewn cisterns you did not hew, and vineyards and olive trees you did not plant—and you eat your fill,
v12: be careful not to forget the LORD, who brought you out of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.
</p>

<p>
v13: Fear the LORD your God, serve him, and swear by his name.
v14: Do not follow other gods, the gods of the peoples around you,
v15: for the LORD your God among you is a jealous God. Otherwise the anger of the LORD your God will burn against you, and he will destroy you from the face of the ground.
</p>

## Do not test the LORD

<p>
v16: Do not put the LORD your God to the test as you tested him at Massah.
</p>

<p>
v17: Be diligent in keeping the commands of the LORD your God, the testimonies and statutes he has commanded you.
v18: Do what is right and good in the LORD's eyes, so that it may go well with you and you may enter and possess the good land the LORD swore to your fathers,
v19: driving out all your enemies before you, as the LORD promised.
</p>

## Tell the next generation

<p>
v20: When your son asks you in days to come, ‘What do the testimonies, statutes, and rulings mean that the LORD our God commanded you?’
v21: tell your son, ‘We were Pharaoh's slaves in Egypt, and the LORD brought us out of Egypt with a strong hand.
v22: Before our eyes the LORD brought great and terrible signs and wonders on Egypt, Pharaoh, and his whole household.
v23: He brought us out of there to bring us in and give us the land he swore to our fathers.
v24: The LORD commanded us to practice all these statutes and to fear the LORD our God, for our good always and to keep us alive, as we are today.
v25: It will be righteousness for us if we are careful to practice this whole commandment before the LORD our God, as he commanded us.’”
</p>

## Notes

v4: The short Hebrew statement permits more than one arrangement: “the LORD our God, the LORD is one” or “the LORD is our God, the LORD alone.” The draft retains “one” and leaves this consequential alternative visible here.

v5: Heart, soul, and strength express total devotion. “Soul” represents the living self; “strength” translates a word ordinarily meaning “very” or “much,” here extending the command to one's full capacity.

v7–9: The intensive teaching verb suggests repeated, incisive instruction. Words are spoken through daily routines and are also bound and written. The rare word rendered “bands” is traditionally “frontlets”; its exact object is uncertain. These concrete instructions are not replaced with an exclusively figurative reading.

v10–12: The repeated “you did not” links towns, houses, cisterns, vineyards, and olive trees. The danger named is forgetting the LORD after eating one's fill.

v20–25: A son's question is answered with the community's story: “we” were slaves, were brought out, and were brought in. The final verse retains the conditional language of righteousness and careful practice without adding a later doctrinal explanation.

## Vocabulary

v4: Hear
Hebrew shema, the opening word by which this passage is traditionally known. Hearing here calls for attentive response.

v8: Sign
Something that marks or recalls the commanded words, here bound to the hand.

v11: Hewn cistern
A water-storage chamber cut out of rock.

v16: Massah
The place name associated with Israel's testing of the LORD over water in Exodus 17.

v25: Righteousness
Right conduct or standing in relation to what is required; here explicitly connected with practicing the commandment before the LORD.
