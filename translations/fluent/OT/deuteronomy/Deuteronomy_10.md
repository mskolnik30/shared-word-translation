## New tablets and the ark

<p>
v01: “At that time the LORD told me, ‘Cut two stone tablets like the first ones, and come up the mountain to me. Also make yourself a wooden ark.
v02: I will write on the tablets the words that were on the first tablets, which you shattered. Put them in the ark.’
</p>

<p>
v03: So I made an ark of acacia wood, cut two stone tablets like the first ones, and went up the mountain with the two tablets in my hands.
</p>

<p>
v04: The LORD wrote on the tablets what he had written before: the Ten Words he spoke to you on the mountain from the fire, on the day of the assembly. Then he gave them to me.
v05: I turned and came down the mountain and put the tablets in the ark I had made. They are still there, as the LORD commanded me.
</p>

## Journey, priesthood, and Levi

<p>
v06: The Israelites traveled from Beeroth Bene-jaakan to Moserah. Aaron died and was buried there, and his son Eleazar served as priest in his place.
v07: From there they traveled to Gudgodah, and from Gudgodah to Jotbathah, a land of flowing streams.
</p>

<p>
v08: At that time the LORD set apart the tribe of Levi to carry the ark of the LORD's covenant, to stand before the LORD to serve him, and to bless in his name, as they do to this day.
v09: That is why Levi has no share or inheritance with his brothers. The LORD is his inheritance, as the LORD your God promised him.
</p>

## The journey is renewed

<p>
v10: I stayed on the mountain forty days and forty nights, as I had the first time. The LORD listened to me that time too; he was not willing to destroy you.
v11: The LORD told me, ‘Get up and lead the people on their journey, so that they may enter and possess the land I swore to their fathers to give them.’
</p>

## What the LORD asks

<p>
v12: Now, Israel, what does the LORD your God ask of you? Only this: fear the LORD your God, walk in all his ways, love him, and serve the LORD your God with all your heart and all your soul,
v13: keeping the LORD's commands and statutes that I give you today for your good.
</p>

<p>
v14: Look, the heavens, even the highest heavens, belong to the LORD your God, along with the earth and everything in it.
v15: Yet the LORD set his affection on your fathers and loved them. He chose their descendants after them—you—out of all peoples, as he has done today.
</p>

<p>
v16: Circumcise your hearts, then, and no longer stiffen your necks.
</p>

## Justice and love for the foreigner

<p>
v17: For the LORD your God is God of gods and Lord of lords, the great, mighty, and fearsome God. He shows no favoritism and accepts no bribe.
v18: He upholds justice for the fatherless and the widow, and loves the resident foreigner, giving that person food and clothing.
v19: You too must love the resident foreigner, because you were resident foreigners in Egypt.
</p>

<p>
v20: Fear the LORD your God, serve him, cling to him, and swear by his name.
v21: He is your praise, and he is your God, who has done for you these great and fearsome things your eyes have seen.
</p>

<p>
v22: Your fathers went down to Egypt as seventy people. Now the LORD your God has made you as numerous as the stars in the sky.”
</p>

## Notes

v1–5: Moses describes making a wooden ark and receiving new tablets. The translation retains his first-person account; compare the ark-making account in Exodus 37.

v6–7: This travel notice names Beeroth Bene-jaakan, Moserah, Gudgodah, and Jotbathah, and places Aaron's death at Moserah. Numbers 33:30–39 gives a different route sequence and names Mount Hor for Aaron's death. Neither account is rewritten to match the other.

v8–9: The functions are assigned here to Levi collectively. The passage does not spell out the distinctions among Levitical groups found in other instructions.

v16: The Hebrew speaks of circumcising the foreskin of the heart and no longer stiffening the neck. The bodily metaphors call for changed responsiveness without turning the verse into a surgical instruction.

v18–19: God's love for the resident foreigner is expressed through food and clothing. Israel's own experience as resident foreigners is the stated reason for the matching command.

## Vocabulary

v1: Ark
A chest, here made of wood to hold the covenant tablets.

v4: Ten Words
The Hebrew name for the commands commonly called the Ten Commandments.

v18: Fatherless
A child deprived of a father's protection and support, named alongside the widow.

v18: Resident foreigner
Someone living among a people without ordinary ancestral membership, Hebrew ger.

v22: Seventy people
The stated number of ancestors entering Egypt; compare Genesis 46:27 and Exodus 1:5.
