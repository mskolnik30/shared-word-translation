## A Limit on Beating

<p>
v01: “When people have a dispute and bring it to court, the judges must decide between them, acquitting the innocent and condemning the guilty.
v02: If the guilty person deserves a beating, the judge must have him lie down and be beaten in the judge's presence, with the number of blows suited to the offense.
v03: Forty blows may be given, but no more. If more are added, your fellow Israelite may be degraded in your eyes by the excessive beating.
</p>

## The Threshing Ox

<p>
v04: Do not muzzle an ox while it is threshing.
</p>

## Preserving a Brother’s Name

<p>
v05: If brothers live together and one dies without a son, his widow must not marry a man outside the family. Her husband's brother must have sex with her, take her as his wife, and fulfill a brother-in-law's duty to her.
v06: The firstborn she bears is to carry on the dead brother's name, so that his name is not erased from Israel.
</p>

<p>
v07: But if the man does not want to marry his brother's widow, she must go to the elders at the gate and say, ‘My husband's brother refuses to preserve his brother's name in Israel. He will not fulfill his duty to me as a brother-in-law.’
v08: The elders of his town must summon him and speak to him. If he stands firm and says, ‘I do not want to marry her,’
v09: his brother's widow must come up to him before the elders, pull the sandal off his foot, spit in his face, and declare, ‘This is what is done to a man who will not build up his brother's house.’
v10: His family will be known in Israel as ‘The house of the man whose sandal was removed.’
</p>

## Injury during a Fight

<p>
v11: If two men are fighting and the wife of one comes to rescue her husband from the man striking him, and she reaches out and seizes that man's genitals,
v12: cut off her hand. Do not let your eye pity her.
</p>

## Honest Weights and Measures

<p>
v13: Do not keep two different weights in your pouch, one heavy and one light.
v14: Do not keep two different measures in your house, one large and one small.
v15: Keep a full and honest weight, and a full and honest measure, so that you may live long on the ground the LORD your God is giving you.
v16: Everyone who does such things, everyone who acts unjustly, is detestable to the LORD your God.
</p>

## Remember Amalek

<p>
v17: Remember what Amalek did to you on the road when you came out of Egypt.
v18: He met you on the road and struck down all the stragglers at your rear while you were tired and weary. He did not fear God.
v19: When the LORD your God gives you rest from all your enemies around you, in the land he is giving you to possess as an inheritance, wipe out the memory of Amalek from under heaven. Do not forget.”
</p>

## Notes

v3: The stated limit is forty blows. The reason given is that excessive beating degrades a fellow member of the community.

v5–6: The Hebrew says the dead brother has no ben, “son,” also rendered “child.” The firstborn is to continue the dead brother’s name and family line; the text does not require a particular given name.

v9: “Spit in his face” follows the ordinary reading of the phrase; “spit before him” is another rendering. The widow publicly performs the action and speaks the declaration.

v11–12: The woman intervenes to rescue her husband. The stipulated punishment is nevertheless the cutting off of her hand. Both her motive and the bodily punishment are retained.

v13–15: The objects are literally stone weights and ephah measures. Unequal versions allow dishonest weighing and measuring; “full and honest” answers both forms of fraud.

v18: “He did not fear God” is read here as referring to Amalek. The Hebrew does not repeat the subject; some attach the clause to Israel. The attack on weary stragglers is explicit.

## Vocabulary

v4: Threshing
Separating grain from stalks and husks, here with an ox at work.

v5: Brother-in-law’s duty
The obligation described here to marry a deceased brother’s widow and preserve his line.

v9: House
Here, the continuing family or household, not only a building.
