## The land is not a reward for your righteousness

<p>
v01: “Hear, Israel! You are about to cross the Jordan to enter and dispossess nations greater and stronger than you, with large towns fortified up to the heavens.
v02: Their people are great and tall, descendants of the Anakim. You know them, and you have heard it said, ‘Who can stand against the descendants of Anak?’
</p>

<p>
v03: Know today that the LORD your God is crossing ahead of you as a consuming fire. He will destroy them and subdue them before you. You will drive them out and quickly destroy them, as the LORD promised you.
</p>

<p>
v04: When the LORD your God drives them out before you, do not say in your heart, ‘The LORD brought me in to possess this land because of my righteousness.’ It is because of these nations' wickedness that the LORD is driving them out before you.
v05: You are not entering to possess their land because of your righteousness or the uprightness of your heart. The LORD your God is driving these nations out before you because of their wickedness, and to fulfill the word he swore to your fathers, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.
</p>

<p>
v06: Know this: the LORD your God is not giving you this good land to possess because of your righteousness. You are a stiff-necked people.
</p>

## Remember the rebellion at Horeb

<p>
v07: Remember—do not forget—how you provoked the LORD your God in the wilderness. From the day you left Egypt until you reached this place, you have been rebellious toward the LORD.
v08: At Horeb you provoked the LORD, and he became angry enough with you to destroy you.
</p>

<p>
v09: When I went up the mountain to receive the stone tablets, the tablets of the covenant the LORD made with you, I stayed on the mountain forty days and forty nights. I ate no bread and drank no water.
v10: The LORD gave me the two stone tablets written with the finger of God. On them were all the words the LORD spoke to you on the mountain from the fire, on the day of the assembly.
v11: At the end of forty days and forty nights, the LORD gave me the two stone tablets, the tablets of the covenant.
</p>

<p>
v12: Then the LORD told me, ‘Get up and go down quickly from here. Your people, whom you brought out of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. They have quickly turned from the way I commanded them and made themselves a cast image.’
</p>

<p>
v13: The LORD also told me, ‘I have seen this people. They are a stiff-necked people.
v14: Leave me alone, so that I may destroy them and wipe out their name from under heaven. Then I will make you into a nation stronger and more numerous than they are.’
</p>

## The shattered tablets and the calf

<p>
v15: I turned and came down the mountain while it was blazing with fire, with the two tablets of the covenant in my two hands.
v16: I looked and saw that you had sinned against the LORD your God. You had made yourselves a cast calf and quickly turned from the way the LORD commanded you.
v17: I took hold of the two tablets, threw them from my two hands, and shattered them before your eyes.
</p>

<p>
v18: Then I lay prostrate before the LORD as before, forty days and forty nights. I ate no bread and drank no water because of all the sin you had committed, doing evil in the LORD's eyes and provoking him to anger.
v19: I was afraid of the anger and fury with which the LORD raged against you, ready to destroy you. But the LORD listened to me that time too.
</p>

<p>
v20: The LORD was angry enough with Aaron to destroy him, and I prayed for Aaron too at that time.
</p>

<p>
v21: I took that sinful thing you had made, the calf, and burned it. I crushed it, grinding it thoroughly until it was as fine as dust, and threw its dust into the stream flowing down the mountain.
</p>

## A history of rebellion

<p>
v22: At Taberah, at Massah, and at Kibroth-hattaavah, you provoked the LORD.
v23: When the LORD sent you from Kadesh-barnea and said, ‘Go up and possess the land I have given you,’ you rebelled against the command of the LORD your God. You did not trust him or listen to his voice.
v24: You have been rebellious toward the LORD ever since I have known you.
</p>

## Moses' prayer for the people

<p>
v25: So I lay prostrate before the LORD for those forty days and forty nights, because the LORD had said he would destroy you.
</p>

<p>
v26: I prayed to the LORD, ‘Lord GOD, do not destroy your people, your inheritance, whom you redeemed by your greatness and brought out of Egypt with a strong hand.
v27: Remember your servants Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Do not look at this people's stubbornness, wickedness, or sin.
v28: Otherwise the people of the land you brought us from will say that the LORD could not bring them into the land he promised them, and that he brought them out to kill them in the wilderness because he hated them.
v29: Yet they are your people and your inheritance, whom you brought out by your great power and outstretched arm.’”
</p>

## Notes

v1–6: The repeated denial that Israel receives the land because of its righteousness is kept through all three statements. The passage gives the nations' wickedness and the ancestral oath as its reasons.

v12, 26–29: The LORD calls them Moses' people whom Moses brought out; Moses' prayer returns to “your people” and the LORD's deliverance. Both forms of address are retained.

v18, 25: The forty-day language recurs as Moses retells his intercession. The draft does not automatically count each retrospective mention as an additional forty-day period.

v20–21: Moses includes his prayer for Aaron and describes the calf's destruction and disposal in a stream. This account is preserved without importing every detail from Exodus 32.

v28: The land is pictured as speaking; the draft makes its inhabitants explicit. Their anticipated accusation combines divine inability and hatred. It is an argument in Moses' prayer, not a statement that the narrator endorses.

## Vocabulary

v3: Consuming fire
An image of destructive divine power, also used in 4:24.

v6: Stiff-necked
An image of stubborn resistance, retained alongside the command not to stiffen one's neck in 10:16.

v10: Finger of God
The source's bodily image for divine writing, retained without explaining its physical mechanism.

v22: Taberah, Massah, and Kibroth-hattaavah
Places recalling burning, testing, and graves of craving; compare Numbers 11 and Exodus 17.

v26: Inheritance
Here the people themselves belong to God as his inheritance, rather than the term naming land.
