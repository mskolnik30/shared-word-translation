## Proverbs Copied in the Days of Hezekiah

<p>
v01: These too are proverbs of Solomon, copied by the men of Hezekiah, king of Judah.
v02: It is God’s glory to conceal a matter;
it is kings’ glory to search a matter out.
v03: The heavens in height, the earth in depth,
and the heart of kings cannot be searched out.
v04: Remove the dross from silver,
and a vessel comes forth for the smith.
v05: Remove the wicked from a king’s presence,
and his throne will be established in righteousness.
</p>

## Do Not Exalt Yourself

<p>
v06: Do not make yourself grand before a king
or stand in the place of the great.
v07: Better to be told, “Come up here,”
than to be brought low before a noble.
What your eyes have seen,
v08: do not rush to bring into a dispute.
What will you do afterward
when your neighbor puts you to shame?
v09: Argue your case with your neighbor,
but do not reveal another person’s confidence,
v10: or the hearer may disgrace you,
and your bad reputation will not go away.
</p>

## Words Fitly Spoken

<p>
v11: Golden apples in silver settings—
such is a word spoken at the right moment.
v12: A gold ring, an ornament of fine gold—
such is a wise correction to a listening ear.
v13: Like the coolness of snow on a harvest day
is a faithful envoy to those who send him;
he refreshes his masters’ lives.
v14: Clouds and wind without rain—
such is one who boasts of a gift never given.
v15: Through patience a ruler is persuaded;
a gentle tongue breaks a bone.
</p>

## Practice Restraint

<p>
v16: Have you found honey? Eat only enough,
or you will be filled with it and vomit.
v17: Let your visits to your neighbor’s house be rare,
or he will grow tired of you and hate you.
v18: A club, a sword, a sharpened arrow—
such is one who gives false testimony against his neighbor.
v19: A broken tooth and an unsteady foot—
such is trust in a faithless person on a day of trouble.
v20: Taking off a garment on a cold day,
pouring vinegar on soda—
such is singing songs to a troubled heart.
</p>

## Care for an Enemy

<p>
v21: If your enemy is hungry, give him bread to eat;
if he is thirsty, give him water to drink.
v22: For you will heap coals on his head,
and the LORD will repay you.
v23: The north wind brings rain,
and a secretive tongue brings angry faces.
v24: Better to live on a corner of the roof
than share a house with a quarrelsome wife.
</p>

<p>
v25: Cold water to a weary soul—
such is good news from a distant land.
v26: A muddied spring, a ruined fountain—
such is a righteous person giving way before the wicked.
v27: Eating much honey is not good;
pursuing your own honor brings no honor.
v28: A breached city without a wall—
such is a person who cannot restrain his spirit.
</p>

## Notes

v07–08,22: The clause “what your eyes have seen” is kept in verse 7 but read with verse 8. The coals on an enemy’s head in verse 22 are not explained in the proverb; shame, changed relations, and judgment are interpretive possibilities.

v27: The second line is unusually compressed and contains no explicit negative. It can be read as “searching out their glory is glory,” with the word’s related sense of weight. The draft carries the first line’s negation forward and reads a warning against pursuing one’s own honor. That negative is implied rather than written; the positive reading remains a serious alternative.

## Vocabulary

v20: **Soda**: A mineral cleaning substance, not a modern soft drink. Vinegar disrupts it, matching the ill-timed song.
