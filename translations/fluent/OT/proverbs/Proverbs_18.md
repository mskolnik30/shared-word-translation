## Words, Wisdom, and Conflict

<p>
v01: One who isolates himself pursues his own desire
and breaks out against all sound wisdom.
v02: A fool takes no pleasure in understanding,
only in exposing what is in his heart.
v03: When wickedness comes, contempt comes too;
with dishonor comes disgrace.
v04: The words of a person’s mouth are deep waters;
the fountain of wisdom is a flowing stream.
</p>

<p>
v05: It is not good to favor the guilty
and turn aside the innocent in judgment.
v06: A fool’s lips enter quarrels;
his mouth calls for blows.
v07: A fool’s mouth is his ruin;
his lips are a snare for his life.
v08: A whisperer’s words are like tasty morsels;
they go down into the innermost belly.
v09: One who is slack in his work
is brother to a destroyer.
</p>

## The Name of the LORD

<p>
v10: The name of the LORD is a strong tower;
the righteous run into it and are safe on high.
v11: The wealth of the rich is their fortified city,
like a high wall in their imagination.
v12: Before destruction a person’s heart grows proud;
before honor comes humility.
v13: Answering before listening
is folly and dishonor for the speaker.
v14: A person’s spirit can sustain him in illness,
but who can bear a crushed spirit?
</p>

## Listen Before You Answer

<p>
v15: A discerning heart acquires knowledge;
the ear of the wise seeks knowledge.
v16: A person’s gift makes room for him
and brings him before the great.
v17: The first to present his case seems right,
until his neighbor comes and examines him.
v18: Casting lots ends disputes
and separates powerful opponents.
v19: An offended brother is harder to win than a fortified city;
quarrels are like the bars of a palace.
</p>

## Life and Death in the Tongue

<p>
v20: A person’s belly is filled by the fruit of his mouth;
he is filled by the produce of his lips.
v21: Death and life are in the power of the tongue;
those who love it will eat its fruit.
v22: Whoever finds a wife finds good
and obtains favor from the LORD.
v23: The poor speak with pleading,
but the rich answer harshly.
v24: A person with many companions may come to ruin,
but there is a friend who clings more closely than a brother.
</p>

## Notes

v19,24: “Harder to win” supplies the comparison implied in verse 19. The first line of verse 24 is difficult; this draft reads it as the danger of many companions, contrasted with one deeply loyal friend.

## Vocabulary

v18: **Lots**: Objects cast to settle a decision; the saying describes their use in ending disputes.
