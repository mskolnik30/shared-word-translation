## Do Not Boast About Tomorrow

<p>
v01: Do not boast about tomorrow,
for you do not know what a day will bring to birth.
v02: Let someone else praise you, not your own mouth;
a stranger, not your own lips.
v03: Stone is heavy and sand a burden,
but a fool’s provocation is heavier than both.
v04: Fury is cruel and anger a flood,
but who can stand before jealousy?
v05: Better an open correction
than a hidden love.
</p>

<p>
v06: A friend’s wounds are trustworthy;
an enemy’s kisses are plentiful.
v07: One who is full tramples honeycomb underfoot;
to the hungry even bitter things are sweet.
v08: Like a bird wandering from its nest
is a person wandering from home.
v09: Oil and incense bring joy to the heart;
a friend’s sweetness comes from heartfelt counsel.
v10: Do not abandon your friend or your father’s friend.
Do not go to your brother’s house on your day of disaster;
better a nearby neighbor than a distant brother.
</p>

## Friends Sharpen One Another

<p>
v11: Be wise, my son, and bring joy to my heart,
so I can answer the one who reproaches me.
v12: The prudent see danger and hide,
but the inexperienced go on and suffer for it.
v13: Take the garment of one who guarantees a stranger’s debt;
hold it as a pledge when he guarantees an outsider woman’s debt.
v14: Whoever blesses his neighbor loudly at dawn
will have it counted as a curse.
v15: A constant dripping on a rainy day
and a quarrelsome wife are alike.
v16: Restraining her is restraining the wind,
trying to grasp oil in one’s right hand.
</p>

<p>
v17: Iron sharpens iron,
and a person sharpens his neighbor’s face.
v18: Whoever tends a fig tree eats its fruit;
whoever guards his master will be honored.
v19: As water reflects face to face,
so a human heart reflects a person.
v20: Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied,
and human eyes are never satisfied.
v21: A crucible is for silver and a furnace for gold,
and a person is tested by his praise.
</p>

## Know the Condition of Your Flocks

<p>
v22: Even if you pound a fool in a mortar
among crushed grain with a pestle,
his folly will not leave him.
v23: Know well the condition of your flock;
pay close attention to your herds,
v24: for wealth does not last forever.
Does a crown endure from generation to generation?
v25: The grass is taken away, fresh growth appears,
and the mountain plants are gathered.
v26: Lambs will provide your clothing,
and goats the price of a field.
v27: There will be enough goats’ milk for your food,
for your household’s food,
and for the livelihood of your young women.
</p>

## Notes

v09,16,19,21: These sayings are compressed. Verse 9 connects friendship’s sweetness with heartfelt counsel. Verse 16’s final verb is uncertain; the draft uses the image of grasping oil. Verse 19 leaves the heart’s reflection broad. “His praise” in verse 21 can concern praise received or what someone praises.

## Vocabulary

v23–27: **Flock and herds**: Living sources of clothing, exchange, and food, contrasted with wealth and status that may not endure.
