## The Words of King Lemuel

<p>
v01: The words of King Lemuel. An oracle his mother taught him.
v02: What, my son? What, son of my womb?
What, son of my vows?
v03: Do not give your strength to women
or your ways to what destroys kings.
v04: It is not for kings, Lemuel,
not for kings to drink wine,
nor for rulers to ask, “Where is strong drink?”
v05: They may drink and forget what is decreed
and distort the rights of all the afflicted.
</p>

## Speak for Those Without a Voice

<p>
v06: Give strong drink to one who is perishing
and wine to those bitter in soul.
v07: Let him drink and forget his poverty
and remember his misery no more.
v08: Open your mouth for those unable to speak,
for the rights of all who are passing away.
v09: Open your mouth, judge righteously,
and defend the rights of the afflicted and needy.
</p>

## A woman of strength

<p>
v10: A woman of strength—who can find her?
Her worth is far beyond jewels.
v11: Her husband’s heart trusts her;
he will lack no gain.
v12: She does him good, not harm,
all the days of her life.
v13: She seeks wool and flax
and works with willing hands.
v14: She is like a merchant’s ships,
bringing her food from far away.
</p>

<p>
v15: She rises while it is still night,
gives food to her household
and an allotment to her young women.
v16: She considers a field and buys it;
from the fruit of her hands she plants a vineyard.
v17: She girds her waist with strength
and makes her arms strong.
v18: She sees that her trading is profitable;
her lamp does not go out at night.
</p>

<p>
v19: She reaches her hands to the distaff;
her palms hold the spindle.
v20: She opens her palm to the afflicted
and reaches out her hands to the needy.
v21: She does not fear snow for her household,
for all her household are clothed in scarlet.
v22: She makes coverings for herself;
her clothing is fine linen and purple.
</p>

## Strength and Dignity

<p>
v23: Her husband is known at the gates,
where he sits with the elders of the land.
v24: She makes linen garments and sells them;
she supplies belts to the merchant.
v25: Strength and dignity are her clothing;
she laughs at the day to come.
v26: She opens her mouth with wisdom;
instruction in faithful love is on her tongue.
v27: She watches over her household’s ways
and does not eat the bread of idleness.
</p>

## A Woman Who Fears the LORD

<p>
v28: Her children rise and call her happy;
her husband rises and praises her:
v29: “Many women have shown strength,
but you surpass them all.”
v30: Charm deceives, and beauty is a breath,
but a woman who fears the LORD is to be praised.
v31: Give her the fruit of her hands,
and let her deeds praise her at the gates.
</p>

## Notes

v01,08: “Oracle” may instead identify Massa. Those “passing away” in verse 8 can be understood as people facing ruin or destitution; the king is told to defend their rights.

v10–31: The twenty-two verses form a Hebrew alphabetic poem, one successive letter at the start of each verse. It praises a woman’s strength, productive work, generosity, teaching, and public standing.

v15,21,24: The young women’s allotment may mean provisions or assigned work. Verse 21 retains the pinned “scarlet,” rather than the alternative “double garments.” “Merchant” in verse 24 is literally “Canaanite,” used in a trading sense.

## Vocabulary

v10,29: **Strength**: Chayil includes ability, resources, worth, and strength. The poem describes these in action rather than narrowing the word to one virtue.
