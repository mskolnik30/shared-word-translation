## Pay Attention to Wisdom

<p>
v01: My son, pay attention to my wisdom;
turn your ear to my understanding,
v02: so that you may guard discretion
and your lips may preserve knowledge.
</p>

## The Strange Woman

<p>
v03: The forbidden woman’s lips drip honey,
and her mouth is smoother than oil,
v04: but in the end she is bitter as wormwood,
sharp as a two-edged sword.
v05: Her feet go down to death;
her steps lead straight to Sheol.
v06: She does not weigh the path of life;
her courses wander, and she does not know it.
</p>

## Keep Far from Her

<p>
v07: Now, sons, listen to me;
do not turn away from what I say.
v08: Keep your way far from her;
do not approach the door of her house,
v09: or you will give your honor to others
and your years to someone cruel;
v10: strangers will feast on your strength,
and your hard-earned goods will end up in another’s house.
v11: At your end you will groan,
when your flesh and body waste away.
v12: You will say, “How I hated discipline!
How my heart despised correction!
v13: I did not listen to my teachers
or turn my ear to those who instructed me.
v14: I was nearly in utter ruin
in the midst of the gathered community.”
</p>

## Rejoice in the Love of Your Marriage

<p>
v15: Drink water from your own cistern,
flowing water from your own well.
v16: Should your springs spill out into the streets,
your streams of water into the public squares?
v17: Let them be yours alone,
not shared with strangers.
v18: May your fountain be blessed;
rejoice in the wife of your youth,
v19: a loving doe, a graceful mountain goat.
May her breasts satisfy you at all times;
may her love always intoxicate you.
</p>

<p>
v20: Why, my son, be intoxicated with a forbidden woman
and embrace the breast of an outsider?
</p>

## The LORD Sees Every Path

<p>
v21: For a man’s ways are before the LORD’s eyes;
he weighs all his paths.
v22: The wicked man’s own wrongs capture him;
the cords of his sin hold him fast.
v23: He dies for lack of discipline;
his great folly leads him astray.
</p>

## Notes

v06,16: Verse 6 is read with the woman as subject, though some forms can address the son. Verse 16 is read as a question; the Hebrew can also be read as an assertion about springs spreading outside.

## Vocabulary

v15–19: **Cistern and fountain**: Water sources become images of marital intimacy. The poem then speaks directly of the wife and her body.
