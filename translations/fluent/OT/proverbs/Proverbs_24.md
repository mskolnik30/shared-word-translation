## By Wisdom a House Is Built

<p>
v01: Do not envy evil men
or desire their company,
v02: for their hearts devise violence
and their lips speak trouble.
v03: By wisdom a house is built;
by understanding it is established;
v04: by knowledge its rooms are filled
with every precious and pleasant treasure.
v05: A wise man has strength;
a knowledgeable man increases his power.
</p>

<p>
v06: For with guidance you wage your war,
and with many counselors comes deliverance.
v07: Wisdom is too high for a fool;
at the gate he does not open his mouth.
v08: One who plans to do evil
will be called a master of schemes.
v09: Folly’s schemes are sin,
and a scoffer is detestable to people.
v10: If you collapse on a day of trouble,
your strength is small.
</p>

## Rescue Those Being Taken to Death

<p>
v11: Rescue those being taken away to death;
hold back those staggering toward slaughter.
v12: If you say, “Look, we did not know this,”
does not the one who weighs hearts understand?
Does not the guardian of your life know?
Will he not repay each person according to his deeds?
</p>

## Wisdom Is Sweet

<p>
v13: My son, eat honey, for it is good;
honey from the comb is sweet to your taste.
v14: Know that wisdom is like this for your soul.
If you find it, there is a future,
and your hope will not be cut off.
</p>

## The Righteous Rise Again

<p>
v15: Do not lie in wait, wicked one, against the home of the righteous;
do not devastate his resting place.
v16: For the righteous fall seven times and rise again,
but the wicked stumble in disaster.
v17: Do not rejoice when your enemy falls;
do not let your heart exult when he stumbles,
v18: or the LORD may see and be displeased
and turn his anger away from him.
</p>

<p>
v19: Do not burn with anger over evildoers;
do not envy the wicked,
v20: for the evil have no future;
the lamp of the wicked will go out.
v21: My son, fear the LORD and the king;
do not associate with those who rebel,
v22: for their disaster rises suddenly,
and who knows the ruin that comes from them both?
</p>

## Further Sayings of the Wise

<p>
v23: These too are sayings of the wise.
Showing partiality in judgment is not good.
v24: Whoever tells the guilty, “You are innocent,”
will be cursed by peoples and denounced by nations.
v25: But it will go well with those who convict the guilty;
a good blessing will come upon them.
v26: An honest answer
is a kiss on the lips.
</p>

<p>
v27: Prepare your work outside;
get your fields ready.
After that, build your house.
v28: Do not testify against your neighbor without cause;
would you deceive with your lips?
v29: Do not say, “I will do to him what he did to me;
I will repay the man according to his deeds.”
</p>

## The Field of the Lazy

<p>
v30: I passed by a sluggard’s field,
by the vineyard of a person without sense.
v31: Thorns had grown over it all;
nettles covered its surface,
and its stone wall was broken down.
v32: I looked and took it to heart;
I saw and received discipline:
v33: “A little sleep, a little rest,
a little folding of the hands to lie down”—
v34: and poverty will come upon you like a prowler,
need like a man with a shield.
</p>

## Notes

v17–22: The warning forbids delight in an enemy’s downfall. In verse 22 “both” most naturally points back to the LORD and the king; the compressed pronouns should not be made into an extra threat against the reader.

## Vocabulary

v16: **Seven times**: A repeated, full measure of falling and rising; the context concerns adversity rather than naming seven particular sins.
