## The approaching day

<p>
v01: Blow the ram’s horn in Zion!
Sound the alarm on my holy mountain!
Let everyone living in the land tremble,
for the day of the LORD is coming; it is near.
v02: A day of darkness and gloom,
a day of cloud and thick darkness!
Like dawn spreading over the mountains
comes a vast and powerful people.
Nothing like it has ever been,
nor will be again for generations to come.
v03: Fire devours ahead of them;
flame burns behind them.
Before them the land is like the garden of Eden;
behind them, a desolate wilderness.
Nothing escapes them.
v04: They look like horses;
they run like cavalry.
v05: With a sound like chariots they leap over the mountaintops,
like the crackle of flame consuming stubble,
like a powerful people drawn up for battle.
v06: Peoples writhe before them;
every face loses its color.
v07: They run like warriors
and scale a wall like soldiers.
Each keeps to its own path;
they do not swerve from their routes.
v08: They do not jostle one another;
each moves in its own lane.
They plunge through the weapons
and are not stopped.
v09: They rush upon the city,
run along the wall,
climb into the houses,
and enter through the windows like a thief.
v10: Before them the earth shakes
and the heavens tremble.
Sun and moon grow dark;
the stars withdraw their light.
v11: The LORD raises his voice before his army.
His camp is vast;
powerful is the one who carries out his word.
The day of the LORD is great and deeply terrifying—
who can endure it?
</p>

## Return with all your heart

<p>
v12: “Even now,” declares the LORD,
“return to me with all your heart,
with fasting, weeping, and mourning.”
v13: Tear your hearts, not your clothes,
and return to the LORD your God.
He is gracious and compassionate,
slow to anger and rich in faithful love,
and he relents from bringing disaster.
v14: Who knows? He may turn and relent
and leave a blessing behind him—
a grain offering and a drink offering
for the LORD your God.
v15: Blow the ram’s horn in Zion!
Set apart a fast;
call a solemn assembly.
v16: Gather the people;
set apart the congregation.
Assemble the elders;
gather children and nursing infants.
Let the bridegroom leave his room
and the bride her wedding chamber.
v17: Between the porch and the altar,
let the priests who serve the LORD weep.
Let them say, “Spare your people, LORD.
Do not make your inheritance a disgrace,
for nations to rule over them.
Why should the peoples say, ‘Where is their God?’”
</p>

## The LORD answers

<p>
v18: Then the LORD became zealous for his land
and had compassion on his people.
v19: The LORD answered his people:
“I am sending you grain, new wine, and oil,
and you will have enough.
I will no longer make you a disgrace among the nations.
v20: I will drive the northern one far from you
and thrust it into a dry, desolate land,
its front into the eastern sea
and its rear into the western sea.
Its stench will rise, its foul smell will go up,
for it has done great things.”
v21: Do not fear, ground!
Celebrate and rejoice,
for the LORD has done great things.
v22: Do not fear, wild animals,
for the wilderness pastures are green.
The trees bear fruit;
fig tree and vine give their full yield.
v23: Children of Zion, celebrate and rejoice
in the LORD your God.
He has given you the autumn rain in righteousness;
he sends rain down for you,
autumn and spring rain as before.
v24: The threshing floors will be full of grain;
the vats will overflow with new wine and oil.
v25: “I will repay you for the years eaten by
the swarming locust, the creeping locust,
the stripping locust, and the cutting locust—
my great army that I sent against you.
v26: You will eat your fill
and praise the name of the LORD your God,
who has done wonders for you.
My people will never again be put to shame.
v27: You will know that I am among Israel,
that I am the LORD your God and there is no other.
My people will never again be put to shame.
</p>

## The Spirit poured out

<p>
v28: Afterward I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh.
Your sons and daughters will prophesy;
your old men will dream dreams;
your young men will see visions.
v29: Even on male and female slaves
I will pour out my Spirit in those days.
v30: I will display wonders in the heavens and on earth:
blood, fire, and columns of smoke.
v31: The sun will turn to darkness
and the moon to blood
before the great and terrifying day of the LORD comes.
v32: Everyone who calls on the name of the LORD will escape,
for on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there will be deliverance,
as the LORD has said,
and among the survivors will be those the LORD calls.
</p>

## Notes

v02-09: Locust and army imagery overlap throughout the description. The translation preserves comparisons rather than assigning every feature to one literal invasion. The facial expression in verse 6 and weapon clause in verse 8 are uncertain.

v17-20: Verse 17 may describe foreign rule or becoming a taunt among nations. Verses 18–19 use narrative forms for God’s response. The “northern one” and its “great things” remain unnamed and unexplained.

v23: Moreh can mean autumn rain or teacher. The agricultural context supports rain here, but “in righteousness” is retained. The final expression can mean “as before” or “in the first month.”

v28-32: These verses form chapter 3 in the Hebrew source. “All flesh” is followed by sons, daughters, old and young men, and male and female slaves. Later quotations should be compared without changing this source’s wording.

## Vocabulary

v13: **Relent**: Turn from an announced course of disaster; the verse stresses compassion while the next preserves “Who knows?”

v29: **Slaves**: People held in servitude; the promise explicitly includes both men and women in that condition.
