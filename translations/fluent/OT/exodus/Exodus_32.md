## The golden calf

<p>
v01: When the people saw that Moses was taking a long time to come down the mountain, they gathered around Aaron and said, “Come, make us gods to go ahead of us. As for this Moses, the man who brought us up from Egypt, we do not know what has happened to him.”
v02: Aaron answered, “Take off the gold earrings your wives, sons, and daughters are wearing, and bring them to me.”
v03: So all the people took off the gold earrings they were wearing and brought them to Aaron.
v04: He took the gold from their hands, shaped it with an engraving tool, and made it into a cast-metal calf. Then they said, “These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from Egypt!”
</p>

<p>
v05: When Aaron saw this, he built an altar in front of the calf and announced, “Tomorrow will be a festival to the LORD.”
v06: Early the next morning they offered burnt offerings and brought peace offerings. The people sat down to eat and drink, then got up to revel.
</p>

## The LORD sends Moses down

<p>
v07: The LORD told Moses, “Go down! Your people, whom you brought up from Egypt, have acted corruptly.
v08: They have quickly turned away from the way I commanded them to follow. They have made themselves a cast-metal calf, bowed down to it, and sacrificed to it, saying, ‘These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from Egypt!’”
</p>

<p>
v09: The LORD said to Moses, “I have seen these people. They are stiff-necked.
v10: Now leave me alone, so my anger may burn against them and I may consume them. Then I will make you into a great nation.”
</p>

## Moses pleads for the people

<p>
v11: But Moses pleaded with the LORD his God: “LORD, why should your anger burn against your people, whom you brought out of Egypt with great power and a strong hand?
v12: Why should the Egyptians say, ‘He brought them out with evil intent, to kill them in the mountains and wipe them off the face of the ground’? Turn from your burning anger. Relent from bringing this disaster on your people.
v13: Remember your servants Abraham, Isaac, and Israel. You swore to them by your own self and said, ‘I will make your offspring as numerous as the stars in the sky. All this land I promised I will give to your offspring, and they will inherit it forever.’”
v14: So the LORD relented from the disaster he had said he would bring on his people.
</p>

## The tablets shattered

<p>
v15: Moses turned and went down the mountain, carrying the two tablets of the testimony in his hand. They were written on both sides, front and back.
v16: The tablets were God’s work, and the writing engraved on them was God’s writing.
</p>

<p>
v17: Joshua heard the people shouting and said to Moses, “There is the sound of battle in the camp!”
v18: Moses answered, “It is not the sound of victory,
nor the sound of defeat;
it is the sound of singing I hear.”
</p>

<p>
v19: As Moses approached the camp and saw the calf and the dancing, his anger burned. He threw the tablets from his hands and shattered them at the foot of the mountain.
v20: He took the calf they had made, burned it in the fire, ground it to powder, scattered it over the water, and made the Israelites drink it.
</p>

## Aaron’s account

<p>
v21: Moses said to Aaron, “What did these people do to you, that you brought such a great sin on them?”
v22: Aaron replied, “Do not let your anger burn, my lord. You know these people and how bent they are on evil.
v23: They said to me, ‘Make us gods to go ahead of us. As for this Moses, the man who brought us up from Egypt, we do not know what has happened to him.’
v24: So I told them, ‘Whoever has gold, take it off.’ They gave it to me, I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf!”
</p>

## The Levites and the killing

<p>
v25: Moses saw that the people were out of control. Aaron had let them loose, making them an object of scorn to their enemies.
</p>

<p>
v26: Moses stood at the entrance to the camp and called, “Whoever is for the LORD, come to me!” All the Levites gathered around him.
v27: He told them, “This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: ‘Each of you, strap your sword to your side. Go back and forth through the camp, from entrance to entrance, and each of you kill his brother, his friend, and his neighbor.’”
v28: The Levites did as Moses said, and about three thousand men from the people fell that day.
v29: Moses said, “Consecrate yourselves to the LORD today, each at the cost of his son and his brother, so that he may give you a blessing today.”
</p>

## Moses returns to the LORD

<p>
v30: The next day Moses told the people, “You have committed a great sin. Now I will go up to the LORD. Perhaps I can make atonement for your sin.”
v31: Moses returned to the LORD and said, “Oh, these people have committed a great sin! They have made themselves gods of gold.
v32: Now, if you will forgive their sin— But if not, please erase me from the book you have written.”
</p>

<p>
v33: The LORD replied to Moses, “Whoever has sinned against me, I will erase from my book.
v34: Now go and lead the people to the place I told you about. My angel will go ahead of you. But when the day comes for me to call them to account, I will call them to account for their sin.”
v35: The LORD struck the people with a plague because they had made the calf—the calf Aaron made.
</p>

## Notes

v1, 4, 8: The Hebrew uses plural forms for “gods,” including “these” in the acclamation, although the narrative describes one calf. Aaron calls the next day’s festival a festival to the LORD. These features are left together rather than resolved into a single explanation.

v4: The manufacturing phrase is difficult. “Engraving tool” is a traditional reading; the precise method and construction of the image are uncertain. [Translator discussion](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus+32&version=NET).

v6: “Revel” renders a verb for play or merrymaking. The chapter names dancing in verse 19, but does not specify sexual acts.

v7, 11, 14: The LORD calls Israel “your people” when speaking to Moses; Moses answers with “your people” addressed to the LORD. The narrator then says “his people.”

v25: The Hebrew describes the people as let loose or unrestrained. It does not require the older rendering “naked.”

v27–29: Moses attributes the killing command to the LORD. “Fell” in verse 28 refers to deaths. Verse 29’s compressed Hebrew links consecration, sons and brothers, and blessing. “Consecrate yourselves” takes the opening as a command; it has also been read as a statement that they have been consecrated. [Translator discussion](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus+32&version=NET).

v32: Moses’ first plea breaks off without completing its conditional sentence. The book is not further identified here.

## Vocabulary

v9: Stiff-necked — qesheh ʿoref
An image of a neck refusing to bend, describing stubborn resistance.

v14: Relent — naḥam
To turn from an announced course of action; here the LORD does not bring the threatened disaster.

v29: Consecrate — milleʾ yad
Literally “fill the hand,” an expression also used for priestly ordination in chapters 28–29.

v30: Atonement — kipper
Dealing with sin or guilt so that a damaged relationship can be restored. Moses says “perhaps” as he offers to seek it.

v34: Call to account — paqad
A verb for attending to or visiting; in this context it concerns reckoning with sin.
