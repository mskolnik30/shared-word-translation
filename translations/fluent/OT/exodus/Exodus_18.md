## Jethro comes to Moses

<p>
v01: Jethro, the priest of Midian and Moses’ father-in-law, heard all that God had done for Moses and his people Israel—how the LORD had brought Israel out of Egypt.
v02: Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law, took Zipporah, Moses’ wife, after she had been sent away,
v03: along with her two sons. One was named Gershom, because Moses had said, “I have been a foreigner in a foreign land.”
v04: The other was named Eliezer, because he had said, “My father’s God was my help and rescued me from Pharaoh’s sword.”
</p>

<p>
v05: Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law, came to Moses with Moses’ wife and sons in the wilderness, where he was camped at God’s mountain.
v06: He sent word to Moses, “I, your father-in-law Jethro, am coming to you with your wife and her two sons.”
</p>

<p>
v07: Moses went out to meet his father-in-law, bowed down, and kissed him. They asked about each other’s well-being and went into the tent.
v08: Moses told his father-in-law everything the LORD had done to Pharaoh and the Egyptians for Israel’s sake, all the hardship they had met along the road, and how the LORD had rescued them.
</p>

<p>
v09: Jethro rejoiced at all the good the LORD had done for Israel by rescuing them from Egypt’s hand.
v10: Jethro said, “Blessed be the LORD, who rescued you from Egypt’s hand and Pharaoh’s hand, who rescued the people from under Egypt’s hand.
v11: Now I know that the LORD is greater than all the gods, for he prevailed in the very matter in which the Egyptians acted arrogantly against Israel.”
</p>

<p>
v12: Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law, brought a burnt offering and sacrifices for God. Aaron and all Israel’s elders came to eat with him before God.
</p>

## Sharing the work of judgment

<p>
v13: The next day Moses sat to judge the people, and they stood around him from morning until evening.
v14: When Moses’ father-in-law saw everything he was doing for the people, he asked, “What is this you’re doing for them? Why do you sit here alone while all the people stand around you from morning until evening?”
</p>

<p>
v15: Moses answered his father-in-law, “The people come to me to inquire of God.
v16: When they have a dispute, it comes to me. I judge between one person and another and make God’s statutes and instructions known to them.”
</p>

<p>
v17: Moses’ father-in-law told him, “What you are doing is not good.
v18: You and these people with you will surely wear out. This burden is too heavy for you; you cannot carry it alone.
</p>

<p>
v19: Now listen to me. I will advise you, and may God be with you. Represent the people before God and bring their matters to him.
v20: Teach them the statutes and instructions. Show them the way they are to walk and the work they are to do.
</p>

<p>
v21: Choose capable men from all the people—men who fear God, who are trustworthy and hate dishonest gain. Put them over the people as leaders of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens.
v22: Let them judge the people at all times. They are to bring every major matter to you, but judge every minor matter themselves. Lighten your load this way, and let them carry it with you.
v23: If you do this, and God commands you to, you will be able to stand, and all these people will go home in peace.”
</p>

<p>
v24: Moses listened to his father-in-law and did everything he said.
v25: Moses chose capable men from all Israel and made them heads over the people, leaders of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens.
v26: They judged the people at all times. They brought the difficult matters to Moses and judged every minor matter themselves.
</p>

<p>
v27: Then Moses sent his father-in-law on his way, and Jethro returned to his own land.
</p>

## Notes

v2–6: The account does not say when or why Zipporah was sent away. It calls her Moses’ wife and identifies the two sons as hers. ‘Sent word’ in verse 6 makes the announcement natural in context; no messenger is explicitly named.

v11: The Hebrew’s last clause is incomplete or highly compressed. ‘He prevailed’ and the names of the opposing groups make one reading explicit. Another reading understands the gods as the arrogant opponents.

v12: Jethro brings a burnt offering and other sacrifices. The verse names Aaron and the elders at the meal before God, without identifying who performed every part of the rite.

v21–26: The proposal specifies capable, trustworthy men who fear God and reject dishonest gain. Verse 23 makes it conditional on God’s command. The implementation calls the cases brought to Moses ‘difficult,’ where the proposal called them ‘major.’

## Vocabulary

v3: Gershom — Gershom
The name is linked here with ger, ‘foreigner,’ recalling Moses’ life away from his own people.

v4: Eliezer — Eli‘ezer
‘My God is help,’ linked to rescue from Pharaoh’s sword.

v12: Burnt offering — ‘olah
An offering burned on the altar; the name is associated with going up.

v20: Instructions — torot
The plural of torah: teachings or directions governing conduct.
