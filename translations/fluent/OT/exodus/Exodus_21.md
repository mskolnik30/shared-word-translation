## Slaves and release

<p>
v01: “These are the rulings you are to set before them.
v02: If you buy a Hebrew slave, he is to serve for six years. In the seventh year he is to go free, without payment.
v03: If he came in alone, he is to leave alone. If he was married, his wife is to leave with him.
v04: If his master gave him a wife and she bore him sons or daughters, the wife and her children belong to her master. He is to leave alone.
</p>

<p>
v05: But if the slave firmly declares, ‘I love my master, my wife, and my children. I will not go free,’
v06: his master is to bring him before God and then bring him to the door or the doorpost. His master is to pierce his ear with an awl, and he will serve him permanently.
</p>

## A daughter sold into slavery

<p>
v07: If a man sells his daughter as a slave, she is not to leave as male slaves do.
v08: If she displeases the master who designated her for himself, he must allow her to be redeemed. He has no right to sell her to a foreign people, because he has betrayed her.
v09: If he designates her for his son, he must treat her according to the rights of daughters.
v10: If he takes another wife, he must not reduce the first woman’s food, clothing, or marital rights.
v11: If he does not provide these three things for her, she is to leave without payment, owing no money.
</p>

## Killing and other capital offenses

<p>
v12: Anyone who strikes a person and kills them must be put to death.
v13: But if the person did not lie in wait, and God let it happen at their hand, I will appoint a place where they can flee.
v14: But if someone deliberately attacks their neighbor to kill them by deceit, take that person even from my altar and put them to death.
</p>

<p>
v15: Anyone who strikes their father or mother must be put to death.
v16: Anyone who kidnaps a person must be put to death, whether they have sold that person or still hold them in their possession.
v17: Anyone who curses their father or mother must be put to death.
</p>

## Injuries and compensation

<p>
v18: If men quarrel and one strikes the other with a stone or his fist, and the injured man does not die but is confined to bed,
v19: then if he gets up and can walk outside with his staff, the one who struck him is cleared. But he must pay for the injured man’s lost time and ensure his full recovery.
</p>

<p>
v20: If a man beats his male or female slave with a rod and the slave dies under his hand, that death must be avenged.
v21: But if the slave survives a day or two, no vengeance is to be taken, because the slave is his money.
</p>

<p>
v22: If men fight and strike a pregnant woman, and her children come out but no harm occurs, the man responsible must pay a fine as her husband demands, subject to the judges’ decision.
v23: But if harm occurs, you must give life for life,
v24: eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot,
v25: burn for burn, wound for wound, bruise for bruise.
</p>

<p>
v26: If a man strikes and destroys the eye of his male or female slave, he must let the slave go free in compensation for the eye.
v27: If he knocks out the tooth of his male or female slave, he must let the slave go free in compensation for the tooth.
</p>

## Injuries caused by an ox

<p>
v28: If an ox gores a man or woman to death, the ox must be stoned, and its meat must not be eaten. Its owner is clear of liability.
v29: But if the ox was known to gore and its owner had been warned but failed to keep it under control, and it kills a man or woman, the ox must be stoned and its owner must also be put to death.
v30: If a ransom is imposed on the owner, he must pay whatever is required to redeem his life.
v31: The same ruling applies if the ox gores a son or daughter.
v32: If the ox gores a male or female slave, its owner must give the slave’s master thirty shekels of silver, and the ox must be stoned.
</p>

## Loss of livestock

<p>
v33: If someone opens a pit, or digs one and leaves it uncovered, and an ox or donkey falls into it,
v34: the owner of the pit must make restitution. He must pay the animal’s owner, and the dead animal becomes his.
</p>

<p>
v35: If one man’s ox strikes his neighbor’s ox and kills it, they must sell the living ox and divide its price. They must also divide the dead animal.
v36: But if the ox was known to gore and its owner failed to keep it under control, he must replace ox for ox, and the dead animal becomes his.”
</p>

## Notes

v2–11: These rulings distinguish a purchased Hebrew man, a wife he already had, a wife supplied by his master, and a daughter sold by her father. The release terms differ; the translation preserves these distinctions and the ownership language.

v6: “Before God” is also understood as “before the judges,” who would conduct the legal proceeding. Compare 22:8–9. [Translator discussion](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus+21&version=NET).

v8: The written Hebrew has “who did not designate her”; the traditional reading has “who designated her for himself.” The translation follows the reading while preserving both forms in the source record.

v10: The rare word rendered “marital rights” is traditionally understood as sexual relations within marriage. Its precise meaning is uncertain; provision of oil or ointment has also been proposed. [Translator discussion](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus+21&version=NET).

v20–21: “Avenged” leaves the penalty unspecified here. Verse 21 makes survival for a day or two decisive without explicitly describing the slave’s later condition. “His money” preserves the Hebrew’s stark description of the enslaved person as the owner’s property.

v22–25: “Her children come out” does not explicitly say whether the birth results in death or survival. The following “harm” is also not assigned explicitly to the woman or her children. Miscarriage and premature delivery are disputed readings. The paired injuries retain the passage’s formal pattern. [Translator discussion](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus+21&version=NET).

v29–32: The owner’s prior knowledge and failure to control the ox change his liability. The ransom in verse 30 is conditional; verse 32 specifies a payment to an enslaved victim’s master.

## Vocabulary

v1: Rulings — mishpatim
Decisions or ordinances governing particular cases; the same word returns at the covenant ceremony in 24:3.

v6: Awl — martseaʿ
A pointed tool used to pierce a hole.

v8: Redeem — padah
Secure someone’s release by payment.

v30: Ransom — kofer
A payment accepted in place of the threatened penalty.

v32: Shekel — sheqel
A unit of weight, used here to measure silver.
