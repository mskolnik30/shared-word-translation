## Preparing for ordination

<p>
v01: “This is what you are to do to consecrate them to serve me as priests. Take one young bull and two rams without blemish,
v02: along with unleavened bread, unleavened cakes mixed with oil, and unleavened wafers brushed with oil. Make them from fine wheat flour.
v03: Put them in one basket and bring them in the basket, together with the bull and the two rams.
</p>

## Washing and clothing the priests

<p>
v04: Bring Aaron and his sons to the entrance of the tent of meeting and wash them with water.
v05: Take the garments and dress Aaron in the tunic, the robe worn with the ephod, the ephod, and the breastpiece. Fasten the ephod on him with its waistband.
v06: Place the turban on his head and put the holy diadem on the turban.
v07: Take the anointing oil, pour it on his head, and anoint him.
v08: Bring his sons forward and dress them in tunics.
v09: Tie sashes on Aaron and his sons, and put the head coverings on them. The priesthood will be theirs by a lasting ordinance. In this way ordain Aaron and his sons.
</p>

## The bull for the sin offering

<p>
v10: Bring the bull before the tent of meeting. Aaron and his sons are to lay their hands on its head.
v11: Slaughter the bull before the LORD at the entrance of the tent of meeting.
v12: With your finger, put some of the bull’s blood on the altar’s horns. Pour all the remaining blood at the base of the altar.
v13: Take all the fat covering the inner organs, the lobe attached to the liver, and both kidneys with their fat, and burn them on the altar as smoke.
v14: Burn the bull’s meat, hide, and dung outside the camp. It is a sin offering.
</p>

## The first ram

<p>
v15: Take one ram, and have Aaron and his sons lay their hands on its head.
v16: Slaughter the ram, take its blood, and splash it against every side of the altar.
v17: Cut the ram into pieces. Wash its inner organs and legs, and place them with the pieces and the head.
v18: Burn the whole ram on the altar as smoke. It is a burnt offering to the LORD, a pleasing aroma, an offering by fire to the LORD.
</p>

## The ram of ordination

<p>
v19: Take the second ram, and have Aaron and his sons lay their hands on its head.
v20: Slaughter the ram. Put some of its blood on the right earlobes of Aaron and his sons, on their right thumbs, and on their right big toes. Splash the blood against every side of the altar.
v21: Take some blood from the altar and some anointing oil, and sprinkle them on Aaron and his garments, and on his sons and their garments. Then he and his garments, and his sons and their garments, will be consecrated.
</p>

<p>
v22: Take from the ram its fat, its fat tail, the fat covering the inner organs, the lobe of the liver, both kidneys with their fat, and the right thigh, because this is the ram of ordination.
v23: From the basket of unleavened bread before the LORD, take one round loaf, one cake made with oil, and one wafer.
v24: Put all of these on the palms of Aaron and his sons, and wave them as a wave offering before the LORD.
v25: Take them from their hands and burn them on the altar over the burnt offering, as a pleasing aroma before the LORD. It is an offering by fire to the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v26: Take the breast of the ram used for Aaron’s ordination and wave it as a wave offering before the LORD. It will be your portion.
v27: Consecrate the breast that was waved and the thigh that was contributed, the portions presented from the ordination ram for Aaron and his sons.
v28: These will belong to Aaron and his sons as a lasting share from the Israelites. It is a contribution, to be given by the Israelites from their peace offerings as their contribution to the LORD.
</p>

## The garments passed on

<p>
v29: Aaron’s holy garments are to pass to his sons after him, to be worn when they are anointed and ordained.
v30: The son who succeeds him as priest and enters the tent of meeting to serve in the sanctuary must wear them for seven days.
</p>

## The ordination meal

<p>
v31: Take the ram of ordination and boil its meat in a holy place.
v32: Aaron and his sons are to eat the ram’s meat and the bread in the basket at the entrance of the tent of meeting.
v33: They are to eat what was used to make atonement for their ordination and consecration. No outsider may eat it, because these things are holy.
v34: If any ordination meat or bread remains until morning, burn what is left. It must not be eaten, because it is holy.
</p>

## Seven days of ordination

<p>
v35: Do all this for Aaron and his sons, just as I commanded you. Their ordination is to continue for seven days.
</p>

<p>
v36: Each day offer a bull as a sin offering for atonement. Purify the altar by making atonement for it, and anoint it to consecrate it.
v37: For seven days make atonement for the altar and consecrate it. Then the altar will be most holy; whatever touches it will become holy.
</p>

## The daily offerings

<p>
v38: This is what you are to offer on the altar regularly: two lambs a year old each day.
v39: Offer one lamb in the morning and the other at twilight.
v40: With the first lamb, offer a tenth of an ephah of fine flour mixed with a quarter of a hin of beaten oil, and a drink offering of a quarter of a hin of wine.
v41: Offer the second lamb at twilight, with the same grain offering and drink offering as in the morning, as a pleasing aroma, an offering by fire to the LORD.
v42: This is to be a regular burnt offering throughout your generations, at the entrance of the tent of meeting before the LORD. There I will meet with you all and speak with you, Moses.
</p>

## God dwelling among Israel

<p>
v43: I will meet with the Israelites there, and the place will be consecrated by my glory.
v44: I will consecrate the tent of meeting and the altar. I will also consecrate Aaron and his sons to serve me as priests.
v45: I will dwell among the Israelites and be their God.
v46: They will know that I am the LORD their God, who brought them out of Egypt to dwell among them. I am the LORD their God.”
</p>

## Notes

v6: The holy diadem is the gold plate described in 28:36–38.

v13, 22: The Hebrew anatomical expression rendered “lobe of the liver” is difficult. It has also been understood as a fatty covering or appendage associated with the liver; the exact anatomy is uncertain.

v24–28: “Wave offering” is the customary name for tenufah, an offering presented before the LORD. Its precise movement is not fully described. The breast in verse 26 is Moses’ portion; verses 27–28 establish portions for Aaron and his sons.

v33: “Outsider” here means someone outside the authorized priestly group, not necessarily someone from another nation.

v37: The final clause can also be understood as requiring whatever touches the altar to be holy. “Will become holy” expresses the transfer of sacred status; it does not mean that contact makes someone morally faultless. [Translator discussion](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus+29&version=NET).

v39–40: “Twilight” renders the Hebrew expression “between the evenings”; its precise timing has been understood in different ways. Verse 40 specifies a tenth without naming the dry measure; “ephah” is supplied from the offering’s context. Ancient measures are retained without asserting exact modern equivalents.

v42–43: The first “you” in verse 42 is plural, while the second is singular, addressed to Moses. His name makes that change clear in English. Verse 43 does not explicitly name what is consecrated; “the place” refers to the meeting location in context.

## Vocabulary

v14: Sin offering — ḥatta’t
An offering also called a purification offering. Verse 36 describes its use in purifying the altar.

v18: Burnt offering — ʿolah
An offering sent up in smoke on the altar; here the whole ram is offered.

v18: Pleasing aroma — reaḥ niḥoaḥ
The familiar sacrificial image of an aroma pleasing to the LORD, also used in Genesis 8:21.

v22: Ordination — millu’im
The installation of priests in their service; the word is related to the expression “fill the hands.”

v28: Peace offerings — zevaḥei shelamim
Sacrifices with portions shared among altar, priests, and worshipers; also called well-being or fellowship offerings.

v36: Atonement — kippur
The ritual dealing with guilt or impurity that obstructs sacred service; here it includes the altar’s purification.

v40: Ephah; hin
Ancient measures for dry goods and liquids respectively.
