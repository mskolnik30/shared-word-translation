## Signs for Moses

<p>
v01: Moses answered, “But what if they do not believe me or listen to me? They may say, ‘The LORD has not appeared to you.’”
</p>

<p>
v02: The LORD asked him, “What is that in your hand?” “A staff,” Moses answered.
v03: “Throw it on the ground,” he said. Moses threw it down, and it became a snake. He ran from it.
</p>

<p>
v04: The LORD told Moses, “Reach out your hand and take it by the tail.” Moses reached out and grasped it, and it became a staff in his hand.
v05: “This is so they will believe that the LORD, the God of their ancestors—the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob—has appeared to you.”
</p>

<p>
v06: Then the LORD said, “Put your hand inside your cloak.” Moses put it inside his cloak. When he drew it out, his hand had a skin disease, white as snow.
v07: “Put your hand back inside your cloak,” he said. Moses put it back, and when he drew it out again, it was restored, like the rest of his skin.
</p>

<p>
v08: “If they do not believe you or listen to the voice of the first sign, they will believe the voice of the second.
v09: If they do not believe even these two signs or listen to you, take some water from the Nile and pour it on the dry ground. The water you take from the Nile will become blood on the ground.”
</p>

## Moses objects to speaking

<p>
v10: Moses said to the LORD, “Please, my Lord, I have never been a man of words—not before, and not since you began speaking to your servant. My mouth and tongue are slow.”
</p>

<p>
v11: The LORD said to him, “Who gave human beings a mouth? Who makes a person unable to speak or deaf, sighted or blind? Is it not I, the LORD?
v12: Now go. I will be with your mouth and teach you what to say.”
</p>

<p>
v13: But Moses said, “Please, my Lord, send someone else.”
</p>

<p>
v14: The LORD’s anger burned against Moses. He said, “What about your brother Aaron the Levite? I know he can speak well. He is already coming to meet you, and when he sees you, his heart will rejoice.
v15: You will speak to him and put the words in his mouth. I will be with your mouth and his, and I will teach you both what to do.
v16: He will speak to the people for you. He will be your mouth, and you will be as God to him.
v17: Take this staff in your hand; with it you will perform the signs.”
</p>

## Moses sets out for Egypt

<p>
v18: Moses went back to his father-in-law Jethro and said, “Please let me return to my own people in Egypt and see whether they are still alive.” Jethro answered, “Go in peace.”
</p>

<p>
v19: The LORD said to Moses in Midian, “Go back to Egypt. All the men who sought your life are dead.”
v20: Moses took his wife and sons, put them on a donkey, and set out for Egypt. He carried the staff of God in his hand.
</p>

<p>
v21: The LORD told Moses, “When you return to Egypt, be sure to perform before Pharaoh all the wonders I have put in your hand. But I will harden his heart, and he will not let the people go.
v22: Tell Pharaoh, ‘This is what the LORD says: Israel is my son, my firstborn.
v23: I told you, “Let my son go so that he may serve me,” but you refused to let him go. Now I will kill your son, your firstborn.’”
</p>

## An encounter on the way

<p>
v24: At a lodging place along the way, the LORD met him and sought to kill him.
v25: Zipporah took a flint, cut off her son’s foreskin, and touched his feet with it. “Truly, you are a bridegroom of blood to me,” she said.
v26: Then he let him alone. It was in connection with circumcision that she said, “A bridegroom of blood.”
</p>

## Moses and Aaron meet the elders

<p>
v27: The LORD said to Aaron, “Go into the wilderness to meet Moses.” He went, met him at the mountain of God, and kissed him.
v28: Moses told Aaron everything the LORD had sent him to say and all the signs he had commanded him to perform.
</p>

<p>
v29: Moses and Aaron went and gathered all the elders of the Israelites.
v30: Aaron spoke all the words the LORD had spoken to Moses, and he performed the signs before the people’s eyes.
v31: The people believed. When they heard that the LORD had attended to the Israelites and had seen their suffering, they bowed down in worship.
</p>

## Notes

v6: The Hebrew skin-condition term is traditionally translated ‘leprous.’ It covers a broader range of conditions than the modern disease called leprosy; no precise diagnosis is intended here.

v8: The signs are given a ‘voice.’ This image connects the signs with the repeated demand to listen.

v10–12: Moses describes a heavy or slow mouth and tongue. The passage does not identify a particular speech disorder. God’s response retains its difficult claim about speech, hearing, and sight.

v13: Literally, ‘send by the hand you will send’: an idiomatic appeal for someone else to be sent.

v16: Moses is to be ‘as God’ to Aaron in the stated relationship of giving words for Aaron to speak.

v18: The Hebrew first uses the short form Jether and then Jethro for the same father-in-law in this verse. Jethro is used throughout the verse for clarity.

v21: The Hebrew verb means to make strong or firm. Here God says he will harden Pharaoh’s heart; later passages must be read according to the agent each one names.

v24–26: The threatened ‘him’ is not named. Moses and his son are possible referents. Whose ‘feet’ are touched is also uncertain; feet may be literal or euphemistic. The phrase ‘bridegroom of blood’ remains obscure, though the narrator connects it with circumcision.

v30: The subject of ‘performed’ is not repeated in Hebrew. Aaron is the continuing grammatical subject, though Moses may be intended.

v31: The Hebrew reads “heard”; the ancient Greek reads “rejoiced.” The translation follows the Hebrew.

## Vocabulary

v2, 17, 20: Staff — matteh
The shepherd’s staff becomes an instrument of signs and is called ‘the staff of God’ in verse 20.

v5, 8–9: Sign — ʾot
An event or action that points beyond itself and calls for a response.

v22–23: Firstborn — bekhor
A term of family relationship and rank. Israel is named as God’s firstborn son in the warning to Pharaoh.
