## A child rescued from the Nile

<p>
v01: A man from the house of Levi married a woman from that same house.
v02: The woman conceived and bore a son. When she saw that he was a fine child, she hid him for three months.
</p>

<p>
v03: When she could hide him no longer, she got a papyrus basket and coated it with bitumen and pitch. She put the child inside and placed it among the reeds at the edge of the Nile.
v04: His sister stood at a distance to find out what would happen to him.
</p>

<p>
v05: Pharaoh’s daughter came down to bathe in the Nile while her attendants walked along the bank. She saw the basket among the reeds and sent her slave woman to fetch it.
v06: She opened it and saw the child—a boy, crying. She felt compassion for him and said, “This is one of the Hebrew children.”
</p>

<p>
v07: His sister asked Pharaoh’s daughter, “Shall I go and call a Hebrew woman to nurse the child for you?”
v08: “Go,” Pharaoh’s daughter said. The young woman went and called the child’s mother.
</p>

<p>
v09: Pharaoh’s daughter told her, “Take this child and nurse him for me, and I will pay you.” So the woman took the child and nursed him.
</p>

<p>
v10: When the child had grown, she brought him to Pharaoh’s daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, saying, “I drew him out of the water.”
</p>

## Moses flees to Midian

<p>
v11: Years later, when Moses had grown up, he went out to his own people and saw their burdens. He saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his own people.
v12: He looked this way and that. Seeing no one, he struck the Egyptian dead and hid him in the sand.
</p>

<p>
v13: The next day he went out and saw two Hebrew men fighting. He asked the one in the wrong, “Why are you hitting your neighbor?”
v14: The man answered, “Who made you ruler and judge over us? Do you intend to kill me as you killed the Egyptian?” Moses was afraid. “What I did is known!” he thought.
</p>

<p>
v15: When Pharaoh heard about it, he sought to kill Moses. Moses fled from Pharaoh and settled in Midian. There he sat down by a well.
</p>

<p>
v16: The priest of Midian had seven daughters. They came to draw water and fill the troughs for their father’s flock.
v17: Some shepherds came and drove them away. But Moses stood up, rescued them, and watered their flock.
</p>

<p>
v18: When they returned to their father Reuel, he asked, “How did you get back so quickly today?”
v19: “An Egyptian rescued us from the shepherds,” they said. “He even drew water for us and watered the flock.”
</p>

<p>
v20: “Where is he?” their father asked. “Why did you leave the man there? Invite him to eat with us.”
</p>

<p>
v21: Moses agreed to stay with the man, who gave him his daughter Zipporah in marriage.
v22: She bore a son, and Moses named him Gershom. “I have been a foreigner in a foreign land,” he said.
</p>

## God hears Israel’s cry

<p>
v23: During that long time, the king of Egypt died. The Israelites groaned under their slavery and cried out. Their cry for help rose to God from their slavery.
v24: God heard their groaning. God remembered his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.
v25: God saw the Israelites. God knew.
</p>

## Notes

v2–3: The mother sees that the child is ‘good’ or ‘fine.’ The basket is called a tevah, the same word used for Noah’s ark. Both stories place vulnerable life in a vessel protected from water.

v3: The basket is placed among reeds at the river’s edge. The verse does not say it floated downstream.

v10: Moses’ name is linked here with the Hebrew verb ‘draw out.’ The naming explanation belongs to Pharaoh’s daughter’s speech.

v18: The father is named Reuel here and Jethro in 3:1. Their relationship is not explained by changing one name to the other.

v22: Gershom sounds like the Hebrew expression ‘a foreigner there.’ Moses’ own explanation emphasizes life in a foreign land.

v24–25: The repeated subject ‘God’ joins hearing, remembering, seeing, and knowing. The final ‘knew’ has no stated object; its brevity is retained.

## Vocabulary

v3: Basket — tevah
A protective container; elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible this word names Noah’s ark.

v22: Foreigner — ger
Someone residing outside their own people or established homeland.

v24: Covenant — berit
A binding commitment. God’s covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob connects this story with Genesis.
