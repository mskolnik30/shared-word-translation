## The Passover meal

<p>
v01: In Egypt, the LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron.
v02: “This month will mark the beginning of your months. It will be the first month of your year.
v03: Tell the whole community of Israel: On the tenth day of this month, each man is to take an animal from the flock for his family, one for each household.
v04: If a household is too small to eat one animal, its head and his nearest neighbor are to share one. Count the people and allow for how much each person will eat.
v05: The animal you choose must be a year-old male with no defect. You may take it from either the sheep or the goats.
v06: Keep it until the fourteenth day of this month. Then the whole assembly of Israel’s community is to slaughter their animals at twilight.
</p>

<p>
v07: They are to take some of the blood and put it on both doorposts and the lintel of the houses where they eat the animals.
v08: That night they are to eat the meat roasted over a fire, along with unleavened bread and bitter herbs.
v09: Do not eat it raw or boiled in water. Roast it over a fire, with its head, legs, and internal organs.
v10: Leave none of it until morning. Burn whatever is left by morning.
v11: Eat it with your clothes tucked into your belt, sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand. Eat in haste. It is the LORD’s Passover.
</p>

<p>
v12: That night I will pass through Egypt and strike down every firstborn in the land, among both people and livestock. I will carry out judgments against all the gods of Egypt. I am the LORD.
v13: The blood will be a sign for you on the houses where you are. When I see the blood, I will pass over you. No destroying plague will fall on you when I strike Egypt.
</p>

## Unleavened bread and remembrance

<p>
v14: This day will be a memorial for you. Celebrate it as a festival to the LORD throughout your generations, a lasting ordinance.
v15: Eat unleavened bread for seven days. On the first day, remove the leaven from your houses. Whoever eats anything leavened from the first day through the seventh will be cut off from Israel.
v16: Hold a sacred assembly on the first day and another on the seventh. No work may be done on those days, except preparing the food each person needs to eat.
v17: Observe the Festival of Unleavened Bread, because on this very day I brought your divisions out of Egypt. Observe this day throughout your generations as a lasting ordinance.
</p>

<p>
v18: In the first month, eat unleavened bread from the evening of the fourteenth day until the evening of the twenty-first.
v19: For seven days, no leaven is to be found in your houses. Anyone who eats leavened food will be cut off from Israel’s community, whether a resident foreigner or someone native to the land.
v20: Eat nothing leavened. Wherever you live, eat unleavened bread.”
</p>

## Instructions for Israel’s households

<p>
v21: Moses summoned all Israel’s elders and told them, “Go and choose animals from your flocks for your families, and slaughter the Passover animals.
v22: Take a bunch of hyssop, dip it in the blood in the basin, and apply some of that blood to the lintel and both doorposts. None of you is to go out through the doorway of your house before morning.
v23: The LORD will pass through to strike Egypt. When he sees the blood on the lintel and both doorposts, he will pass over the doorway and will not let the destroyer enter your houses to strike you.
</p>

<p>
v24: Keep this instruction as an ordinance for you and your children forever.
v25: When you enter the land the LORD will give you as he promised, keep this observance.
v26: When your children ask you, ‘What does this observance mean to you?’
v27: you are to say, ‘It is the Passover sacrifice to the LORD. He passed over the Israelites’ houses in Egypt when he struck the Egyptians, and he spared our homes.’” Then the people bowed down in worship.
</p>

<p>
v28: The Israelites went and did what the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron. That is what they did.
</p>

## Death and departure

<p>
v29: At midnight the LORD struck down every firstborn in Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh, who sat on his throne, to the firstborn of the captive in the dungeon, and every firstborn among the livestock.
v30: Pharaoh got up that night, along with all his officials and all the Egyptians. A great cry rose through Egypt, for there was no house without someone dead.
</p>

<p>
v31: That night he summoned Moses and Aaron and said, “Get up! Leave my people, both you and the Israelites. Go and serve the LORD, as you asked.
v32: Take your flocks and herds too, as you asked, and go. And bless me too!”
</p>

<p>
v33: The Egyptians pressed the people to leave the land quickly. “We’ll all be dead!” they said.
v34: So the people took their dough before it was leavened, carrying their kneading bowls on their shoulders, wrapped in their clothes.
v35: The Israelites had done as Moses told them: they had asked the Egyptians for silver and gold articles and clothing.
v36: The LORD had made the Egyptians look favorably on the people, and they gave them what they asked for. So the Israelites plundered Egypt.
</p>

<p>
v37: The Israelites traveled from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand men on foot, besides the children.
v38: A large, mixed crowd also went up with them, along with flocks and herds—a great many animals.
v39: They baked the dough they had brought from Egypt into unleavened cakes. It had not been leavened because they were driven out of Egypt and could not delay; they had not even prepared provisions for themselves.
</p>

<p>
v40: The Israelites’ stay in Egypt lasted four hundred thirty years.
v41: At the end of those four hundred thirty years, on that very day, all the LORD’s divisions left Egypt.
v42: It was a night of watchfulness for the LORD as he brought them out of Egypt. This same night belongs to the LORD, a night for all Israelites to keep watch throughout their generations.
</p>

## Who may share the Passover

<p>
v43: The LORD said to Moses and Aaron, “This is the ordinance for Passover: No foreign outsider may eat it.
v44: Any slave a man has bought with money may eat it once you have circumcised him.
v45: A temporary resident or a hired worker may not eat it.
v46: It must be eaten in one house. Do not take any meat outside the house, and do not break any of its bones.
v47: The whole community of Israel is to observe it.
</p>

<p>
v48: If a foreigner living among you wants to observe Passover to the LORD, every male in his household must be circumcised. Then he may take part and will be treated like someone native to the land. No uncircumcised male may eat it.
v49: The same instruction applies to the native and to the foreigner living among you.”
</p>

<p>
v50: All the Israelites did what the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron. That is what they did.
v51: On that very day, the LORD brought the Israelites out of Egypt, division by division.
</p>

## Notes

v3–6: The animal may be a lamb or a young goat. ‘At twilight’ represents ‘between the evenings’; the expression’s precise time span is disputed.

v13, 23, 27: ‘Pass over’ keeps the connection with Passover. In this account it includes sparing and protecting the marked houses. Verse 23 names ‘the destroyer’ but does not further identify it.

v15, 19: ‘Cut off’ is a severe exclusion formula. These verses do not specify whether the sanction is carried out by the community or by God, or describe its precise form.

v22: The word rendered ‘basin’ can also mean ‘threshold.’ The translation retains the basin reading; it does not add a separate rite at the threshold.

v37–40: The text gives about six hundred thousand men, besides children, and also mentions a mixed crowd. It states four hundred thirty years in Egypt. These figures are retained without attempting to resolve the chronological and population questions they raise.

v43–49: The Hebrew distinguishes several kinds of outsiders and household dependents. Verse 48 permits a resident foreigner to participate after the stated circumcision requirement; verse 44 speaks explicitly of a purchased slave.

## Vocabulary

v7: Lintel — mashqof
The horizontal part of a doorway above its two doorposts.

v11: Passover — pesaḥ
The name of the observance and, in some verses, its sacrificial animal.

v15: Unleavened bread — matstsot
Bread made without leavening. Se’or is the leavening material; ḥamets describes what has been leavened.

v42: Watchfulness — shimmurim
Keeping watch. The repeated word connects the departure night with Israel’s continuing observance.
