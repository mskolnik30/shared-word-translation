## Pharaoh refuses to let Israel go

<p>
v01: Afterward, Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh and said, “This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: ‘Let my people go so they can celebrate a festival for me in the wilderness.’”
</p>

<p>
v02: Pharaoh answered, “Who is the LORD, that I should listen to his voice and let Israel go? I do not know the LORD, and I will not let Israel go.”
</p>

<p>
v03: They said, “The God of the Hebrews has met with us. Please let us travel three days into the wilderness to sacrifice to the LORD our God, or he may strike us with plague or sword.”
</p>

<p>
v04: The king of Egypt said to them, “Moses and Aaron, why are you drawing the people away from their work? Get back to your burdens!”
v05: Pharaoh continued, “Look how many people there are in the land now, and you are stopping them from carrying their burdens!”
</p>

## Bricks without straw

<p>
v06: That same day Pharaoh gave orders to the people’s taskmasters and foremen:
v07: “Stop supplying the people with straw for making bricks as you did before. They must go and gather their own straw.
v08: But require them to make as many bricks as before. Do not reduce the quota. They are lazy—that is why they keep crying, ‘Let us go and sacrifice to our God.’
v09: Make the work weigh more heavily on the men. Keep them at it so they pay no attention to lying words.”
</p>

<p>
v10: The taskmasters and foremen went out and told the people, “This is what Pharaoh says: ‘I will give you no straw.
v11: Go and get your own straw wherever you can find it. Your work will not be reduced at all.’”
</p>

<p>
v12: So the people scattered throughout Egypt to gather stubble to use as straw.
</p>

<p>
v13: The taskmasters kept pressing them: “Finish each day’s work, just as you did when straw was supplied!”
v14: The Israelite foremen whom Pharaoh’s taskmasters had put over the people were beaten. “Why have you not met your brick quota,” they were asked, “either yesterday or today, as you did before?”
</p>

## The foremen appeal to Pharaoh

<p>
v15: The Israelite foremen went to Pharaoh and cried out, “Why are you treating your servants like this?
v16: Your servants receive no straw, yet we are told, ‘Make bricks!’ Look, your servants are being beaten, but the fault lies with your own people.”
</p>

<p>
v17: “Lazy!” Pharaoh said. “You are lazy! That is why you say, ‘Let us go and sacrifice to the LORD.’
v18: Now go back to work. You will get no straw, but you must deliver the full brick quota.”
</p>

<p>
v19: The Israelite foremen saw that they were in trouble when they were told, “You must not reduce the number of bricks required each day.”
</p>

<p>
v20: As they left Pharaoh, they came upon Moses and Aaron, who were standing there to meet them.
v21: They said to Moses and Aaron, “May the LORD look at you and judge! You have made us stink in the eyes of Pharaoh and his servants. You have put a sword in their hands to kill us.”
</p>

## Moses brings his complaint to the LORD

<p>
v22: Moses returned to the LORD and said, “Lord, why have you brought harm on this people? Why did you send me?
v23: Ever since I went to Pharaoh to speak in your name, he has brought harm on this people, and you have not rescued your people at all.”
</p>

## Notes

v3: The warning says ‘us,’ referring to the speakers and their people. It does not say here that plague or sword will strike Pharaoh.

v6, 14: The taskmasters enforce Pharaoh’s demands. The foremen are Israelite intermediaries appointed over the workers; they are themselves beaten when the quota is missed.

v8–9, 17: ‘Lazy’ and ‘lying words’ belong to Pharaoh’s accusations, not the narrator’s assessment.

v16: The final Hebrew clause is grammatically difficult. The translation understands the fault as belonging to Pharaoh’s people. Another ancient reading makes the accusation ‘you are wronging your own people.’

v21: The foremen combine smell and sight: they have been made to stink ‘in the eyes’ of Pharaoh’s household. The sword image voices their fear of being killed.

v22–23: Moses first asks why God has brought harm, then says Pharaoh has brought harm. The same Hebrew verb connects the two accusations; his protest is left in its direct form.

## Vocabulary

v1: Festival — ḥagag
To keep a festival, here directed to the LORD in the wilderness.

v12: Stubble — qash
Stalks left after harvesting; the workers gather these in place of supplied straw.

v14, 18: Quota
The fixed amount of brick production demanded from the workers each day.
