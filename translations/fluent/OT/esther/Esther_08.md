## Esther appeals for her people

<p>
v01: That day King Ahasuerus gave Queen Esther the estate of Haman, the enemy of the Jews. Mordecai came into the king’s presence, because Esther had explained how he was related to her.
v02: The king removed his signet ring, which he had taken back from Haman, and gave it to Mordecai. Esther put Mordecai in charge of Haman’s estate.
v03: Esther spoke to the king again. She fell at his feet, wept, and begged him to undo the evil of Haman the Agagite and the scheme he had devised against the Jews.
v04: The king extended the gold scepter to Esther, and she rose and stood before him.
v05: She said, “If it pleases the king, if I have won his favor, if he considers it right, and if he is pleased with me, let an order be written to revoke the letters devised by Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, which he wrote to destroy the Jews in every royal province.
v06: How could I bear to see the disaster that would overtake my people? How could I bear to see my own family destroyed?”
</p>

## A second decree

<p>
v07: King Ahasuerus told Queen Esther and Mordecai the Jew, “I have given Haman’s estate to Esther, and they have hung him on the stake because he raised his hand against the Jews.
v08: Now you may write concerning the Jews as you see fit, in the king’s name, and seal it with the king’s signet ring. A document written in the king’s name and sealed with his ring cannot be revoked.”
v09: The king’s scribes were summoned at once, on the twenty-third day of the third month, the month of Sivan. Everything Mordecai commanded was written to the Jews, the satraps, the governors, and the officials of the 127 provinces from India to Cush—to each province in its own script and each people in its own language, and to the Jews in their own script and language.
v10: He wrote in King Ahasuerus’s name, sealed the letters with the king’s signet ring, and sent them by mounted couriers riding swift horses bred from the royal herd.
v11: The king permitted the Jews in every city to gather and defend their lives: to destroy, kill, and annihilate any armed force of a people or province that attacked them, including children and women, and to plunder their property,
v12: on a single day throughout King Ahasuerus’s provinces—the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month of Adar.
v13: A copy of the document was to be issued as law in every province and made known to all the peoples, so that the Jews would be ready on that day to take revenge on their enemies.
v14: The couriers rode out on the swift royal horses, hurried along by the king’s command. The decree was also issued in the citadel of Susa.
</p>

<p>
v15: Mordecai left the king’s presence wearing royal clothing of blue-violet and white, a great gold crown, and a mantle of fine linen and purple wool. The city of Susa shouted for joy.
v16: For the Jews there was light and joy, gladness and honor.
v17: In every province and every city reached by the king’s command and decree, the Jews celebrated with joy and gladness, banquets and a holiday. Many among the peoples of the land declared themselves Jews, because fear of the Jews had fallen on them.
</p>

## Notes

v10: The Hebrew terms for the couriers’ horses are uncommon. The wording identifies swift animals of royal breeding without treating every term as a separate species.

v11: The decree permits lethal action and plunder. Its mention of children and women can attach to the hostile population being destroyed or to the Jews being attacked. The draft keeps the former reading; the ambiguity should not be used to hide the decree’s disturbing breadth.

v17: The rare verb can mean becoming Jews or identifying themselves as Jews. The text gives fear of the Jews as the motive; it does not describe an inward conversion.

## Vocabulary

v08: Signet ring
The royal seal authenticated a decree issued in the king’s name.

v13: Avenge
The decree authorizes retaliation; its language goes beyond simply escaping danger.
