## Esther names the enemy

<p>
v01: So the king and Haman came to drink with Queen Esther.
v02: On this second day, as they drank wine, the king again asked Esther, “What is your petition, Queen Esther? It will be granted. What is your request? Even if it is half the kingdom, it will be done.”
v03: Queen Esther answered, “If I have won your favor, Your Majesty, and if it pleases the king, grant me my life—that is my petition—and the lives of my people—that is my request.
v04: For my people and I have been sold to be destroyed, killed, and annihilated. If we had only been sold as male and female slaves, I would have kept silent, for such distress would not justify troubling the king.”
v05: King Ahasuerus demanded of Queen Esther, “Who is he? Where is the man who has dared to do this?”
v06: Esther said, “An adversary and an enemy—this wicked Haman!” Haman was terrified before the king and queen.
</p>

## Haman is executed

<p>
v07: The king rose in fury from the wine banquet and went into the palace garden. Haman stayed behind to plead with Queen Esther for his life, for he saw that the king had determined his destruction.
v08: When the king returned from the palace garden to the banquet hall, Haman was falling onto the couch where Esther lay. The king exclaimed, “Will he even assault the queen while I am here in the house?” As soon as the words left the king’s mouth, they covered Haman’s face.
v09: Then Harbona, one of the eunuchs attending the king, said, “There is also the stake Haman made for Mordecai, who spoke to save the king. It stands at Haman’s house, fifty cubits high.” The king said, “Hang him on it!”
v10: They hung Haman on the stake he had prepared for Mordecai. Then the king’s fury subsided.
</p>

## Notes

v04: The last clause is difficult. It can mean that enslavement would not warrant troubling the king, or that the enemy could not compensate for the king’s loss. Esther’s argument distinguishes enslavement from the planned destruction.

v08: The king accuses Haman of assaulting Esther. The narrator has described Haman falling onto her couch while pleading for his life.

v09: The Hebrew names a wooden structure fifty cubits high. Whether execution involved hanging or impalement is uncertain. “Stake” continues the wording of chapter 5.

## Vocabulary

v04: Sold
Esther uses the language of sale for the people’s delivery into the enemy’s power.

v09: Cubit
An ancient measure based on the forearm; fifty cubits was roughly seventy-five feet.
