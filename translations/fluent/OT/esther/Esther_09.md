## The reversal

<p>
v01: On the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month of Adar, the king’s command and decree were due to take effect. The Jews’ enemies had hoped to overpower them that day, but the reverse happened: the Jews overpowered those who hated them.
v02: The Jews gathered in their cities throughout King Ahasuerus’s provinces to strike those who sought their harm. No one could withstand them, for fear of them had fallen on all the peoples.
v03: All the provincial officials, satraps, governors, and those who conducted the king’s business supported the Jews, because fear of Mordecai had fallen on them.
v04: Mordecai was powerful in the king’s palace, and his reputation spread through all the provinces. The man Mordecai was growing more and more powerful.
</p>

<p>
v05: The Jews struck all their enemies with the sword, killing and destroying them. They did as they pleased to those who hated them.
v06: In the citadel of Susa, the Jews killed and destroyed five hundred men.
v07: They also killed Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha,
v08: Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha,
v09: Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai, and Vaizatha—
v10: the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews. But they did not lay hands on the plunder.
</p>

## Another day in Susa

<p>
v11: That day the number killed in the citadel of Susa was reported to the king.
v12: The king said to Queen Esther, “In the citadel of Susa, the Jews have killed and destroyed five hundred men and Haman’s ten sons. What must they have done in the other royal provinces! Now what is your petition? It will be granted. What further request do you have? It will be done.”
v13: Esther answered, “If it pleases the king, let the Jews in Susa act tomorrow as today’s decree permits. And let Haman’s ten sons be hung on the stake.”
</p>

<p>
v14: The king ordered this done. A decree was issued in Susa, and Haman’s ten sons were hung.
v15: The Jews in Susa gathered again on the fourteenth day of Adar and killed three hundred men in Susa. But they did not lay hands on the plunder.
</p>

## Rest and celebration

<p>
v16: The other Jews in the king’s provinces gathered to defend their lives and gain relief from their enemies. They killed seventy-five thousand of those who hated them. But they did not lay hands on the plunder.
v17: This happened on the thirteenth day of Adar. On the fourteenth they rested and made it a day of banqueting and joy.
v18: The Jews in Susa, however, gathered on both the thirteenth and fourteenth. They rested on the fifteenth and made it a day of banqueting and joy.
v19: That is why Jews in the countryside, those living in unwalled towns, celebrate the fourteenth day of Adar with joy, banqueting, and a holiday, sending portions of food to one another.
</p>

## The days of Purim

<p>
v20: Mordecai recorded these events and sent letters to all the Jews in every province of King Ahasuerus, near and far,
v21: requiring them to observe the fourteenth and fifteenth days of Adar every year,
v22: as the days when the Jews gained relief from their enemies, and the month when sorrow turned to joy and mourning to a holiday. They were to make these days a time of banqueting and joy, sending food to one another and gifts to the poor.
v23: The Jews accepted what they had already begun to practice and what Mordecai had written to them.
v24: For Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had plotted to destroy them. He had cast the Pur—that is, the lot—to throw them into panic and destroy them.
v25: But when the matter came before the king, he ordered in writing that Haman’s evil scheme against the Jews should come back on his own head. Haman and his sons were hung on the stake.
v26: That is why these days were called Purim, after the word Pur. Because of everything in this letter, and because of what they had seen and what had happened to them,
v27: the Jews established and accepted an enduring obligation for themselves, their descendants, and all who joined them: to observe these two days every year, as written and at the appointed time.
v28: These days were to be remembered and observed in every generation, every family, every province, and every city. These days of Purim must never cease among the Jews, nor their memory disappear from their descendants.
</p>

<p>
v29: Queen Esther daughter of Abihail, together with Mordecai the Jew, wrote with full authority to confirm this second letter concerning Purim.
v30: He sent letters to all the Jews in the 127 provinces of Ahasuerus’s kingdom, with words of peace and assurance,
v31: confirming the days of Purim at their appointed times, as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had established for them, and as they had established for themselves and their descendants the practices of fasting and lamenting.
v32: Esther’s decree confirmed these matters concerning Purim, and it was recorded in a book.
</p>

## Notes

v10: The refusal to take plunder recurs in verses 15 and 16, although the second decree had permitted it.

v13: Haman’s sons have already been killed in verses 7–10. Esther now requests the public display of their bodies on the wooden structure.

v25: “When the matter came” may also be read “when she came,” referring to Esther. This summary compresses events recounted at greater length earlier.

v30: “Peace and assurance” renders an expression literally linking peace and truth or reliability.

## Vocabulary

v26: Purim
The plural of pur, the lot cast to choose the date for the Jews’ destruction.

v22: Gifts to the poor
The commemoration includes sharing food with one another and giving to those in need.
