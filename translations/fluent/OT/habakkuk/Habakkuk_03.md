## Habakkuk’s prayer

<p>
v01: A prayer of Habakkuk the prophet, according to Shigionoth.
</p>

<p>
v02: LORD, I have heard the report about you;
I am afraid.
LORD, bring your work to life in these years;
make it known in these years.
In your turmoil, remember compassion.
</p>

## God comes in splendor

<p>
v03: God comes from Teman,
the Holy One from Mount Paran. Selah.
His splendor covers the heavens;
his praise fills the earth.
v04: His brightness is like light;
rays flash from his hand,
and there his power is hidden.
v05: Plague goes before him,
and pestilence follows at his feet.
</p>

<p>
v06: He stands and measures the earth;
he looks, and nations leap in alarm.
Ancient mountains shatter;
age-old hills bow down.
His paths are everlasting.
v07: I see the tents of Cushan under affliction;
the tent curtains of Midian tremble.
</p>

## The march of deliverance

<p>
v08: Was the LORD angry with the rivers?
Was your anger against the rivers,
your fury against the sea,
when you rode your horses,
your chariots of salvation?
v09: You draw your bow bare—
oaths, rods, a word. Selah.
You split the earth with rivers.
v10: The mountains see you and writhe;
a torrent of water sweeps past.
The deep raises its voice
and lifts its hands high.
v11: Sun and moon stand still in their dwelling
at the light of your flying arrows,
at the gleam of your flashing spear.
</p>

<p>
v12: In indignation you march through the earth;
in anger you thresh the nations.
v13: You go out to save your people,
to save your anointed one.
You crush the head of the wicked house,
laying it bare from foundation to neck. Selah.
v14: With his own shafts you pierce the head of his warriors,
who storm out to scatter me,
delighting as though to devour the poor in hiding.
v15: You tread through the sea with your horses,
through the surge of mighty waters.
</p>

## Waiting and rejoicing

<p>
v16: I hear, and my body trembles;
at the sound my lips quiver.
Decay enters my bones,
and my legs shake beneath me.
Yet I will wait quietly for the day of distress
to come upon the people who raid us.
</p>

<p>
v17: Though the fig tree does not blossom
and there is no fruit on the vines,
though the olive crop fails
and the fields produce no food,
though the flock is cut off from the pen
and there are no cattle in the stalls,
v18: yet I will celebrate in the LORD;
I will rejoice in the God who saves me.
v19: The LORD, the Lord, is my strength.
He makes my feet like a deer’s
and leads me on my heights.
For the music leader, with my stringed instruments.
</p>

## Notes

v01,03: “Shigionoth” and “Selah” are retained as ancient performance or musical terms whose precise force is uncertain.

v09: “Oaths, rods, a word” represents an exceptionally difficult Hebrew phrase. Its syntax and relation to the bow image remain uncertain; the draft does not claim to resolve it.

v13: The Hebrew moves from a house’s foundation to a neck. The mixed image is preserved rather than reconstructed as a single clear scene.

v16: The final clause about the attacking people is difficult. The draft preserves waiting and distress without fixing an otherwise unstated timetable.

## Vocabulary

v03: **Teman; Paran**: Southern place names within the poem’s depiction of God’s approach.

v19: **High places**: The heights in the deer-footed movement image; not necessarily worship sites here.
