## Justice at the palace

<p>
v01: The **LORD** said, “Go down to the palace of the king of Judah and speak this word there:
v02: ‘Hear the word of the **LORD**, king of Judah, you who sit on David's throne—you, your servants, and your people who enter these gates.
v03: The **LORD** says: Do what is just and right. Rescue the one who has been robbed from the oppressor's hand. Do not wrong or mistreat the resident foreigner, the orphan, or the widow. Do not shed innocent blood in this place.
v04: If you truly carry out this word, kings who sit on David's throne will enter the gates of this palace, riding in chariots and on horses, each with his servants and his people.
v05: But if you will not listen to these words, I swear by myself, declares the **LORD**, this palace will become a ruin.’
</p>

<p>
v06: “For this is what the **LORD** says about the palace of the king of Judah:
Though you are Gilead to me,
the summit of Lebanon,
I will make you a wilderness,
a place of uninhabited towns.
v07: I will set apart destroyers against you,
each with his weapons.
They will cut down your finest cedars
and throw them into the fire.
v08: “Many nations will pass by this city and ask one another, ‘Why did the **LORD** do this to this great city?’
v09: They will answer, ‘Because they abandoned the covenant of the **LORD** their God, bowed down to other gods, and served them.’
</p>

## The king who will not return

<p>
v10: Do not weep for the dead king
or mourn for him.
Weep bitterly for the one who is leaving,
for he will never return
or see the land of his birth again.
v11: For the **LORD** says this about Shallum son of Josiah, king of Judah, who became king in place of his father Josiah and left this place: “He will never return here.
v12: He will die in the place where they have exiled him. He will never see this land again.”
</p>

## Cedar without justice

<p>
v13: Woe to the man who builds his house through injustice
and his upper rooms through wrongdoing,
who makes his neighbor work for nothing
and never pays him his wages!
v14: He says, “I will build myself a spacious house
with airy upper rooms.”
He cuts windows for it,
panels it with cedar,
and paints it vermilion.
v15: Does competing in cedar make you a king?
Did not your father eat and drink
and do what was just and right?
Then things went well for him.
v16: He defended the cause of the poor and needy;
then things went well.
Is that not what it means to know me?
declares the **LORD**.
v17: But your eyes and your heart
are set only on your dishonest gain,
on shedding innocent blood,
and on practicing oppression and violence.
</p>

<p>
v18: So this is what the **LORD** says about Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah:
They will not mourn for him,
“Alas, my brother! Alas, my sister!”
They will not mourn for him,
“Alas, my lord! Alas, his splendor!”
v19: He will have a donkey's burial—
dragged away and thrown
beyond the gates of Jerusalem.
</p>

## A dynasty brought down

<p>
v20: Climb Lebanon and cry out!
Raise your voice in Bashan!
Cry out from Abarim,
for all your lovers have been crushed.
v21: I spoke to you when you were secure,
but you said, “I will not listen.”
This has been your way from your youth:
you have never listened to my voice.
v22: The wind will shepherd all your shepherds away,
and your lovers will go into captivity.
Then you will be ashamed and humiliated
because of all your evil.
v23: You who live in Lebanon,
who nest among the cedars,
how you will be pitied when pangs come upon you,
pain like that of a woman giving birth!
</p>

<p>
v24: “As I live, declares the **LORD**, even if you, Coniah son of Jehoiakim, king of Judah, were a signet ring on my right hand, I would pull you off.
v25: I will hand you over to those seeking your life, to those you fear—to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon and to the Chaldeans.
v26: I will hurl you and the mother who gave birth to you into another country, where neither of you was born. There you will die.
v27: They will never return to the land they long to return to.”
</p>

<p>
v28: Is this man Coniah a despised, broken pot,
a vessel no one wants?
Why have he and his offspring been hurled away,
thrown into a land they do not know?
v29: Land, land, land!
Hear the word of the **LORD**!
v30: The **LORD** says:
“Record this man as childless,
a man who will not prosper in his lifetime.
For none of his offspring will prosper
by sitting on David's throne
and ruling again in Judah.”
</p>

## Notes

v10–12: The dead king and the departing king are contrasted. Verse 11 names the departing king Shallum; his exile, not merely his absence, is in view.

v22–23: The wind “shepherds” the shepherds away. The following verb, rendered “how you will be pitied,” is difficult; it has also been understood as an ironic expression of favor or as groaning. The labor-pain image is clear.

v24: Coniah is a shortened name of Jeconiah, named in 24:1. The signet ring carries the image of something close to the king and bearing his authority.

v28–30: The passage mentions Coniah's offspring before calling him “childless.” Verse 30 specifies failure to continue the ruling line on David's throne; it need not deny that he had children.

## Vocabulary

v03: **Resident foreigner**: A person living among a people without belonging to its native family or landholding structure.

v14: **Vermilion**: A vivid red pigment.

v24: **Signet ring**: A ring bearing a seal used to authenticate documents or possessions.
