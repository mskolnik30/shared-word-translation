## The yoke of Babylon

<p>
v01: At the beginning of the reign of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah, this word came to Jeremiah from the **LORD**:
v02: “The **LORD** said this to me: Make straps and yoke bars for yourself, and put them on your neck.
v03: Send them to the kings of Edom, Moab, the Ammonites, Tyre, and Sidon through the messengers who have come to Jerusalem to King Zedekiah of Judah.
v04: Give the messengers this charge for their masters: ‘The **LORD** of hosts, the God of Israel, says: Tell your masters this:
v05: I made the earth, the people, and the animals on the face of the earth by my great power and my outstretched arm. I give it to whomever I see fit.
v06: Now I have put all these lands into the hand of my servant Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon. I have even given him the wild animals to serve him.
v07: All the nations will serve him, his son, and his grandson, until the time comes for his own land as well. Then many nations and great kings will enslave him.
</p>

<p>
v08: Any nation or kingdom that will not serve Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, that will not put its neck under the yoke of the king of Babylon, I will call to account with sword, famine, and plague, declares the **LORD**, until I finish them off by his hand.
v09: So do not listen to your prophets, diviners, dreams, omen readers, or sorcerers who tell you, “You will not serve the king of Babylon.”
v10: They are prophesying lies to you, which will take you far from your soil. I will drive you out, and you will perish.
v11: But any nation that puts its neck under the yoke of the king of Babylon and serves him, I will leave on its own soil, declares the **LORD**, to cultivate it and live there.’”
</p>

## The word to Zedekiah

<p>
v12: I spoke these same words to Zedekiah king of Judah: “Put your necks under the yoke of the king of Babylon. Serve him and his people, and live.
v13: Why should you and your people die by sword, famine, and plague, as the **LORD** has said about any nation that will not serve the king of Babylon?
v14: Do not listen to the words of the prophets who tell you, ‘You will not serve the king of Babylon.’ They are prophesying lies to you.
v15: ‘I did not send them,’ declares the **LORD**, ‘yet they prophesy falsely in my name. The result will be that I drive you out, and you perish—you and the prophets who prophesy to you.’”
</p>

## The temple vessels

<p>
v16: I also spoke to the priests and all this people: “The **LORD** says: Do not listen to your prophets who prophesy to you, ‘Look, the vessels of the house of the **LORD** will soon be brought back from Babylon!’ They are prophesying lies to you.
v17: Do not listen to them. Serve the king of Babylon, and live. Why should this city become a ruin?
v18: If they really are prophets, and if the word of the **LORD** is with them, let them plead with the **LORD** of hosts that the vessels still in the house of the **LORD**, in the palace of the king of Judah, and in Jerusalem will not go to Babylon.
v19: For the **LORD** of hosts says this about the pillars, the Sea, the stands, and the rest of the vessels left in this city,
v20: which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon did not take when he exiled Jeconiah son of Jehoiakim, king of Judah, from Jerusalem to Babylon, along with all the nobles of Judah and Jerusalem:
v21: Yes, the **LORD** of hosts, the God of Israel, says this about the vessels left in the house of the **LORD**, in the palace of the king of Judah, and in Jerusalem:
v22: ‘They will be brought to Babylon and remain there until the day I attend to them, declares the **LORD**. Then I will bring them up and restore them to this place.’”
</p>

## Notes

v01,03,12: The pinned Hebrew names Jehoiakim in verse 1, but verses 3 and 12 name Zedekiah, as does 28:1. The discrepancy is preserved rather than silently replacing the heading's king.

v07: “His son and his grandson” preserves the stated dynastic sequence. The text is not expanded into a list of historical rulers.

v09: The Hebrew literally lists “your dreams” among the sources of misleading guidance, though the surrounding list names people.

v19–22: The promise concerns the furnishings still in Jerusalem, not an immediate return of the vessels already taken. The repeated introductions keep that distinction in view.

## Vocabulary

v02: **Yoke**: A bar and straps used to harness working animals; here a physical sign of subjection to Babylon.

v19: **Sea**: The large water basin among the temple furnishings, not a geographical sea.
