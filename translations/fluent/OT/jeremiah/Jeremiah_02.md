## A people who abandoned their spring

<p>
v01: The **LORD**’s word came to me:
</p>

<p>
v02: “Go and proclaim within Jerusalem’s hearing: This is what the **LORD** says:
‘I remember the faithful love of your youth,
the love of your bridal days,
how you followed me through the wilderness,
through a land never sown.
v03: Israel was holy to the **LORD**,
the firstfruits of his harvest.
All who devoured him incurred guilt;
disaster came upon them,’ declares the **LORD**.”
</p>

<p>
v04: Hear the **LORD**’s word, house of Jacob,
and all the clans of the house of Israel.
</p>

<p>
v05: This is what the **LORD** says:
“What wrong did your ancestors find in me
that they went so far from me,
followed emptiness and became empty themselves?
v06: They did not ask, ‘Where is the **LORD**,
who brought us up from Egypt,
who led us through the wilderness,
a land of deserts and pits,
a land of drought and deep darkness,
a land no one crossed and no one inhabited?’
v07: I brought you into a fruitful land
to eat its fruit and goodness.
But you came and defiled my land;
you made my inheritance offensive.
v08: The priests did not ask, ‘Where is the **LORD**?’
Those who handle instruction did not know me.
The shepherds rebelled against me;
the prophets prophesied by Baal
and followed things that cannot help.
v09: So I will still bring a case against you,” declares the **LORD**,
“and against your children’s children.
</p>

<p>
v10: Cross to Kittim’s coastlands and look;
send to Kedar and examine carefully.
See whether anything like this has happened:
v11: Has a nation exchanged its gods,
even though they are not gods?
Yet my people have exchanged their Glory
for what cannot help.
v12: Be appalled at this, heavens!
Shudder; be utterly desolate!” declares the **LORD**.
v13: “For my people have done two evils:
they abandoned me, the spring of living water,
and dug themselves cisterns,
cracked cisterns that cannot hold water.
</p>

<p>
v14: Is Israel a slave,
a slave born in the household?
Why then has he become plunder?
v15: Young lions roared against him;
they raised their voices
and made his land a waste.
His cities are burned, without inhabitants.
v16: Even the people of Memphis and Tahpanhes
have grazed the crown of your head.
v17: Have you not brought this on yourself
by abandoning the **LORD** your God
while he led you along the way?
v18: Now why take the road to Egypt
to drink Shihor’s waters?
Why take the road to Assyria
to drink the River’s waters?
v19: Your own wickedness will discipline you;
your turning away will rebuke you.
Know and see how evil and bitter it is
to abandon the **LORD** your God
and have no fear of me,”
declares the Lord **GOD** of hosts.
</p>

<p>
v20: “Long ago I broke your yoke
and tore apart your bonds.
But you said, ‘I will not serve!’
On every high hill and under every green tree
you sprawled as a prostitute.
v21: I planted you as a choice vine,
entirely sound stock.
How did you turn into
a degenerate foreign vine before me?
v22: Though you wash with soda
and use soap in abundance,
the stain of your guilt remains before me,”
declares the Lord **GOD**.
</p>

<p>
v23: “How can you say, ‘I am not defiled;
I have not followed the Baals’?
Look at your path in the valley;
know what you have done,
you swift young she-camel zigzagging on her paths,
v24: wild donkey accustomed to the wilderness,
sniffing the wind in her desire!
Who can turn her back in heat?
None who seek her need weary themselves;
in her month they will find her.
v25: Keep your feet from going bare
and your throat from thirst.
But you said, ‘It is hopeless! No!
I love strangers, and I will follow them.’
</p>

<p>
v26: As a thief is ashamed when caught,
so the house of Israel is ashamed—
they, their kings, officials, priests and prophets.
v27: They say to wood, ‘You are my father,’
and to stone, ‘You gave birth to us.’
They turn their backs to me, not their faces,
but in trouble they say, ‘Rise and save us!’
v28: Where are the gods you made for yourself?
Let them rise, if they can save you in trouble!
For your gods are as numerous as your cities, Judah.
v29: Why bring a case against me?
All of you have rebelled against me,” declares the **LORD**.
</p>

<p>
v30: “I struck your children for nothing;
they accepted no discipline.
Your sword devoured your prophets
like a destructive lion.
v31: You of this generation, consider the **LORD**’s word!
Have I been a wilderness to Israel,
a land of thick darkness?
Why do my people say, ‘We roam freely;
we will come to you no more’?
v32: Does a young woman forget her jewelry,
a bride her wedding sash?
Yet my people have forgotten me
for days beyond counting.
</p>

<p>
v33: How well you arrange your way to seek love!
You have even taught wicked women your ways.
v34: Even on your skirts is found
the lifeblood of the innocent poor.
You did not find them breaking in.
Yet despite all this,
v35: you say, ‘I am innocent;
surely his anger has turned away from me.’
Look, I am bringing you to judgment
for saying, ‘I have not sinned.’
v36: Why do you rush about so much,
changing your way?
Egypt will put you to shame
just as Assyria did.
v37: You will leave there too
with your hands on your head,
for the **LORD** has rejected those you trust;
you will not prosper through them.”
</p>

## Notes

v20,34: Verse 20 retains the written “I will not serve”; the recorded reading has “I will not transgress.” The opening “I broke” in verse 20 can also be understood as an older form of “you broke.” Verse 34 uses an older second-person feminine form, “you found them.” The accusation concerns innocent poor people killed without a break-in defense.

v16,24: The head-grazing image can suggest shaving or devastation. The wild donkey’s sexual behavior is part of the poem’s accusation, not a description of women in general.

## Vocabulary

v13: **Living water**: Fresh, flowing water from a spring, contrasted with stored water in a cracked cistern.
