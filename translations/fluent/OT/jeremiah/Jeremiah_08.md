## Bones beneath the heavens

<p>
v01: “At that time,” declares the **LORD**, “the bones of Judah’s kings and officials, the bones of the priests and prophets, and the bones of Jerusalem’s inhabitants will be brought out of their graves.
v02: They will be spread out before the sun, the moon, and all the host of heaven—the ones they loved, served, followed, sought, and bowed down to. The bones will not be gathered or buried. They will lie like dung on the ground.
v03: All who remain of this evil family will prefer death to life, wherever I have driven the survivors,” declares the **LORD** of hosts.
</p>

## Refusing to return

<p>
v04: “Tell them: This is what the **LORD** says:
When people fall, don’t they get up?
When someone turns away, doesn’t that person turn back?
v05: Then why has this people turned away—
Jerusalem—in a lasting refusal to return?
They cling to deceit;
they refuse to turn back.
v06: I have listened closely,
but they do not speak honestly.
No one regrets their evil
and asks, ‘What have I done?’
Everyone returns to their own course,
like a horse charging into battle.
v07: Even the stork in the sky knows its appointed seasons;
the turtledove, swift, and crane
keep the time of their return.
But my people do not know
the **LORD**’s judgment.
</p>

<p>
v08: How can you say, ‘We are wise;
the **LORD**’s instruction is with us’?
Look—the lying pen of the scribes
has turned it into a lie.
v09: The wise are put to shame,
shattered and trapped.
They have rejected the **LORD**’s word;
what wisdom do they have?
</p>

<p>
v10: So I will give their wives to others
and their fields to new owners.
From the least to the greatest,
everyone is greedy for gain.
From prophet to priest,
everyone deals in lies.
v11: They treat the wound of my daughter, my people,
as though it were slight,
saying, ‘Peace, peace,’
when there is no peace.
v12: Were they ashamed of the detestable things they did?
They felt no shame at all;
they did not know how to blush.
So they will fall among the fallen;
when I call them to account, they will stumble,”
says the **LORD**.
</p>

<p>
v13: “I will sweep them away,” declares the **LORD**.
“No grapes on the vine,
no figs on the fig tree;
the leaves have withered.
What I gave them will pass away from them.”
v14: “Why are we sitting here?
Gather together!
Let’s enter the fortified cities
and perish there.
For the **LORD** our God has doomed us
and given us poisoned water to drink,
because we sinned against the **LORD**.
v15: We hoped for peace, but no good came;
for a time of healing, but look—terror!”
</p>

<p>
v16: From Dan comes the snorting of his horses;
at the neighing of his stallions,
the whole land trembles.
They come and devour the land and all it holds,
the city and those who live in it.
v17: “Look, I am sending snakes among you,
adders that cannot be charmed.
They will bite you,” declares the **LORD**.
</p>

## A wound without healing

<p>
v18: My comfort fails in sorrow;
my heart is sick within me.
v19: Listen! My daughter, my people, cries out
from a distant land:
‘Is the **LORD** no longer in Zion?
Is her king no longer there?’
‘Why have they provoked me with their carved images,
with foreign things of emptiness?’
v20: “The harvest is past,
the summer is over,
and we have not been saved.”
v21: At the breaking of my daughter, my people, I am broken.
I mourn;
horror has taken hold of me.
v22: Is there no balm in Gilead?
Is there no healer there?
Why, then, has healing not come
to my daughter, my people?
v23: If only my head were water
and my eyes a spring of tears!
I would weep day and night
for the slain of my daughter, my people.
</p>

## Notes

v8: The charge concerns the scribes’ handling of the LORD’s instruction. The Hebrew does not explain here precisely how their pen has made it false.

v11,19,21–23: “My daughter, my people” keeps the poem’s picture of the people as a wounded daughter. Voices move between the people, the lamenting speaker, and God; not every transition is explicitly identified.

v13,18: Verse 13’s gathering/removal verb and final clause are difficult. “Sweep them away” is one reading; the ending may also concern things given to them that overtake them. The rare opening expression in verse 18 is also uncertain; the rendering connects it with failing comfort in sorrow.

v23: This edition preserves the existing Hebrew numbering: 8:23 is 9:1 in many English Bibles, and the following chapter has twenty-five verses.

## Vocabulary

v7: **Judgment**: God’s ruling or ordered way; the contrast is with birds that recognize their seasons.

v22: **Balm**: An aromatic substance used for healing. Gilead was associated with this product.
