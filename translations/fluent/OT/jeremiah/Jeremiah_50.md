## Babylon and its gods

<p>
v01: The word the **LORD** spoke concerning Babylon, the land of the Chaldeans, through Jeremiah the prophet:
v02: “Announce it among the nations; proclaim it!
Raise a signal flag; proclaim it, hide nothing!
Say, ‘Babylon is captured!
Bel is disgraced; Merodach is shattered.
Her images are disgraced;
her idols are shattered.’
v03: A nation has come against her from the north.
It will turn her land into a desolation,
with no one living there.
People and animals alike
have fled and gone.
</p>

## Lost sheep and their return

<p>
v04: In those days, at that time,” declares the **LORD**,
“the people of Israel and Judah will come together.
They will walk along weeping
and seek the **LORD** their God.
v05: They will ask the way to Zion,
their faces turned toward her:
‘Come, let us join ourselves to the **LORD**
in an everlasting covenant that will not be forgotten.’
v06: My people have been lost sheep.
Their shepherds led them astray,
turning them loose on the mountains.
They wandered from mountain to hill
and forgot their resting place.
v07: Everyone who found them devoured them.
Their enemies said, ‘We are not guilty,
because they sinned against the **LORD**,
the pasture of righteousness,
the **LORD**, the hope of their ancestors.’
v08: Flee from Babylon;
leave the land of the Chaldeans.
Be like male goats at the head of the flock.
v09: I am stirring up and bringing against Babylon
an assembly of great nations from the north.
They will line up against her;
from there she will be captured.
Their arrows are like a skilled warrior
who does not return empty-handed.
v10: Chaldea will become plunder;
all who plunder her will have their fill,” declares the **LORD**.
v11: “Though you rejoice and exult,
you who pillage my inheritance,
though you frisk like a heifer threshing grain
and neigh like stallions,
v12: your mother will be deeply ashamed;
the one who bore you will be disgraced.
Look! She is last among the nations—
a wilderness, a dry and barren land.
v13: Because of the **LORD**'s wrath, no one will live there;
she will be wholly desolate.
Everyone passing Babylon will be appalled
and hiss at all her wounds.
</p>

## Repayment for Babylon

<p>
v14: Take your positions all around Babylon,
all you who draw the bow.
Shoot at her; spare no arrows,
for she has sinned against the **LORD**.
v15: Raise the battle cry against her on every side!
She has given her hand in surrender.
Her foundations have fallen;
her walls are torn down.
This is the **LORD**'s vengeance.
Take vengeance on her;
do to her what she has done.
v16: Cut off the sower from Babylon
and the one who wields the sickle at harvest.
Before the oppressor's sword,
each will turn to their own people;
each will flee to their own land.
v17: Israel is a scattered sheep
driven away by lions.
First the king of Assyria devoured it;
now Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon
has gnawed its bones.”
v18: Therefore this is what the **LORD** of hosts, the God of Israel, says: “I will call the king of Babylon and his land to account, as I called the king of Assyria to account.
v19: I will bring Israel back to its pasture.
It will graze on Carmel and Bashan;
in Ephraim's hills and Gilead it will have its fill.
v20: In those days, at that time,” declares the **LORD**,
“Israel's guilt will be sought, but there will be none;
Judah's sins will not be found.
For I will forgive those I leave as a remnant.
v21: Advance against the land of Merathaim
and the inhabitants of Pekod.
Pursue them with the sword; devote them to destruction,” declares the **LORD**.
“Do everything I have commanded you.
v22: The sound of battle in the land!
Great destruction!
v23: How the hammer of the whole earth
has been cut down and broken!
How Babylon has become
a horror among the nations!
v24: I set a trap for you, Babylon,
and you were caught without knowing it.
You were found and seized,
for you challenged the **LORD**.
v25: The **LORD** has opened his armory
and brought out the weapons of his wrath.
The Lord **GOD** of hosts has work to do
in the land of the Chaldeans.
v26: Come against her from every side.
Open her granaries;
pile her up like heaps of grain.
Devote her to destruction;
leave her no remnant.
v27: Slaughter all her bulls;
let them go down to the killing place.
Woe to them! Their day has come,
the time of their reckoning.
v28: Listen! Fugitives and survivors
from the land of Babylon
announce in Zion the vengeance of the **LORD** our God,
vengeance for his temple.
v29: Summon archers against Babylon,
all who draw the bow.
Camp all around her;
let no one escape.
Repay her for her deeds;
do to her everything she has done.
For she has acted arrogantly against the **LORD**,
against the Holy One of Israel.
v30: Therefore her young men will fall in her squares,
and all her fighting men will be silenced that day,”
declares the **LORD**.
</p>

## Arrogance and the Redeemer

<p>
v31: “I am against you, Arrogance,”
declares the Lord **GOD** of hosts.
“Your day has come,
the time when I call you to account.
v32: Arrogance will stumble and fall,
with no one to raise it.
I will set its towns on fire,
and the fire will consume everything around it.”
v33: This is what the **LORD** of hosts says:
“The people of Israel and Judah are oppressed together.
All their captors hold them fast
and refuse to release them.
v34: Their Redeemer is strong;
the **LORD** of hosts is his name.
He will vigorously plead their case,
to give the earth rest
and make Babylon's inhabitants tremble.
v35: A sword against the Chaldeans,” declares the **LORD**,
“against Babylon's inhabitants,
her officials and her wise men!
v36: A sword against the false diviners—
let them become fools!
A sword against her warriors—
let them be shattered!
v37: A sword against her horses and chariots,
against all the mixed troops within her—
they will become women!
A sword against her treasures—
let them be plundered!
v38: A drought against her waters—
let them dry up!
For this is a land of carved images,
and they go mad over their fearsome idols.
v39: So desert creatures and howling animals will live there,
and ostriches will make their home there.
It will never again be inhabited,
never settled from generation to generation.
v40: As when God overthrew Sodom, Gomorrah, and their neighbors,” declares the **LORD**, “no one will live there; no human being will settle there.
</p>

## A people from the north

<p>
v41: “Look! A people comes from the north,
a great nation and many kings
stirred up from the ends of the earth.
v42: They grasp bow and javelin.
They are cruel and show no mercy.
Their sound roars like the sea;
they ride on horses,
arrayed as one for battle against you,
daughter Babylon.
v43: Babylon's king hears the report,
and his hands go limp.
Distress grips him,
pain like a woman giving birth.
v44: Look!
Like a lion he comes up from the Jordan's thickets
against a lasting pasture.
In a moment I will drive them away from it.
Who is the chosen one I will appoint over it?
Who is like me?
Who can summon me?
What shepherd can stand before me?
v45: So hear the **LORD**'s plan against Babylon,
what he has devised against the land of the Chaldeans:
Even the smallest of the flock will be dragged away;
he will lay the pasture waste because of them.
v46: At the sound of Babylon's capture the earth shakes;
a cry is heard among the nations.”
</p>

## Notes

v2: Bel means “lord,” and Merodach is the Hebrew form of Marduk. The two designations concern Babylon's leading deity.

v11,15: The heifer is pictured threshing; the rare form has also been related to grass. Giving the hand in verse 15 is a gesture of submission. The term rendered “foundations” can also refer to supports or defenses.

v21: Merathaim and Pekod are geographical names that also suggest “double rebellion” and “reckoning.” The oracle uses their sound as part of its judgment language.

v37–38: “Become women” is the oracle's ancient taunt about warriors losing strength; it is retained as the speaker's language. “Sword” and “drought” have the same consonants but different vowels, producing the shift in verse 38.

v44–45: This repeats 49:19–20 against a new nation. The first arrival is third person, followed by divine first-person speech. Difficult pronouns and the flock/pasture syntax remain matters for editorial review.

## Vocabulary

v21: **Devote to destruction**: The Hebrew destruction-ban term marks persons or things for complete destruction in this war oracle.

v34: **Redeemer**: One who acts to recover or protect those belonging to him. Here the Redeemer also argues their legal case.
