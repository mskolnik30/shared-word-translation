## A letter to the exiles

<p>
v01: These are the words of the letter Jeremiah the prophet sent from Jerusalem to the surviving elders of the exiles, the priests, the prophets, and all the people whom Nebuchadnezzar had exiled from Jerusalem to Babylon.
v02: This was after King Jeconiah, the queen mother, the court officers, the officials of Judah and Jerusalem, and the craftworkers and smiths had left Jerusalem.
v03: He sent it through Elasah son of Shaphan and Gemariah son of Hilkiah, whom Zedekiah king of Judah had sent to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon in Babylon. The letter said:
</p>

## Build, plant, and seek the city’s well-being

<p>
v04: “The **LORD** of hosts, the God of Israel, says this to all the exiles whom I sent from Jerusalem to Babylon:
v05: Build houses and live in them. Plant gardens and eat their fruit.
v06: Take wives and have sons and daughters. Find wives for your sons and give your daughters in marriage, so that they too may have sons and daughters. Increase there; do not dwindle.
v07: Seek the well-being of the city where I have exiled you, and pray to the **LORD** on its behalf. Its well-being will be your well-being.
</p>

<p>
v08: For the **LORD** of hosts, the God of Israel, says: Do not let the prophets and diviners among you deceive you. Do not listen to the dreams you encourage them to dream.
v09: They are prophesying lies to you in my name. I did not send them, declares the **LORD**.
</p>

## A future after exile

<p>
v10: “For the **LORD** says: When seventy years are complete for Babylon, I will attend to you and fulfill my good promise to bring you back to this place.
v11: For I know the plans I have for you, declares the **LORD**—plans for well-being, not for harm, to give you a future and hope.
v12: Then you will call on me, come and pray to me, and I will listen to you.
v13: You will seek me and find me when you search for me with your whole heart.
v14: I will let you find me, declares the **LORD**. I will restore your fortunes and gather you from all the nations and all the places where I drove you, declares the **LORD**. I will bring you back to the place from which I exiled you.
</p>

## Warnings at home and in Babylon

<p>
v15: “You have said, ‘The **LORD** has raised up prophets for us in Babylon.’
v16: But this is what the **LORD** says about the king who sits on David's throne and all the people living in this city, your brothers who did not go into exile with you.
v17: The **LORD** of hosts says: I am sending sword, famine, and plague against them. I will make them like rotten figs, so bad they cannot be eaten.
v18: I will pursue them with sword, famine, and plague. I will make them an object of horror to all the kingdoms of the earth—a curse, a desolation, something to hiss at, and a reproach among all the nations where I drive them.
v19: They did not listen to my words, declares the **LORD**, though I sent my servants the prophets to them again and again. And you did not listen either, declares the **LORD**.
</p>

<p>
v20: “But you, all the exiles whom I sent from Jerusalem to Babylon, hear the word of the **LORD**.
v21: The **LORD** of hosts, the God of Israel, says this about Ahab son of Kolaiah and Zedekiah son of Maaseiah, who prophesy lies to you in my name: I will hand them over to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, and he will strike them down before your eyes.
v22: All the exiles of Judah in Babylon will use their fate as a curse: ‘May the **LORD** make you like Zedekiah and Ahab, whom the king of Babylon roasted in the fire.’
v23: For they have done an outrage in Israel: they committed adultery with their neighbors' wives and spoke lies in my name that I did not command them to speak. I am the one who knows, and I am witness, declares the **LORD**.”
</p>

## Shemaiah’s letter and its answer

<p>
v24: Tell Shemaiah the Nehelamite:
v25: “The **LORD** of hosts, the God of Israel, says: You sent letters in your own name to all the people in Jerusalem, to the priest Zephaniah son of Maaseiah, and to all the priests. You wrote:
v26: ‘The **LORD** has made you priest in place of Jehoiada the priest, so that there will be officers in the house of the **LORD** to deal with every madman who acts as a prophet. You must put him in the stocks and the neck restraint.
v27: So why have you not rebuked Jeremiah of Anathoth, who acts as a prophet among you?
v28: He has sent word to us in Babylon: “It will be a long time. Build houses and live in them. Plant gardens and eat their fruit.”’”
</p>

<p>
v29: Zephaniah the priest read this letter aloud to Jeremiah the prophet.
v30: Then the word of the **LORD** came to Jeremiah:
v31: “Send this message to all the exiles: ‘The **LORD** says this about Shemaiah the Nehelamite: Because Shemaiah prophesied to you when I had not sent him, and made you trust a lie,
v32: the **LORD** says: I will call Shemaiah the Nehelamite and his offspring to account. He will have no one living among this people, and he will not see the good I am going to do for my people, declares the **LORD**, because he has preached rebellion against the **LORD**.’”
</p>

## Notes

v02: The word rendered “court officers” can also designate eunuchs. The queen mother is distinguished from the king's wife.

v07,10–11: The promise addresses the exiled community. The seventy years are “for Babylon.” The repeated word shalom links the city's well-being in verse 7 with the promised well-being in verse 11.

v08: The unusual causative form can mean dreams the audience encourages others to dream; it can also be understood as dreams they themselves dream.

v19: The Hebrew moves from “they did not listen” to “you did not listen,” drawing the exiled hearers into the accusation.

v21–23: Ahab son of Kolaiah and Zedekiah son of Maaseiah are prophets in exile, not the kings bearing those names. The source's written/read difference in verse 23 is rendered according to the reading “the one who knows.”

v24–28: Shemaiah's letter accuses Jeremiah of acting as a mad prophet. “Madman” belongs to that hostile speaker's claim. The second restraint in verse 26 is a rare word, probably a neck restraint; its exact construction is uncertain.

## Vocabulary

v07: **Well-being**: Shalom can include peace, safety, and communal welfare, not only the absence of fighting.

v14: **Restore your fortunes**: An expression of reversal and recovery that includes return from exile in this setting.
