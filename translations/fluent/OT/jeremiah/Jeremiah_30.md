## Write the words of restoration

<p>
v01: This is the word that came to Jeremiah from the **LORD**:
v02: “The **LORD**, the God of Israel, says: Write in a scroll all the words I have spoken to you.
v03: For look, days are coming, declares the **LORD**, when I will restore the fortunes of my people Israel and Judah, says the **LORD**. I will bring them back to the land I gave their ancestors, and they will possess it.”
</p>

## Distress and deliverance

<p>
v04: These are the words the **LORD** spoke concerning Israel and Judah:
v05: “The **LORD** says:
We have heard a cry of terror,
fear, and no peace.
v06: Ask and see:
Can a male give birth?
Why, then, do I see every man
with his hands on his waist like a woman in labor?
Why has every face turned pale?
v07: Alas, that day is great;
there is none like it!
It is a time of distress for Jacob,
yet he will be saved out of it.
</p>

<p>
v08: “On that day, declares the **LORD** of hosts, I will break his yoke from your neck and tear off your straps. Foreigners will no longer enslave him.
v09: They will serve the **LORD** their God and David their king, whom I will raise up for them.
</p>

<p>
v10: So do not be afraid, my servant Jacob,
declares the **LORD**;
do not be dismayed, Israel.
I will save you from far away,
and your offspring from the land of their captivity.
Jacob will return and be at rest, at ease,
with no one to make him afraid.
v11: For I am with you to save you, declares the **LORD**.
I will make an end of all the nations
among whom I scattered you,
but I will not make an end of you.
I will discipline you with justice;
I will certainly not leave you unpunished.
</p>

## A wound and its healing

<p>
v12: “For the **LORD** says:
Your fracture is beyond healing;
your wound is grievous.
v13: No one pleads your case.
There is no treatment for your sore,
no healing for you.
v14: All your lovers have forgotten you;
they no longer seek you out.
I struck you as an enemy strikes,
with cruel discipline,
because your guilt is so great
and your sins so many.
v15: Why cry out over your fracture?
Your pain is beyond healing.
Because your guilt is so great
and your sins so many,
I have done these things to you.
</p>

<p>
v16: Yet all who devour you will be devoured;
all your enemies, every one, will go into captivity.
Those who plunder you will be plundered,
and all who prey on you I will give up as prey.
v17: For I will restore your health
and heal your wounds, declares the **LORD**,
because they call you an outcast:
‘She is Zion; no one cares about her.’
</p>

## A rebuilt community

<p>
v18: “The **LORD** says:
I will restore the fortunes of Jacob's tents
and have compassion on his dwellings.
The city will be rebuilt on its own mound,
and the palace will stand in its rightful place.
v19: Thanksgiving will come from them,
and the sound of people celebrating.
I will make them numerous; they will not dwindle.
I will give them honor; they will not be insignificant.
v20: Jacob's children will be as they were before;
his community will be established before me.
I will call all his oppressors to account.
v21: Their noble leader will be one of their own;
their ruler will come from among them.
I will bring him near, and he will approach me.
For who would pledge his own heart
to approach me? declares the **LORD**.
v22: You will be my people,
and I will be your God.”
</p>

## The storm of the LORD

<p>
v23: Look! The storm of the **LORD**!
Wrath has burst out, a sweeping storm;
it whirls down on the heads of the wicked.
v24: The **LORD**'s burning anger will not turn back
until he has done and accomplished
what his heart intends.
In days to come you will understand it.
</p>

## Notes

v08–09: The Hebrew moves between “your neck,” “him,” and “they.” Jacob or Israel remains the people addressed. “David their king” is retained without substituting an interpretive identification.

v12–17: The poem moves from a wound called incurable to promised healing. Both the severe accusation and the reversal are retained.

v21: “Pledge his own heart” is a striking expression of committing or risking oneself in approaching God. The preceding line makes the ruler's approach possible through God's invitation.

v23–24: These lines closely echo 23:19–20. The storm description differs, but the completion of God's purpose and the promise of later understanding recur.

## Vocabulary

v18: **Mound**: The elevated site on which a town stood, including the remains of earlier settlement.

v22: **My people … your God**: A recurring covenant formula expressing the relationship promised to the community.
