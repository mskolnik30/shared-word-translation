## The ruined waistcloth

<p>
v01: This is what the **LORD** said to me: “Go and buy a linen waistcloth. Put it around your waist, but do not put it in water.”
</p>

<p>
v02: So I bought the waistcloth as the **LORD** had said and put it around my waist.
</p>

<p>
v03: The **LORD**’s word came to me a second time:
</p>

<p>
v04: “Take the waistcloth you bought, the one around your waist. Go at once to Perath and hide it there in a crevice in the rock.”
</p>

<p>
v05: I went and hid it at Perath, as the **LORD** had commanded me.
</p>

<p>
v06: Many days later, the **LORD** said to me, “Go at once to Perath and get the waistcloth I told you to hide there.”
</p>

<p>
v07: I went to Perath, dug up the waistcloth, and took it from the place where I had hidden it. It was ruined—good for nothing.
</p>

<p>
v08: Then the **LORD**’s word came to me:
</p>

<p>
v09: “This is what the **LORD** says: In the same way, I will ruin Judah’s pride and Jerusalem’s great pride.
</p>

<p>
v10: This evil people refuses to listen to my words. They follow their stubborn hearts and go after other gods to serve and worship them. They will be like this waistcloth, good for nothing.
</p>

<p>
v11: As a waistcloth clings to a man’s waist, so I made the whole house of Israel and the whole house of Judah cling to me,” declares the **LORD**, “to be my people, my name, my praise, and my glory. But they would not listen.
</p>

## Jars filled with wine

<p>
v12: “Tell them this: This is what the **LORD**, Israel’s God, says: Every jar will be filled with wine. They will answer, ‘Don’t we know that every jar will be filled with wine?’
</p>

<p>
v13: Then tell them: This is what the **LORD** says: Look, I am filling everyone who lives in this land with drunkenness—the kings who sit on David’s throne, the priests, the prophets, and everyone who lives in Jerusalem.
</p>

<p>
v14: I will smash them against one another, fathers and sons together,” declares the **LORD**. “No pity, no mercy, no compassion will keep me from destroying them.”
</p>

## Pride and humiliation

<p>
v15: Listen and pay attention;
do not be proud,
for the **LORD** has spoken.
v16: Give glory to the **LORD** your God
before he brings darkness,
before your feet stumble on the mountains at dusk.
You hope for light,
but he will turn it to deep darkness,
make it thick gloom.
v17: But if you will not listen,
my soul will weep in secret because of your pride.
My eyes will weep bitterly and stream with tears,
for the **LORD**’s flock has been taken captive.
</p>

<p>
v18: Say to the king and the queen mother,
“Take a low seat,
for your glorious crown
has fallen from your heads.”
</p>

<p>
v19: The cities of the Negeb are shut,
with no one to open them.
All Judah has gone into exile,
wholly carried away.
</p>

<p>
v20: Lift your eyes and see those coming from the north!
Where is the flock entrusted to you,
your glorious sheep?
</p>

<p>
v21: What will you say when he sets over you as rulers
those you trained to be your close friends?
Will pain not seize you
like a woman in labor?
</p>

<p>
v22: And if you ask yourself,
‘Why has this happened to me?’—
it is because of your great guilt
that your skirts have been stripped back
and your body violated.
</p>

<p>
v23: Can a Cushite change his skin,
or a leopard its spots?
Then you too could do good,
though you are trained in doing evil.
</p>

<p>
v24: “I will scatter them like straw
blown by a wilderness wind.
</p>

<p>
v25: This is your lot,
the portion I have measured out for you,”
declares the **LORD**,
“because you forgot me
and trusted in falsehood.
</p>

<p>
v26: I myself will strip your skirts up over your face,
and your shame will be seen.
</p>

<p>
v27: Your adulteries, your lustful neighing,
your shameless prostitution—
on the hills, in the open country,
I have seen your abominations.
Woe to you, Jerusalem!
How much longer before you are clean?”
</p>

## Notes

v4–7: Perath is the Hebrew name commonly used for the Euphrates. A local site near Jeremiah’s home has also been proposed for this sign-act. The place-name is retained without settling the question.

v20–22: Verse 20’s written form addresses a feminine singular figure; the recorded reading begins with plural commands. Verse 21’s appointment/friendship clause is difficult. Verse 22 literally says the “heels” are violated; with the exposed skirts, it evokes bodily and sexual humiliation.

v23: Cush lay south of Egypt. The comparison concerns the inability to change an established condition; it does not call the Cushite’s skin evil.

v26–27: Jerusalem is addressed as a woman. The accusation and violent exposure belong to the passage’s own imagery and are retained as such.

## Vocabulary

v1: **Waistcloth**: A linen garment worn around the waist, used here in a symbolic action.

v18: **Queen mother**: The king’s mother, who could hold a recognized position at court.

v19: **Negeb**: The southern region of Judah.
