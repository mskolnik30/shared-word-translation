## A covenant proposed

<p>
v01: While Ezra prayed and confessed, weeping and throwing himself down before God’s house, a very large assembly of Israelite men, women, and children gathered around him. The people wept bitterly.
v02: Shecaniah son of Jehiel, from the family of Elam, said to Ezra, “We have been unfaithful to our God by marrying foreign women from the peoples of the land. Yet even now there is hope for Israel in this matter.
</p>

<p>
v03: Let us now make a covenant with our God to send away all these wives and the children born to them, following the counsel of my lord and of those who tremble at our God’s command. Let it be done according to the instruction.
v04: Get up! This matter is your responsibility, and we are with you. Be strong and act.”
</p>

<p>
v05: Ezra got up and made the leading priests, the Levites, and all Israel swear to do as proposed. They swore the oath.
</p>

## The assembly summoned

<p>
v06: Then Ezra left the front of God’s house and went to the room of Jehohanan son of Eliashib. There he ate no bread and drank no water, mourning the returned exiles’ unfaithfulness.
v07: A proclamation went out through Judah and Jerusalem, calling all the returned exiles to gather in Jerusalem.
v08: Anyone who failed to come within three days, as the officials and elders had decided, would forfeit all his property and be excluded from the assembly of the returned exiles.
</p>

<p>
v09: Within three days all the men of Judah and Benjamin gathered in Jerusalem. It was the twentieth day of the ninth month. The people sat in the open square before God’s house, trembling because of the matter and the heavy rain.
</p>

## A decision and an investigation

<p>
v10: Ezra the priest stood up and told them, “You have been unfaithful. By marrying foreign women, you have added to Israel’s guilt.
v11: Now give confession to the LORD, the God of your ancestors, and do his will. Separate yourselves from the peoples of the land and from the foreign wives.”
</p>

<p>
v12: The whole assembly answered loudly, “Yes! We must do as you say.
v13: But there are many people, and it is the rainy season. We cannot stand outside. This is not work for one day or two, for we have transgressed greatly in this matter.
v14: Let our officials represent the whole assembly. Let everyone in our towns who has married a foreign woman come at an appointed time, with the elders and judges of each town, until our God’s fierce anger over this matter has turned away from us.”
</p>

<p>
v15: Only Jonathan son of Asahel and Jahzeiah son of Tikvah opposed this, supported by Meshullam and Shabbethai the Levite.
</p>

<p>
v16: The returned exiles did so. Ezra the priest and men who were family heads were set apart by ancestral house, all designated by name. They began sitting to investigate the matter on the first day of the tenth month.
v17: By the first day of the first month, they had finished dealing with all the men who had married foreign women.
</p>

## Notes

v03: “My lord” is taken as an address to Ezra. The vocalized Hebrew can be read “the Lord.” The proposal explicitly includes sending away children as well as wives; their later circumstances are not described.

v08: Attendance was enforced by threatened loss of property and exclusion from the community.

v15: The phrase rendered “opposed this” can also mean “stood over this,” describing responsibility for the matter. The nature of the four men’s involvement is uncertain.

v25: The name Malchijah occurs twice in this entry; both occurrences are retained.

v34–42: The Hebrew continues a long list after a second Bani heading. No additional family heading is inserted at verse 38.

v44: The final Hebrew clause is difficult. The draft understands it to say that some of the women had borne children. Other renderings describe sending away wives with their children. This verse alone does not settle how the final action is to be expressed.

## Vocabulary

v03: Covenant
A binding commitment made here with God.

v11: Confession
An acknowledgment before God; the Hebrew term can also express thanksgiving or praise.

v19: Guilt offering
A sacrifice connected with acknowledged liability or an offense.
