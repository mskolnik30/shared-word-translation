## A report of unfaithfulness

<p>
v01: After these things were done, the officials approached me and said, “The people of Israel, including the priests and Levites, have not kept apart from the peoples of the lands and their detestable practices—the Canaanites, Hittites, Perizzites, Jebusites, Ammonites, Moabites, Egyptians, and Amorites.
v02: They have taken some of those peoples’ daughters as wives for themselves and their sons, mixing the holy seed with the peoples of the lands. The officials and leaders have taken the lead in this unfaithfulness.”
</p>

<p>
v03: When I heard this, I tore my garment and robe, pulled hair from my head and beard, and sat appalled.
v04: Everyone who trembled at the words of the God of Israel gathered around me because of the returned exiles’ unfaithfulness. I sat there appalled until the evening offering.
</p>

## Ezra’s confession

<p>
v05: At the evening offering I rose from my self-humbling, with my garment and robe torn. I fell to my knees, spread out my hands to the LORD my God,
v06: and said, “My God, I am ashamed and humiliated to lift my face to you, my God. Our wrongs have risen above our heads, and our guilt has reached the heavens.
</p>

<p>
v07: From our ancestors’ days until now, our guilt has been great. Because of our wrongs, we, our kings, and our priests have been handed over to the kings of the lands—to sword, captivity, plunder, and shame, as we are today.
</p>

<p>
v08: But now, for a brief moment, the LORD our God has shown us favor, leaving us survivors and giving us a tent peg in his holy place. Our God has brightened our eyes and given us a little life in our slavery.
v09: We are slaves, yet our God has not abandoned us in our slavery. He has extended faithful love to us before the kings of Persia, giving us life to raise up our God’s house and restore its ruins, and giving us a protective wall in Judah and Jerusalem.
</p>

<p>
v10: “Now, our God, what can we say after this? We have abandoned your commands,
v11: which you gave through your servants the prophets: ‘The land you are entering to possess is a land polluted by the peoples of the lands and their detestable practices. They have filled it from end to end with their impurity.
v12: Do not give your daughters to their sons or take their daughters for your sons. Never seek their peace or prosperity, so that you may be strong, eat the good of the land, and leave it as an inheritance to your sons forever.’
</p>

<p>
v13: “After everything that has come upon us because of our evil deeds and great guilt—you, our God, have punished us less than our wrongs deserve and have left us survivors like these—
v14: will we again break your commands and intermarry with peoples who practice these detestable things? Would you not be angry enough to finish us off, leaving neither remnant nor survivor?
v15: LORD, God of Israel, you are righteous, for we remain as survivors today. Here we are before you in our guilt, though because of it none of us can stand before you.”
</p>

## Notes

v02: “Holy seed” describes the community as offspring set apart for God. The officials use the phrase in their accusation about marriages. The text’s wording is retained without turning it into a modern racial classification.

v08: A tent peg suggests a small but secure hold in God’s holy place. The brief respite does not end the community’s political subjection.

v09: The protective wall is an image of security here; the verse does not establish that Jerusalem’s physical walls had already been rebuilt.

v11–12: The prayer brings together themes of commands about the land and marriage. It does not quote one identifiable passage word for word.

## Vocabulary

v02: Unfaithfulness
A breach of an obligation or sacred trust.

v08–09: Slavery
Ezra describes the community’s continuing subjection even after return from exile.

v14: Remnant
Those left after disaster or judgment.
