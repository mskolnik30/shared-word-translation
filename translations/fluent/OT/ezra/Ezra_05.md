## The work begins again

<p>
v01: The prophets Haggai and Zechariah son of Iddo prophesied to the Jews in Judah and Jerusalem in the name of the God of Israel, who was over them.
v02: Then Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and Jeshua son of Jozadak began rebuilding God’s house in Jerusalem. God’s prophets were there with them, supporting them.
</p>

<p>
v03: At that time Tattenai, governor of Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai, and their associates came to them and asked, “Who authorized you to build this house and complete this structure?”
v04: Then we asked them, “What are the names of the men constructing this building?”
v05: But their God’s eye was on the Jewish elders. They were not stopped while a report went to Darius and a written reply about the matter was awaited.
</p>

## A report to Darius

<p>
v06: This is a copy of the letter that Tattenai, governor of Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai, and his associates, the officials in Beyond the River, sent to King Darius.
v07: They sent him a report that read:
“To King Darius: Complete peace.
</p>

<p>
v08: “Let the king know that we went to the province of Judah, to the house of the great God. It is being rebuilt with large stones, and timber is being set into the walls. The work is being done diligently and is progressing well under their hands.
v09: We asked those elders, ‘Who authorized you to build this house and complete this structure?’
v10: We also asked their names, so we could inform you by writing down the names of the men leading them.
</p>

<p>
v11: “This was their answer to us: ‘We are servants of the God of heaven and earth. We are rebuilding the house that was built many years ago, which a great king of Israel built and completed.
v12: But because our ancestors angered the God of heaven, he handed them over to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, the Chaldean. He demolished this house and exiled the people to Babylon.
</p>

<p>
v13: In the first year of Cyrus king of Babylon, however, King Cyrus issued an order to rebuild this house of God.
v14: King Cyrus also brought out the gold and silver vessels of God’s house that Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple in Jerusalem to the temple in Babylon. He gave them to a man named Sheshbazzar, whom he appointed governor.
v15: He told him, “Take these vessels. Go and place them in the temple in Jerusalem, and let God’s house be rebuilt on its site.”
v16: So this Sheshbazzar came and laid the foundations of God’s house in Jerusalem. From then until now it has been under construction, but it is not finished.’
</p>

<p>
v17: “Now, if it pleases the king, let a search be made in the royal archives there in Babylon to find whether King Cyrus issued an order to rebuild this house of God in Jerusalem. Then let the king send us his decision about this.”
</p>

## Notes

v03: The architectural term rendered “structure” is uncertain; it has also been understood as wall or woodwork.

v04: The Aramaic says “we,” creating an abrupt change of voice. It is retained here. Some readings take the sentence as a report of the builders’ answer about their names; others understand the officials’ question, as in 5:9–10.

v06: The term rendered “officials” may instead name a people or group.

v14: “Temple” in Babylon can also be understood as “palace.”

v16: Sheshbazzar’s role is reported here without equating him with Zerubbabel. The elders’ summary is retained alongside the earlier account of interrupted work.

## Vocabulary

v05: God’s eye
An image of attentive care and protection.

v11: God of heaven and earth
The elders’ title for the God they serve; the letter preserves their own answer.
