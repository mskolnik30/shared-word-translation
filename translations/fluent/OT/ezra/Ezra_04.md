## Opposition to the rebuilding

<p>
v01: The enemies of Judah and Benjamin heard that the returned exiles were building a temple for the LORD, the God of Israel.
v02: They approached Zerubbabel and the family heads and said, “Let us build with you. We seek your God just as you do, and we have been sacrificing to him since Esarhaddon king of Assyria brought us here.”
</p>

<p>
v03: But Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the other family heads of Israel answered, “You have no part with us in building a house for our God. We ourselves will build for the LORD, the God of Israel, as Cyrus king of Persia commanded us.”
</p>

<p>
v04: Then the people of the land discouraged the people of Judah and frightened them away from building.
v05: They hired advisers against them to frustrate their plans throughout the reign of Cyrus king of Persia and until the reign of Darius king of Persia.
</p>

## Accusations in later reigns

<p>
v06: At the beginning of Ahasuerus’s reign, they wrote an accusation against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem.
v07: In the days of Artaxerxes, Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel, and their other associates wrote to Artaxerxes king of Persia. The letter was written in Aramaic script and expressed in Aramaic.
</p>

<p>
v08: Rehum the commanding official and Shimshai the scribe wrote the following letter about Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes.
v09: It came from Rehum the commanding official, Shimshai the scribe, and their other associates: the Dinaites, Apharsathchites, Tarpelites, Persians, people of Erech, Babylonians, people of Susa, Dahae, Elamites,
v10: and the other peoples whom the great and honored Osnappar had deported and settled in the city of Samaria and elsewhere in Beyond the River. And now:
</p>

<p>
v11: This is a copy of the letter they sent him:
“To King Artaxerxes, from your servants, the men of Beyond the River. And now:
v12: “Let the king know that the Jews who came up from you to us have reached Jerusalem. They are rebuilding that rebellious, wicked city, completing its walls and repairing its foundations.
</p>

<p>
v13: Let the king also know that if this city is rebuilt and its walls completed, they will stop paying tribute, tax, and toll, and the royal revenue will suffer.
v14: We eat the palace’s salt, and it would not be right for us to watch the king be dishonored. That is why we are sending this report to the king.
v15: Have a search made in the records of your ancestors. There you will find that this city has been rebellious, harmful to kings and provinces, and a place of revolt from long ago. That is why the city was destroyed.
v16: We inform the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls completed, you will have no territory left in Beyond the River.”
</p>

## The king orders a halt

<p>
v17: The king sent this answer:
“To Rehum the commanding official, Shimshai the scribe, and their other associates living in Samaria and elsewhere in Beyond the River: Peace. And now:
v18: “The letter you sent us has been read and explained in my presence.
</p>

<p>
v19: I ordered a search, and it was found that from long ago this city has risen against kings, and rebellion and revolt have taken place in it.
v20: Powerful kings have ruled Jerusalem and governed all Beyond the River. Tribute, tax, and toll were paid to them.
</p>

<p>
v21: Now issue an order to stop these men. This city must not be rebuilt until I give the order.
v22: Take care not to neglect this matter. Why should the damage grow and harm the royal interests?”
</p>

<p>
v23: As soon as King Artaxerxes’s letter was read before Rehum, Shimshai the scribe, and their associates, they hurried to the Jews in Jerusalem and forcibly stopped them.
</p>

## The temple work remains stopped

<p>
v24: Work on God’s house in Jerusalem had come to a stop. It remained stopped until the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.
</p>

## Notes

v02: “To him” follows the recorded Hebrew reading tradition. The written form is “not.”

v06–24: Verses 6–23 collect later examples of opposition to Jerusalem under Ahasuerus and Artaxerxes. Verse 24 returns to the temple work under Darius. The letters about the city’s walls should not be mistaken for a continuous chronology of the temple project.

v07–08: The biblical text switches from Hebrew to Aramaic at 4:8 and continues in Aramaic through 6:18.

v09: Several group names are uncertain and may represent peoples or administrative groups. “Dahae” follows the recorded read form; the written form can be understood as “who are.”

v13: The expression rendered “royal revenue” is uncertain; the sentence warns of financial harm to the king.

v14: Eating the palace’s salt expresses dependence on, and an obligation of loyalty to, the king.

## Vocabulary

v10: Beyond the River
The Persian province west of the Euphrates, viewed from the empire’s eastern center.

v13: Tribute, tax, and toll
Three kinds of royal payment; the exact distinctions between these terms are uncertain.
