## Woe to the city of blood

<p>
v01: Woe to the city of blood!
It is full of lies and plunder;
the prey never runs out.
v02: Whips crack, wheels rumble,
horses gallop, chariots bounce.
v03: Horsemen charge, swords flash,
spears glitter!
So many slain, heaps of corpses,
bodies without end—
they stumble over the bodies.
</p>

<p>
v04: All because of the countless prostitutions
of the alluring prostitute,
the mistress of sorcery,
who sells nations through her prostitution
and peoples through her sorcery.
v05: “Look, I am against you,” declares the LORD of Armies.
“I will lift your skirts over your face
and show the nations your nakedness,
the kingdoms your disgrace.
v06: I will throw filth on you,
treat you with contempt,
and make you a spectacle.
v07: Everyone who sees you will run away and say,
‘Nineveh is ruined! Who will mourn for her?’
Where can I find anyone to comfort you?”
</p>

## No better than No-amon

<p>
v08: Are you better than No-amon,
seated among the Nile’s channels,
with water all around her,
the sea her defense,
water her wall?
v09: Cush and Egypt were her boundless strength;
Put and the Libyans were your helpers.
v10: Yet she too was carried away into captivity.
Her little children were dashed to pieces
at every street corner.
Lots were cast for her honored men,
and all her great men were bound in chains.
</p>

<p>
v11: You too will become drunk;
you will go into hiding.
You too will seek shelter from the enemy.
v12: All your fortresses are fig trees
with early-ripe fruit.
Shake them, and the figs fall
straight into the eater’s mouth.
v13: Look at your people: women in your midst!
Your land’s gates stand wide open to your enemies;
fire has eaten through their bars.
</p>

## No healing for the wound

<p>
v14: Draw water for the siege!
Strengthen your fortresses!
Step into the mud, tread the clay,
take hold of the brick mold!
v15: There the fire will consume you;
the sword will cut you down,
devouring you like a locust.
Multiply like the locust!
Multiply like the swarm!
v16: You made your merchants more numerous
than the stars of heaven.
The locust strips the land and flies away.
</p>

<p>
v17: Your officials are like locusts,
your marshals like swarms
settling on walls on a cold day.
When the sun rises, they flee;
no one knows where they have gone.
v18: Your shepherds sleep, king of Assyria;
your nobles lie at rest.
Your people are scattered over the mountains,
and no one gathers them.
</p>

<p>
v19: There is no relief for your fracture;
your wound is fatal.
Everyone who hears the news about you claps their hands.
For who has not suffered your endless cruelty?
</p>

## Notes

v05–06: The exposure imagery depicts public sexual humiliation in the judgment on Nineveh; its violence is left visible.

v08–10: No-amon is the city named in the Hebrew. The comparison recalls its defeat despite natural defenses and allies; the killing and enslavement of its people are explicit.

v13: The comparison of warriors to women is an ancient military taunt, not a claim about women’s worth.

v16: The locust verb can describe stripping a place or shedding its skin before flying away; the image stresses disappearance after devastation.

## Vocabulary

v08: **No-amon**: A name for Thebes in Egypt.

v17: **Locusts**: The swarming insects become an image of officials who vanish when conditions change.
