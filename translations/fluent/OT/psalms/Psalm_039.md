## The measure of my days

<p>
v01: For the music leader. For Jeduthun. A psalm of David.
I said, “I will guard my ways so I do not sin with my tongue.
I will muzzle my mouth while the wicked are before me.”
v02: I was silent, saying nothing, holding back even good words;
but my pain grew worse.
v03: My heart grew hot within me;
as I brooded, fire burned.
Then my tongue spoke:
</p>

<p>
v04: “LORD, let me know my end and the measure of my days.
Let me understand how fleeting I am.
v05: You have made my days a few handbreadths;
my lifetime is nothing before you.
Surely every person, even standing firm, is only a breath. Selah.
v06: People walk about as mere shadows;
their bustle is only a breath.
They heap things up without knowing who will gather them.
</p>

<p>
v07: And now, Lord, what do I wait for?
My hope is in you.
v08: Rescue me from all my rebellions;
do not make me a fool’s laughingstock.
v09: I am silent; I do not open my mouth,
for you are the one who has done this.
</p>

<p>
v10: Remove your blow from me;
I am spent by the assault of your hand.
v11: When you discipline someone with rebukes for wrongdoing,
you consume what they cherish like a moth.
Surely every person is only a breath. Selah.
</p>

<p>
v12: Hear my prayer, LORD; listen to my cry for help.
Do not be silent at my tears.
I am a resident alien with you, a temporary settler like all my ancestors.
v13: Look away from me, so I may brighten again
before I go and am no more.”
</p>

## Notes

v01–02: Jeduthun is named in the musical direction. The silence “even from good” may mean withholding good words, or being silent to no good effect.

v12–13: The speaker is a guest even with God, like earlier generations. The final request is for God to look away, leaving the lament’s tension unresolved.

## Vocabulary

v05: **Handbreadth**: A short measure based on the width of a hand, used here to picture a brief life.

v05–06: **Breath — hevel**: Something fleeting and insubstantial. It describes human duration and restless accumulation.
