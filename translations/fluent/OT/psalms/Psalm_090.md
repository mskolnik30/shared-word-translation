## Teach us to count our days

<p>
v01: A prayer of Moses, the man of God.
Lord, you have been our dwelling place
from generation to generation.
v02: Before the mountains were born,
before you brought forth the earth and the world,
from eternity to eternity, you are God.
</p>

<p>
v03: You turn human beings back to dust
and say, “Return, children of humanity.”
v04: A thousand years in your sight
are like yesterday once it has passed,
like a watch in the night.
v05: You sweep them away; they are like sleep,
like grass that springs up in the morning.
v06: In the morning it blooms and grows;
by evening it withers and dries up.
</p>

<p>
v07: Your anger consumes us;
your fury leaves us terrified.
v08: You have set our wrongs before you,
our hidden sins in the light of your face.
v09: All our days pass under your wrath;
we finish our years like a sigh.
v10: Our years amount to seventy,
or eighty if we have the strength.
Yet all they boast of is toil and sorrow;
they pass quickly, and we fly away.
</p>

<p>
v11: Who understands the strength of your anger,
your fury in keeping with the fear you deserve?
v12: Teach us to count our days rightly,
so we may gain a heart of wisdom.
</p>

<p>
v13: Return, LORD! How long?
Have compassion on your servants.
v14: Satisfy us in the morning with your faithful love,
so we may sing for joy and be glad all our days.
v15: Give us as many days of joy as days you afflicted us,
as many years as we have known trouble.
</p>

<p>
v16: Let your servants see your work,
and their children your splendor.
v17: May the favor of the Lord our God rest on us.
Establish the work of our hands for us—
yes, establish the work of our hands.
</p>

## Notes

v03,05: The word rendered dust suggests crushed material. Flooding away, sleep, and grass overlap as pictures of brief human life.

v10–12: Seventy and eighty are the poem's lifespan figures, not a promise that each person will reach either age. Counting days means reckoning with their limits.

## Vocabulary

v01: **Dwelling place**: The image presents God as a lasting home across generations.
