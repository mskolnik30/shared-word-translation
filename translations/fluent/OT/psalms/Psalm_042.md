## Thirsting for the living God

<p>
v01: For the music leader. A maskil of the sons of Korah.
As a deer longs for streams of water,
so I long for you, God.
v02: I thirst for God, for the living God.
When may I come and appear before God’s face?
v03: My tears have been my food day and night,
while people keep asking me, “Where is your God?”
</p>

<p>
v04: I remember these things and pour out my life within me:
how I went with the crowd, leading them to God’s house,
with joyful cries and thanksgiving, a multitude keeping festival.
</p>

<p>
v05: Why are you bowed down, my soul? Why do you roar within me?
Hope in God, for I will yet thank him
for the saving help of his presence.
</p>

<p>
v06: My God, my soul is bowed down within me.
So I remember you from the land of the Jordan,
from the heights of Hermon, from Mount Mizar.
v07: Deep calls to deep at the roar of your waterfalls;
all your breakers and waves have swept over me.
v08: By day the LORD commands his faithful love;
by night his song is with me, a prayer to the God of my life.
</p>

<p>
v09: I say to God, my rock, “Why have you forgotten me?
Why must I go about mourning under the enemy’s oppression?”
v10: Like a deadly blow to my bones, my foes taunt me,
asking all day long, “Where is your God?”
</p>

<p>
v11: Why are you bowed down, my soul? Why do you roar within me?
Hope in God, for I will yet thank him,
my saving help, my God.
</p>

## Notes

v01–05: The sons of Korah are named in the title; maskil is a song designation whose exact meaning is uncertain. The repeated question and refrain continue in Psalm 43, which has no separate Hebrew title.

v02,05–06,11: “Appear before God’s face” follows the Hebrew vocalization; the consonants can also be read as seeing God’s face. The first refrain has “his face/presence,” followed by “my God” in verse 6. The later refrain has “the saving help of my face,” rendered “my saving help.” Its wording is not identical to the first.

v06,08: Mizar is not securely located. Verse 8 follows the traditional reading “his song” rather than the written feminine form.

## Vocabulary

v05: **Soul — nephesh**: The self being addressed. The speaker can speak both to God and to their own troubled life.
