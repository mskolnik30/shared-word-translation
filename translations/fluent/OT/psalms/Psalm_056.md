## Tears kept in God’s record

<p>
v01: For the music leader. According to “The Silent Dove Far Away.” Of David. A miktam, when the Philistines seized him in Gath.
Be gracious to me, God, for people trample me;
all day an attacker presses me.
v02: Those watching for my fall trample me all day;
many fight against me arrogantly.
</p>

<p>
v03: On the day I am afraid,
I put my trust in you.
v04: In God, whose word I praise—
in God I trust; I will not be afraid.
What can flesh do to me?
</p>

<p>
v05: All day they twist my words;
all their thoughts about me aim at harm.
v06: They gather and hide,
watching my footsteps,
hoping to take my life.
v07: Will they escape despite their wrongdoing?
God, bring the peoples down in anger!
</p>

<p>
v08: You have counted my wanderings.
Put my tears in your skin-bottle—
are they not in your record?
v09: Then my enemies will turn back on the day I call.
This I know: God is for me.
</p>

<p>
v10: In God I praise the word;
in the LORD I praise the word.
v11: In God I trust; I will not be afraid.
What can a human do to me?
</p>

<p>
v12: God, my vows to you are binding on me;
I will fulfill them with thank offerings.
v13: You have rescued my life from death,
and my feet from stumbling,
so I may walk before God in the light of the living.
</p>

## Notes

v01–02,07: The title’s dove phrase is uncertain and may name a tune; miktam is also an unexplained designation. “Arrogantly” renders a word meaning height that could instead address God as exalted. The escape question in verse 7 preserves a difficult clause without adding a negative to the Hebrew.

v08: The skin-bottle and written record picture tears and wanderings being kept rather than lost. The Hebrew sound-play between wandering and bottle cannot be reproduced fully in English.

## Vocabulary

v04,11: **Flesh and human**: The two refrains use different words for vulnerable humanity; both ask what human opponents can ultimately do.
