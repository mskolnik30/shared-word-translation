## A prayer from the sickbed

<p>
v01: For the music leader. A psalm of David.
Happy is the one who attends to the weak;
the LORD will rescue that person on a day of trouble.
v02: The LORD will guard them and keep them alive;
they will be called happy in the land.
Do not hand them over to their enemies’ desires.
v03: The LORD will sustain them on their sickbed;
you will turn their whole bed in their illness.
</p>

<p>
v04: I said, “LORD, be gracious to me;
heal me, for I have sinned against you.”
</p>

<p>
v05: My enemies say maliciously,
“When will he die and his name disappear?”
v06: A visitor comes and speaks empty words,
gathering harmful gossip in his heart;
then he goes outside and spreads it.
v07: All who hate me whisper together against me;
they imagine the worst for me:
v08: “A deadly thing has fastened on him;
now that he lies down, he will never rise again.”
</p>

<p>
v09: Even my close friend, whom I trusted,
who ate my bread, has raised his heel against me.
</p>

<p>
v10: But you, LORD, be gracious to me and raise me up,
so I can repay them.
v11: By this I know you delight in me:
my enemy does not shout in triumph over me.
v12: You uphold me in my integrity
and set me in your presence forever.
</p>

<p>
v13: Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel,
from everlasting to everlasting.
Amen and amen.
</p>

## Notes

v03,08: Turning the sick person’s whole bed may picture tending the bed or restoring the sufferer. The enemies’ “deadly thing” does not identify a particular disease.

v09–13: Raising a heel pictures a trusted friend’s betrayal. The prayer openly asks to repay enemies. The final blessing closes the first major collection of psalms.

## Vocabulary

v01: **Weak — dal**: Someone vulnerable, poor, or lacking strength; the word is broader than physical illness alone.
