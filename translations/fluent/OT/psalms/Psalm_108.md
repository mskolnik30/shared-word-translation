## Steadfast praise and a plea for victory

<p>
v01: A song. A psalm of David.
My heart is steadfast, God.
I will sing and make music with my glory.
v02: Wake up, harp and lyre!
I will wake the dawn.
v03: I will thank you among the peoples, LORD;
I will make music for you among the nations.
v04: For your faithful love is great beyond the heavens;
your faithfulness reaches the clouds.
v05: Be exalted above the heavens, God;
let your glory be over all the earth.
</p>

<p>
v06: So that those you love may be rescued,
save with your right hand and answer me.
</p>

<p>
v07: God has spoken in his holiness:
“I will rejoice; I will divide Shechem
and measure out the valley of Succoth.
v08: Gilead is mine, and Manasseh is mine.
Ephraim is the defense of my head;
Judah is my ruler’s staff.
v09: Moab is my washbasin;
over Edom I throw my sandal;
over Philistia I shout in triumph.”
</p>

<p>
v10: Who will bring me into the fortified city?
Who will guide me to Edom?
v11: Have you not rejected us, God?
You no longer go out with our armies, God.
v12: Give us help against the foe;
human deliverance is worthless.
v13: With God we will act valiantly;
he himself will trample our foes.
</p>

## Notes

v01–13: This psalm reuses material also found in Psalms 57 and 60, with differences of wording. Its separate title is included in verse 1; Hebrew body labels are one higher.

v01,07–09: “My glory” can refer to the speaker’s whole honorable self or poetic voice. “In his holiness” can also be understood as “in his sanctuary.” The territorial images proclaim rule and subjugation.

## Vocabulary

v08: **Ruler’s staff**: A symbol of governing authority associated here with Judah.

v09: **Thrown sandal**: An ancient gesture of possession or contempt; the exact force is debated.
