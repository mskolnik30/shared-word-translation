## Waters at my throat

<p>
v01: For the music leader. According to “Lilies.” Of David.
Save me, God! The waters have reached my throat.
v02: I sink into deep mud with nowhere to stand.
I have entered deep waters; the current sweeps me away.
v03: I am worn out from calling; my throat is parched.
My eyes fail as I wait for my God.
v04: Those who hate me without cause outnumber the hairs on my head.
My treacherous enemies, bent on destroying me, are powerful.
Must I restore what I never stole?
</p>

<p>
v05: God, you know my folly;
my guilty deeds are not hidden from you.
v06: Do not let those who wait for you be ashamed because of me,
Lord, LORD of Armies.
Do not let those who seek you be disgraced because of me,
God of Israel.
v07: For your sake I bear reproach;
disgrace covers my face.
v08: I have become a stranger to my brothers,
an outsider to my mother’s sons.
</p>

<p>
v09: Zeal for your house has consumed me;
the insults of those who insult you have fallen on me.
v10: When I wept and fasted,
it became another reproach against me.
v11: I put on sackcloth,
and I became their byword.
v12: Those sitting at the gate talk about me;
I am the song of those drinking strong drink.
</p>

<p>
v13: But my prayer is to you, LORD, at a time of favor.
God, in your abundant faithful love,
answer me with your dependable salvation.
v14: Pull me from the mud; do not let me sink.
Let me escape those who hate me and the deep waters.
v15: Do not let the current sweep me away,
the deep swallow me,
or the pit close its mouth over me.
v16: Answer me, LORD, for your faithful love is good;
in your great compassion, turn toward me.
v17: Do not hide your face from your servant.
I am in distress; hurry and answer!
v18: Come near and redeem my life;
because of my enemies, ransom me.
</p>

<p>
v19: You know my reproach, my shame, my disgrace;
all my adversaries are before you.
v20: Reproach has broken my heart, and I am desperately ill.
I looked for sympathy—there was none;
for comforters—I found no one.
v21: They put poison in my food
and gave me vinegar when I was thirsty.
</p>

<p>
v22: Let their own table become a trap before them,
a snare when they are at ease.
v23: Let their eyes grow dark so they cannot see;
make their loins tremble continually.
v24: Pour your indignation on them;
let your burning anger catch them.
v25: Let their encampment lie desolate;
let no one live in their tents.
v26: They pursue the one you struck
and recount the pain of those you wounded.
v27: Add wrongdoing to their wrongdoing;
do not let them enter your righteousness.
v28: Let them be erased from the book of the living,
not enrolled with the righteous.
</p>

<p>
v29: But I am afflicted and in pain;
God, let your salvation set me high.
v30: I will praise God’s name in song
and magnify him with thanksgiving.
v31: This will please the LORD more than an ox,
more than a bull with horns and hooves.
v32: The humble will see and rejoice.
You who seek God, let your hearts revive!
v33: The LORD hears the needy
and does not despise his prisoners.
</p>

<p>
v34: Let heaven and earth praise him,
the seas and everything moving in them.
v35: God will save Zion and rebuild Judah’s towns;
they will live there and possess it.
v36: His servants’ offspring will inherit it;
those who love his name will dwell there.
</p>

## Notes

v01–04: Floodwater, failing throat, unjust hatred, and repayment for an unstolen loss express the sufferer's danger. Verse 5 also acknowledges personal wrongdoing; the claims are not flattened into one another.

v21–22: The bitter substance called rosh is not securely identified. The second line of verse 22 can refer to people at ease, recompense, or companions; the draft follows the first reading.

v25–28: The prayer asks for severe judgment on multiple enemies. The “book of the living” evokes a register of life; the verse does not specify a developed account of the afterlife.

## Vocabulary

v09: **Zeal**: Intense commitment to God's house, described here as consuming the speaker.

v27: **Righteousness**: Here it can include a favorable or vindicating standing before God, not only personal virtue.
