## Wealth cannot ransom a life

<p>
v01: For the music leader. Of the sons of Korah. A psalm.
Hear this, all peoples;
listen, everyone who lives in the world,
v02: people of low and high standing,
rich and needy together.
v03: My mouth will speak wisdom;
my heart’s meditation will bring understanding.
v04: I will bend my ear to a proverb;
with a lyre I will unfold my riddle.
</p>

<p>
v05: Why should I fear in evil days,
when the wrongdoing of those at my heels surrounds me—
v06: those who trust in their resources
and boast of their great wealth?
v07: No one can ransom a brother
or pay God the price for his life—
v08: the ransom for a life is too costly;
one must give up the attempt forever—
v09: so that he might live on forever
and never see the pit.
</p>

<p>
v10: Anyone can see that the wise die;
fools and the senseless perish together
and leave their wealth to others.
v11: Their inward thought is that their houses will last forever,
their dwellings through every generation;
they name lands after themselves.
v12: Human beings in splendor do not endure;
they are like animals that perish.
</p>

<p>
v13: This is the way of those with foolish confidence,
and of those after them who approve their words. Selah.
v14: Like sheep they are destined for Sheol; Death will shepherd them.
The upright will rule over them at morning.
Their form will waste away in Sheol, far from their dwelling.
v15: But God will ransom my life from Sheol’s hand,
for he will take me. Selah.
</p>

<p>
v16: Do not fear when someone grows rich,
when the splendor of their house increases.
v17: At death they take none of it;
their splendor does not descend after them.
v18: Though they congratulate themselves while alive—
and people praise you when you do well for yourself—
v19: they will go to the generation of their ancestors,
who will never see light again.
v20: Human beings in splendor, without understanding,
are like animals that perish.
</p>

## Notes

v07–11: The argument concerns wealth’s inability to buy a brother’s life indefinitely. Verse 11 follows “their inward thought”; the alternative “their graves” changes the picture to the tomb as a lasting house.

v12,14–15,20: The first refrain says people in splendor do not endure; the second says they lack understanding. Death as shepherd and God taking the speaker leave the manner and timing of rescue unstated. The last clause of verse 14 is especially difficult.

## Vocabulary

v07–08: **Ransom — padah**: Release at a cost. The poem contrasts the unaffordable human ransom with God’s rescue in verse 15.
