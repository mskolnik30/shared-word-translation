## Shelter and trust amid danger

<p>
v01: Whoever dwells in the shelter of the Most High
will spend the night in Shaddai's shadow.
v02: I say to the LORD, “My refuge and my fortress,
my God, in whom I trust.”
</p>

<p>
v03: He will rescue you from the bird-catcher's snare
and from deadly plague.
v04: He will cover you with his feathers;
you will find refuge beneath his wings.
His faithfulness is a shield and a protective wall.
</p>

<p>
v05: You will not fear the terror of night
or the arrow that flies by day,
v06: nor the plague that walks in darkness
nor the destruction that ravages at noon.
v07: A thousand may fall beside you,
ten thousand at your right hand,
but it will not come near you.
v08: You will only look with your eyes
and see the wicked receive their due.
</p>

<p>
v09: For you, LORD, are my refuge.
You have made the Most High your dwelling place;
v10: no harm will meet you,
no plague will come near your tent.
</p>

<p>
v11: For he will command his angels concerning you,
to guard you in all your ways.
v12: They will carry you on their hands,
so you do not strike your foot against a stone.
v13: You will tread on lion and cobra;
you will trample young lion and serpent.
</p>

<p>
v14: “Because he clings to me in love, I will free him;
I will set him safely on high, because he knows my name.
v15: He will call to me, and I will answer.
I will be with him in distress;
I will rescue him and honor him.
v16: I will satisfy him with a long life
and let him see my salvation.”
</p>

## Notes

v02,09,14: The psalm changes speakers and addressees. Verse 2 retains “I say”; verse 9 retains “my refuge” followed by “your dwelling.” The final three verses speak in God's first person.

v07–15: The poem voices strong assurances of protection and also promises God's presence “in distress.” Both are retained without turning the poetry into a formula for testing danger.

## Vocabulary

v01: **Shaddai**: An ancient divine title, traditionally Almighty, whose derivation is uncertain.

v04,13: **Protective wall; serpent**: Soherah is an uncommon defense term, also rendered buckler. Tannin can denote a large serpent or monster; the animal imagery is retained.
