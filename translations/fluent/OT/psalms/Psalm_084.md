## Longing for the courts of God

<p>
v01: For the music leader. According to the Gittith. Of the sons of Korah. A psalm.
How beloved are your dwellings,
LORD of Armies!
v02: I long for the LORD's courts until I grow faint;
my heart and my flesh cry out to the living God.
</p>

<p>
v03: Even a bird finds a home,
and a swallow a nest for her young,
near your altars, LORD of Armies,
my king and my God.
</p>

<p>
v04: How fortunate are those who live in your house!
They keep praising you. Selah.
</p>

<p>
v05: How fortunate are those whose strength is in you,
whose hearts hold the roads of pilgrimage!
v06: As they pass through the Valley of Baca,
they make it a place of springs;
the early rain clothes it with blessings.
v07: They go from strength to strength;
each appears before God in Zion.
</p>

<p>
v08: LORD, God of Armies, hear my prayer;
listen, God of Jacob. Selah.
v09: God, look on our shield;
look upon the face of your anointed one.
</p>

<p>
v10: One day in your courts is better than a thousand.
I would rather stand at the threshold of my God's house
than live in the tents of wickedness.
v11: For the LORD God is a sun and a shield;
the LORD gives favor and honor.
He holds back nothing good
from those who walk with integrity.
</p>

<p>
v12: LORD of Armies,
how fortunate is the one who trusts in you!
</p>

## Notes

v05–06: The Hebrew speaks of highways in the heart. Baca may be a place name, a plant-related term, or wordplay suggesting weeping. The line about early rain and blessings has also been understood with pools or a teacher.

v09–10: “Our shield” likely stands parallel to the anointed ruler, though it can be addressed to God. Standing at a threshold need not identify a formal doorkeeper's office.

## Vocabulary

v01: **Sons of Korah**: A group associated with temple song; the title is retained as part of the source.

v09: **Anointed one**: Someone set apart with oil, here in a royal setting; the phrase is not automatically expanded into a later identification.
