## A refuge amid upheaval

<p>
v01: For the music leader. Of the sons of Korah. According to Alamoth. A song.
God is our refuge and strength,
a help readily found in distress.
v02: So we will not fear though the earth changes
and mountains slip into the heart of the seas,
v03: though their waters roar and foam
and mountains shake at their swelling. Selah.
</p>

<p>
v04: A river’s streams gladden God’s city,
the holy dwelling place of the Most High.
v05: God is within her; she will not fall.
God will help her when morning dawns.
v06: Nations roar, kingdoms fall;
he raises his voice, and the earth melts.
</p>

<p>
v07: The LORD of Armies is with us;
the God of Jacob is our stronghold. Selah.
</p>

<p>
v08: Come and see the LORD’s works,
the desolations he has brought upon the earth.
v09: He stops wars to the ends of the earth.
He breaks the bow, cuts the spear apart,
and burns the wagons with fire.
</p>

<p>
v10: “Let go, and know that I am God.
I will be exalted among the nations;
I will be exalted throughout the earth.”
</p>

<p>
v11: The LORD of Armies is with us;
the God of Jacob is our stronghold. Selah.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Alamoth is an uncertain musical direction, possibly connected with young women or a high register. It is retained rather than assigned a definite performance style.

v04,09–10: The river gives life to God’s city. The Hebrew in verse 9 names wagons, here among weapons of war. “Let go” can also be “cease” or “be still”; in this setting it interrupts conflict as God addresses the nations.

## Vocabulary

v07: **LORD of Armies — YHWH tsevaʾot**: A title of divine command, traditionally “LORD of hosts.” The scope of the armies is not specified in the title alone.
