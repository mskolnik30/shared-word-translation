## Justice and peace for the king

<p>
v01: Of Solomon.
God, give the king your judgments,
the king’s son your righteousness.
v02: May he judge your people with righteousness
and your afflicted ones with justice.
v03: May the mountains bring peace to the people,
and the hills bring it through righteousness.
v04: May he defend the afflicted among the people,
save the children of the needy,
and crush the oppressor.
</p>

<p>
v05: May they revere you while the sun endures,
as long as the moon, generation after generation.
v06: May he come down like rain on cut grass,
like showers watering the earth.
v07: In his days may the righteous flourish
and peace abound until the moon is no more.
</p>

<p>
v08: May he rule from sea to sea,
from the River to earth’s ends.
v09: May desert dwellers bow before him
and his enemies lick the dust.
v10: May the kings of Tarshish and the coastlands bring tribute,
the kings of Sheba and Seba offer gifts.
v11: May all kings bow before him,
all nations serve him.
</p>

<p>
v12: For he rescues the needy who cry out,
the afflicted who have no helper.
v13: He has pity on the weak and needy
and saves the lives of the needy.
v14: He redeems their lives from oppression and violence;
their blood is precious in his eyes.
</p>

<p>
v15: May he live, and may gold from Sheba be given to him.
May prayer be made for him continually,
and blessing offered to him all day.
v16: May grain abound in the land, even on the mountain tops;
may its fruit wave like Lebanon,
and people bloom from the city like the earth’s grass.
v17: May his name last forever,
may his name flourish as long as the sun.
May all nations bless themselves by him and call him happy.
</p>

<p>
v18: Blessed be the LORD God, the God of Israel,
who alone does wonders.
v19: Blessed be his glorious name forever;
may his glory fill the whole earth.
Amen and amen.
</p>

<p>
v20: The prayers of David son of Jesse are ended.
</p>

## Notes

v01,20: “Of Solomon” may mean by, for, or concerning Solomon. The final notice closes a collection of David's prayers; it does not erase this title or imply that no later psalm bears David's name.

v05,15: Verse 5 follows “May they fear you,” not a changed text saying that the king endures. Verse 15 leaves several pronoun relationships uncertain; the receiver of life and gold is not identified afresh.

v16–17: The abundant grain and flourishing city clauses are compressed. Verse 17 follows the reading tradition's flourishing name and retains the reflexive possibility of people blessing themselves by him.

v06,09: The word rendered cut grass can also mean fleece. The desert dwellers of verse 9 may be understood as wilderness creatures; the submission image remains clear.

## Vocabulary

v08: **The River**: A conventional designation for the Euphrates in this territorial picture.

v10: **Sheba and Seba**: Two distinct names in the Hebrew, both retained.
