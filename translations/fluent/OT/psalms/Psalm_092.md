## A song for the Sabbath

<p>
v01: A psalm. A song for the Sabbath day.
It is good to thank the LORD
and sing to your name, Most High,
v02: to tell of your faithful love in the morning
and your faithfulness through the nights,
v03: with the ten-stringed instrument and the harp,
with music resounding on the lyre.
</p>

<p>
v04: For your work has made me glad, LORD;
at the works of your hands I shout for joy.
v05: How great your works are, LORD!
How very deep your thoughts!
</p>

<p>
v06: A senseless person does not know;
a fool cannot understand this:
v07: though the wicked sprout like grass
and all who do wrong flourish,
it is only for them to be destroyed forever.
</p>

<p>
v08: But you, LORD, are exalted forever.
</p>

<p>
v09: Look, your enemies, LORD—
look, your enemies will perish!
All who do wrong will be scattered.
</p>

<p>
v10: You have raised my horn like a wild ox's;
I am refreshed with fresh oil.
v11: My eyes have looked upon those watching for my downfall;
my ears have heard about the evildoers who rise against me.
</p>

<p>
v12: The righteous flourish like a palm tree
and grow tall like a cedar in Lebanon.
v13: Planted in the LORD's house,
they flourish in the courts of our God.
v14: Even in old age they still bear fruit;
they remain full of sap and green,
</p>

<p>
v15: declaring, “The LORD is upright.
He is my rock; there is no injustice in him.”
</p>

## Notes

v10–11: The oil verb is difficult and can suggest anointing or refreshing. Verse 11 does not state exactly what the eyes see and ears hear; the draft does not add delight in an enemy's death.

v07,12–14: The wicked's quick-growing grass contrasts with enduring, fruitful palm and cedar trees, including fruit in old age.

## Vocabulary

v01: **Sabbath**: The seventh-day rest; this is the only psalm whose Hebrew title names the Sabbath day.
