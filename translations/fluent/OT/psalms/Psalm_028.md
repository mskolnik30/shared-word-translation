## Hear my plea

<p>
v01: Of David.
To you I call, LORD, my rock.
Do not be deaf to me.
If you stay silent, I will be like those who descend to the pit.
v02: Hear my plea for mercy when I cry to you,
when I lift my hands toward your holy inner sanctuary.
</p>

<p>
v03: Do not drag me away with the wicked, with evildoers,
who speak peace to their neighbors while harboring evil.
v04: Give them what their deeds deserve,
what their evil actions deserve.
Repay the work of their hands;
return to them what they have earned.
v05: They pay no attention to the LORD’s deeds,
to the work of his hands.
He will tear them down and never build them up.
</p>

<p>
v06: Blessed be the LORD!
He has heard my plea for mercy.
v07: The LORD is my strength and my shield.
My heart trusted him, and I was helped.
My heart leaps for joy; with my song I thank him.
</p>

<p>
v08: The LORD is their strength,
a stronghold of salvation for his anointed.
v09: Save your people and bless your inheritance.
Shepherd them and carry them forever.
</p>

## Notes

v08–09: The Hebrew has “their strength,” leaving the people unnamed until the closing prayer. The anointed person and the people are held together in the appeal.

## Vocabulary

v02: **Inner sanctuary — debir**: The temple’s innermost sacred space, toward which the worshiper raises hands.

v08: **Anointed — mashiaḥ**: Someone set apart by anointing, here in a royal setting. The word itself does not name a particular later figure.
