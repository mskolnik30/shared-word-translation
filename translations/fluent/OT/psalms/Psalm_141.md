## Guard my mouth and my heart

<p>
v01: A psalm of David.
LORD, I call to you; hurry to me.
Listen to my voice when I call to you.
v02: Let my prayer stand before you like incense,
my lifted hands like the evening offering.
</p>

<p>
v03: Set a guard over my mouth, LORD;
keep watch at the door of my lips.
v04: Do not incline my heart toward anything evil,
to take part in wicked deeds with evildoers;
do not let me eat their delicacies.
</p>

<p>
v05: Let a righteous person strike me—it is faithful love;
let him correct me—it is oil on my head.
Let my head not refuse it,
for my prayer is still against their evil deeds.
v06: When their judges are thrown down along the sides of a cliff,
they will hear my words, for they are pleasant.
v07: As when someone plows and breaks up the earth,
our bones are scattered at the mouth of Sheol.
</p>

<p>
v08: But my eyes are toward you, LORD, my Lord;
in you I take refuge. Do not pour out my life.
v09: Keep me from the grip of the trap they have laid for me,
from the snares of evildoers.
v10: Let the wicked fall together into their nets
while I pass through.
</p>

## Notes

v05–07,10: These are difficult Hebrew lines. The draft takes the righteous person’s correction as welcome oil, but the last clause of verse 5 and the judges/cliff scene in verse 6 remain uncertain. Verse 7 says “our bones.” Verse 10’s net pronoun is singular in Hebrew; English “their” connects the trap with the wicked without claiming a certain original referent.

## Vocabulary

v02: **Evening offering**: A regular act of worship used here as a comparison for raised hands in prayer.

v07: **Sheol**: The realm of the dead, pictured as having a mouth receiving scattered bones.
