## Walk around Zion

<p>
v01: A song. A psalm of the sons of Korah.
Great is the LORD and greatly to be praised
in our God’s city, his holy mountain.
v02: Beautiful in height, the joy of all the earth,
is Mount Zion, in the far north,
the city of the great king.
v03: Within her citadels,
God has made himself known as a stronghold.
</p>

<p>
v04: Look, the kings assembled;
together they advanced.
v05: They saw and were astonished;
terrified, they fled in haste.
v06: Trembling seized them there,
anguish like a woman in labor.
v07: With an east wind
you shatter the ships of Tarshish.
</p>

<p>
v08: As we heard, so we have seen
in the city of the LORD of Armies, in our God’s city.
God will establish her forever. Selah.
</p>

<p>
v09: We ponder your faithful love, God,
within your temple.
v10: Like your name, God, your praise reaches the ends of the earth.
Your right hand is full of righteousness.
v11: Let Mount Zion rejoice;
let the daughters of Judah be glad because of your judgments.
</p>

<p>
v12: Walk around Zion; make a circuit.
Count her towers.
v13: Take her rampart to heart; examine her citadels,
so you can tell the next generation:
v14: “This is God, our God forever and ever.
He will guide us unto death.”
</p>

## Notes

v02: “Far north” also evokes Zaphon, a mountain associated with divine rule in ancient imagery. The line describes Zion’s exalted significance, not a modern compass location for Jerusalem.

v07,11,14: Ships of Tarshish are large seagoing vessels. “Daughters of Judah” may mean Judah’s towns or their inhabitants. The final Hebrew phrase is uncertain: “unto death” is retained, while alternatives include continuing guidance or a musical direction.

## Vocabulary

v12–13: **Citadel and rampart**: Defensive structures. Walking around them gives the next generation a concrete story of protection.
