<p>
For the director. Of the sons of Korah. A Maskil.
</p>

## What Our Ancestors Told Us

<p>
v01: O God, with our ears we have heard;
our ancestors have told us
the work you worked in their days,
in days of old.

v02: With your hand you drove out nations,
but you planted our people;
you afflicted peoples,
but you spread our people out.

v03: For not by their sword
did they possess the land,
and their arm did not save them;
but your right hand and your arm,
and the light of your face,
because you delighted in them.
</p>

## You Are My King

<p>
v04: You are my King, O God;
command saving help for Jacob.

v05: Through you we push back our foes;
through your name we tread down
people who rise against us.

v06: For I do not trust in my bow,
and my sword does not save me.

v07: But you have saved us from our foes
and put to shame people who hate us.

v08: In God we boast all day long,
and we praise your name forever.
Selah.
</p>

## Yet You Have Rejected Us

<p>
v09: Yet you have rejected us
and put us to shame,
and you do not go out
with our armies.

v10: You make us turn back from the foe,
and people who hate us
plunder for themselves.

v11: You give us like sheep for food,
and you scatter us among the nations.

v12: You sell your people for no gain;
you gain nothing from their price.
</p>

<p>
v13: You make us a reproach to our neighbors,
a scorn and derision
to those around us.

v14: You make us a proverb among the nations,
a shaking of the head among the peoples.

v15: All day my disgrace is before me,
and the shame of my face covers me,

v16: at the voice of the one who taunts and reviles,
before the enemy and the avenger.
</p>

## We Have Not Forgotten You

<p>
v17: All this has come upon us,
yet we have not forgotten you,
and we have not dealt falsely
with your covenant.

v18: Our heart has not turned back,
and our steps have not departed
from your path,

v19: though you crushed us
in a place of jackals
and covered us
with the shadow of death.
</p>

<p>
v20: If we had forgotten
the name of our God
or spread out our hands
to a foreign god,

v21: would not God discover this?
For God knows
the secrets of the heart.

v22: Yet for your sake
we are killed all day long;
we are counted
as sheep for slaughter.
</p>

## Rise Up and Help Us

<p>
v23: Awake!
Why do you sleep, Lord?
Rouse yourself;
do not reject us forever.

v24: Why do you hide your face?
Why do you forget
our affliction and oppression?

v25: For our soul is bowed down to the dust;
our belly clings to the earth.

v26: Rise up;
be our help.
Redeem us
for the sake of your steadfast love.
</p>

## Notes

v01: “our ancestors have told us” — The psalm begins with received communal memory. The present generation knows the earlier acts of God through testimony passed down to them.

v02: “you planted our people” — The Hebrew shifts rapidly between the nations driven out and the people established in the land. The referent is made explicit in English to prevent the pronouns from reversing the sense of the parallel lines.

v03: “the light of your face” — Israel’s possession of the land is attributed not to military ability but to God’s presence and favor.

v04: “command saving help for Jacob” — The Hebrew uses an unusual plural expression for acts of salvation or deliverance. “Saving help” preserves the request without specifying what form the deliverance must take.

v09: “Yet” — The sharp turn is essential to the psalm. The community’s confession of God’s past saving action is placed directly beside its present experience of rejection.

v12: “You sell your people for no gain” — The image is deliberately humiliating: the people are portrayed as sold without even enriching the seller.

v17–18: The community protests that its suffering has come despite continued remembrance of God and fidelity to the covenant. The translation leaves that claim intact rather than explaining the suffering as hidden punishment.

v19: “a place of jackals” — Jackals evoke abandoned and desolate places. “The shadow of death” retains the Hebrew image of extreme darkness and mortal danger.

v20: “spread out our hands” — Extended hands are a bodily posture of prayer or appeal. The hypothetical act would therefore represent worship directed to another god.

v21: “the secrets of the heart” — The appeal assumes that God can test the community’s claim of faithfulness because nothing inward is hidden from God.

v22: “sheep for slaughter” — The image returns to the sheep language of v11, but now emphasizes continual vulnerability and death.

v23: “Why do you sleep, Lord?” — The psalm uses bold human language for perceived divine inactivity. The translation preserves the accusation without qualifying it.

v26: The final appeal rests not on military strength or even on the community’s claim of innocence, but on God’s steadfast love.

## Vocabulary

v02: נָטַע (nata)
“To plant.” Used metaphorically for establishing a people securely in a land.

v03: פָּנִים (panim)
“Face” or “presence.” The “light” of God’s face expresses divine favor and active presence.

v04: יְשׁוּעָה (yeshuah)
“Saving help,” “deliverance,” or “salvation.” The Hebrew appears here in a plural form with a sense of acts or manifestations of deliverance.

v13: חֶרְפָּה (cherpah)
“Reproach,” “disgrace,” or “shame.” Public contempt directed toward a person or community.

v17: בְּרִית (berit)
“Covenant.” A binding relationship characterized by obligation, promise, and faithfulness.

v19: צַלְמָוֶת (tsalmavet)
“Shadow of death” or “deep darkness.” An image of profound danger, darkness, and nearness to death.

v21: סֵתֶר (seter)
“Hidden place” or “secret.” Here used for what lies concealed within the human heart.

v26: חֶסֶד (ḥesed)
“Steadfast love.” Faithful, enduring love that becomes the final ground of the community’s appeal for redemption.
