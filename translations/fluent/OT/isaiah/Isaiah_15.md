## An Oracle Against Moab

<p>
v01: An oracle concerning Moab:
In a night Ar of Moab is devastated, silenced;
in a night Kir of Moab is devastated, silenced.
v02: Moab has gone up to the temple,
to Dibon’s high places, to weep.
Over Nebo and Medeba it wails.
Every head is shaved;
every beard is cut off.
v03: In its streets they wear sackcloth;
on rooftops and in squares
everyone wails,
melting into tears.
v04: Heshbon and Elealeh cry out;
their voices are heard as far as Jahaz.
So Moab’s armed men cry aloud;
its very life trembles.
</p>

<p>
v05: My heart cries out for Moab.
Its fugitives reach Zoar, Eglath-shelishiyah.
They climb Luhith’s ascent weeping;
on the road to Horonaim
they raise a cry of destruction.
v06: Nimrim’s waters are desolate;
the grass has dried, new growth has ended,
and nothing green remains.
v07: So the wealth they have gained,
all they have stored,
they carry across the Wadi of the Willows.
v08: The cry circles Moab’s borders;
its wailing reaches Eglaim,
its wailing reaches Beer-elim.
</p>

<p>
v09: Dimon’s waters are full of blood,
yet I will bring more upon Dimon:
a lion against Moab’s fugitives
and those left in the land.
</p>

## Notes

v02: “The temple” may instead represent a place named Bayith. The relationship of the temple, Dibon, and the high places is compressed; the draft understands the high places as belonging to Dibon.

v05: Eglath-shelishiyah may be a place name or mean “a three-year-old heifer.” The translation preserves it as a name while leaving that alternative visible.

## Vocabulary

v09: **Dimon**: The name sounds like the Hebrew word for blood, intensifying the line’s mourning.
