## The Servant and a New Song

<p>
v01: “Here is my servant, whom I uphold,
my chosen one, in whom I delight.
I have put my Spirit upon him;
he will bring justice to the nations.
v02: He will not shout or raise his voice,
or make it heard in the street.
v03: A bruised reed he will not break;
a dimly burning wick he will not put out.
He will faithfully bring justice.
v04: He will not burn dim or be crushed
until he establishes justice on earth;
the islands wait for his instruction.”
</p>

<p>
v05: Thus says God, the **LORD**,
who created the heavens and stretched them out,
spread the earth and all that springs from it,
gives breath to its people
and spirit to those who walk upon it:
v06: “I, the **LORD**, called you in righteousness;
I will take your hand and guard you.
I will give you as a covenant for the people,
a light for the nations,
v07: to open blind eyes,
to bring prisoners out of confinement,
those dwelling in darkness out of prison.
v08: I am the **LORD**; that is my name.
I will not give my glory to another
or my praise to carved images.
</p>

<p>
v09: Look, the former things have come to pass;
now I announce new things.
Before they sprout, I make them known to you.”
v10: Sing the **LORD** a new song,
his praise from earth’s ends—
you who go down to the sea, and all that fills it,
islands and those who inhabit them!
v11: Let wilderness and its towns lift their voices,
the settlements where Kedar lives.
Let Sela’s inhabitants sing for joy,
shout from the mountaintops.
v12: Let them give glory to the **LORD**
and proclaim his praise in the islands.
</p>

<p>
v13: The **LORD** goes out like a warrior;
like a man of battle he stirs his zeal.
He cries out, raises a battle shout,
shows his might against his enemies.
v14: “For a long time I have kept silent,
kept still and restrained myself.
Now I cry out like a woman in labor;
I gasp and pant together.
v15: I will lay mountains and hills waste
and dry up all their vegetation.
I will turn rivers into islands
and dry up pools.
v16: I will lead the blind by a way they do not know,
guide them on paths they do not know.
I will turn darkness before them into light,
and rough places into level ground.
These things I will do; I will not abandon them.
</p>

<p>
v17: Those who trust in carved images,
who tell cast idols, ‘You are our gods,’
will be turned back, deeply ashamed.
v18: Listen, you deaf!
Look and see, you blind!
v19: Who is blind but my servant,
as deaf as the messenger I send?
Who is blind like the one committed to me,
blind like the **LORD**’s servant?
v20: Seeing many things, you pay no attention;
with ears open, he does not hear.
</p>

<p>
v21: The **LORD** desired, for his righteousness’ sake,
to magnify instruction and make it glorious.
v22: But this is a people robbed and plundered,
all trapped in holes, hidden in prisons.
They have become prey, with no rescuer,
plunder, with no one saying, ‘Give them back!’
v23: Who among you will listen to this,
pay attention and hear for the time to come?
v24: Who handed Jacob over to plunder, Israel to robbers?
Was it not the **LORD**, against whom we sinned?
They would not walk in his ways
and did not listen to his instruction.
</p>

<p>
v25: So he poured on him his burning anger and the violence of war.
It blazed around him, but he did not understand;
it burned him, but he did not take it to heart.
</p>

## Notes

v01,19: The servant is not given a personal name here. Verse 19 also calls a blind figure the servant; the relationship between these descriptions should not be resolved by inserting an identity into the text.

v13–14,24: God is compared both to a male warrior and to a woman in labor. Verse 24 changes from “we” to “they”; that change remains.

## Vocabulary

v06: **Covenant**: The servant is given “as a covenant,” not merely as someone who announces one.
