## Woe to Those Who Go Down to Egypt

<p>
v01: Woe to those who go down to Egypt for help,
leaning on horses,
trusting chariots because they are many
and horsemen because they are very strong,
but not looking to Israel’s Holy One
or seeking the **LORD**!
v02: Yet he too is wise; he brings disaster
and does not take back his words.
He will rise against the house of evildoers
and against the helpers of those who do wrong.
v03: The Egyptians are human, not God;
their horses are flesh, not spirit.
When the **LORD** stretches out his hand,
the helper will stumble and the helped will fall;
together they will all perish.
v04: For the **LORD** said this to me:
“As a lion, a young lion, growls over its prey,
though a whole band of shepherds is called against it,
not frightened by their voices
nor subdued by their uproar,
so the **LORD** of hosts will descend
to fight on Mount Zion and its hill.
</p>

<p>
v05: Like birds in flight,
the **LORD** of hosts will shield Jerusalem;
shielding, he will deliver it;
passing over, he will rescue it.”
v06: Return to the one against whom you Israelites have rebelled so deeply.
v07: On that day each of you will reject the silver idols and gold idols your own hands made for you as a sin.
v08: “Assyria will fall by a sword not of man;
a sword not of humanity will devour him.
He will flee before the sword,
and his young men will become forced laborers.
</p>

<p>
v09: His rock will pass away in terror;
his officials will panic at the signal,”
declares the **LORD**, whose fire is in Zion,
whose furnace is in Jerusalem.
</p>

## Notes

v04–05: The lion and hovering birds picture divine action around Zion. The wording about fighting “on” Mount Zion leaves the direction of that action less explicit than some translations do.

## Vocabulary

v03: **Flesh and spirit**: The contrast here distinguishes mortal strength from divine power; it is not a claim that bodies are evil.
