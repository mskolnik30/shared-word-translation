## The Valley of Vision

<p>
v01: An oracle concerning the Valley of Vision:
What is happening to you,
that all of you have climbed onto the roofs?
v02: You are full of uproar,
a noisy city, a town of revelry.
Your slain were not killed by the sword
or did they die in battle.
v03: All your leaders fled together;
without a bow being drawn they were captured.
All of you who were found were captured together,
though you had fled far away.
v04: So I said, “Look away from me;
let me weep bitterly.
Do not press me to be comforted
over the devastation of my people’s daughter.”
</p>

<p>
v05: The Lord **GOD** of hosts has a day
of tumult, trampling, and confusion
in the Valley of Vision—
walls battered down,
cries reaching the mountains.
v06: Elam carries the quiver,
with chariots, men, and horsemen;
Kir uncovers the shield.
v07: Your finest valleys fill with chariots;
horsemen take their stand at the gate.
v08: Judah’s defenses are stripped away.
On that day you looked to the weapons
in the House of the Forest.
</p>

<p>
v09: You saw the many breaches in David’s City
and collected the water of the lower pool.
v10: You counted Jerusalem’s houses
and tore houses down to strengthen the wall.
v11: You made a reservoir between the two walls
for the old pool’s water.
But you did not look to its Maker
or see the one who fashioned it long ago.
v12: On that day the Lord **GOD** of hosts called
for weeping and mourning,
shaved heads and sackcloth.
</p>

<p>
v13: But look—joy and revelry,
killing cattle and slaughtering sheep,
eating meat and drinking wine!
“Let us eat and drink,
for tomorrow we die!”
v14: The **LORD** of hosts revealed this in my hearing: “Surely this guilt will not be atoned for until you die,” says the Lord **GOD** of hosts.
v15: The Lord **GOD** of hosts says, “Go to this steward, to Shebna, who is in charge of the palace, and say:
v16: ‘What do you have here? Whom do you have here, that you have cut yourself a tomb here—hewing your grave on a height, carving yourself a dwelling in the rock?
</p>

<p>
v17: Look, the **LORD** is about to hurl you away, mighty man! He will seize you tightly,
v18: wrap you round and round like a ball, and throw you into a broad land. There you will die, and there your splendid chariots will be—you disgrace to your master’s house!
v19: I will thrust you from your office; he will pull you down from your position.
v20: On that day I will call my servant Eliakim son of Hilkiah.
</p>

<p>
v21: I will clothe him in your robe, bind your sash around him, and place your authority in his hand. He will be a father to Jerusalem’s inhabitants and the house of Judah.
v22: I will place the key of David’s house on his shoulder. What he opens, no one will shut; what he shuts, no one will open.
v23: I will drive him like a peg into a firm place, and he will become a throne of honor for his father’s house.
v24: On him they will hang all the weight of his father’s house—offspring and offshoots, every small vessel, from bowls to every sort of jar.’
</p>

<p>
v25: On that day, declares the **LORD** of hosts, the peg driven into a firm place will give way, be cut down, and fall. The load hanging on it will be cut off, for the **LORD** has spoken.”
</p>

## Notes

v03: The Hebrew can suggest being bound without a bow being used, or being captured by bowmen. The draft follows the former interpretation.

v19: The source shifts from “I” to “he” in describing removal from office. The translation retains the shift.

v25: The peg once driven into a firm place now gives way. The draft preserves the reversal rather than harmonizing it with the earlier promise.

## Vocabulary

v22: **Key of David’s house**: An image of entrusted authority over access, developed by the paired actions of opening and shutting.
