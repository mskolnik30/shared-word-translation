## A Righteous King and the Spirit from Above

<p>
v01: Look! A king will reign in righteousness,
and officials will govern with justice.
v02: Each will be like a shelter from wind,
a refuge from a storm,
like streams in a dry place,
like a great rock’s shade in a weary land.
v03: The eyes of those who see will no longer be closed,
and the ears of those who hear will pay attention.
v04: Impulsive minds will understand and know;
stammering tongues will speak readily and clearly.
</p>

<p>
v05: A fool will no longer be called noble,
nor a scoundrel be described as generous.
v06: For a fool speaks folly and plots wrongdoing in his heart:
practicing godlessness, speaking error about the **LORD**,
leaving the hungry empty
and depriving the thirsty of drink.
v07: The scoundrel’s tools are evil.
He devises schemes to ruin the poor with lying words,
even when the needy plead a just case.
v08: But the noble person devises noble things
and stands by noble things.
</p>

<p>
v09: Rise, women at ease, and hear my voice!
Confident daughters, listen to what I say!
v10: In a year and some days you confident women will tremble,
for the grape harvest will fail
and no fruit gathering will come.
v11: Shudder, women at ease! Tremble, confident women!
Strip yourselves bare
and tie sackcloth around your waists.
v12: Beat your breasts
for the pleasant fields, for the fruitful vine,
</p>

<p>
v13: for my people’s soil, overgrown with thorns and briers,
for every joyful house in the jubilant city.
v14: The palace is abandoned, the noisy city deserted.
Citadel and watchtower become caves forever,
a delight for wild donkeys, pasture for flocks,
v15: until a spirit is poured on us from on high.
Then the wilderness becomes fruitful land,
and fruitful land is regarded as forest.
v16: Justice will dwell in the wilderness,
and righteousness live in fruitful land.
</p>

<p>
v17: The work of righteousness will be peace,
its effect quietness and confidence forever.
v18: My people will live in a home of peace,
in secure dwellings and restful places free from fear.
v19: Though hail brings the forest down
and the city is brought very low,
v20: happy are you who sow beside every water,
letting ox and donkey roam free.
</p>

## Notes

v09–12: The address is to complacent women. The breast-beating and exposed bodies belong to the coming lament; they have not been changed into references to fields.

v14–15: “Forever” in the description of desolation is followed by “until” the spirit is poured out. The poem’s reversal remains visible.

## Vocabulary

v15: **Spirit**: Hebrew ruach can mean spirit, wind or breath. Here its pouring out from above brings renewal.
