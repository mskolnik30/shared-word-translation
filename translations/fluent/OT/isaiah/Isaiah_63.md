## The Day of Vengeance and the LORD's Steadfast Love

<p>
v01: Who is this coming from Edom,
from Bozrah in crimson-stained clothes,
splendid in his garments,
striding in his great strength?
“It is I, speaking in righteousness,
mighty to save.”
v02: Why are your clothes red,
your garments like those of someone treading a winepress?
v03: “I have trodden the press alone;
no one from the peoples was with me.
I trod them in my anger
and trampled them in my fury.
Their lifeblood spattered my clothes;
I stained all my garments.
v04: For a day of vengeance was in my heart,
and the year of my redeemed had come.
</p>

<p>
v05: I looked, but there was no helper;
I was appalled, but there was no supporter.
So my own arm brought me salvation,
and my fury sustained me.
v06: I trampled peoples in my anger,
made them drunk in my fury,
and poured their lifeblood onto the ground.”
v07: I will recount the **LORD**’s faithful acts of love,
the **LORD**’s praiseworthy deeds,
for all the **LORD** has done for us—
his great goodness to the house of Israel,
shown according to his compassion
and his abundant faithful love.
v08: He said, “Surely they are my people,
children who will not deal falsely.”
So he became their Savior.
</p>

<p>
v09: In all their distress he was distressed,
and the messenger of his presence saved them.
In his love and pity he redeemed them;
he lifted them up and carried them all the days of old.
v10: But they rebelled and grieved his holy Spirit.
So he turned into their enemy;
he himself fought against them.
v11: Then his people remembered the days of old, the days of Moses:
“Where is the one who brought them up from the sea
with the shepherds of his flock?
Where is the one who put his holy Spirit among them,
v12: who sent his glorious arm
at Moses’ right hand,
who split the waters before them
to make an everlasting name for himself,
</p>

<p>
v13: who led them through the depths?
Like a horse in open country,
they did not stumble.
v14: Like cattle going down into a valley,
the **LORD**’s Spirit gave them rest.
So you led your people
to make yourself a glorious name.”
v15: Look down from heaven and see,
from your holy and glorious dwelling.
Where are your zeal and your mighty deeds?
Your deep stirring and compassion
are withheld from me.
v16: For you are our Father,
though Abraham does not know us
and Israel does not recognize us.
You, **LORD**, are our Father;
Our Redeemer from of Old is your name.
</p>

<p>
v17: Why, **LORD**, do you make us wander from your ways
and harden our hearts so we do not fear you?
Return for your servants’ sake,
the tribes of your inheritance.
v18: Your holy people possessed it only a little while;
our adversaries have trampled your sanctuary.
v19: We have become like those you never ruled,
those over whom your name was never called.
</p>

## Notes

v09,11,17: Verse 9 follows the recorded reading “he was distressed”; the written “not” permits a different sense. The remembering subject in verse 11 is uncertain. Verse 17 directly asks why God causes wandering and hardening.

## Vocabulary

v03: **Winepress**: A place where grapes are trodden; the poem transforms grape juice into the peoples’ lifeblood.
