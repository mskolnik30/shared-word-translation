<p>
v01: Let me sing for my beloved
my beloved’s song about his vineyard.
My beloved had a vineyard
on a fertile hillside.
v02: He dug it, cleared its stones,
and planted choice vines.
He built a tower in its midst
and cut out a winepress.
He expected grapes,
but it produced foul fruit.
</p>

<p>
v03: “Now, inhabitants of Jerusalem and people of Judah,
judge between me and my vineyard.
v04: What more could I have done for my vineyard
that I have not done?
Why, when I expected grapes,
did it produce foul fruit?
</p>

<p>
v05: Now I will tell you
what I will do to my vineyard:
remove its hedge, and it will be devoured;
break its wall, and it will be trampled.
v06: I will make it a wasteland,
neither pruned nor hoed,
overgrown with thorns and briers.
I will command the clouds
to send no rain on it.”
</p>

<p>
v07: The vineyard of the **LORD** of hosts is the house of Israel;
the people of Judah are the planting he delighted in.
He expected justice—but look, bloodshed!
Righteousness—but listen, a cry!
</p>

<p>
v08: Woe to those who join house to house
and add field to field,
until no room remains
and you live alone in the land!
v09: The **LORD** of hosts has spoken in my hearing:
“Surely many houses will lie desolate,
large and beautiful, with no one living there.
v10: Ten yoke of vineyard will yield one bath,
and a homer of seed will yield an ephah.”
</p>

<p>
v11: Woe to those who rise early to chase strong drink
and linger late, inflamed by wine!
v12: Lyre and harp, tambourine and flute,
and wine fill their feasts.
But they do not regard the **LORD**’s work
or see what his hands have done.
</p>

<p>
v13: So my people go into exile for lack of knowledge.
Their honored people die of hunger;
their masses are parched with thirst.
v14: So Sheol widens its appetite
and opens its mouth without limit.
Down go Jerusalem’s splendor and crowds,
her uproar and revelers.
</p>

<p>
v15: Humanity bows down; people are brought low;
proud eyes are lowered.
v16: But the **LORD** of hosts is exalted through justice;
the holy God shows himself holy through righteousness.
v17: Lambs will graze as in their pasture,
and strangers will eat among the ruins of the well-fed.
</p>

<p>
v18: Woe to those who drag guilt with cords of falsehood
and sin as with cart ropes,
v19: who say, “Let him hurry!
Let him speed his work so we can see it!
Let the Holy One of Israel’s plan come near,
let it arrive so we may know!”
</p>

<p>
v20: Woe to those who call evil good and good evil,
who turn darkness into light and light into darkness,
who turn bitter into sweet and sweet into bitter!
</p>

<p>
v21: Woe to those wise in their own eyes
and discerning in their own sight!
v22: Woe to heroes at drinking wine,
valiant men at mixing strong drink,
v23: who acquit the wicked for a bribe
and take the righteous person’s rights away!
</p>

<p>
v24: As a tongue of fire eats stubble
and dry grass collapses in flame,
their root will rot
and their blossom rise like dust.
They have rejected the instruction of the **LORD** of hosts
and scorned the Holy One of Israel’s word.
</p>

<p>
v25: So the **LORD**’s anger burned against his people.
He stretched out his hand and struck them;
mountains shook,
and their corpses lay like refuse in the streets.
For all this, his anger has not turned away;
his hand is still stretched out.
</p>

<p>
v26: He will raise a signal for distant nations
and whistle for them from the earth’s end.
Look, they come swiftly, speedily!
v27: None grows tired or stumbles;
none dozes or sleeps.
No belt at the waist comes loose;
no sandal strap breaks.
v28: Their arrows are sharp,
all their bows drawn.
Their horses’ hooves seem like flint,
their wheels like a whirlwind.
</p>

<p>
v29: Their roar is like a lion’s;
they roar like young lions.
They growl, seize prey,
and carry it off with no one to rescue it.
v30: On that day they will growl over it
like the sea’s roar.
Look toward the land:
darkness and distress!
Even the light is darkened by its clouds.
</p>

## Notes

v07: The Hebrew pairs mishpat, “justice,” with mispach, “bloodshed,” and tsedaqah, “righteousness,” with tseaqah, “outcry.” The similar sounds sharpen the failed expectations.

v10: A “yoke” describes the land a team plows, not a fixed modern acre. The harvest is disastrously small: one bath of wine and one ephah returned from a homer of seed.

v17: The Hebrew text used here refers to strangers or sojourners in the second line. “Lambs” there reflects a different reading.

## Vocabulary

v10: **Bath, homer, ephah**: Ancient capacity measures. A homer is ten ephahs; the yield here is only a tenth of the seed measure.
