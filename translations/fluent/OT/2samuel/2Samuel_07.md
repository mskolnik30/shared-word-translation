## A House for the LORD, a House for David

<p>
v01: When the king was settled in his house and the LORD had given him rest from all his enemies around him,
v02: he said to the prophet Nathan, “Look, I live in a cedar house, but the ark of God stays inside tent curtains.”
v03: Nathan told the king, “Go ahead and do what is in your heart, for the LORD is with you.”
</p>

<p>
v04: But that night the word of the LORD came to Nathan:
v05: “Go and tell my servant David, ‘This is what the LORD says: Are you the one to build a house for me to live in?
v06: I have not lived in a house from the day I brought Israel out of Egypt until now. I have moved about in a tent, in a dwelling-place.
v07: Wherever I moved among all Israel, did I ever ask any of Israel’s tribes, whom I commanded to shepherd my people Israel, “Why have you not built me a cedar house?”’
</p>

<p>
v08: “Now tell my servant David, ‘This is what the LORD of Armies says: I took you from the pasture, from following the flock, to be ruler over my people Israel.
v09: I have been with you wherever you went and have cut off all your enemies before you. I will make your name great, like the names of the great ones of the earth.
v10: I will provide a place for my people Israel and plant them there. They will live in their own place, disturbed no more. Violent people will no longer oppress them as they did before,
v11: ever since I appointed judges over my people Israel. I will give you rest from all your enemies. And the LORD declares to you that the LORD will make a house for you.
</p>

<p>
v12: When your days are complete and you lie down with your ancestors, I will raise up your offspring after you, one born from your own body, and establish his kingdom.
v13: He will build a house for my name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever.
v14: I will be his father, and he will be my son. When he does wrong, I will correct him with a human rod and with blows from human hands.
v15: But my steadfast love will not leave him as I took it away from Saul, whom I removed from before you.
v16: Your house and your kingdom will stand firm forever before you; your throne will be established forever.’”
</p>

<p>
v17: Nathan told David all these words and the whole vision.
</p>

## David’s Prayer

<p>
v18: Then King David went in and sat before the LORD. He said, “Who am I, Lord GOD, and what is my house, that you have brought me this far?
v19: Even this was a small thing in your eyes, Lord GOD. You have spoken about your servant’s house far into the future. Is this the way of humankind, Lord GOD?
v20: What more can David say to you? You know your servant, Lord GOD.
v21: For the sake of your word and according to your own heart, you have done all this greatness and made it known to your servant.
</p>

<p>
v22: How great you are, Lord GOD! No one is like you; there is no God besides you, as all we have heard with our own ears confirms.
v23: Who is like your people Israel, the one nation on earth whom God went to redeem as his people and make a name for himself? You did great things for them, awesome things for your land, before your people whom you redeemed for yourself from Egypt—from nations and their gods.
v24: You established your people Israel as your own people forever, and you, LORD, became their God.
</p>

<p>
v25: Now, LORD God, establish forever the word you have spoken about your servant and his house. Do as you have said.
v26: May your name be great forever, as people say, ‘The LORD of Armies is God over Israel.’ And may the house of your servant David stand firm before you.
v27: LORD of Armies, God of Israel, you have revealed this to your servant: ‘I will build you a house.’ That is why your servant has found the courage to pray this prayer to you.
</p>

<p>
v28: Now, Lord GOD, you are God, and your words are true. You have promised this good thing to your servant.
v29: So please bless your servant’s house, that it may remain before you forever. You, Lord GOD, have spoken, and with your blessing your servant’s house will be blessed forever.”
</p>

## Notes

v07: The pinned Hebrew says “tribes,” not “judges.” The relationship between the tribes and the command to shepherd is compressed.

v12–16: “House” moves between a building and a continuing family or dynasty. The promise keeps offspring, name, kingdom, and throne connected. Verse 16 reads “before you.”

v19: The final phrase is difficult: literally, “and this is the instruction/law of humankind.” It can be read as a statement about instruction for humanity or, as here, a question about the human way of proceeding.

v23: The Hebrew shifts between speaking about God and addressing him, and briefly uses a plural “you,” apparently addressing Israel. The English makes these shifts more continuous. The final “nations and their gods” is grammatically compressed; its attachment remains uncertain.

v27: The Hebrew says God “uncovered the ear” of his servant and the servant “found his heart” to pray. The English unfolds these as revelation and courage.

## Vocabulary

v10: Plant
An image of giving the people a settled place in which to live.

v15: Steadfast love
Committed love that continues through the relationship; the promise explicitly distinguishes discipline from abandonment.

v18: Lord GOD
This form represents the title “Lord” followed by the divine name, here printed GOD.
