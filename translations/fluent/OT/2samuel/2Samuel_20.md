## Sheba’s Rebellion

<p>
v01: A worthless man happened to be there, a Benjaminite named Sheba son of Bichri. He blew the ram’s horn and said, “We have no share in David, no inheritance in Jesse’s son. Every man to his tents, Israel!”
v02: All the men of Israel deserted David to follow Sheba son of Bichri. But Judah’s men clung to their king from the Jordan all the way to Jerusalem.
</p>

## The Women Left behind

<p>
v03: David came home to Jerusalem. The king took the ten concubines he had left to look after the house and placed them in a guarded house. He provided for them but did not sleep with them. They remained shut away until their deaths, living as widows.
</p>

## Joab Kills Amasa

<p>
v04: The king told Amasa, “Summon Judah’s men for me within three days, and be here yourself.”
v05: Amasa went to summon Judah, but he delayed beyond the time set for him.
</p>

<p>
v06: David told Abishai, “Now Sheba son of Bichri will do us more harm than Absalom. Take your lord’s servants and pursue him before he finds fortified cities and escapes our sight.”
v07: Joab’s men, the Cherethites, the Pelethites, and all the warriors followed Abishai out of Jerusalem to pursue Sheba son of Bichri.
</p>

<p>
v08: They were at the great stone in Gibeon when Amasa came to meet them. Joab was wearing his military garment, with a sword strapped over it in a sheath at his waist. As he stepped forward, the sword fell out.
</p>

<p>
v09: Joab said to Amasa, “Are you well, my brother?” With his right hand he took Amasa by the beard to kiss him.
v10: Amasa did not guard himself against the sword in Joab’s hand. Joab struck him in the belly and spilled his intestines onto the ground. He did not strike a second time, and Amasa died. Then Joab and his brother Abishai pursued Sheba son of Bichri.
</p>

<p>
v11: One of Joab’s young men stood beside Amasa and called, “Whoever favors Joab, whoever belongs to David, follow Joab!”
</p>

<p>
v12: Amasa lay wallowing in blood in the middle of the highway. When the man saw everyone stopping, he dragged Amasa off the highway into a field and threw a garment over him, because everyone who came upon him stopped.
</p>

<p>
v13: Once Amasa was removed from the highway, everyone went on after Joab to pursue Sheba son of Bichri.
</p>

## The Siege of Abel

<p>
v14: Sheba passed through all Israel’s tribes to Abel and Beth-maacah, and to all the Berites. They gathered together and followed him.
</p>

<p>
v15: Joab’s men came and besieged him in Abel-beth-maacah. They piled a siege ramp against the city, up to the outer defenses, while everyone with Joab battered the wall to bring it down.
</p>

<p>
v16: A wise woman called from the city, “Listen! Listen! Please tell Joab, ‘Come here so I can speak to you.’”
</p>

<p>
v17: He came toward her, and she asked, “Are you Joab?” “I am,” he answered. She said, “Listen to your servant’s words.” He replied, “I am listening.”
</p>

<p>
v18: She said, “In the past people used to say, ‘Ask at Abel,’ and that would settle the matter.
v19: I am among the peaceful and faithful in Israel. You are trying to kill a city, a mother in Israel. Why would you swallow up the LORD’s inheritance?”
</p>

<p>
v20: Joab answered, “Far be it from me—far be it! I would never swallow up or destroy.
</p>

<p>
v21: That is not the matter. A man from the hill country of Ephraim, Sheba son of Bichri, has raised his hand against King David. Hand over that man alone, and I will withdraw from the city.” The woman told Joab, “His head will be thrown to you over the wall.”
</p>

<p>
v22: The woman went to all the people with her wise counsel. They cut off Sheba son of Bichri’s head and threw it to Joab. He blew the ram’s horn, and the men scattered from the city, each to his own tent. Joab returned to the king in Jerusalem.
</p>

## The King’s Officials

<p>
v23: Joab was over Israel’s whole army. Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and Pelethites.
v24: Adoram was over the forced labor, and Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was the recorder.
v25: Sheva was the scribe, and Zadok and Abiathar were priests.
v26: Ira the Jairite was also a priest for David.
</p>

## Notes

v03: The ten women receive provision but remain confined until death. “Living as widows” is the source’s description; it does not make them responsible for what Absalom did.

v08–10: The sword’s movement is compressed. The text does not explain how it fell from its sheath and came to be in Joab’s hand. The fatal violence is stated explicitly.

v14: The source says Berites; their identity is uncertain. The draft understands Sheba as the unnamed traveler and preserves the difficult sequence of place names.

v18–22: The woman invokes Abel’s reputation for counsel. Joab’s denial of destruction is his speech. The city is spared through Sheba’s killing, not through a bloodless settlement.

v26: The source calls Ira a priest for David. His precise duties are not specified.

## Vocabulary

v01: Share and inheritance
The rebellion’s slogan rejects a stake in David’s rule, answering the preceding argument over shares.

v19: A mother in Israel
The city is pictured as a mother; the image emphasizes a community facing destruction.

v24: Forced labor
Compulsory work administered by a royal official.
