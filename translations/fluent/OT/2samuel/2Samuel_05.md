## David becomes king of all Israel

<p>
v01: All the tribes of Israel came to David at Hebron and said, “We are your own bone and flesh.
v02: Even when Saul was king over us, you were the one who led Israel out and brought Israel back. And the LORD said to you, ‘You will shepherd my people Israel; you will be ruler over Israel.’”
</p>

<p>
v03: All the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron. King David made a covenant with them there before the LORD, and they anointed David king over Israel.
</p>

<p>
v04: David was thirty years old when he became king, and he reigned forty years.
v05: He reigned over Judah in Hebron for seven years and six months. In Jerusalem he reigned over all Israel and Judah for thirty-three years.
</p>

## Jerusalem captured

<p>
v06: The king and his men went to Jerusalem against the Jebusites who lived in the land. They told David, “You will not get in here! Even the blind and the lame will turn you back.” They thought, “David will never get in here.”
v07: But David captured the stronghold of Zion, which is the City of David.
</p>

<p>
v08: That day David said, “Whoever strikes the Jebusites must reach the water shaft—and the lame and the blind, those David hates.” That is why people say, “The blind and the lame must not enter the house.”
</p>

<p>
v09: David settled in the stronghold and called it the City of David. He built up the surrounding area, from the Millo inward.
v10: David grew greater and greater, and the LORD, the God of Armies, was with him.
</p>

## David’s house grows

<p>
v11: Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, along with cedar timber, carpenters, and stonemasons. They built a house for David.
v12: David knew that the LORD had established him as king over Israel and had raised up his kingdom for the sake of his people Israel.
</p>

<p>
v13: After coming from Hebron, David took more concubines and wives from Jerusalem, and more sons and daughters were born to him.
</p>

<p>
v14: These are the names of those born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon,
v15: Ibhar, Elishua, Nepheg, Japhia,
v16: Elishama, Eliada, and Eliphelet.
</p>

## Two Philistine attacks

<p>
v17: When the Philistines heard that David had been anointed king over Israel, all the Philistines came up in search of him. David heard about it and went down to the stronghold.
v18: The Philistines came and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim.
</p>

<p>
v19: David asked the LORD, “Should I go up against the Philistines? Will you give them into my hand?” The LORD answered, “Go up. I will certainly give the Philistines into your hand.”
</p>

<p>
v20: David went to Baal-perazim and defeated them there. He said, “The LORD has broken through my enemies before me like a burst of water.” That is why he named the place Baal-perazim.
v21: The Philistines abandoned their idols there, and David and his men carried them away.
</p>

<p>
v22: Once again the Philistines came up and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim.
</p>

<p>
v23: David asked the LORD, and he answered, “Do not go straight up. Circle around behind them and approach them opposite the balsam trees.
v24: When you hear the sound of marching in the treetops, act decisively. Then the LORD will have gone out ahead of you to strike the Philistine camp.”
</p>

<p>
v25: David did as the LORD commanded him and struck down the Philistines from Geba all the way to Gezer.
</p>

## Notes

v04–05: The stated forty-year reign and the separate periods of seven years six months and thirty-three years are retained without adjusting their totals.

v06: The taunt’s Hebrew can also be understood as saying David cannot enter unless he removes the blind and the lame.

v08: This sentence is difficult. “Water shaft” is an uncertain identification, and its relation to the attack is compressed. The read form describes the lame and blind as hated by David. The concluding “house” is not explicitly called the temple. No reward clause is imported from another account. The disability language and exclusionary saying remain visible.

v09: The Millo is a named feature of the city’s defenses or built-up structure; its precise form is not supplied here.

v21: The Hebrew says the images were carried away. It does not say here that they were burned.

v23–24: The trees are often identified as balsam trees, but the botanical identification is uncertain. The sound is described as marching in their tops.

v25: The pinned Hebrew names Geba, retained here rather than replaced with Gibeon.

## Vocabulary

v01: Bone and flesh
A kinship expression used in the tribes’ appeal to David.

v02: Shepherd
A governing image that connects care for a flock with leading the people.

v20: Baal-perazim
The name echoes the breaking-through language of David’s comparison with a burst of water.
