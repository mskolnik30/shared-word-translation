## The Woman from Tekoa

<p>
v01: Joab son of Zeruiah knew that the king’s heart was turned toward Absalom.
v02: Joab sent to Tekoa for a wise woman and told her, “Pretend to be in mourning. Put on mourning clothes and do not anoint yourself with oil. Act like a woman who has mourned for the dead a long time.
v03: Go to the king and speak to him as I tell you.” Then Joab put the words in her mouth.
</p>

<p>
v04: The woman from Tekoa spoke to the king. She fell facedown to the ground, bowed, and said, “Help me, O king!”
</p>

<p>
v05: The king asked her, “What is wrong?” She answered, “I am a widow. My husband is dead.
v06: Your servant had two sons. They fought in the field, and there was no one to separate them. One struck the other and killed him.
</p>

<p>
v07: Now the whole clan has risen against your servant. They say, ‘Hand over the one who struck down his brother. We will put him to death for his brother’s life, which he took, and destroy the heir as well.’ They would quench the last ember I have left, leaving my husband no name or survivor on the face of the earth.”
</p>

<p>
v08: The king told the woman, “Go home. I will give orders concerning you.”
v09: The woman from Tekoa said, “My lord the king, let the guilt rest on me and my father’s house. May the king and his throne be innocent.”
v10: The king answered, “If anyone speaks against you, bring him to me. He will not trouble you again.”
</p>

<p>
v11: She said, “Please invoke the LORD your God, so the avenger of blood will not add to the destruction and they will not kill my son.” He answered, “As the LORD lives, not one hair of your son’s head will fall to the ground.”
</p>

<p>
v12: Then the woman said, “Please let your servant say one more thing to my lord the king.” “Speak,” he answered.
v13: The woman said, “Why, then, have you planned such a thing against God’s people? In saying what he has said, the king stands guilty, since he does not bring back the one he has banished.
v14: We must all die. We are like water spilled on the ground that cannot be gathered up again. Yet God does not take away a life; he devises ways so that someone banished need not remain banished from him.
</p>

<p>
v15: I have come to say this to my lord the king because the people have frightened me. Your servant thought, ‘I will speak to the king. Perhaps he will do what his servant asks.
v16: The king will listen and rescue his servant from the hand of the man who would destroy both me and my son from God’s inheritance.’
v17: Your servant also thought, ‘May the word of my lord the king bring rest, for my lord the king is like an angel of God in hearing good and evil.’ May the LORD your God be with you.”
</p>

## Joab’s Part Revealed

<p>
v18: The king answered the woman, “Do not hide anything I am about to ask you.” She said, “Let my lord the king speak.”
</p>

<p>
v19: The king asked, “Is Joab’s hand in all this with you?” The woman answered, “As surely as you live, my lord the king, no one can turn right or left from anything my lord the king says. Yes, your servant Joab commanded me; he put all these words in your servant’s mouth.
v20: Your servant Joab did this to change the course of the matter. But my lord is wise, with the wisdom of an angel of God, and knows everything in the land.”
</p>

## Absalom Returns

<p>
v21: The king told Joab, “Very well. I grant this request. Go and bring back the young man Absalom.”
v22: Joab fell facedown to the ground, bowed, and blessed the king. He said, “Today your servant knows I have found favor in your eyes, my lord the king, because the king has done what your servant asked.”
</p>

<p>
v23: Then Joab went to Geshur and brought Absalom to Jerusalem.
v24: But the king said, “Let him go to his own house. He must not see my face.” So Absalom went to his own house and did not see the king’s face.
</p>

<p>
v25: No man in all Israel was praised for his beauty as much as Absalom. From the sole of his foot to the crown of his head, he had no blemish.
v26: Whenever he cut his hair—he cut it each year because it grew heavy on him—he weighed it: two hundred shekels by the royal weight.
v27: Absalom had three sons and one daughter named Tamar. She was a beautiful woman.
</p>

<p>
v28: Absalom lived in Jerusalem for two full years without seeing the king’s face.
</p>

<p>
v29: He sent for Joab to send him to the king, but Joab refused to come. He sent a second time, and Joab still refused.
v30: Then Absalom told his servants, “Look, Joab’s field is next to mine, and he has barley there. Go and set it on fire.” So Absalom’s servants set the field on fire.
</p>

<p>
v31: Joab got up and came to Absalom’s house. “Why have your servants burned my field?” he asked.
v32: Absalom answered Joab, “I sent word to you: ‘Come here, so I can send you to the king to ask, “Why did I come from Geshur? I would be better off still there.”’ Now let me see the king’s face. If I am guilty, let him put me to death.”
</p>

<p>
v33: Joab went to the king and told him. The king summoned Absalom, who came and bowed facedown to the ground before him. Then the king kissed Absalom.
</p>

## Notes

v06–07: The woman’s story is a performance arranged by Joab. Its threatened destruction of the remaining heir is part of her argument.

v14: The statement about God not taking life is difficult, and belongs to the woman’s persuasive speech. The draft does not expand it into a universal claim that God never judges or that every banishment ends.

v24–28: Return to Jerusalem does not yet mean access to the king. The repeated prohibition on seeing his face remains.

v26–27: The source states two hundred shekels of hair by the royal weight, and three sons and one daughter. It does not explain the later statement “I have no son” in 18:18.

## Vocabulary

v07: Last ember
An image for the surviving son through whom the family’s name could continue.

v11: Avenger of blood
A relative charged with pursuing a killing; the story invokes this role in the threat to the remaining son.

v19: Hand in this
An image of involvement or agency, here identifying Joab’s part.
