## Ziba’s Report

<p>
v01: David had gone a little beyond the summit when Ziba, Mephibosheth’s servant, met him. He had a pair of saddled donkeys loaded with two hundred loaves, a hundred raisin cakes, a hundred portions of summer fruit, and a skin of wine.
v02: The king asked Ziba, “What are these for?” Ziba answered, “The donkeys are for the king’s household to ride. The bread and summer fruit are for the young men to eat, and the wine is for those who grow faint in the wilderness.”
</p>

<p>
v03: The king asked, “Where is your master’s grandson?” Ziba answered, “He is staying in Jerusalem. He said, ‘Today the house of Israel will restore my grandfather’s kingdom to me.’”
</p>

<p>
v04: The king told Ziba, “Everything belonging to Mephibosheth is now yours.” Ziba said, “I bow before you. May I find favor in your eyes, my lord the king.”
</p>

## Shimei Curses David

<p>
v05: As King David came to Bahurim, a man from Saul’s clan came out. His name was Shimei son of Gera, and he kept cursing as he came.
v06: He threw stones at David and all King David’s servants, though all the people and all the warriors were on David’s right and left.
</p>

<p>
v07: As he cursed, Shimei shouted, “Get out! Get out, you man of blood, you worthless man!
v08: The LORD has brought back on you all the blood of Saul’s house, in whose place you became king. The LORD has put the kingdom into your son Absalom’s hand. Now disaster has caught you, because you are a man of blood.”
</p>

<p>
v09: Abishai son of Zeruiah said to the king, “Why should this dead dog curse my lord the king? Let me go over and take off his head.”
</p>

<p>
v10: The king answered, “What do you and I have to do with this, sons of Zeruiah? Let him curse. If the LORD has told him, ‘Curse David,’ who can ask, ‘Why have you done this?’”
</p>

<p>
v11: David told Abishai and all his servants, “Look, my own son, who came from my body, is seeking my life. How much more this Benjaminite! Leave him alone and let him curse, for the LORD has told him to.
v12: Perhaps the LORD will see my tears and repay me with good for the curse on me today.”
</p>

<p>
v13: David and his men continued along the road. Shimei walked along the hillside opposite him, cursing, throwing stones toward him, and flinging dust.
v14: The king and all the people with him arrived exhausted, and he refreshed himself there.
</p>

## Absalom in Jerusalem

<p>
v15: Meanwhile, Absalom and all the men of Israel entered Jerusalem, with Ahithophel beside him.
</p>

<p>
v16: When Hushai the Archite, David’s friend, came to Absalom, he said, “Long live the king! Long live the king!”
v17: Absalom asked Hushai, “Is this your steadfast love for your friend? Why didn’t you go with your friend?”
</p>

<p>
v18: Hushai answered Absalom, “No. I belong to the one whom the LORD, these people, and all the men of Israel have chosen. I will stay with him.
v19: Besides, whom should I serve? Shouldn’t I serve before his son? Just as I served before your father, so I will serve before you.”
</p>

<p>
v20: Absalom said to Ahithophel, “Give us your counsel. What should we do?”
</p>

<p>
v21: Ahithophel told Absalom, “Sleep with your father’s concubines, whom he left to look after the house. All Israel will hear that you have become a stench to your father, and the hands of all who are with you will grow strong.”
</p>

<p>
v22: They pitched a tent for Absalom on the roof, and he slept with his father’s concubines in the sight of all Israel.
v23: In those days, Ahithophel’s counsel was treated like an answer received by someone consulting God. That was how all his counsel was regarded by both David and Absalom.
</p>

## Notes

v03–04: Ziba reports Mephibosheth’s alleged words. In chapter 19 Mephibosheth gives a conflicting account. The translation does not settle that dispute by changing either speaker’s claim.

v10–12: David considers and then asserts a divine role in Shimei’s cursing; these are David’s words. Verse 12’s read form is “my eye,” interpreted here as tears. The written form is “my guilt”; the expression remains uncertain.

v18–19: Hushai initially leaves the chosen king unnamed. His words retain their possible double meaning.

v21–22: Absalom’s action uses the women left behind as a public claim against his father. Their consent is not reported. Their later confinement is narrated in 20:3.

## Vocabulary

v07: Man of blood
Shimei’s accusation of bloodshed or bloodguilt.

v17: Steadfast love
Here the word concerns loyalty between friends.

v21: Strengthened hands
An image of increased resolve among Absalom’s followers.
