## David’s Army Goes Out

<p>
v01: David mustered the people with him and appointed commanders over thousands and over hundreds.
v02: He sent the army out in thirds: one under Joab, one under Joab’s brother Abishai son of Zeruiah, and one under Ittai the Gittite. The king told the people, “I will certainly go out with you too.”
</p>

<p>
v03: But the people said, “You must not go. If we flee, they will pay no attention to us. Even if half of us die, they will pay no attention to us. As things stand, there are ten thousand like us. It is better for you to help us from the city.”
</p>

<p>
v04: The king answered, “I will do what seems best to you.” He stood beside the gate as all the people marched out by hundreds and thousands.
</p>

<p>
v05: The king commanded Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, “For my sake, deal gently with the young man Absalom.” All the people heard the king give the commanders this order about Absalom.
</p>

## Absalom’s Death

<p>
v06: The army went into the field to meet Israel, and the battle took place in the forest of Ephraim.
v07: There Israel’s army was defeated by David’s servants. The slaughter that day was great: twenty thousand men.
v08: The battle spread across the whole region. That day the forest devoured more people than the sword did.
</p>

<p>
v09: Absalom encountered David’s servants while riding his mule. The mule went under the tangled branches of a great terebinth, and his head caught in the tree. He was left hanging between heaven and earth while the mule beneath him went on.
</p>

<p>
v10: A man saw it and told Joab, “I saw Absalom hanging in a terebinth!”
</p>

<p>
v11: Joab said to the man who told him, “You saw him? Why didn’t you strike him to the ground there? I would have given you ten pieces of silver and a belt.”
</p>

<p>
v12: The man answered Joab, “Even if a thousand pieces of silver were weighed into my hands, I would not raise my hand against the king’s son. We heard the king command you, Abishai, and Ittai, ‘Take care that no one harms the young man Absalom.’
v13: If I had acted treacherously and risked my own life, nothing would have been hidden from the king, and you would have stood aside.”
</p>

<p>
v14: Joab said, “I will not waste time here with you.” He took three shafts in his hand and drove them into Absalom’s heart while he was still alive in the heart of the terebinth.
v15: Then ten young men who carried Joab’s weapons surrounded Absalom, struck him, and killed him.
</p>

<p>
v16: Joab blew the ram’s horn, and the army stopped pursuing Israel, for Joab held the people back.
v17: They took Absalom and threw him into a great pit in the forest, then raised a huge pile of stones over him. All Israel fled, each man to his own tent.
</p>

<p>
v18: During his lifetime, Absalom had set up a pillar for himself in the King’s Valley. He said, “I have no son to keep my name in remembrance.” He named the pillar after himself, and to this day it is called Absalom’s Monument.
</p>

## The Runners Bring News

<p>
v19: Ahimaaz son of Zadok said, “Please let me run and bring the king the good news that the LORD has vindicated him against his enemies.”
v20: Joab told him, “You are not the man to carry news today. You may carry news another day, but today you must not, because the king’s son is dead.”
</p>

<p>
v21: Then Joab told the Cushite, “Go and tell the king what you have seen.” The Cushite bowed to Joab and ran.
</p>

<p>
v22: Ahimaaz son of Zadok again said to Joab, “Whatever happens, please let me run after the Cushite too.” Joab asked, “Why do you want to run, my son? This news will bring you no reward.”
v23: “Whatever happens, I will run.” Joab said, “Run.” Ahimaaz ran by the plain road and overtook the Cushite.
</p>

<p>
v24: David was sitting between the two gates. The watchman went up to the roof of the gate by the wall, looked out, and saw a man running alone.
v25: The watchman called down and told the king. The king said, “If he is alone, he has news to tell.” The runner kept coming closer.
</p>

<p>
v26: The watchman saw another man running and called to the gatekeeper, “Another man is running alone!” The king said, “He is bringing news too.”
v27: The watchman said, “The first man runs like Ahimaaz son of Zadok.” The king answered, “He is a good man. He must be coming with good news.”
</p>

<p>
v28: Ahimaaz called to the king, “All is well!” He bowed facedown to the ground before the king and said, “Blessed be the LORD your God! He has handed over the men who raised their hand against my lord the king.”
v29: The king asked, “Is the young man Absalom well?” Ahimaaz answered, “I saw a great commotion when Joab sent the king’s servant and me, your servant, but I did not know what it was.”
</p>

<p>
v30: The king said, “Step aside and stand here.” So he stepped aside and stood there.
</p>

<p>
v31: Then the Cushite arrived and said, “Good news for my lord the king! Today the LORD has vindicated you against all who rose against you.”
v32: The king asked the Cushite, “Is the young man Absalom well?” The Cushite answered, “May my lord the king’s enemies, and all who rise against you to do harm, become like that young man.”
</p>

## David’s Lament

<p>
v33: The king trembled. He went up to the room above the gate and wept. As he went, he cried, “My son Absalom! My son, my son Absalom! If only I had died instead of you! Absalom, my son, my son!”
</p>

## Notes

v03: The pinned text reads “now,” followed by “like us, ten thousand.” Its syntax is uncertain. The familiar “you are worth ten thousand of us” understands or changes the opening as “you.” The draft keeps the difficult reading and flags it for editorial review.

v09: The text says Absalom’s head caught in the tree, not specifically his hair.

v12–14: The soldier’s report of the protective order is compressed. Verse 13 follows the read “my life,” not the written “his life.” Verse 14 repeats “heart” for Absalom and the tree.

v18: The statement “I have no son” is retained alongside the three sons reported in 14:27. The text does not explain the difference.

v22: The final phrase may mean news that brings no reward, or news without a useful outcome.

v33: This verse is numbered 19:1 in the pinned Hebrew source. The following public chapter 19 is consequently offset by one.

## Vocabulary

v18: Monument
The Hebrew word is “hand,” used here for a memorial that carries Absalom’s name.

v21: Cushite
A man identified by origin in Cush; no personal name is given.

v28–32: Well
The word also means peace. The victory greetings lead to David’s repeated question about Absalom’s well-being.
