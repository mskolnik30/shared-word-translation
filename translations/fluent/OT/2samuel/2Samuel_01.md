## News of Saul’s death

<p>
v01: After Saul’s death, David returned from striking the Amalekites and stayed in Ziklag for two days.
v02: On the third day a man arrived from Saul’s camp, his clothes torn and dirt on his head. When he reached David, he fell to the ground and bowed.
</p>

<p>
v03: David asked, “Where have you come from?” He answered, “I escaped from Israel’s camp.”
v04: “What happened?” David asked. “Please tell me.” He said, “The troops fled from the battle. Many fell and died. Saul and his son Jonathan are dead too.”
</p>

<p>
v05: David asked the young man bringing the news, “How do you know that Saul and his son Jonathan are dead?”
v06: The young man answered, “I happened to be on Mount Gilboa. Saul was leaning on his spear, and the chariots and horsemen were closing in on him.
</p>

<p>
v07: He turned, saw me, and called. I answered, ‘Here I am.’
v08: He asked, ‘Who are you?’ I told him, ‘I am an Amalekite.’
</p>

<p>
v09: Then he said, ‘Stand over me and kill me. Agony has gripped me, yet my life is still in me.’
v10: So I stood over him and killed him, knowing he would not survive his fall. I took the crown from his head and the armlet from his arm and brought them here to my lord.”
</p>

## David mourns

<p>
v11: David seized his clothes and tore them. All the men with him did the same.
v12: They mourned, wept, and fasted until evening for Saul, for his son Jonathan, for the LORD’s people, and for the house of Israel, because they had fallen by the sword.
</p>

<p>
v13: David asked the young messenger, “Where are you from?” He answered, “I am the son of an Amalekite resident foreigner.”
v14: David said, “How were you not afraid to raise your hand and destroy the LORD’s anointed?”
</p>

<p>
v15: He called one of the young men and said, “Go and strike him down.” The man struck him, and he died.
v16: David said to him, “Your blood is on your own head. Your own mouth testified against you when you said, ‘I killed the LORD’s anointed.’”
</p>

## The lament of the bow

<p>
v17: David lamented Saul and his son Jonathan with this lament,
v18: and ordered that the people of Judah be taught “The Bow.” It is written in the Book of Jashar:
</p>

<p>
v19: “Your splendor, Israel, lies slain on your heights.
How the mighty have fallen!
v20: Do not tell it in Gath;
do not announce it in Ashkelon’s streets,
lest the daughters of the Philistines rejoice,
lest the daughters of the uncircumcised celebrate.
v21: Mountains of Gilboa,
let no dew or rain fall on you,
let there be no fields yielding offerings!
There the warriors’ shield was defiled,
Saul’s shield, no longer anointed with oil.
v22: From the blood of the slain,
from the fat of warriors,
Jonathan’s bow did not turn back;
Saul’s sword did not return empty.
v23: Saul and Jonathan, loved and dear in life,
were not parted in death.
They were swifter than eagles,
stronger than lions.
v24: Daughters of Israel, weep over Saul,
who clothed you in scarlet and finery,
who set ornaments of gold on your garments.
v25: How the mighty have fallen
in the midst of battle!
Jonathan lies slain on your heights.
v26: I grieve for you, my brother Jonathan.
You were deeply dear to me.
Your love for me was wondrous,
beyond the love of women.
v27: How the mighty have fallen,
and the weapons of war have perished!”
</p>

## Notes

v06–10: The Amalekite says he killed Saul. In 1 Samuel 31 Saul falls on his sword and is already dead when his armor-bearer sees him. The two accounts are retained without an added verdict about how they fit.

v09: The word rendered “agony” is rare and its precise sense is uncertain.

v18: “The Bow” is understood as the lament’s title. The wording has also been understood as instruction in archery. The Book of Jashar means the Book of the Upright.

v19: The word rendered “splendor” can also mean a gazelle. The lament’s address and imagery allow more than one resonance.

v21: “Fields yielding offerings” is difficult, literally fields of contributions or offerings. The reference to oil can be understood with the shield, as here, or with Saul.

v23: The poem says Saul and Jonathan were not separated in death; it does not remove the conflicts narrated during their lives.

v26: David calls Jonathan’s love more wonderful than the love of women. The comparison is retained in full without assigning a modern identity category.

## Vocabulary

v16: Blood on your head
An expression assigning responsibility for the death being imposed.

v19, 25, 27: How the mighty have fallen
The recurring lament holds the poem together; it is not varied merely for stylistic difference.
