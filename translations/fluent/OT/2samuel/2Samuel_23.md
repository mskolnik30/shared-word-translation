## David’s Last Words

<p>
v01: These are David’s last words:
“The declaration of David son of Jesse,
the declaration of the man raised on high,
the anointed of the God of Jacob,
the sweet singer of Israel’s songs:
</p>

<p>
v02: The Spirit of the LORD has spoken through me;
his word is on my tongue.
</p>

<p>
v03: The God of Israel has spoken;
the Rock of Israel has said to me:
‘One who rules people justly,
who rules in the fear of God,
v04: is like morning light when the sun rises,
a morning without clouds,
like brightness after rain
that brings grass from the earth.’
</p>

<p>
v05: Is not my house like this with God?
He has made an everlasting covenant with me,
ordered in every part and secure.
Will he not make all my salvation
and all my desire spring up?
</p>

<p>
v06: But the worthless are all like thorns thrown aside;
they cannot be taken with the hand.
v07: Whoever touches them must be armed
with iron and a spear shaft.
They will be completely burned with fire
where they lie.”
</p>

## David’s Warriors

<p>
v08: These are the names of David’s warriors: Josheb-basshebeth the Tahchemonite, chief of the Three. He was Adino the Eznite, who struck down eight hundred at one time.
</p>

<p>
v09: After him was Eleazar son of Dodo, son of Ahohi, one of the three warriors with David when they taunted the Philistines gathered there for battle. The men of Israel withdrew,
v10: but he stood his ground and struck the Philistines until his hand grew weary and clung to the sword. The LORD brought about a great victory that day. The people returned after him only to strip the dead.
</p>

<p>
v11: After him was Shammah son of Agee the Hararite. The Philistines gathered in a band where there was a field full of lentils, and the people fled before them.
v12: But he stood in the middle of the field, defended it, and struck down the Philistines. The LORD brought about a great victory.
</p>

## Water from Bethlehem

<p>
v13: At harvest time, three of the thirty chiefs went down to David at the cave of Adullam, while a Philistine band was camped in the Valley of Rephaim.
v14: David was in the stronghold then, and a Philistine garrison was in Bethlehem.
</p>

<p>
v15: David voiced a longing: “If only someone would give me water from the well beside Bethlehem’s gate!”
</p>

<p>
v16: The three warriors broke through the Philistine camp, drew water from the well beside Bethlehem’s gate, and carried it back to David. But he refused to drink it and poured it out to the LORD.
v17: He said, “LORD, far be it from me to do this! Is this not the blood of the men who went at the risk of their lives?” He would not drink it. These were the deeds of the three warriors.
</p>

## Abishai and Benaiah

<p>
v18: Abishai, Joab’s brother and a son of Zeruiah, was chief of the Three. He raised his spear against three hundred and killed them, winning a name among the Three.
v19: He was the most honored of the Three and became their commander, but he did not attain the rank of the Three.
</p>

<p>
v20: Benaiah son of Jehoiada was the son of a valiant man from Kabzeel, a man of many deeds. He struck down Moab’s two ariels. He also went down into a pit on a snowy day and killed a lion.
v21: He also struck down an imposing Egyptian. The Egyptian had a spear in his hand, but Benaiah went against him with a staff, seized the spear from his hand, and killed him with his own spear.
</p>

<p>
v22: Benaiah son of Jehoiada did these things and won a name among the three warriors.
v23: He was more honored than the Thirty, though he did not attain the rank of the Three. David put him over his personal guard.
</p>

## Notes

v01: “Sweet singer of Israel’s songs” renders a compressed description that may instead mean the favored one of Israel’s songs. “Last words” need not identify these as his final spoken syllables.

v05: The draft takes the two negative clauses as questions expecting agreement. They can also be read as statements beginning “my house is not so” and “he does not make it spring up.” The covenant language between them is explicit.

v08: The source’s names and syntax are difficult. Josheb-basshebeth, Adino the Eznite, and the eight hundred slain are retained rather than reconstructed as a spear-wielding clause.

v18–19: The pinned text repeatedly says Three here, even while distinguishing Abishai from the Three. The draft leaves that tension visible instead of silently substituting Thirty.

v20: The Hebrew has “two ariels of Moab.” It does not explicitly include “sons.” The referents are uncertain.

v32–39: Several roster relationships are compressed, especially the sons of Jashen and the Maacathite entry. The source’s names and final total of thirty-seven are retained without inventing missing persons or deleting entries to force a count.

## Vocabulary

v01: Declaration
A solemn utterance, also used to introduce prophetic speech.

v05: Everlasting covenant
The enduring commitment that David names in relation to his house.

v20: Ariels
A difficult term that can suggest lionlike champions or be associated with a name or cultic object; the verse does not settle the identification.

v24: The Thirty
A named group of warriors; the title and the final stated total are both retained.
