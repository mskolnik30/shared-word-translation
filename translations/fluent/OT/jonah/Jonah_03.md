## Jonah Sent Again to Nineveh
<p>
v01: The word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time:
v02: “Get up, go to Nineveh, that great city, and proclaim to it the message I tell you.”
v03: So Jonah got up and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD. Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, a three-day journey across.
</p>

<p>
v04: Jonah began to go into the city, a day's journey. He cried out:
“Forty more days,
and Nineveh will be overthrown.”
</p>

## Nineveh Repents
<p>
v05: The people of Nineveh believed God. They proclaimed a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least.
v06: When the news reached the king of Nineveh, he rose from his throne, took off his robe, covered himself with sackcloth, and sat in ashes.
v07: Then he issued a proclamation throughout Nineveh:
</p>

<p>
“By decree of the king and his nobles:
Neither people nor animals,
herds nor flocks,
are to taste anything.
They must not eat
or drink water.
v08: Let both people and animals be covered with sackcloth,
and let them cry out urgently to God.
Let each person turn from his evil way
and from the violence in his hands.
v09: Who knows?
God may turn and relent;
he may turn from his burning anger,
so that we do not perish.”
</p>

## God Relents from Judgment
<p>
v10: God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way. Then God relented from the disaster he had said he would bring upon them, and he did not do it.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Jonah receives the prophetic commission a second time.

v02: The command repeats the central language of chapter 1, but Jonah's response now changes.

v03: The Hebrew describes Nineveh as a city “great to God,” an expression commonly understood as an intensifier. The exact force of the phrase is debated; the main text retains TSW's “exceedingly great.”

v04: Jonah's message is brief and entirely focused on impending overthrow.

v04: The Hebrew verb translated “overthrown” can describe destruction or overturning; the narrative goes on to show a city radically turned around without being destroyed.

v05: The people of Nineveh respond immediately with belief, fasting, and public mourning.

v05: Their response begins before the king issues his decree.

v06–08: The king humbles himself publicly and extends the signs of mourning throughout the city.

v08: The decree calls not only for mourning but for turning away from evil conduct and violence.

v09: The king appeals to the possibility of divine mercy without presuming upon it.

v10: The repeated language of “turning” links Nineveh's change of direction with God's relenting from announced disaster.

v10: The verse presents God as responding to what the Ninevites do; the translation does not add a philosophical explanation of how divine constancy and divine relenting relate.

## Vocabulary

v01: שֵׁנִית (šēnîṯ)
“Second time.” A repeated action or renewed opportunity.

v02: קָרָא (qārāʾ)
“To proclaim” or “cry out.” To announce a message publicly and forcefully.

v03: נִינְוֵה (Nînĕwēh)
“Nineveh.” The great Assyrian city at the center of Jonah's commission.

v04: הָפַךְ (hāpaḵ)
“To overthrow” or “overturn.” To destroy, reverse, or turn something over.

v05: אָמַן (ʾāman)
“To believe” or “trust.” To regard something as reliable and respond accordingly.

v05: צוֹם (ṣôm)
“Fast.” Abstaining from food as an expression of mourning or repentance.

v05: שַׂק (śaq)
“Sackcloth.” Coarse clothing associated with mourning and humility.

v06: אֵפֶר (ʾēp̄er)
“Ashes.” A symbol of mourning and humiliation.

v08: זָעַק (zāʿaq)
“To cry out.” To call urgently for help or mercy.

v08: דֶּרֶךְ רָעָה (dereḵ rāʿâ)
“Evil way.” A pattern of harmful or wrongful conduct.

v08: חָמָס (ḥāmās)
“Violence.” Wrongful force, oppression, or brutality.

v09: נָחַם (nāḥam)
“To relent.” To withhold announced judgment in response to changed circumstances.

v10: שׁוּב (šûḇ)
“To turn.” To return or change direction; an important repeated word in this chapter.

v10: רָעָה (rāʿâ)
“Evil,” “harm,” or “disaster,” depending on context. Jonah repeatedly uses this word family for human evil, threatened disaster, Jonah's displeasure, and his distress.
