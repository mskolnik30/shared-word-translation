## The second call

<p>
v01: The word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time:
v02: “Get up and go to Nineveh, the great city, and proclaim to it the message I give you.”
v03: Jonah got up and went to Nineveh as the LORD had said. Nineveh was a city great before God, a three-day journey.
v04: Jonah began to enter the city, going a day’s journey. He proclaimed, “Forty more days, and Nineveh will be overturned!”
</p>

## Nineveh turns

<p>
v05: The people of Nineveh believed God. They proclaimed a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest to the least.
v06: When the news reached Nineveh’s king, he rose from his throne, removed his robe, covered himself with sackcloth, and sat in ashes.
v07: He had this proclamation issued in Nineveh: “By decree of the king and his nobles: No person or animal, herd or flock, may taste anything. They must not graze or drink water.
v08: Let people and animals be covered with sackcloth. Let them call urgently to God. Let everyone turn from their evil way and from the violence in their hands.
v09: Who knows? God may turn and relent, turning from his burning anger so that we do not perish.”
v10: God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way. God relented from the disaster he had said he would bring on them, and he did not bring it.
</p>

## Notes

v03-04: “Great before God” preserves the Hebrew phrase, also understood as “exceedingly great.” The three-day journey is not explained as a circumference or a precise city diameter. “Overturned” can describe destruction or a reversal of condition.

v09-10: The king says “Who knows?” God’s response is then connected with the people’s deeds and turning from violence. Raah appears as both evil conduct and threatened disaster.

## Vocabulary

v05: **Sackcloth**: Coarse cloth worn in mourning or humbling oneself.

v07-08: **People and animals**: Both are included in the decree’s fasting and sackcloth; the unusual animal participation remains explicit.
