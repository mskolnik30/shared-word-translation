## Jonah Flees from the LORD
<p>
v01: The word of the LORD came to Jonah son of Amittai:
v02: “Get up, go to Nineveh, that great city, and cry out against it, because its evil has come up before me.”
v03: But Jonah got up to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD. He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish. He paid the fare and went down into the ship to go with them to Tarshish, away from the presence of the LORD.
</p>

## The Great Storm
<p>
v04: But the LORD hurled a great wind onto the sea, and a great storm arose on the sea, so that the ship was about to break apart.
v05: The sailors were afraid, and each cried out to his god. They threw the cargo into the sea to lighten the ship. But Jonah had gone down into the inner part of the ship, where he lay down and fell into a deep sleep.
v06: The captain came to him and said, “How can you be sleeping? Get up! Call on your god. Perhaps your god will take notice of us so that we do not perish.”
</p>

<p>
v07: Then the sailors said to one another, “Come, let us cast lots so we can learn whose fault it is that this trouble has come upon us.” They cast lots, and the lot fell on Jonah.
v08: They said to him, “Tell us! Who is responsible for this trouble coming upon us? What is your occupation? Where do you come from? What is your country? What people are you from?”
v09: Jonah said to them, “I am a Hebrew, and I fear the LORD, the God of heaven, who made the sea and the dry land.”
</p>

<p>
v10: Then the men became terrified and said to him, “What have you done?” They knew that he was fleeing from the presence of the LORD because he had told them.
v11: They asked him, “What should we do to you so that the sea will calm down for us?” For the sea was becoming more and more stormy.
v12: He said to them, “Pick me up and throw me into the sea, and the sea will calm down for you. I know that this great storm has come upon you because of me.”
</p>

<p>
v13: But the men rowed hard to get back to dry land, and they could not, because the sea was growing more and more stormy against them.
v14: So they cried out to the LORD: “Please, LORD, do not let us perish because of this man’s life, and do not charge us with innocent blood. For you, LORD, have done as you pleased.”
v15: Then they picked up Jonah and threw him into the sea, and the sea stopped raging.
v16: The men feared the LORD greatly. They offered a sacrifice to the LORD and made vows.
</p>

## The Great Fish
<p>
v17: The LORD appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah, and Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.
</p>

## Notes

v01–02: The book begins with a prophetic commission directing Jonah to Nineveh, the capital of Assyria.

v03: Jonah's flight is not merely geographical. He attempts to escape the mission God has given him.

v03: The repeated “went down” language marks Jonah's downward movement away from the commission.

v04: The storm is presented as a direct act of the LORD rather than a random natural event.

v05: While the sailors pray, Jonah sleeps, creating a sharp contrast between their concern and Jonah's withdrawal.

v06: The captain unknowingly echoes the language of the LORD's original command: “Get up.”

v07: The lot identifies Jonah as the person connected with the storm; the narrative does not pause to explain the mechanism by which the lot reaches its result.

v09: Jonah's confession identifies the LORD as Creator of both sea and land, making his attempted escape especially ironic.

v10: The sailors recognize the seriousness of Jonah's flight immediately.

v12: Jonah accepts responsibility for the storm, though he still does not directly obey the original command to go to Nineveh.

v13–14: The sailors resist taking Jonah's life and appeal to the LORD concerning innocent blood.

v15–16: The sailors move from fear of the storm to fear of the LORD.

v16: The sailors worship the LORD before Nineveh ever hears Jonah's message.

v17: The great fish is presented as an appointed means of preserving Jonah from death.

v17: Public English versification places this sentence at Jonah 1:17; in the Masoretic/WLC numbering it is Jonah 2:1.

## Vocabulary

v01: דְּבַר־יְהוָה (dĕḇar-YHWH)
“Word of the LORD.” A prophetic message originating from God.

v02: קוּם (qûm)
“To arise” or “get up.” A command calling for immediate action.

v02: קָרָא (qārāʾ)
“To cry out” or “proclaim.” To announce a message publicly.

v03: תַּרְשִׁישׁ (Taršîš)
“Tarshish.” A distant location representing the opposite direction from Nineveh.

v04: טוּל (ṭûl)
“To hurl” or “throw.” Used of the LORD casting the wind upon the sea.

v05: יָרֵא (yārēʾ)
“To fear.” To be afraid or to stand in reverence.

v07: גּוֹרָל (gôrāl)
“Lot.” An object used to determine an outcome.

v09: עִבְרִי (ʿiḇrî)
“Hebrew.” Jonah's ethnic identification.

v09: אֱלֹהֵי הַשָּׁמַיִם (ʾĕlōhê haššāmayim)
“God of heaven.” A title emphasizing God's sovereignty.

v14: דָּם נָקִי (dām nāqî)
“Innocent blood.” The unjust taking of a life.

v16: נֶדֶר (neder)
“Vow.” A solemn promise made before God.

v17: מָנָה (mānâ)
“To appoint.” To assign or provide for a particular purpose.

v17: דָּג גָּדוֹל (dāg gādôl)
“Great fish.” The creature appointed to swallow Jonah.
