## Jonah’s Anger at the LORD’s Mercy
<p>
v01: But this greatly displeased Jonah, and he became angry.
v02: He prayed to the LORD:
“Please, LORD, isn't this what I said when I was still in my own land?
That is why I fled to Tarshish in the first place.
I knew that you are a gracious and compassionate God,
slow to anger and abundant in steadfast love,
and one who relents from disaster.
v03: So now, LORD,
please take my life from me,
because it is better for me to die than to live.”
</p>

<p>
v04: The LORD said,
“Is it right for you to be angry?”
</p>

## Jonah and the Plant
<p>
v05: Jonah went out of the city and sat down east of the city. He made a shelter for himself there and sat under it in the shade, waiting to see what would happen to the city.
v06: The LORD God appointed a plant and made it grow over Jonah to provide shade for his head and relieve his discomfort. Jonah was delighted with the plant.
</p>

<p>
v07: But at dawn the next day, God appointed a worm, and it attacked the plant so that it withered.
v08: When the sun rose, God appointed a scorching east wind. The sun beat down on Jonah's head until he grew faint. He asked to die and said,
“It is better for me to die than to live.”
</p>

<p>
v09: God said to Jonah,
“Is it right for you to be angry about the plant?”
Jonah said,
“It is right for me to be angry—
angry enough to die.”
</p>

## The LORD’s Final Question
<p>
v10: Then the LORD said,
“You had compassion on the plant,
though you did not labor for it
or make it grow.
It came up in a night
and perished in a night.
v11: Should I not have compassion on Nineveh,
that great city,
where there are more than one hundred twenty thousand people
who do not know their right hand from their left—
and many animals besides?”
</p>

## Notes

v01: The Hebrew uses the same broad word-family that elsewhere describes Nineveh's evil and threatened disaster. Jonah regards God's merciful response as profoundly displeasing.

v02: Jonah reveals that the LORD's mercy was the very reason he fled in chapter 1.

v02: Jonah's description of God echoes the covenant declaration associated with Exodus 34:6–7.

v03: Jonah values the judgment he expected more than his own continued life.

v04: The LORD responds with a question rather than an explanation.

v05: Jonah waits outside the city to see what will happen, leaving open the possibility that judgment might still come.

v06: The plant is another thing the LORD “appoints,” linking it with the great fish, the worm, and the east wind.

v06: The word translated “discomfort” belongs to the same broad Hebrew word-family used for evil, harm, and disaster elsewhere in Jonah. Natural English requires different renderings, but the Hebrew connection remains significant.

v07–08: God appoints the worm and the wind just as the LORD previously appointed the great fish and plant.

v09: Jonah openly defends his anger about the plant.

v10–11: The LORD's final question contrasts Jonah's compassion for a temporary plant with God's compassion for a great city.

v11: “Who do not know their right hand from their left” is deliberately retained. The expression may emphasize limited discernment, helplessness, or moral ignorance; the text itself does not specify one interpretation.

v11: The mention of animals broadens the final question beyond human beings alone.

v11: The book ends with the LORD's unanswered question, leaving the reader to respond.

## Vocabulary

v01: רָעַע (rāʿaʿ)
“To be displeased” or regard something as bad or wrong. The word belongs to Jonah's recurring evil/disaster word-family.

v01: חָרָה (ḥārâ)
“To burn with anger.” A common expression for intense anger.

v02: חַנּוּן (ḥannûn)
“Gracious.” Showing favor and generosity.

v02: רַחוּם (raḥûm)
“Compassionate” or “merciful.” Deeply moved toward another in need.

v02: אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם (ʾereḵ ʾappayim)
“Slow to anger.” Literally “long of nostrils,” an idiom for patient restraint.

v02: חֶסֶד (ḥeseḏ)
“Steadfast love.” Loyal, enduring love and covenant faithfulness.

v05: סֻכָּה (sukkâ)
“Shelter” or “booth.” A temporary structure providing shade.

v06: קִיקָיוֹן (qîqāyôn)
“Plant.” The species is uncertain; the narrative emphasizes its appointed function rather than botanical identification.

v06: מָנָה (mānâ)
“To appoint.” Repeated for the fish, plant, worm, and wind.

v07: תּוֹלַעַת (tôlaʿaṯ)
“Worm.” The creature appointed to attack the plant.

v08: קָדִים (qādîm)
“East wind.” A hot, dry wind associated with harsh conditions.

v10: חוּס (ḥûs)
“To have compassion” or “pity.” To spare or care about the fate of another.

v11: נִינְוֵה (Nînĕwēh)
“Nineveh.” The great Assyrian city toward which the LORD directs Jonah.

v11: בְּהֵמָה (bĕhēmâ)
“Animal” or “beast.” Domestic animals explicitly included in the LORD's final question.
