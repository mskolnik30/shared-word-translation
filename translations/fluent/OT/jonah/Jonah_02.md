## Jonah’s Prayer from the Fish
<p>
v01: Then Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish,
v02: saying,
“In my distress I called to the LORD,
and he answered me.
From the belly of Sheol I cried for help;
you heard my voice.
</p>

<p>
v03: You threw me into the deep,
into the heart of the seas;
the current surrounded me.
All your breakers and your waves
swept over me.
v04: Then I said,
‘I have been driven away from before your eyes;
yet I will look again
toward your holy temple.’
</p>

<p>
v05: The waters closed over me, threatening my life;
the deep surrounded me;
seaweed wrapped around my head
v06: at the roots of the mountains.
I went down to the land
whose bars closed behind me forever;
yet you brought my life up from the pit,
LORD my God.
</p>

<p>
v07: As my life was fading away,
I remembered the LORD,
and my prayer came to you,
into your holy temple.
v08: Those who cling to worthless idols
abandon their covenant loyalty.
v09: But I, with a voice of thanksgiving,
will sacrifice to you.
What I have vowed I will fulfill.
Salvation belongs to the LORD.”
</p>

## Jonah Delivered
<p>
v10: Then the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah onto dry land.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Jonah's prayer comes after his descent into the sea and confinement within the fish.

v02: The prayer describes Jonah's experience as a descent toward death while affirming that God heard him.

v02: “The belly of Sheol” is poetic language portraying extreme peril and nearness to death.

v03: Jonah addresses the LORD as the one who ultimately cast him into the deep, even though the sailors physically threw him into the sea.

v04: Though feeling cast away, Jonah expresses hope that he will again look toward God's holy temple.

v05–06: The imagery depicts complete helplessness, surrounded by the forces of death and chaos.

v06: Jonah's descent reaches its lowest point before the LORD reverses it and brings his life up from the pit.

v07: Remembering the LORD becomes the turning point of the prayer.

v08: The Hebrew phrase translated “abandon their covenant loyalty” is difficult. It can refer to abandoning one's own loyalty, abandoning a source or hope of steadfast love, or turning away from covenantal devotion. The main text preserves the current TSW interpretation while the ambiguity remains open.

v09: Thanksgiving, sacrifice, and fulfilled vows are presented as responses to deliverance.

v09: “Salvation belongs to the LORD” expresses a central theological claim of the prayer and book.

v10: The fish responds immediately when the LORD addresses it, in contrast to Jonah's earlier resistance.

v10: Public English Jonah 2:1–10 corresponds to Masoretic/WLC Jonah 2:2–11.

## Vocabulary

v01: תְּפִלָּה (tĕpillâ)
“Prayer.” A spoken appeal directed to God.

v02: צָרָה (ṣārâ)
“Distress” or “trouble.” Severe hardship or affliction.

v02: שְׁאוֹל (Šĕʾôl)
“Sheol.” The realm of the dead, often associated with death and destruction.

v03: מְצוּלָה (mĕṣûlâ)
“The deep.” The depths of the sea.

v03: מִשְׁבָּר (mišbār)
“Breaker” or “wave.” A powerful surge of water.

v04: הֵיכַל קָדְשֶׁךָ (hêḵal qodšeḵā)
“Your holy temple.” The place associated with God's presence and worship.

v06: שַׁחַת (šaḥaṯ)
“Pit.” A term associated with destruction, death, or the grave.

v07: זָכַר (zāḵar)
“To remember.” To call to mind and turn one's attention toward.

v08: הֶבֶל (heḇel)
“Vanity” or “worthlessness.” Something empty, futile, or powerless.

v08: חֶסֶד (ḥeseḏ)
“Steadfast love,” “covenant loyalty,” or loyal devotion. The syntax of this verse leaves the precise relationship debated.

v09: נֶדֶר (neder)
“Vow.” A solemn promise made before God.

v09: יְשׁוּעָה (yĕšûʿâ)
“Salvation” or “deliverance.” Rescue that comes from God.

v10: קִיא (qîʾ)
“To vomit.” To expel forcefully, describing the fish releasing Jonah onto the land.
