## At ease in the cities

<p>
v01: Woe to those at ease in Zion
and those secure on Samaria’s mountain—
the renowned men of the foremost nation,
to whom the house of Israel comes!
v02: Cross over to Calneh and look;
go from there to great Hamath,
then down to Gath of the Philistines.
Are they better than these kingdoms?
Is their territory larger than yours?
v03: You push the evil day far away
while bringing the reign of violence near.
v04: You lie on ivory beds
and sprawl on couches,
eating lambs from the flock
and calves from the stall.
v05: You improvise to the sound of the harp
and invent musical instruments for yourselves like David.
v06: You drink wine from bowls
and anoint yourselves with the finest oils,
but you do not grieve over Joseph’s ruin.
v07: So now you will lead the way into exile,
and the revelry of those who sprawl will end.
</p>

## The city handed over

<p>
v08: The Lord GOD has sworn by himself,
declares the LORD, the God of Armies:
“I detest Jacob’s pride and hate his citadels.
I will hand over the city and everything in it.”
v09: If ten men remain in one house, they too will die.
v10: When a man’s relative, the one who burns the body, carries out the bones, he will ask someone in the back of the house, “Is anyone else with you?” The answer will be, “No one.” Then he will say, “Hush! We must not mention the name of the LORD.”
v11: For look: the LORD gives the command,
and the great house is smashed to pieces,
the small house split apart.
v12: Do horses run on rock?
Does anyone plow it with oxen?
Yet you have turned justice into poison
and the fruit of righteousness into wormwood.
v13: You rejoice over Lo-debar
and say, “Did we not capture Karnaim by our own strength?”
v14: “Look, house of Israel, I am raising a nation against you,”
declares the LORD, the God of Armies.
“They will oppress you from Lebo-hamath
to the Wadi of the Arabah.”
</p>

## Notes

v02,10,12: The comparison’s pronouns in verse 2 are not fully explicit. The body-burning term in verse 10 is rare. Verse 12 follows the pinned “oxen”; a different division of the consonants yields plowing the sea.

v13: Lo-debar sounds like “nothing,” and Karnaim means “horns.” The place-names also expose the boast in their own strength.

## Vocabulary

v06: **Joseph**: A collective ancestral name for the people whose ruin is ignored by the revelers.

v14: **Wadi**: A valley or stream channel, which may carry seasonal water.
