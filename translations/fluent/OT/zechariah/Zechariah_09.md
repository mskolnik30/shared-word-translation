## A burden against the nations

<p>
v01: An oracle: the word of the LORD against the land of Hadrach,
with Damascus its resting place.
For the eye of humanity and of all Israel’s tribes
is toward the LORD,
v02: and against Hamath on its border,
Tyre and Sidon, though they are very wise.
v03: Tyre built herself a fortress
and piled up silver like dust,
gold like mud in the streets.
v04: Look, the Lord will dispossess her
and strike her strength into the sea;
she will be consumed by fire.
</p>

<p>
v05: Ashkelon will see and be afraid;
Gaza will writhe in anguish,
and Ekron too, because her hope has failed.
Gaza will lose her king,
and Ashkelon will no longer be inhabited.
v06: A mixed people will settle in Ashdod.
“I will cut off the pride of the Philistines.
v07: I will remove the blood from his mouth
and the abominations from between his teeth.
He too will remain for our God
and become like a clan leader in Judah,
and Ekron like the Jebusite.
v08: I will camp by my house as a guard
against those passing back and forth.
No oppressor will pass over them again,
for now I have seen with my own eyes.”
</p>

## Your king is coming

<p>
v09: Celebrate greatly, daughter Zion!
Shout, daughter Jerusalem!
Your king is coming to you,
righteous and saved,
humble and riding on a donkey,
on a colt, the foal of a donkey.
v10: “I will cut off the chariot from Ephraim
and the warhorse from Jerusalem;
the battle bow will be cut off.
He will speak peace to the nations.
His rule will reach from sea to sea,
from the River to the ends of the earth.
</p>

## Prisoners of hope

<p>
v11: As for you, because of the blood of your covenant
I have released your prisoners
from the waterless pit.
v12: Return to the stronghold, prisoners of hope!
Even today I declare:
I will restore double to you.
v13: For I have bent Judah as my bow
and fitted Ephraim to it.
I will rouse your sons, Zion,
against your sons, Greece,
and make you like a warrior’s sword.”
</p>

<p>
v14: The LORD will appear over them;
his arrow will flash like lightning.
The Lord GOD will sound the horn
and advance in the storms of the south.
v15: The LORD of Armies will shield them.
They will devour and trample slingstones;
they will drink and roar as with wine.
They will be full like a sacrificial bowl,
like the corners of an altar.
v16: The LORD their God will save them on that day,
the flock of his people.
They will be jewels in a crown,
gleaming over his land.
v17: How great its goodness! How great its beauty!
Grain will make the young men flourish,
and new wine the young women.
</p>

## Notes

v01: The clause about humanity’s eye and the LORD is difficult; the draft retains the eye image rather than replacing it with an inferred meaning.

v06: The term for the people settling in Ashdod can suggest mixed or illegitimate descent; its precise social sense here is uncertain.

v09: “Saved” preserves the Hebrew passive form; “victorious” is another contextual rendering. Matthew 21 quotes only part of this verse.

v15,17: The slingstone image and the final “its” are difficult. The draft does not supply a fully certain referent or reconstruct the scene.

## Vocabulary

v11: **Blood of your covenant**: Blood marking the covenant relationship invoked in the release of prisoners.

v13: **Bow**: Judah and Ephraim are pictured as weapons readied for action.
