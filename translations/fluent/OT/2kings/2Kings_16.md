## Ahaz seeks Assyrian help

<p>
v01: In the seventeenth year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Ahaz son of Jotham became king of Judah.
v02: Ahaz was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem for sixteen years. He did not do what was right in the eyes of the LORD his God, as his ancestor David had done.
v03: He followed the way of Israel’s kings. He even made his son pass through the fire, following the abhorrent practices of the nations the LORD had driven out before the Israelites.
v04: He sacrificed and burned offerings at the high places, on the hills, and under every leafy tree.
</p>

<p>
v05: Then Rezin king of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah, king of Israel, came up to wage war on Jerusalem. They besieged Ahaz but could not overcome him.
v06: At that time Rezin king of Aram recovered Elath for Aram and drove the Judeans out of Elath. Edomites came to Elath and have lived there to this day.
</p>

<p>
v07: Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria: “I am your servant and your son. Come up and save me from the king of Aram and the king of Israel, who are attacking me.”
v08: Ahaz took the silver and gold found in the LORD’s house and the palace treasuries and sent them as a bribe to the king of Assyria.
v09: The king of Assyria listened to him. He marched against Damascus, captured it, deported its people to Kir, and killed Rezin.
</p>

## The Damascus altar

<p>
v10: King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria. When he saw the altar in Damascus, he sent Uriah the priest a likeness of the altar and detailed plans for its construction.
v11: Uriah the priest built the altar according to everything King Ahaz had sent from Damascus. He finished it before King Ahaz returned.
</p>

<p>
v12: When the king returned from Damascus and saw the altar, he approached it and offered on it.
v13: He burned his burnt offering and grain offering, poured out his drink offering, and dashed the blood of his well-being offerings against the altar.
</p>

<p>
v14: He moved the bronze altar that stood before the LORD from the front of the house, from between the new altar and the LORD’s house, and put it on the north side of the new altar.
</p>

<p>
v15: King Ahaz commanded Uriah the priest, “On the large altar burn the morning burnt offering, the evening grain offering, the king’s burnt offering and grain offering, and the burnt offerings, grain offerings, and drink offerings of all the people of the land. Dash against it all the blood from burnt offerings and sacrifices. The bronze altar will be for me to inquire by.”
v16: Uriah the priest did everything King Ahaz commanded.
</p>

<p>
v17: King Ahaz cut away the side panels of the stands and removed the basins from them. He took the Sea down from the bronze oxen beneath it and set it on a stone pavement.
v18: Because of the king of Assyria, he altered the covered Sabbath passage built at the house and the king’s outer entrance to the LORD’s house.
</p>

<p>
v19: The rest of Ahaz’s acts are written in the annals of the kings of Judah.
v20: Ahaz rested with his ancestors and was buried with them in the City of David. His son Hezekiah became king in his place.
</p>

## Notes

v03: Passing a child through fire occurs in the context of prohibited sacrificial practices. The wording is retained without treating it as a harmless ceremony.

v06: The written text has Arameans, while the read tradition has Edomites. The earlier phrase still says Elath was recovered for Aram.

v15: “For me to inquire by” is one reading of the bronze altar’s purpose. The verb may also mean examining or considering what to do with it.

v18: The precise layout of the Sabbath passage and royal entrance, and the exact alteration, are uncertain. The stated connection to Assyria is retained.

## Vocabulary

v07: Servant and son
Language of submission to a powerful ruler, not a literal adoption statement.

v13: Well-being offering
A sacrifice associated with peace, thanksgiving, or shared fellowship.

v17: Sea
The large bronze basin made for the temple.
