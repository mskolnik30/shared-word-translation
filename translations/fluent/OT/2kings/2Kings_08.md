## The woman’s land restored

<p>
v01: Elisha had said to the woman whose son he had restored to life, “Get up and go away with your household. Stay wherever you can, because the LORD has called for a famine, and it will come on the land for seven years.”
v02: The woman did as the man of God said. She went away with her household and stayed in the land of the Philistines for seven years.
v03: At the end of seven years she returned from the land of the Philistines and went to appeal to the king for her house and field.
</p>

<p>
v04: The king was talking with Gehazi, the man of God’s servant. “Tell me all the great things Elisha has done,” he said.
v05: Just as Gehazi was telling the king how Elisha had restored the dead to life, the woman whose son he had restored came to appeal to the king for her house and field. Gehazi said, “My lord the king, this is the woman, and this is her son whom Elisha restored to life!”
v06: The king questioned the woman, and she told him her story. He assigned a court official to her and said, “Restore everything that belongs to her, including all the produce of the field from the day she left the land until now.”
</p>

## Elisha and Hazael

<p>
v07: Elisha went to Damascus. Ben-hadad king of Aram was ill, and someone told him, “The man of God has come here.”
v08: The king said to Hazael, “Take a gift with you and go meet the man of God. Ask the LORD through him, ‘Will I recover from this illness?’”
</p>

<p>
v09: Hazael went to meet him, taking a gift of every kind of fine thing Damascus had to offer—forty camel loads. He stood before Elisha and said, “Your son Ben-hadad king of Aram has sent me to ask, ‘Will I recover from this illness?’”
v10: Elisha said to him, “Go and tell him, ‘You will certainly recover.’ But the LORD has shown me that he will certainly die.”
</p>

<p>
v11: He held his gaze steady until it became uncomfortable. Then the man of God wept.
v12: Hazael asked, “Why is my lord weeping?” He answered, “Because I know the harm you will do to the Israelites. You will set their fortresses on fire, kill their young men with the sword, dash their little children to pieces, and rip open their pregnant women.”
</p>

<p>
v13: Hazael said, “But what is your servant, a mere dog, that he could do such a great thing?” Elisha answered, “The LORD has shown me that you will be king over Aram.”
v14: He left Elisha and returned to his master, who asked, “What did Elisha say to you?” Hazael answered, “He told me that you would certainly recover.”
</p>

<p>
v15: The next day Hazael took a coverlet, dipped it in water, and spread it over the king’s face, and he died. Hazael became king in his place.
</p>

## Jehoram and Ahaziah of Judah

<p>
v16: In the fifth year of Joram son of Ahab, king of Israel, while Jehoshaphat was king of Judah, Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat began to reign as king of Judah.
v17: He was thirty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem for eight years.
v18: He followed the way of Israel’s kings, as the house of Ahab had done, for Ahab’s daughter was his wife. He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
v19: Yet the LORD was unwilling to destroy Judah because of David his servant, since he had promised to give David a lamp for his sons always.
</p>

<p>
v20: In his days Edom rebelled against Judah’s rule and set up a king of its own.
v21: Joram crossed over to Zair with all his chariots. He rose at night and struck the Edomites surrounding him and the chariot commanders. But his people fled to their tents.
v22: Edom has remained in rebellion against Judah’s rule to this day. Libnah also rebelled at that time.
</p>

<p>
v23: The rest of Joram’s acts, and everything he did, are written in the annals of the kings of Judah.
v24: Joram rested with his ancestors and was buried with them in the City of David. His son Ahaziah became king in his place.
</p>

<p>
v25: In the twelfth year of Joram son of Ahab, king of Israel, Ahaziah son of Jehoram became king of Judah.
v26: Ahaziah was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem for one year. His mother’s name was Athaliah, daughter of Omri king of Israel.
v27: He followed the way of the house of Ahab and did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes, as the house of Ahab had done, for he was related by marriage to Ahab’s house.
</p>

<p>
v28: He went with Joram son of Ahab to fight Hazael king of Aram at Ramoth-gilead. The Arameans wounded Joram.
v29: King Joram returned to Jezreel to recover from the wounds the Arameans had inflicted on him at Ramah when he fought Hazael king of Aram. Ahaziah son of Jehoram, king of Judah, went down to Jezreel to see Joram son of Ahab because he was wounded.
</p>

## Notes

v10: The read text is “tell him”; the written text has “not,” allowing “You will certainly not recover.” The draft follows the read tradition and retains the ensuing declaration that the king will die.

v11: The Hebrew does not clearly identify whose fixed gaze causes whose discomfort.

v12: The predicted harm includes killing children and ripping open pregnant women. Hazael’s “great thing” in verse 13 is not rewritten as a denial of moral capacity.

v16: Jehoram and Joram are forms of the same name, borne here by kings of Judah and Israel. The overlapping reign notice is retained.

v26: Ahaziah is twenty-two here. Athaliah is called Omri’s daughter; “daughter” can also refer to a later female descendant.

## Vocabulary

v06: Court official
Hebrew saris can denote a court officer or a eunuch; the role is clear here, the bodily status is not.

v19: Lamp
An image for the continuation of David’s royal line.
