## Jerusalem’s final siege

<p>
v01: In the ninth year of Zedekiah’s reign, on the tenth day of the tenth month, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came against Jerusalem with his whole army. He camped against it, and they built siegeworks all around it.
v02: The city remained under siege until King Zedekiah’s eleventh year.
</p>

<p>
v03: By the ninth day of the month, the famine in the city was severe, and there was no bread for the people of the land.
v04: The city wall was breached. That night all the fighting men fled through the gate between the two walls beside the king’s garden, while the Chaldeans surrounded the city. The king went toward the Arabah.
</p>

<p>
v05: The Chaldean army pursued the king and overtook him in the plains of Jericho. His whole army had scattered from him.
v06: They seized the king and brought him to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where judgment was pronounced against him.
</p>

<p>
v07: They slaughtered Zedekiah’s sons before his eyes. Then the king of Babylon put out Zedekiah’s eyes, bound him in bronze shackles, and took him to Babylon.
</p>

## The city and temple destroyed

<p>
v08: On the seventh day of the fifth month, in the nineteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan, commander of the guard and servant of Babylon’s king, came to Jerusalem.
v09: He burned the LORD’s house, the palace, and all Jerusalem’s houses. Every great house he burned down.
v10: The whole Chaldean army with the commander of the guard tore down the walls around Jerusalem.
</p>

<p>
v11: Nebuzaradan, commander of the guard, deported the people left in the city, those who had deserted to the king of Babylon, and the rest of the population.
v12: But the commander of the guard left some of the poorest people of the land to tend vineyards and fields.
</p>

<p>
v13: The Chaldeans broke up the bronze pillars, the stands, and the bronze Sea in the LORD’s house, and carried their bronze to Babylon.
v14: They took the pots, shovels, snuffers, dishes, and all the bronze utensils used in the service.
v15: The commander of the guard also took the firepans and bowls—whatever was gold as gold, and whatever was silver as silver.
</p>

<p>
v16: The bronze of the two pillars, the one Sea, and the stands Solomon had made for the LORD’s house—all these objects—was beyond weighing.
v17: Each pillar was eighteen cubits high and had a bronze capital three cubits high, with bronze latticework and pomegranates all around it. The second pillar, with its latticework, was the same.
</p>

## Executions at Riblah

<p>
v18: The commander of the guard took Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the second priest, and the three threshold guards.
v19: From the city he took one official in charge of the fighting men, five of the king’s close advisers who were found there, the army secretary responsible for mustering the people of the land, and sixty men of the land found in the city.
</p>

<p>
v20: Nebuzaradan, commander of the guard, took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.
v21: The king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death at Riblah in the land of Hamath. So Judah went into exile from its land.
</p>

## Gedaliah and the flight to Egypt

<p>
v22: Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon appointed Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, over the people he had left in the land of Judah.
v23: When all the army commanders and their men heard that Babylon’s king had appointed Gedaliah, they came to him at Mizpah: Ishmael son of Nethaniah, Johanan son of Kareah, Seraiah son of Tanhumeth the Netophathite, and Jaazaniah son of the Maacathite, with their men.
</p>

<p>
v24: Gedaliah swore an oath to them and their men: “Do not be afraid of the Chaldean officials. Stay in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and things will go well for you.”
</p>

<p>
v25: In the seventh month Ishmael son of Nethaniah, son of Elishama, a member of the royal line, came with ten men. They struck Gedaliah dead, along with the Judeans and Chaldeans who were with him at Mizpah.
v26: Then all the people, from the least to the greatest, and the army commanders left for Egypt because they were afraid of the Chaldeans.
</p>

## Jehoiachin released

<p>
v27: In the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, on the twenty-seventh day of the twelfth month, Evil-merodach king of Babylon, in the year he became king, lifted Jehoiachin’s head and released him from prison.
v28: He spoke kindly to him and gave him a seat above the seats of the other kings with him in Babylon.
v29: Jehoiachin changed out of his prison clothes and regularly ate at the king’s table for the rest of his life.
v30: The king gave him a regular allowance, a daily portion every day, for the rest of his life.
</p>

## Notes

v03: Kings gives the ninth day but omits the month in this verse. The fourth month given in Jeremiah 52 is not silently added.

v07: Zedekiah witnesses his sons’ deaths before his own eyes are put out. The sequence is explicit.

v08: The arrival is dated to the seventh day here, compared with the tenth in Jeremiah 52:12.

v17: The capitals are three cubits high in this verse. Other temple descriptions give five; this local figure is retained.

v19: Kings names five royal advisers; Jeremiah 52 names seven. The counts are not harmonized.

v27: “Lifted his head” recalls a release-and-favor idiom. The book ends with provision for the exiled king, not a narrated restoration of Judah’s kingdom.

## Vocabulary

v01: Siegeworks
Structures built to surround and attack a fortified city.

v07: Bronze shackles
Metal restraints for a prisoner.

v13: Sea
The temple’s large bronze basin.

v27: Evil-merodach
The name of the Babylonian ruler who releases Jehoiachin; “Evil” here is part of the traditional name, not an English moral description.
