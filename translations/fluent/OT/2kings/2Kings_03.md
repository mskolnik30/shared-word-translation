## Three kings march against Moab

<p>
v01: Jehoram son of Ahab became king over Israel in Samaria in the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah. He ruled for twelve years.
v02: He did evil in the LORD’s eyes, though not like his father and mother. He removed the pillar of Baal his father had made.
v03: But he held fast to the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who led Israel into sin. He did not turn away from them.
</p>

<p>
v04: Mesha king of Moab raised sheep. He supplied Israel’s king with a hundred thousand lambs and the wool of a hundred thousand rams.
v05: But after Ahab died, Moab’s king rebelled against Israel’s king.
</p>

<p>
v06: King Jehoram left Samaria at that time and mustered all Israel.
v07: He sent word to Jehoshaphat king of Judah: “Moab’s king has rebelled against me. Will you join me in war against Moab?” “I will,” he replied. “I am as you are, my people as your people, my horses as your horses.”
v08: “Which route shall we take?” he asked. The answer was, “Through the wilderness of Edom.”
</p>

<p>
v09: The kings of Israel, Judah, and Edom set out. After a roundabout journey of seven days, there was no water for the army or the animals following them.
v10: Israel’s king cried, “Alas! The LORD has called these three kings together to hand them over to Moab!”
</p>

<p>
v11: Jehoshaphat asked, “Is there no prophet of the LORD here through whom we can consult him?” A servant of Israel’s king answered, “Elisha son of Shaphat is here—the one who poured water over Elijah’s hands.”
v12: Jehoshaphat said, “The LORD’s word is with him.” So Israel’s king, Jehoshaphat, and Edom’s king went down to him.
</p>

## Water in the valley

<p>
v13: Elisha said to Israel’s king, “What have you to do with me? Go to your father’s prophets and your mother’s prophets.” The king answered, “No! The LORD has called these three kings together to hand them over to Moab.”
v14: Elisha said, “As the LORD of Armies lives, whom I serve, if I did not respect Jehoshaphat king of Judah, I would not so much as look at you.
</p>

<p>
v15: Now bring me a musician.” As the musician played, the LORD’s hand came upon Elisha.
v16: He said, “The LORD says: Make this valley full of ditches.
v17: For the LORD says: You will see neither wind nor rain, yet this valley will fill with water. You, your cattle, and your other animals will drink.
v18: This is a small thing in the LORD’s eyes. He will also hand Moab over to you.
v19: You will strike every fortified town and every choice town. You will fell every good tree, stop up every spring, and ruin every good field with stones.”
</p>

<p>
v20: In the morning, at the time of the offering, water came from the direction of Edom and filled the land.
</p>

## Moab’s defeat

<p>
v21: All Moab heard that the kings had come to fight them. Every man old enough to strap on a belt and all who were older were called out, and they took their stand at the border.
v22: They rose early. The sun shone on the water, and from across the way the Moabites saw it red as blood.
v23: “It is blood!” they said. “The kings have destroyed each other! Now, Moab, to the plunder!”
</p>

<p>
v24: But when they reached Israel’s camp, the Israelites rose and struck them. Moab fled, and Israel continued striking into Moab.
v25: They demolished the towns. Each man threw stones onto every good field until it was covered. They stopped every spring and felled every good tree. Only Kir-hareseth’s stones were left, and slingers surrounded and attacked it.
</p>

<p>
v26: When Moab’s king saw the battle overpowering him, he took seven hundred swordsmen to break through toward Edom’s king. They could not.
v27: Then he took his firstborn son, who was to succeed him, and offered him as a burnt offering on the wall. Great wrath came against Israel. They withdrew from him and returned to their own land.
</p>

## Notes

v16: The Hebrew can be heard as an instruction to make ditches or as a declaration that the valley will become full of pools.

v19: The destruction of trees, springs, and farmland is explicit both in the prediction and in its execution in verse 25.

v27: The king offers his firstborn son as a burnt offering. The “great wrath” against Israel is not assigned an explicit source: divine wrath and human fury are among the interpretations.

## Vocabulary

v04: Tribute
Goods paid by a subordinate ruler to an overlord.

v15: Musician
The instrument player accompanies the prophetic scene; the text does not describe the music.

v27: Burnt offering
An offering consumed by fire on the altar.
