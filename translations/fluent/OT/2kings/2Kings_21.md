## Manasseh reverses the reforms

<p>
v01: Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem for fifty-five years. His mother’s name was Hephzibah.
v02: He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes, following the abhorrent practices of the nations the LORD had driven out before the Israelites.
</p>

<p>
v03: He rebuilt the high places his father Hezekiah had destroyed, erected altars for Baal, and made an Asherah as Ahab king of Israel had done. He bowed to all the army of heaven and served them.
v04: He built altars in the LORD’s house, of which the LORD had said, “In Jerusalem I will place my name.”
v05: He built altars to all the army of heaven in both courtyards of the LORD’s house.
</p>

<p>
v06: He made his son pass through the fire, practiced soothsaying and read omens, and dealt with mediums and spiritists. He did much that was evil in the LORD’s eyes, provoking him to anger.
</p>

<p>
v07: He placed the carved Asherah image he had made in the house about which the LORD had told David and his son Solomon, “In this house and in Jerusalem, which I chose from all Israel’s tribes, I will place my name forever.
v08: I will no longer make Israel’s feet wander from the land I gave their ancestors, if only they carefully do everything I commanded them, following all the instruction my servant Moses gave them.”
</p>

<p>
v09: But they would not listen. Manasseh led them astray to do more evil than the nations the LORD had destroyed before the Israelites.
</p>

## Judgment announced

<p>
v10: The LORD spoke through his servants the prophets:
v11: “Because Manasseh king of Judah has committed these abhorrent acts, doing more evil than all the Amorites before him and also leading Judah into sin with his idols,
v12: this is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I am bringing such disaster on Jerusalem and Judah that both ears of everyone who hears of it will ring.
</p>

<p>
v13: I will stretch over Jerusalem the measuring line used on Samaria and the plumb line used on Ahab’s house. I will wipe Jerusalem as someone wipes a dish, wiping it and turning it upside down.
v14: I will abandon the remnant of my inheritance and hand them over to their enemies. They will become plunder and spoil for all their enemies,
v15: because they have done what is evil in my eyes and have provoked me from the day their ancestors came out of Egypt until today.”
</p>

<p>
v16: Manasseh also shed so much innocent blood that he filled Jerusalem from end to end, besides the sin he led Judah to commit by doing what was evil in the LORD’s eyes.
</p>

<p>
v17: The rest of Manasseh’s acts, everything he did, and the sin he committed are written in the annals of the kings of Judah.
v18: Manasseh rested with his ancestors and was buried in the garden of his house, the Garden of Uzza. His son Amon became king in his place.
</p>

## Amon of Judah

<p>
v19: Amon was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem for two years. His mother’s name was Meshullemeth, daughter of Haruz, from Jotbah.
v20: He did what was evil in the LORD’s eyes, as his father Manasseh had done.
v21: He followed his father’s whole way of life, serving the idols his father had served and bowing to them.
v22: He abandoned the LORD, the God of his ancestors, and did not walk in the LORD’s way.
</p>

<p>
v23: Amon’s servants conspired against him and killed the king in his own house.
v24: The people of the land struck down everyone who had conspired against King Amon. Then they made his son Josiah king in his place.
</p>

<p>
v25: The rest of Amon’s acts are written in the annals of the kings of Judah.
v26: He was buried in his tomb in the Garden of Uzza. His son Josiah became king in his place.
</p>

## Notes

v06: The son’s fire rite belongs with the account’s prohibited practices; the text does not explain the mechanics.

v13: The measuring line and plumb line connect Jerusalem’s judgment to Samaria and Ahab’s house. The wiped and overturned dish is a separate image of emptying.

v16: Innocent bloodshed is expressly added to the cultic sins. The victims are not individually identified.

v18: Kings’ account does not include the later repentance narrative found in Chronicles; it is not inserted here.

## Vocabulary

v03: Army of heaven
The heavenly bodies or host treated here as objects of worship.

v14: Inheritance
The people belonging to the LORD, described as his allotted possession.
