## Hezekiah asks Isaiah to pray

<p>
v01: When King Hezekiah heard this, he tore his clothes, covered himself with sackcloth, and went into the LORD’s house.
v02: He sent Eliakim the palace administrator, Shebna the secretary, and the senior priests, all covered with sackcloth, to the prophet Isaiah son of Amoz.
</p>

<p>
v03: They said to him, “This is what Hezekiah says: Today is a day of distress, rebuke, and disgrace. Children have reached the opening of the womb, but there is no strength to give birth.
v04: Perhaps the LORD your God will hear all the words of the Rab-shakeh, whom his master the king of Assyria sent to mock the living God, and will rebuke him for the words the LORD your God has heard. Lift up a prayer for the remnant still here.”
</p>

<p>
v05: When King Hezekiah’s servants came to Isaiah,
v06: Isaiah said to them, “Tell your master: This is what the LORD says: Do not be afraid of the words you have heard, with which the king of Assyria’s servants have insulted me.
v07: I am going to put a spirit in him, so that he will hear a report and return to his own land. There I will make him fall by the sword.”
</p>

## A second threat

<p>
v08: The Rab-shakeh returned and found the king of Assyria fighting against Libnah, for he had heard that the king had left Lachish.
v09: The king heard this report about Tirhakah king of Cush: “He has come out to fight you.” So he again sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying,
</p>

<p>
v10: “Tell Hezekiah king of Judah: Do not let the God you trust deceive you by saying, ‘Jerusalem will not be handed over to the king of Assyria.’
v11: You have heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all the lands, devoting them to destruction. Will you be rescued?
v12: Did the gods of the nations my ancestors destroyed rescue them—Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and the people of Eden in Telassar?
v13: Where are the king of Hamath, the king of Arpad, and the king of the city of Sepharvaim, Hena, and Ivvah?”
</p>

## Hezekiah’s prayer

<p>
v14: Hezekiah took the letters from the messengers and read them. Then he went up to the LORD’s house and spread the message out before the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v15: Hezekiah prayed before the LORD: “LORD, God of Israel, enthroned above the cherubim, you alone are God over all the kingdoms of the earth. You made heaven and earth.
v16: Bend your ear, LORD, and hear. Open your eyes, LORD, and see. Hear Sennacherib’s words, which he has sent to mock the living God.
</p>

<p>
v17: It is true, LORD, that the kings of Assyria have devastated the nations and their lands.
v18: They threw their gods into the fire, for those were not gods but the work of human hands, wood and stone. So they destroyed them.
v19: Now, LORD our God, please save us from his hand, so that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that you alone, LORD, are God.”
</p>

## The LORD answers

<p>
v20: Isaiah son of Amoz sent word to Hezekiah: “This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: I have heard your prayer to me about Sennacherib king of Assyria.
</p>

<p>
v21: This is the word the LORD has spoken against him:
‘Virgin daughter Zion despises you and mocks you.
Daughter Jerusalem shakes her head behind you.
v22: Whom have you mocked and insulted?
Against whom have you raised your voice
and lifted your eyes in pride?
Against the Holy One of Israel!
v23: Through your messengers you have mocked the Lord.
You have said, “With my many chariots
I climbed the mountain heights, the far reaches of Lebanon.
I cut down its tallest cedars, its finest cypresses.
I reached its remotest lodging, its luxuriant forest.
v24: I dug wells and drank foreign waters.
With the soles of my feet I dried up all the streams of Egypt.”
v25: Have you not heard? Long ago I made this happen;
in ancient days I shaped it.
Now I have brought it about:
you have turned fortified cities into heaps of ruins.
v26: Their people, powerless, were dismayed and ashamed.
They became like plants in the field, like tender grass,
like grass on rooftops, scorched before it grows tall.
v27: But I know your sitting down, your going out and coming in,
and your raging against me.
v28: Because you rage against me,
and your arrogance has reached my ears,
I will put my hook in your nose and my bridle in your lips.
I will turn you back by the road you came.’
</p>

<p>
v29: “This will be the sign for you: This year you will eat what grows by itself, and in the second year what springs from that. In the third year, sow and reap, plant vineyards and eat their fruit.
</p>

<p>
v30: The survivors left of Judah’s house
will again take root below and bear fruit above.
v31: For a remnant will go out from Jerusalem,
and survivors from Mount Zion.
The zeal of the LORD of Armies will do this.
</p>

<p>
v32: “Therefore this is what the LORD says about the king of Assyria: He will not enter this city, shoot an arrow here, advance against it with a shield, or build a siege ramp against it.
v33: He will return by the road he came. He will not enter this city, declares the LORD.
v34: I will defend this city and save it, for my own sake and for the sake of David my servant.”
</p>

## Assyria withdraws

<p>
v35: That night the LORD’s angel went out and struck down a hundred and eighty-five thousand in the Assyrian camp. When people rose early in the morning, there they were—all dead bodies.
v36: Sennacherib king of Assyria broke camp, left, and returned to Nineveh, where he stayed.
</p>

<p>
v37: While he was bowing in the temple of his god Nisroch, his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer struck him down with the sword. They escaped to the land of Ararat, and his son Esar-haddon became king in his place.
</p>

## Notes

v07: The promised “spirit” may be understood as an impulse or disposition, or as a spirit acting on the king. The text does not specify further.

v21: Zion and Jerusalem are personified as daughters. “Virgin” is retained as part of the poetic address, not converted into a claim about individual residents.

v31: “Of Armies” is supplied in the source’s read tradition though absent from its written consonantal line.

v35: The stated number is 185,000. No plague, disease, or other natural mechanism is named.

v37: “His sons” is present as a read addition in the pinned source. The account does not specify the interval between the withdrawal and the assassination.

## Vocabulary

v15: Cherubim
Heavenly beings associated with the divine throne; the temple also contained their representations.

v21: Zion
A name associated with Jerusalem and its holy hill.

v28: Hook and bridle
Images of forcibly leading the proud invader back.
