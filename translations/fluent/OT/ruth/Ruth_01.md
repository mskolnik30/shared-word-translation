## Naomi’s family in Moab

<p>
v01: In the days when the judges governed, a famine struck the land. A man from Bethlehem in Judah went to live for a time in the country of Moab, taking his wife and two sons.
v02: The man was named Elimelech, his wife Naomi, and his two sons Mahlon and Chilion. They were Ephrathites from Bethlehem in Judah. They came to Moab and stayed there.
</p>

<p>
v03: Then Naomi’s husband Elimelech died, leaving her with her two sons.
v04: Her sons married Moabite women, one named Orpah and the other Ruth. They lived there for about ten years.
v05: Then both Mahlon and Chilion died too. The woman was left without her two children and without her husband.
</p>

## The road back to Judah

<p>
v06: Naomi set out with her daughters-in-law to return from Moab. While there, she had heard that the LORD had attended to his people and given them food.
v07: She left the place where she had been living, with both daughters-in-law beside her. They took the road back to the land of Judah.
</p>

<p>
v08: Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, “Go, return, each of you, to your mother’s home. May the LORD show you steadfast love, as you have shown it to the dead and to me.
v09: May the LORD grant each of you rest in the home of a husband.” She kissed them, and they wept aloud.
</p>

<p>
v10: They said to her, “No, we will return with you to your people.”
</p>

<p>
v11: But Naomi said, “Return, my daughters. Why would you come with me? Do I still have sons in my womb who could become your husbands?
v12: Return, my daughters. Go. I am too old to marry again. Even if I thought there was hope for me, even if I had a husband tonight and then bore sons,
v13: would you wait until they grew up? Would you keep yourselves from marrying for their sake? No, my daughters. My bitterness is greater than yours, for the LORD’s hand has turned against me.”
</p>

<p>
v14: Again they wept aloud. Orpah kissed her mother-in-law goodbye, but Ruth held tightly to her.
</p>

<p>
v15: Naomi said, “Look, your sister-in-law has returned to her people and her gods. Return with her.”
</p>

<p>
v16: But Ruth said, “Do not press me to leave you and turn back from following you. Wherever you go, I will go. Wherever you spend the night, I will spend the night. Your people will be my people, and your God my God.
v17: Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the LORD do this to me, and more besides, if anything but death separates you and me.”
</p>

<p>
v18: When Naomi saw that Ruth was determined to go with her, she stopped trying to persuade her.
</p>

## Full and empty

<p>
v19: The two of them traveled on until they reached Bethlehem. Their arrival stirred the whole town. The women asked, “Can this be Naomi?”
</p>

<p>
v20: She answered, “Do not call me Naomi. Call me Mara, for the Almighty has made my life deeply bitter.
v21: I went away full, but the LORD has brought me back empty. Why call me Naomi when the LORD has testified against me and the Almighty has brought disaster on me?”
</p>

<p>
v22: So Naomi returned from the country of Moab, with Ruth the Moabite, her daughter-in-law, beside her. They reached Bethlehem as the barley harvest began.
</p>

## Notes

v13: “Greater than yours” may also mean “bitter for me because of you.” Naomi speaks from her own grief.

v17: The oath leaves its threatened punishment unnamed. The final clause can also be understood as “if even death separates you and me.”

v20–21: Naomi suggests pleasantness; Mara means bitter. “The Almighty” renders Shaddai, a divine title whose origin is uncertain. “Testified against me” can also be understood as “afflicted me.”

## Vocabulary

v08: Steadfast love — ḥesed
Loyal care expressed in action. The same word returns in 2:20 and 3:10.

v09: Rest — menuḥah
Here a settled, secure home. Related rest language returns in 3:1; Boaz will not rest in 3:18.
