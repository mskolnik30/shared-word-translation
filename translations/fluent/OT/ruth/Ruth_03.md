## Naomi’s Plan
<p>
v01: Naomi, Ruth’s mother-in-law, said to her, “My daughter, should I not seek rest for you, so that it may go well with you?
v02: Now, isn’t Boaz our relative, whose young women you have been with? Tonight he is winnowing barley at the threshing floor.
v03: Wash, anoint yourself, put on your cloak, and go down to the threshing floor. But do not let him know you are there until he has finished eating and drinking.
v04: When he lies down, notice the place where he lies. Then go, uncover his feet, and lie down. He will tell you what to do.”
v05: Ruth said to her, “Everything you say, I will do.”
</p>

## Ruth Approaches Boaz
<p>
v06: So Ruth went down to the threshing floor and did everything her mother-in-law had instructed her.
v07: Boaz ate and drank, and he was in good spirits. Then he went to lie down at the end of the grain pile. Ruth came quietly, uncovered his feet, and lay down.
v08: At midnight the man was startled and turned over—and there was a woman lying at his feet.
v09: He asked, “Who are you?” She answered, “I am Ruth, your servant. Spread the corner of your garment over your servant, because you are a redeemer.”
</p>

<p>
v10: Boaz said, “May you be blessed by the LORD, my daughter. You have shown greater steadfast love now than at first, because you have not gone after younger men, whether poor or rich.
v11: Now, my daughter, do not be afraid. I will do everything you ask, because everyone in town knows that you are a woman of standing.
v12: It is true that I am a redeemer, but there is a redeemer nearer than I.
v13: Stay here tonight. In the morning, if he will redeem you, good—let him do it. But if he is not willing to redeem you, then as the LORD lives, I will redeem you. Lie down until morning.”
</p>

## Boaz’s Promise
<p>
v14: So Ruth lay at his feet until morning, but she got up before one person could recognize another. Boaz said, “It must not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor.”
v15: Then he said, “Bring me the cloak you are wearing and hold it out.” She held it out, and he measured six measures of barley and placed it on her. Then he went into the town.
</p>

<p>
v16: When Ruth came to her mother-in-law, Naomi asked, “How did it go, my daughter?” Ruth told her everything the man had done for her.
v17: She said, “He gave me these six measures of barley, because he told me, ‘You must not go back to your mother-in-law empty-handed.’”
v18: Naomi said, “Wait, my daughter, until you learn how the matter turns out. The man will not rest until he settles the matter today.”
</p>

## Notes

v01: Naomi's concern shifts from daily provision to Ruth's long-term security and future.

v02–04: Naomi's instructions reflect customs connected with kinship redemption and marriage, though the exact details are culturally specific.

v05: Ruth again demonstrates faithful obedience and trust.

v09: Ruth asks Boaz to spread his garment over her and appeals to his role as redeemer. Within the narrative context, the request carries marital as well as redemption implications.

v09: The imagery of spreading a garment echoes the earlier image of finding refuge under God's wings (2:12).

v10: Boaz interprets Ruth's request as an act of covenant loyalty rather than personal ambition.

v11: Ruth's reputation for noble character is publicly recognized within Bethlehem.

v12–13: Boaz is willing to redeem Ruth but insists on following the proper legal order.

v13: Boaz's oath demonstrates both integrity and commitment.

v14: The careful handling of the situation protects the reputations of both Ruth and Boaz.

v15: The gift of barley serves as both provision and a tangible sign of Boaz's promise.

v18: Naomi expresses confidence that Boaz will act decisively and honorably.

## Vocabulary

v01: מָנוֹחַ (mānôaḥ)
“Rest” or “security.” A settled condition of peace, provision, and belonging.

v02: גֹּרֶן (gōren)
“Threshing floor.” A place where harvested grain was processed and separated.

v03: סוּךְ (sûḵ)
“To anoint.” To apply oil as part of preparation, refreshment, or honor.

v07: לָט (lāṭ)
“Softly” or “secretly.” With careful and quiet movement.

v09: כָּנָף (kānāph)
“Wing,” “edge,” or “corner of a garment.” Used both literally and symbolically for protection.

v09: גֹּאֵל (gōʾēl)
“Redeemer.” A close relative responsible for preserving family inheritance and welfare.

v10: חֶסֶד (ḥeseḏ)
“Steadfast love.” Loyal covenant faithfulness expressed through action.

v11: אֵשֶׁת חַיִל (ʾēšeṯ ḥayil)
“Worthy woman.” A woman of noble character, strength, and virtue.

v13: גָּאַל (gāʾal)
“To redeem.” To reclaim, restore, or fulfill family obligations on behalf of another.

v15: שְׂעֹרִים (śĕʿōrîm)
“Barley.” The grain harvested during the season in which the story occurs.

v17: רֵיקָם (rêqām)
“Empty-handed.” Without provision, blessing, or gift.

v18: שָׁקַט (šāqaṭ)
“To be quiet” or “at rest.” Here contrasted with Boaz's determination to act promptly.
