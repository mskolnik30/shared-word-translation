## Naomi seeks rest for Ruth

<p>
v01: Naomi, her mother-in-law, said to her, “My daughter, should I not seek a place of rest for you, where you can fare well?
v02: Now Boaz, whose young women you have been with, is our relative. Tonight he will be winnowing barley at the threshing floor.
v03: Wash, put on oil, get dressed, and go down to the threshing floor. Do not let him know you are there until he has finished eating and drinking.
v04: When he lies down, notice where he is lying. Then go in, uncover his feet, and lie down. He will tell you what to do.”
</p>

<p>
v05: Ruth answered, “I will do everything you tell me.”
</p>

## At the threshing floor

<p>
v06: She went down to the threshing floor and did everything her mother-in-law had instructed.
v07: Boaz ate and drank, and his heart was content. He went to lie down at the far end of the grain heap. Ruth came quietly, uncovered his feet, and lay down.
</p>

<p>
v08: In the middle of the night the man startled awake and turned over. There was a woman lying at his feet!
</p>

<p>
v09: “Who are you?” he asked. “I am Ruth, your servant,” she replied. “Spread the wing of your garment over your servant, for you are a redeemer.”
</p>

<p>
v10: He said, “May the LORD bless you, my daughter. This last act of steadfast love is greater than the first: you have not pursued young men, whether poor or rich.
v11: Now, my daughter, do not be afraid. I will do everything you ask. Everyone at the gate of my town knows that you are a woman of standing.
</p>

<p>
v12: It is true that I am a redeemer, but there is another redeemer more closely related than I am.
v13: Stay here tonight. In the morning, if he will redeem you, good—let him do it. But if he does not want to redeem you, then, as the LORD lives, I will. Lie down until morning.”
</p>

<p>
v14: She lay at his feet until morning, then got up before one person could recognize another. Boaz said, “No one must know that a woman came to the threshing floor.”
</p>

<p>
v15: He said, “Bring the shawl you are wearing and hold it out.” She held it, and he measured out six measures of barley and lifted the load onto her. Then he went into town.
</p>

## Ruth returns to Naomi

<p>
v16: When Ruth came to her mother-in-law, Naomi asked, “How did it go, my daughter?” Ruth told her everything the man had done for her.
</p>

<p>
v17: She added, “He gave me these six measures of barley. He told me, ‘Do not go back to your mother-in-law empty-handed.’”
</p>

<p>
v18: Naomi said, “Stay here, my daughter, until you know how this turns out. The man will not rest until he has settled the matter today.”
</p>

## Notes

v04–09: The text describes uncovering Boaz’s feet and lying down. The nighttime setting and garment request carry intimate and marital implications, but the narrative does not explicitly describe intercourse.

v09: “Wing of your garment” renders kanaph, which can mean a wing or the edge of clothing. Compare the refuge under the LORD’s wings in 2:12.

v15: The Hebrew does not name the unit for the six measures. Its final verb is masculine: “he went,” referring to Boaz.

v16: Literally, “Who are you, my daughter?” In context Naomi is asking about the outcome or Ruth’s changed situation; the words echo Boaz’s question in 3:9.

## Vocabulary

v11: Woman of standing — eshet ḥayil
A woman of worth and capability. The same ḥayil describes Boaz in 2:1.
