## David rescues Keilah

<p>
v01: David was told, “The Philistines are attacking Keilah and plundering the threshing floors.”
v02: He inquired of the LORD, “Should I go and strike these Philistines?” The LORD answered, “Go. Strike the Philistines and save Keilah.”
</p>

<p>
v03: But David’s men said, “We are afraid even here in Judah. How much more if we go to Keilah against the Philistine ranks!”
</p>

<p>
v04: David inquired of the LORD again. The LORD answered, “Get up and go down to Keilah. I am handing the Philistines over to you.”
v05: David and his men went to Keilah and fought the Philistines. They drove off their livestock and struck them a heavy blow. David saved Keilah’s inhabitants.
</p>

## Will Keilah surrender him?

<p>
v06: When Abiathar son of Ahimelech fled to David at Keilah, he brought an ephod with him.
</p>

<p>
v07: Saul was told that David had entered Keilah. “God has delivered him into my hand,” he said. “He has shut himself in by entering a town with gates and a bar.”
v08: Saul summoned all the troops for war, to go down to Keilah and besiege David and his men.
</p>

<p>
v09: David learned that Saul was plotting harm against him. He told Abiathar the priest, “Bring the ephod here.”
v10: David said, “LORD, God of Israel, your servant has heard that Saul intends to come to Keilah and destroy the town because of me.
v11: Will Keilah’s leaders hand me over to him? Will Saul come down, as your servant has heard? LORD, God of Israel, please tell your servant.” The LORD answered, “He will come down.”
</p>

<p>
v12: David asked, “Will Keilah’s leaders hand me and my men over to Saul?” The LORD answered, “They will.”
</p>

<p>
v13: David and his men, about six hundred, left Keilah and kept moving wherever they could. When Saul learned that David had escaped from Keilah, he called off the expedition.
</p>

## Jonathan visits David

<p>
v14: David stayed in wilderness strongholds, in the hill country of the wilderness of Ziph. Saul sought him every day, but God did not give him into Saul’s hand.
v15: David saw that Saul had come out to seek his life. David was at Horesh in the wilderness of Ziph.
</p>

<p>
v16: Saul’s son Jonathan went to David at Horesh and strengthened his hand in God.
v17: “Do not be afraid,” he told him. “My father Saul’s hand will not reach you. You will be king over Israel, and I will be second to you. My father Saul knows this too.”
</p>

<p>
v18: The two made a covenant before the LORD. David stayed at Horesh, and Jonathan went home.
</p>

## Saul closes in

<p>
v19: The Ziphites went up to Saul at Gibeah and said, “David is hiding among us in the strongholds at Horesh, on the hill of Hachilah south of Jeshimon.
v20: Come down whenever you wish, Your Majesty. We will hand him over to the king.”
v21: Saul said, “May the LORD bless you for having compassion on me.
</p>

<p>
v22: Go and make sure. Find out where his feet take him and who has seen him there. I am told he is very crafty.
v23: Find out all his hiding places. Come back with reliable information, and I will go with you. If he is in the land, I will search him out among all Judah’s clans.”
</p>

<p>
v24: They set out for Ziph ahead of Saul. David and his men were in the wilderness of Maon, in the Arabah south of Jeshimon.
v25: Saul and his men went to search. When David was told, he went down to the rock and stayed in the wilderness of Maon. Saul heard and pursued him there.
</p>

<p>
v26: Saul went along one side of the mountain, David and his men along the other. David hurried to get away, while Saul and his men were closing around them to seize them.
v27: Then a messenger came to Saul: “Hurry and come! The Philistines have raided the land.”
</p>

<p>
v28: Saul broke off his pursuit of David and went to meet the Philistines. That is why the place was called Sela-hammahlekoth.
v29: David went up from there and stayed in the strongholds of En-gedi.
</p>

## Notes

v07, 14: Saul claims God has delivered David into his hand; the narrator later says God did not give David into Saul’s hand. The different speakers matter.

v11–12: David asks whether the town will surrender him and whether Saul will come. The answers arrive separately, with the second question answered first.

v17: Jonathan’s expectation that he will be second to David remains Jonathan’s statement, not an added promise by the narrator.

v28: Sela-hammahlekoth is associated with division or separation, fitting the armies’ parting here.

v29: This public verse is the complete Hebrew record 24:1; the following public chapter begins at Hebrew 24:2.

## Vocabulary

v16: Strengthened his hand in God
Jonathan’s visit gives David courage and support in relation to God; the hand image remains visible.

v23: Clans
The Hebrew word can designate thousands or social and military groupings. Here it locates David among Judah’s groups.
