## The command concerning Amalek

<p>
v01: Samuel told Saul, “The LORD sent me to anoint you king over his people Israel. Now listen to the LORD’s words.
v02: This is what the LORD of Armies says: ‘I will call Amalek to account for what it did to Israel, opposing Israel on the road out of Egypt.
v03: Now go and strike Amalek. Devote everything it has to destruction. Do not spare anyone. Put to death men and women, children and nursing infants, cattle and sheep, camels and donkeys.’”
</p>

<p>
v04: Saul summoned the troops and mustered them at Telaim: two hundred thousand foot soldiers and ten thousand men of Judah.
</p>

<p>
v05: Saul reached Amalek’s city and set an ambush in the wadi.
</p>

<p>
v06: He told the Kenites, “Go, leave the Amalekites. Get away from them so I do not sweep you away with them. You showed steadfast love to all the Israelites when they came up from Egypt.” So the Kenites withdrew from among the Amalekites.
</p>

<p>
v07: Saul struck Amalek from Havilah as far as Shur, east of Egypt.
</p>

<p>
v08: He captured Agag king of Amalek alive, but devoted all the people to destruction by the sword.
</p>

<p>
v09: Saul and the troops spared Agag and the best sheep, cattle, fattened animals, and lambs—everything good. They refused to devote these to destruction. They destroyed only what was despised and worthless.
</p>

## Saul and Samuel confront each other

<p>
v10: The LORD’s word came to Samuel:
</p>

<p>
v11: “I regret making Saul king. He has turned away from following me and has not carried out my words.” Samuel burned with anger and cried out to the LORD all night.
</p>

<p>
v12: Samuel rose early to meet Saul. He was told, “Saul went to Carmel and set up a monument for himself. Then he turned and went on down to Gilgal.”
</p>

<p>
v13: When Samuel reached him, Saul said, “May the LORD bless you! I have carried out the LORD’s word.”
</p>

<p>
v14: Samuel asked, “Then what is this sound of sheep in my ears? What is this sound of cattle I hear?”
</p>

<p>
v15: Saul answered, “They brought them from the Amalekites. The troops spared the best sheep and cattle to sacrifice to the LORD your God. We devoted the rest to destruction.”
</p>

<p>
v16: “Stop,” Samuel told Saul. “Let me tell you what the LORD said to me last night.” Saul replied, “Speak.”
</p>

<p>
v17: Samuel said, “Though you may be small in your own eyes, are you not the head of Israel’s tribes? The LORD anointed you king over Israel.
v18: The LORD sent you on a mission: ‘Go and devote the sinners, the Amalekites, to destruction. Fight against them until you have wiped them out.’
</p>

<p>
v19: Why did you not listen to the LORD’s voice? Why did you pounce on the plunder and do what is evil in his sight?”
</p>

<p>
v20: Saul answered Samuel, “But I did listen to the LORD’s voice! I went on the mission he sent me on. I brought back Agag king of Amalek and devoted Amalek to destruction.
</p>

<p>
v21: The troops took sheep and cattle from the plunder, the best of what was devoted to destruction, to sacrifice to the LORD your God at Gilgal.”
</p>

## Listening and sacrifice

<p>
v22: Samuel said,
“Does the LORD delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices
as much as in listening to his voice?
Listen: obedience is better than sacrifice,
attention better than the fat of rams.
v23: Rebellion is like the sin of divination,
and stubbornness like wickedness and household gods.
Because you have rejected the LORD’s word,
he has rejected you as king.”
</p>

## The torn robe

<p>
v24: Saul told Samuel, “I have sinned. I violated the LORD’s command and your words because I feared the people and listened to their voice.
</p>

<p>
v25: Now please forgive my sin and return with me so I can worship the LORD.”
</p>

<p>
v26: Samuel answered, “I will not return with you. You have rejected the LORD’s word, and the LORD has rejected you as king over Israel.”
</p>

<p>
v27: As Samuel turned to go, Saul seized the edge of his robe, and it tore.
</p>

<p>
v28: Samuel told him, “Today the LORD has torn the kingdom of Israel away from you and given it to someone near you who is better than you.
</p>

<p>
v29: The Glory of Israel does not lie or change his mind. He is not a human being who changes his mind.”
</p>

<p>
v30: Saul said, “I have sinned. But please honor me before the elders of my people and before Israel. Return with me so I can worship the LORD your God.”
</p>

<p>
v31: Samuel returned with Saul, and Saul worshiped the LORD.
</p>

## Agag’s death

<p>
v32: Samuel said, “Bring me Agag king of Amalek.” Agag came to him confidently and said, “Surely the bitterness of death has passed.”
</p>

<p>
v33: Samuel said, “As your sword has made women childless, so your mother will be childless among women.” Then Samuel cut Agag in pieces before the LORD at Gilgal.
</p>

<p>
v34: Samuel went to Ramah, and Saul went home to Gibeah of Saul.
</p>

<p>
v35: Samuel did not see Saul again until the day of his death, though he mourned for him. The LORD regretted making Saul king over Israel.
</p>

## Notes

v03: The command expressly includes women, children, nursing infants, and animals. “Devote to destruction” does not reduce those deaths to a metaphor.

v09: The word rendered “fattened animals” is uncertain; it may denote a valued class of animals, sometimes understood as second-born animals.

v11,29,35: The same Hebrew root, naḥam, underlies “regret” and “change his mind.” The chapter speaks both of the LORD’s regret over Saul and of the firmness of the announced rejection. Both statements remain in the text.

v32: Agag’s manner of approach is uncertain: “confidently” or “delicately,” but also understood as “trembling.”

## Vocabulary

v03: Devote to destruction — ḥaram
Place under a ban that, in this command, entails destruction and death.

v23: Household gods — teraphim
Cult objects named here beside divination and wickedness.

v29: Glory of Israel — netsaḥ Yisra’el
A divine title; the first word can suggest enduring eminence or strength.
