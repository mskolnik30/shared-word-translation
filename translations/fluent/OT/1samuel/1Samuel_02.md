## Hannah’s prayer

<p>
v01: Hannah prayed:
“My heart rejoices in the LORD;
my horn is raised high in the LORD.
My mouth opens wide against my enemies,
for I rejoice in your deliverance.
v02: No one is holy like the LORD.
There is no one besides you;
no rock is like our God.
v03: Stop your lofty, lofty talk.
Let no arrogance come from your mouths.
The LORD is a God who knows,
and by him deeds are weighed.
v04: The bows of warriors are shattered,
but those who stumbled put on strength.
v05: Those once full hire themselves out for bread;
those once hungry hunger no more.
The woman who could not bear children has borne seven,
but the mother of many grows feeble.
v06: The LORD kills and gives life;
he brings down to Sheol and raises up.
v07: The LORD makes poor and makes rich;
he brings low and also lifts high.
v08: He raises the poor from the dust
and lifts the needy from the ash heap,
to seat them with nobles
and give them a throne of honor.
The pillars of the earth belong to the LORD;
he has set the world upon them.
v09: He guards the feet of his faithful ones,
but the wicked are silenced in darkness.
For no one prevails by strength.
v10: Those who contend with the LORD will be shattered;
he will thunder against them from heaven.
The LORD will judge the ends of the earth.
He will give strength to his king
and raise high the horn of his anointed.”
</p>

## Samuel and Eli’s sons

<p>
v11: Elkanah went home to Ramah. The boy stayed to serve the LORD under Eli the priest.
</p>

<p>
v12: Eli’s sons were worthless men. They did not know the LORD.
v13: This was the priests’ practice with the people: whenever someone offered a sacrifice, the priest’s servant came with a three-pronged fork while the meat was boiling.
v14: He plunged it into the basin, kettle, cauldron, or pot, and the priest took whatever the fork brought up. This is what they did to all the Israelites who came to Shiloh.
v15: Even before the fat was burned, the priest’s servant would come and tell the person sacrificing, “Give the priest meat to roast. He will not accept boiled meat from you, only raw.”
v16: If the person said, “Let them burn the fat first; then take whatever you want,” the servant answered, “No. Give it now, or I will take it by force.”
v17: The young men’s sin was very great before the LORD, for they treated the LORD’s offering with contempt.
</p>

<p>
v18: Meanwhile Samuel served before the LORD, a boy wearing a linen ephod.
</p>

<p>
v19: His mother made him a little robe and brought it up each year when she came with her husband to offer the annual sacrifice.
</p>

<p>
v20: Eli would bless Elkanah and his wife: “May the LORD give you offspring through this woman in place of the one given over to the LORD.” Then they went home.
</p>

<p>
v21: The LORD attended to Hannah, and she conceived and bore three sons and two daughters. The boy Samuel grew up in the LORD’s presence.
</p>

<p>
v22: Eli was very old. He heard everything his sons were doing to all Israel, including how they slept with the women who served at the entrance to the tent of meeting.
</p>

<p>
v23: He said to them, “Why are you doing these things? I hear reports of your evil deeds from all these people.
v24: No, my sons. The report I hear circulating among the LORD’s people is not good.
v25: If one person sins against another, God can intervene. But if someone sins against the LORD, who can intercede for them?” Yet they would not listen to their father, because the LORD intended to put them to death.
</p>

<p>
v26: The boy Samuel kept growing and gaining favor with both the LORD and people.
</p>

## A word against Eli’s house

<p>
v27: A man of God came to Eli and said, “This is what the LORD says: ‘Did I not reveal myself to your father’s house when they were in Egypt, subject to Pharaoh’s house?
v28: I chose him from all Israel’s tribes to be my priest, to go up to my altar, burn incense, and wear an ephod before me. I gave your father’s house all the Israelites’ food offerings.
v29: Why do you kick at my sacrifices and offerings, which I commanded for my dwelling? Why do you honor your sons more than me, fattening yourselves on the best of every offering my people Israel bring?
</p>

<p>
v30: “‘Therefore the LORD, the God of Israel, declares: I did promise that your house and your father’s house would walk before me forever. But now the LORD declares: Far be it from me! I will honor those who honor me; those who despise me will be held in contempt.
v31: The days are coming when I will cut off your arm and the arm of your father’s house. No one in your house will reach old age.
v32: You will see distress in my dwelling, despite all the good done for Israel. No one in your house will ever reach old age.
v33: Any man of yours I leave at my altar will wear out your eyes and grieve your soul. All your descendants will die in the prime of life.
v34: What happens to your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, will be your sign: both will die on the same day.
v35: I will raise up a faithful priest for myself. He will do what is in my heart and soul. I will build him a lasting house, and he will walk before my anointed always.
v36: Everyone left in your house will come and bow to him for a piece of silver and a loaf of bread, saying, “Please attach me to one of the priestly duties so I can have a bite of bread.”’”
</p>

## Notes

v03: “By him” follows the traditional reading. The written form has “not,” producing a different and difficult clause about the weighing of deeds.

v20: The Hebrew repeats the asking/giving-over language of chapter 1. The wording and the subject of the final verb are difficult.

v24–25: Verse 24 may instead accuse the sons of causing the LORD’s people to transgress. “God can intervene” in verse 25 may also refer to divine judgment or a judge’s mediation.

v31–33: “Arm” represents strength and family power. Verse 32 can also be understood as seeing a rival in the dwelling. Verse 33 literally says the household’s increase will “die as men”; “in the prime of life” is a contextual reading.

## Vocabulary

v01,10: Horn — qeren
An image of strength and raised standing. Hannah’s own horn and the anointed king’s horn frame the prayer.

v06: Sheol
The realm of the dead; the poem pairs descent with being raised up.

v18: Ephod
A garment associated with sacred service; here it is made of linen.

v35: Anointed — mashiaḥ
Someone marked out by anointing. The passage does not name the future figure.
