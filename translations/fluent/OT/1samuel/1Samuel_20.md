## David asks Jonathan for help

<p>
v01: David fled from Naioth in Ramah and came to Jonathan. “What have I done?” he asked. “What is my guilt? How have I sinned against your father, that he is seeking my life?”
v02: Jonathan answered, “Never! You will not die. My father does nothing, great or small, without telling me. Why would he hide this from me? It cannot be.”
</p>

<p>
v03: But David swore again: “Your father knows very well that I have found favor with you. He thinks, ‘Jonathan must not know; it would grieve him.’ Yet as the LORD lives, and as you live, there is only a step between me and death.”
v04: Jonathan said, “Whatever you want, I will do for you.”
</p>

<p>
v05: David said, “Tomorrow is the new moon, and I am expected to sit and eat with the king. Let me go and hide in the field until the evening of the third day.
v06: If your father notices I am missing, say, ‘David urgently asked my permission to hurry to his hometown, Bethlehem. His whole clan has its annual sacrifice there.’
v07: If he says, ‘Good,’ your servant is safe. But if he becomes furious, you will know he has resolved to harm me.
v08: Show steadfast love to your servant, for you brought me into a covenant with you before the LORD. If I am guilty, kill me yourself. Why hand me over to your father?”
</p>

<p>
v09: Jonathan answered, “Never! If I learn that my father has resolved to harm you, would I not tell you?”
v10: David asked, “Who will tell me if your father answers you harshly?”
v11: “Come,” Jonathan said. “Let us go out to the field.” So the two of them went out.
</p>

## A covenant between the houses

<p>
v12: Jonathan said to David, “By the LORD, the God of Israel! I will sound out my father about this time tomorrow or the next day. If he is well disposed toward David, will I not send word and tell you?
v13: May the LORD do this to Jonathan, and more, if my father intends you harm and I fail to tell you and send you away safely. May the LORD be with you as he was with my father.
</p>

<p>
v14: If I am still alive, show me the LORD’s steadfast love, so that I do not die.
v15: Never cut off your steadfast love from my house, even when the LORD cuts off every one of David’s enemies from the face of the ground.”
v16: So Jonathan made a covenant with David’s house: “May the LORD call David’s enemies to account.”
v17: Jonathan had David swear again because of his love for him; he loved David as his own life.
</p>

<p>
v18: Jonathan told him, “Tomorrow is the new moon. You will be missed because your seat will be empty.
v19: On the third day, go far down to the place where you hid when this affair began, and stay beside the Ezel stone.
v20: I will shoot three arrows beside it, as though aiming at a target.
v21: Then I will send the boy: ‘Go and find the arrows.’ If I call to him, ‘The arrows are on this side of you; pick them up,’ come back. As the LORD lives, you are safe; there is no danger.
v22: But if I tell the boy, ‘The arrows are beyond you,’ go away, for the LORD has sent you away.
v23: As for what you and I have spoken, the LORD stands between us forever.”
</p>

## At the new moon feast

<p>
v24: David hid in the field. When the new moon came, the king sat down to eat.
v25: The king took his usual seat beside the wall. Jonathan stood up, and Abner sat beside Saul. David’s place was empty.
v26: Saul said nothing that day. He thought, “Something has happened. He is not clean—surely he is not clean.”
</p>

<p>
v27: The next day, the second day of the new moon, David’s place was still empty. Saul asked his son Jonathan, “Why has Jesse’s son not come to the meal, yesterday or today?”
v28: Jonathan answered, “David urgently asked my permission to go to Bethlehem.
v29: He said, ‘Please let me go. We have a clan sacrifice in town, and my brother has ordered me to be there. If I have found favor with you, let me get away and see my brothers.’ That is why he has not come to the king’s table.”
</p>

<p>
v30: Saul blazed with anger at Jonathan. “Son of a twisted, rebellious woman!” he said. “Do I not know you have chosen Jesse’s son, to your own shame and the shame of your mother’s nakedness?
v31: As long as Jesse’s son lives on the ground, neither you nor your kingdom will be secure. Send for him and bring him to me. He is marked for death!”
</p>

<p>
v32: Jonathan answered his father Saul, “Why should he be put to death? What has he done?”
v33: Saul hurled his spear at Jonathan to strike him. Then Jonathan knew his father had resolved to kill David.
</p>

<p>
v34: Jonathan rose from the table in fierce anger. He ate nothing that second day of the new moon, grieving over David because his father had shamed him.
</p>

## The signal and farewell

<p>
v35: In the morning Jonathan went out to the field at the time arranged with David, taking a young boy with him.
v36: He told the boy, “Run and find the arrows I shoot.” As the boy ran, Jonathan shot an arrow beyond him.
v37: When the boy reached the place where Jonathan’s arrow had landed, Jonathan called after him, “Is the arrow not beyond you?”
</p>

<p>
v38: He called again, “Quick! Hurry! Do not stand still!” The boy gathered the arrows and came back to his master.
v39: The boy knew nothing of this. Only Jonathan and David knew what it meant.
v40: Jonathan gave his equipment to the boy and told him, “Go. Take this back to town.”
</p>

<p>
v41: After the boy left, David rose from the south side, fell facedown to the ground, and bowed three times. They kissed one another and wept together, David weeping the more.
v42: Jonathan said, “Go in peace. We have both sworn in the LORD’s name: ‘The LORD will stand between me and you, and between my offspring and yours, forever.’” Then David got up and left, and Jonathan went into town.
</p>

## Notes

v10: David’s question is compressed in Hebrew and includes “or” before the possibility of a harsh answer. The draft unfolds the concern about receiving Jonathan’s report.

v14–16: The sequence of negatives in Jonathan’s request is difficult. The draft takes it as a plea for steadfast love during his life and toward his house afterward. Verse 16 retains “David’s enemies” rather than replacing it with “David.”

v19: The occasion associated with the hiding place is not entirely clear; the Hebrew can refer to the day of work or of the affair.

v25: The pinned Hebrew says Jonathan stood up. The draft does not change this to sitting opposite Saul.

v30: Saul’s insult targets Jonathan and his mother with sexual shaming. Its force is preserved as Saul’s abusive speech.

v34: “His father had shamed him” may refer to David or Jonathan. The pronoun is left open.

v41: The pinned Hebrew locates David on the south side. The greater weeping is attributed to David.

v42: This public verse includes both Hebrew 20:42 and 21:1, including David’s departure and Jonathan’s return.

## Vocabulary

v14–15: Steadfast love — hesed
The loyalty requested extends beyond the two men to their households.

v30: Nakedness
The image conveys exposure and shame; it is not a neutral description of family reputation.
