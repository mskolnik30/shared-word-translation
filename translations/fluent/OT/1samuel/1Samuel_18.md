## Jonathan and David

<p>
v01: When David finished speaking with Saul, Jonathan’s life became bound to David’s life, and Jonathan loved him as himself.
v02: Saul kept David with him that day and would not let him return to his father’s house.
</p>

<p>
v03: Jonathan made a covenant with David because he loved him as himself.
v04: Jonathan took off the robe he was wearing and gave it to David, along with his gear, his sword, his bow, and his belt.
v05: David succeeded wherever Saul sent him. Saul put him in charge of the fighting men, and this pleased all the people, including Saul’s servants.
</p>

## Saul fears David

<p>
v06: As they returned, when David came back from killing the Philistine, women came out from all Israel’s towns to meet King Saul. They sang and danced joyfully, with tambourines and other instruments.
v07: As they celebrated, the women answered one another:
“Saul has struck down his thousands,
and David his tens of thousands.”
</p>

<p>
v08: Saul was furious. The song displeased him, and he said, “They have given David tens of thousands, but me only thousands. What is left for him but the kingdom?”
v09: From that day on, Saul kept a suspicious eye on David.
</p>

<p>
v10: The next day a harmful spirit from God rushed upon Saul, and he prophesied inside the house. David was playing as he did each day, and Saul had a spear in his hand.
v11: Saul hurled the spear, saying, “I will pin David to the wall!” But David escaped him twice.
</p>

<p>
v12: Saul feared David because the LORD was with David but had departed from Saul.
v13: So Saul removed David from his presence and made him commander of a thousand. David led the troops out and brought them back.
</p>

<p>
v14: David succeeded in all he did, and the LORD was with him.
v15: Seeing David’s great success, Saul became afraid of him.
v16: But all Israel and Judah loved David, because he led them out and brought them back.
</p>

## Saul’s daughters

<p>
v17: Saul told David, “Here is my older daughter Merab. I will give her to you as your wife. Only be a brave warrior for me and fight the LORD’s battles.” Saul was thinking, “Let the Philistines’ hand strike him, not mine.”
v18: David answered, “Who am I, and what is my family, my father’s clan in Israel, that I should become the king’s son-in-law?”
v19: But when the time came to give Saul’s daughter Merab to David, she was given as wife to Adriel of Meholah.
</p>

<p>
v20: Saul’s daughter Michal loved David. When Saul was told, he was pleased.
v21: “I will give her to him,” Saul thought. “She can be a snare for him, so the Philistines’ hand will strike him.” He told David, “Now you have a second chance to become my son-in-law.”
</p>

<p>
v22: Saul ordered his servants, “Speak to David privately. Say, ‘The king delights in you, and all his servants love you. Become the king’s son-in-law now.’”
v23: When Saul’s servants repeated this to David, he said, “Do you think becoming the king’s son-in-law is a small thing? I am poor and of little standing.”
v24: The servants reported David’s words to Saul.
</p>

<p>
v25: Saul said, “Tell David, ‘The king wants no bride-price except a hundred Philistine foreskins, to take vengeance on his enemies.’” Saul planned to make David fall into Philistine hands.
v26: His servants told David, and David was pleased to become the king’s son-in-law. Before the allotted days were over,
v27: David went out with his men and killed two hundred Philistines. He brought their foreskins and presented the full number to the king, so he could become his son-in-law. Saul gave him his daughter Michal as his wife.
</p>

<p>
v28: Saul saw and knew that the LORD was with David and that his daughter Michal loved him.
v29: He became still more afraid of David and remained his enemy for the rest of his days.
</p>

<p>
v30: Whenever the Philistine commanders went out to battle, David succeeded more than all Saul’s other servants. His name became highly honored.
</p>

## Notes

v06: The last musical instrument named in Hebrew is uncertain; “other instruments” leaves its identity open.

v10: “Prophesied” uses the same Hebrew verb found in prophetic scenes elsewhere in Samuel. The harmful spirit and violent setting do not justify silently substituting a modern medical diagnosis.

v18: The middle expression in David’s question is difficult. It is taken here as his family or kin; it can also be understood in relation to his life or standing.

v21: The phrase rendered “a second chance” is literally “with two” or “by the second”; it appears to refer to the second daughter or offer.

v25–27: Saul demands one hundred foreskins; David brings two hundred. The bodily nature of the demand and the different numbers are retained.

## Vocabulary

v01–03: Life and love
Jonathan’s life is bound to David’s life, and he loves David as his own life. The wording connects affection with their covenant.

v03: Covenant — berit
A binding commitment; the gifts in verse 4 accompany this commitment without an explicit statement that Jonathan renounces succession.
