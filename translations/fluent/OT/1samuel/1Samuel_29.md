## The Philistine commanders object

<p>
v01: The Philistines gathered all their armies at Aphek, while Israel camped by the spring at Jezreel.
v02: The Philistine rulers marched past in hundreds and thousands. David and his men came at the rear with Achish.
</p>

<p>
v03: The Philistine commanders asked, “What are these Hebrews doing here?” Achish answered, “This is David, servant of Saul king of Israel. He has been with me these days or these years—and I have found no fault in him from the day he came over until now.”
</p>

<p>
v04: The commanders were angry with Achish. “Send the man back to the place you assigned him,” they said. “He must not go into battle with us and become our adversary. How better could he regain his master’s favor than with these men’s heads?
v05: Is this not the David they sang about as they danced:
‘Saul has struck down his thousands,
and David his tens of thousands’?”
</p>

## Achish sends David away

<p>
v06: Achish called David and said, “As the LORD lives, you have been upright. I have been pleased to have you go out and come in with me in the army. I have found no evil in you since you came to me, but the rulers do not approve of you.
v07: Now turn back and go in peace. Do nothing to displease the Philistine rulers.”
</p>

<p>
v08: David asked, “But what have I done? What fault have you found in your servant all the time I have been with you, that I cannot go and fight the enemies of my lord the king?”
</p>

<p>
v09: Achish answered, “I know. To me you are as good as a messenger of God. But the Philistine commanders have said, ‘He must not go to battle with us.’
v10: Get up early tomorrow with your master’s servants who came with you. Rise early, and leave as soon as it is light.”
v11: David and his men got up early to return to Philistine territory, while the Philistines went up to Jezreel.
</p>

## Notes

v03: Achish’s phrase “these days or these years” is imprecise. It is not used to revise the duration given in 27:7.

v06: Achish swears by the LORD in the Hebrew. The divine name is retained in his speech.

v08: David’s reference to fighting the enemies of “my lord the king” remains in his protest; the draft does not explain away the scene’s ambiguity.

v10: “Your master’s servants” is retained without replacing the unnamed master with an interpretive identification.

## Vocabulary

v04: Adversary — satan
Here the word describes the military opponent the commanders fear David could become.

v05: Thousands and tens of thousands
The repeated song recalls 18:7 and 21:11; its language remains recognizable.
