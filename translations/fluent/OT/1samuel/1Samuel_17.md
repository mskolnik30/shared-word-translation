## The challenge

<p>
v01: The Philistines gathered their armies for war at Socoh in Judah. They camped between Socoh and Azekah, at Ephes-dammim.
v02: Saul and the men of Israel gathered and camped in the Valley of Elah. They formed their battle lines facing the Philistines.
v03: The Philistines stood on one hillside and Israel on the other, with the valley between them.
v04: A champion came out from the Philistine camps. His name was Goliath, from Gath, and he stood six cubits and a span tall.
v05: He wore a bronze helmet and a coat of scale armor. The bronze armor weighed five thousand shekels.
v06: Bronze greaves covered his legs, and a bronze javelin rested between his shoulders.
v07: His spear shaft was like a weaver’s beam, and its iron head weighed six hundred shekels. A shield-bearer walked ahead of him.
</p>

<p>
v08: He stood and shouted to Israel’s ranks, “Why come out and form battle lines? Am I not the Philistine, and are you not Saul’s servants? Choose a man and let him come down to me.
v09: If he can fight me and kill me, we will be your slaves. But if I defeat him and kill him, you will be our slaves and serve us.”
v10: The Philistine said, “Today I defy Israel’s ranks! Give me a man, and let us fight.”
</p>

<p>
v11: When Saul and all Israel heard the Philistine’s words, they were shattered and terrified.
</p>

## David comes to the camp

<p>
v12: David was the son of an Ephrathite from Bethlehem in Judah named Jesse. Jesse had eight sons and was already an old man in Saul’s time.
v13: Jesse’s three oldest sons had gone to war with Saul: Eliab the firstborn, Abinadab the second, and Shammah the third.
v14: David was the youngest. The three oldest followed Saul,
v15: while David went back and forth from Saul to tend his father’s flock at Bethlehem.
</p>

<p>
v16: For forty days the Philistine came forward, taking his stand morning and evening.
v17: Jesse told his son David, “Take this ephah of roasted grain and these ten loaves to your brothers. Hurry them to the camp.
v18: Take these ten portions of cheese to their commander of a thousand. Find out how your brothers are and bring back a token from them.
v19: They are with Saul and all the men of Israel in the Valley of Elah, fighting the Philistines.”
</p>

<p>
v20: David got up early, left the flock with a keeper, and carried off the supplies as Jesse had instructed. He reached the camp enclosure as the army was marching out to its lines, raising the battle cry.
v21: Israel and the Philistines drew up their lines, army facing army.
v22: David left his load with the keeper of the supplies and ran to the battle line to greet his brothers.
v23: As he spoke with them, the champion came up from the Philistine ranks—Goliath, the Philistine from Gath. He repeated his challenge, and David heard it.
v24: Whenever the Israelites saw the man, they fled from him in terror.
</p>

<p>
v25: The Israelites were saying, “Have you seen this man coming up? He comes to defy Israel. The king will make the man who kills him very rich, give him his daughter, and free his father’s household from obligations in Israel.”
v26: David asked the men standing near him, “What will be done for the man who kills this Philistine and removes Israel’s disgrace? Who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the ranks of the living God?”
v27: They repeated what they had said about the reward for the man who killed him.
</p>

<p>
v28: His oldest brother Eliab heard him talking with the men and grew angry. “Why have you come down here? Who did you leave those few sheep with in the wilderness? I know your arrogance and your wicked heart. You came down to watch the battle!”
v29: David answered, “What have I done now? Can I not ask a question?”
v30: He turned away to someone else and asked the same thing. The people answered as before.
</p>

## David faces the Philistine

<p>
v31: What David said was overheard and reported to Saul, who sent for him.
</p>

<p>
v32: David told Saul, “Let no one lose heart because of him. Your servant will go and fight this Philistine.”
v33: Saul answered, “You cannot go and fight this Philistine. You are only a boy, and he has been a warrior since he was young.”
</p>

<p>
v34: David said, “Your servant has tended his father’s flock. When a lion or a bear came and carried off a sheep,
v35: I went after it, struck it, and rescued the sheep from its mouth. If it turned on me, I caught it by the beard, struck it, and killed it.
v36: Your servant has killed both lion and bear. This uncircumcised Philistine will be like one of them, because he has defied the ranks of the living God.”
v37: David added, “The LORD rescued me from the paw of the lion and the paw of the bear. He will rescue me from this Philistine’s hand.” Saul said, “Go, and may the LORD be with you.”
</p>

<p>
v38: Saul dressed David in his own gear. He put a bronze helmet on his head and fitted him with armor.
v39: David strapped Saul’s sword over the gear and tried to walk, for he had never tested it. “I cannot walk in this,” he told Saul. “I have not tried it before.” So David took it off.
v40: He took his staff and chose five smooth stones from the wadi, putting them in his shepherd’s bag, in its pouch. With his sling in hand, he approached the Philistine.
</p>

<p>
v41: The Philistine kept coming closer to David, with his shield-bearer in front.
v42: He looked David over and despised him: he was just a boy, ruddy and handsome.
v43: The Philistine said to David, “Am I a dog, that you come at me with sticks?” Then he cursed David by his gods.
v44: “Come here,” he said, “and I will give your flesh to the birds of the sky and the beasts of the field!”
</p>

<p>
v45: David answered, “You come at me with sword, spear, and javelin. I come at you in the name of the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel’s ranks, whom you have defied.
v46: Today the LORD will hand you over to me. I will strike you down and cut off your head. Today I will give the bodies of the Philistine army to the birds of the sky and the beasts of the earth. Then the whole earth will know that Israel has a God.
v47: Everyone gathered here will know that the LORD does not save by sword or spear. The battle belongs to the LORD, and he will hand you over to us.”
</p>

## The battle turns

<p>
v48: As the Philistine moved forward to meet him, David hurried toward the battle line and ran to meet the Philistine.
v49: David reached into his bag, took a stone, and slung it. It struck the Philistine in the forehead and sank in. He fell facedown to the ground.
</p>

<p>
v50: So David overpowered the Philistine with a sling and a stone. He struck him down and killed him, though David had no sword in his hand.
v51: David ran and stood over him. He took the Philistine’s sword, drew it from its sheath, and killed him, cutting off his head with it. When the Philistines saw that their champion was dead, they fled.
</p>

<p>
v52: The men of Israel and Judah rose with a shout and pursued the Philistines as far as the entrance to the valley and the gates of Ekron. Philistine bodies lay along the road to Shaaraim, all the way to Gath and Ekron.
v53: After chasing the Philistines, the Israelites returned and plundered their camps.
</p>

<p>
v54: David took the Philistine’s head to Jerusalem and put his weapons in his own tent.
</p>

## Whose son is he?

<p>
v55: When Saul had seen David going out to face the Philistine, he asked his army commander Abner, “Whose son is that young man, Abner?” “As you live, Your Majesty, I do not know,” Abner replied.
v56: The king said, “Find out whose son the young man is.”
</p>

<p>
v57: When David returned from killing the Philistine, Abner took him to Saul. David still held the Philistine’s head.
v58: Saul asked, “Whose son are you, young man?” David answered, “I am the son of your servant Jesse of Bethlehem.”
</p>

## Notes

v04: The pinned Hebrew gives six cubits and a span. The ancient measures are retained rather than converted to an exact modern height.

v12: The last description of Jesse is difficult, literally an old man “coming among men.” It is understood here as emphasizing his age.

v18: “A token” renders a word associated with a pledge or security; the precise object Jesse wants is not explained.

v19: This verse can be read as continuing Jesse’s speech, as here, or as a return to the narrator. Hebrew does not mark the end of the quotation.

v25: The promised freedom for the victor’s father’s household may concern taxation or other royal obligations; the Hebrew does not specify.

v29: David’s last question can also be understood as “Is there not a reason?” The Hebrew word can mean a word, matter, or reason.

v50–51: Verse 50 says David killed Goliath with the sling and stone; verse 51 again speaks of killing him, now with the sword. Both statements remain.

v54–58: The reference to Jerusalem and the questions about David’s father remain where the narrative places them. The questions are not rewritten to settle their relationship to David’s earlier service to Saul.

## Vocabulary

v04: Champion
Literally, a man between the armies: a fighter who represents his side in single combat.

v05, 17: Shekel; ephah
A shekel is a weight here, not a minted coin. An ephah is a dry measure.

v26, 36, 45: Defy
The repeated taunt becomes David’s description of Goliath’s challenge to the living God.
