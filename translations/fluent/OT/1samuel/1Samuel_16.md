## Samuel goes to Bethlehem

<p>
v01: The LORD said to Samuel, “How long will you mourn for Saul when I have rejected him as king over Israel? Fill your horn with oil and go. I am sending you to Jesse of Bethlehem; I have seen among his sons a king for myself.”
</p>

<p>
v02: Samuel asked, “How can I go? Saul will hear and kill me.” The LORD answered, “Take a heifer with you and say, ‘I have come to sacrifice to the LORD.’
</p>

<p>
v03: Invite Jesse to the sacrifice. I will tell you what to do, and you will anoint for me the one I name to you.”
</p>

<p>
v04: Samuel did what the LORD said and came to Bethlehem. The elders of the town trembled as they met him. “Do you come in peace?” they asked.
</p>

<p>
v05: “In peace,” he answered. “I have come to sacrifice to the LORD. Consecrate yourselves and come with me to the sacrifice.” He consecrated Jesse and his sons and invited them.
</p>

## The LORD looks at the heart

<p>
v06: When they arrived, Samuel saw Eliab and thought, “Surely the LORD’s anointed stands here before him.”
v07: But the LORD told Samuel, “Do not look at his appearance or his height. I have rejected him. I do not see as people see. People look at what meets the eyes, but the LORD looks at the heart.”
</p>

<p>
v08: Jesse called Abinadab and had him pass before Samuel. Samuel said, “The LORD has not chosen this one either.”
</p>

<p>
v09: Jesse then had Shammah pass by. Samuel said, “The LORD has not chosen this one either.”
</p>

<p>
v10: Jesse had seven of his sons pass before Samuel. Samuel told him, “The LORD has chosen none of these.”
</p>

<p>
v11: Samuel asked Jesse, “Are these all the young men?” “The youngest is still out tending the flock,” he replied. Samuel said, “Send for him. We will not sit down until he gets here.”
</p>

<p>
v12: Jesse sent for him and brought him in. He was ruddy, with beautiful eyes and a handsome appearance. The LORD said, “Get up and anoint him. He is the one.”
</p>

<p>
v13: Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him among his brothers. From that day onward the LORD’s Spirit rushed upon David. Then Samuel left for Ramah.
</p>

## David enters Saul’s service

<p>
v14: The LORD’s Spirit departed from Saul, and a harmful spirit from the LORD tormented him.
</p>

<p>
v15: Saul’s servants said to him, “A harmful spirit from God is tormenting you.
</p>

<p>
v16: Let our lord command his servants here to find someone skilled at playing the lyre. When the harmful spirit from God comes upon you, he can play, and you will feel relief.”
</p>

<p>
v17: Saul told his servants, “Find me someone who plays well and bring him to me.”
</p>

<p>
v18: One of the young men answered, “I have seen a son of Jesse of Bethlehem who knows how to play. He is a strong, capable man, a warrior, discerning in speech, and handsome. The LORD is with him.”
</p>

<p>
v19: Saul sent messengers to Jesse: “Send me your son David, who is with the flock.”
</p>

<p>
v20: Jesse took a donkey loaded with bread, a skin of wine, and one young goat and sent them to Saul with his son David.
</p>

<p>
v21: David came to Saul and entered his service. Saul loved him greatly, and David became his armor-bearer.
</p>

<p>
v22: Saul sent word to Jesse, “Please let David remain in my service. He has found favor with me.”
</p>

<p>
v23: Whenever the spirit from God came upon Saul, David took the lyre and played. Saul could breathe freely and felt better, and the harmful spirit left him.
</p>

## Notes

v07: The Hebrew contrasts seeing “to the eyes” with seeing “to the heart.” The draft supplies the implied divine comparison without turning “heart” into outward attractiveness.

v11: “We will not sit down” takes the verb in the setting of the sacrificial meal; “we will not turn aside” is also possible.

v12: “Ruddy” describes a reddish appearance; the text does not specify red hair.

v14–23: “Harmful spirit” renders ruaḥ ra‘ah. The narrative attributes it to the LORD and describes torment and relief; it does not provide a modern medical diagnosis.

v18: The servant calls David a warrior here. This description is retained without harmonizing it with later portrayals of his youth or inexperience.

## Vocabulary

v13: Spirit — ruaḥ
The Spirit rushes upon David as earlier upon Saul; the following verse describes departure from Saul.

v23: Breathe freely — rawaḥ
The word for relief echoes the sound of ruaḥ, “spirit,” in the same sentence.
