## David goes to Philistine territory

<p>
v01: David said to himself, “One day Saul’s hand will sweep me away. My best course is to escape to Philistine territory. Then Saul will give up searching for me throughout Israel, and I will escape his hand.”
v02: David set out with his six hundred men and crossed over to Achish son of Maoch, king of Gath.
</p>

<p>
v03: David and his men settled with Achish in Gath, each with his household. David had his two wives with him: Ahinoam of Jezreel and Abigail of Carmel, Nabal’s wife.
v04: When Saul was told that David had fled to Gath, he stopped searching for him.
</p>

## Ziklag and the raids

<p>
v05: David said to Achish, “If I have found favor with you, give me a place in one of the country towns to live. Why should your servant live with you in the royal city?”
v06: That day Achish gave him Ziklag. This is why Ziklag has belonged to Judah’s kings to this day.
</p>

<p>
v07: David lived in Philistine territory for a year and four months.
</p>

<p>
v08: David and his men went out raiding the Geshurites, the Gizrites, and the Amalekites. These peoples had long inhabited the land stretching toward Shur and the land of Egypt.
v09: Whenever David struck a district, he left neither man nor woman alive. He took sheep, cattle, donkeys, camels, and clothing, then returned to Achish.
</p>

<p>
v10: Achish would ask, “Where did you raid today?” David would answer, “The Negeb of Judah,” or “The Negeb of the Jerahmeelites,” or “The Negeb of the Kenites.”
v11: David left neither man nor woman alive to bring back to Gath. “They might report on us,” he said, “‘This is what David did.’” This was his practice all the time he lived in Philistine territory.
</p>

<p>
v12: Achish trusted David. “He has made himself a stench to his people Israel,” he thought. “He will be my servant forever.”
</p>

## Notes

v08: “Gizrites” follows the Hebrew reading note; the written form is Girzites.

v09–11: The narrative says David killed men and women to prevent reports of his actions reaching Gath. This concealment is not softened into a battle-only description.

v10: The question about the day’s raid is difficult in its written form; the draft understands it as Achish asking where David raided.

v12: Achish believes David has become a stench to Israel. The narration has just shown why Achish’s information is unreliable.

## Vocabulary

v07: A year and four months
The Hebrew combines “days” with four months; days is understood as a year in this duration.

v12: Stench
An image of becoming detestable to one’s people, preserved as Achish’s judgment.
