## David’s company

<p>
v01: David left there and escaped to the cave of Adullam. When his brothers and all his father’s household heard, they went down to him there.
v02: Everyone in distress, everyone in debt, and everyone bitter in spirit gathered around him. He became their leader, with about four hundred men under him.
</p>

<p>
v03: From there David went to Mizpeh in Moab. He asked Moab’s king, “Please let my father and mother come and stay with you until I know what God will do for me.”
v04: He brought them before the king of Moab, and they stayed with him as long as David remained in the stronghold.
</p>

<p>
v05: The prophet Gad told David, “Do not stay in the stronghold. Leave and go into Judah.” So David went to the forest of Hereth.
</p>

## Saul turns against the priests

<p>
v06: Saul heard that David and his men had been found. He was sitting at Gibeah under the tamarisk on the height, spear in hand, with all his servants standing around him.
v07: Saul said to the servants around him, “Listen, you Benjaminites! Will Jesse’s son give every one of you fields and vineyards? Will he make all of you commanders of thousands and hundreds?
</p>

<p>
v08: Is that why you have all conspired against me? No one tells me when my son makes a covenant with Jesse’s son. None of you feels sorry for me or tells me that my son has stirred up my servant to lie in wait for me, as he does today.”
</p>

<p>
v09: Doeg the Edomite, who stood with Saul’s servants, answered, “I saw Jesse’s son come to Ahimelech son of Ahitub at Nob.
v10: Ahimelech inquired of the LORD for him, gave him provisions, and gave him Goliath the Philistine’s sword.”
</p>

<p>
v11: The king summoned Ahimelech son of Ahitub the priest and all his father’s household, the priests at Nob. They all came to the king.
v12: Saul said, “Listen, son of Ahitub.” “Here I am, my lord,” he answered.
v13: Saul asked him, “Why have you and Jesse’s son conspired against me? You gave him bread and a sword and inquired of God for him, so he could rise against me and lie in wait, as he does today.”
</p>

<p>
v14: Ahimelech answered the king, “Who among all your servants is as faithful as David—your son-in-law, obedient to your commands, and honored in your house?
v15: Was today the first time I inquired of God for him? Far be it from me! Do not lay any charge on your servant or any of my father’s household. Your servant knew nothing of all this, great or small.”
</p>

<p>
v16: The king said, “You must die, Ahimelech—you and all your father’s household.”
v17: He ordered the guards around him, “Turn and kill the LORD’s priests! Their hand is with David too. They knew he was fleeing and did not tell me.” But the king’s servants refused to raise their hands against the LORD’s priests.
</p>

<p>
v18: Then the king told Doeg, “You turn and strike the priests.” Doeg the Edomite turned and attacked them. That day he killed eighty-five men who wore the linen ephod.
v19: He also put Nob, the priests’ town, to the sword: men and women, children and nursing infants, cattle, donkeys, and sheep—all put to the sword.
</p>

## Abiathar escapes

<p>
v20: But one son of Ahimelech son of Ahitub escaped. His name was Abiathar, and he fled to David.
v21: Abiathar told David that Saul had killed the LORD’s priests.
v22: David said to Abiathar, “That day, when Doeg the Edomite was there, I knew he would tell Saul. I am responsible for the deaths of everyone in your father’s household.
v23: Stay with me. Do not be afraid. Whoever seeks my life seeks yours too. You will be under my protection.”
</p>

## Notes

v09: Doeg’s position “over” or “beside” Saul’s servants can describe a role of oversight or where he stood. The draft leaves him standing with them.

v14: The phrase rendered “obedient to your commands” is difficult and has also been understood as a title concerning the royal guard.

v15: Ahimelech’s question about beginning to inquire for David denies a new conspiratorial act; it need not deny every earlier inquiry.

v18–19: The eighty-five priests and the killing of Nob’s women, children, nursing infants, and animals are retained explicitly.

v22: David acknowledges responsibility for the lives of Abiathar’s family. The draft does not turn his words into a claim that he personally carried out the killing.

## Vocabulary

v02: Bitter in spirit
People in deep distress join those burdened by debt; the expression need not describe a fixed personality.

v17: Runners
The royal guards who stand ready to carry out the king’s commands.
