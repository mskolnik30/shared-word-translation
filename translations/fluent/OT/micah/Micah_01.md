## The witness against the cities

<p>
v01: The word of the LORD came to Micah of Moresheth during the reigns of Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah—what he saw concerning Samaria and Jerusalem.
v02: Hear, all you peoples!
Listen, earth and everything in it!
Let the Lord GOD be a witness against you,
the Lord from his holy temple.
v03: Look, the LORD is coming out of his place.
He will come down
and tread the heights of the earth.
v04: The mountains will melt beneath him
and the valleys split apart,
like wax before fire,
like water pouring down a slope.
v05: All this is because of Jacob’s rebellion
and the sins of the house of Israel.
What is Jacob’s rebellion? Is it not Samaria?
What are Judah’s high places? Are they not Jerusalem?
v06: “I will make Samaria a heap in the open country,
a place for planting vineyards.
I will pour her stones into the valley
and lay her foundations bare.
v07: All her carved images will be smashed;
all her prostitute’s wages burned with fire.
I will devastate all her idols,
for she gathered them from a prostitute’s wages,
and to a prostitute’s wages they will return.”
</p>

## A lament for the towns

<p>
v08: Because of this I will lament and wail;
I will go stripped and naked.
I will lament like jackals
and mourn like ostriches.
v09: Her wound is incurable.
It has reached Judah;
it has touched the gate of my people,
Jerusalem itself.
v10: Do not tell it in Gath;
do not weep at all.
In Beth-leaphrah, roll yourself in dust.
v11: Pass on, inhabitant of Shaphir,
naked and shamed.
The inhabitant of Zaanan has not come out.
Beth-ezel’s mourning takes away its support from you.
v12: The inhabitant of Maroth writhes for good,
for disaster has come down from the LORD
to Jerusalem’s gate.
v13: Harness the chariot to the team, inhabitant of Lachish!
She was the beginning of sin for daughter Zion,
for Israel’s rebellions were found in you.
v14: So you will give parting gifts to Moresheth-gath.
The houses of Achzib will be a deception
to the kings of Israel.
v15: I will again bring a conqueror to you, inhabitant of Mareshah.
Israel’s glory will come to Adullam.
v16: Shave your head and cut off your hair
for the children you cherish.
Make your baldness wide as a vulture’s,
for they have gone into exile away from you.
</p>

## Notes

v10-15: The towns’ names participate in a dense series of soundplays: dust, beauty and shame, going out, bitterness, horses, deception, and possession. The names are retained rather than replaced by invented English towns. Several short clauses, especially concerning Beth-ezel, remain difficult.

v10,16: Verse 10 follows the traditional read imperative “roll yourself,” rather than the written first-person form. The bird in verse 16 is rendered vulture because of the baldness image; the Hebrew bird term also commonly denotes an eagle.

## Vocabulary

v07: **Prostitute’s wages**: The poem depicts wealth associated with false worship through a sexual-economic image.

v13: **Daughter Zion**: Jerusalem and its people portrayed as a woman.
