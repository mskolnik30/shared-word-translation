## The faithful have vanished

<p>
v01: Woe is me!
I am like someone after the summer fruit has been gathered,
after the vineyard has been gleaned:
no cluster to eat,
none of the first-ripe figs I crave.
v02: The faithful have vanished from the land;
no upright person remains among humanity.
All lie in wait for blood;
each hunts his brother with a net.
v03: Their hands are skilled at evil.
The ruler demands, the judge takes payment,
and the great man declares his own desire;
together they weave it into a scheme.
v04: The best of them is like a brier,
the most upright worse than a thorn hedge.
The day your watchmen announced, your reckoning, has come.
Now comes their confusion.
v05: Do not trust a neighbor
or put confidence in a friend.
Guard the doors of your mouth
even from the woman who lies in your embrace.
v06: A son treats his father with contempt;
a daughter rises against her mother,
a daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law.
A person’s enemies are the members of his own household.
</p>

## Waiting for God

<p>
v07: But I will watch for the LORD;
I will wait for the God who saves me.
My God will hear me.
v08: Do not rejoice over me, my enemy.
Though I have fallen, I will rise.
Though I sit in darkness, the LORD will be my light.
v09: I will bear the LORD’s indignation
because I have sinned against him,
until he pleads my case and secures justice for me.
He will bring me into the light;
I will see his righteousness.
v10: My enemy will see, and shame will cover her,
she who said to me, ‘Where is the LORD your God?’
My eyes will look upon her;
now she will be trampled like mud in the streets.
v11: A day for rebuilding your walls!
On that day the boundary will be pushed far out.
v12: On that day people will come to you
from Assyria and Egypt’s cities,
from Egypt to the River,
from sea to sea and mountain to mountain.
v13: Yet the earth will become desolate because of its inhabitants,
as the fruit of their deeds.
</p>

## Shepherd your people

<p>
v14: Shepherd your people with your staff,
the flock that is your inheritance,
living alone in woodland amid fruitful land.
Let them pasture in Bashan and Gilead as in ancient days.
v15: “As in the days when you came out of the land of Egypt,
I will show him wonders.”
v16: Nations will see and be ashamed despite all their might.
They will put a hand over their mouths;
their ears will become deaf.
v17: They will lick dust like a snake;
like creatures crawling on the ground,
they will come trembling from their strongholds.
They will turn in dread to the LORD our God
and be afraid of you.
</p>

## Who is a God like you?

<p>
v18: Who is a God like you,
forgiving guilt and passing over rebellion
for the remnant of his inheritance?
He does not hold on to anger forever,
because he delights in faithful love.
v19: He will again have compassion on us;
he will trample our guilt underfoot.
You will throw all their sins into the depths of the sea.
v20: You will show faithfulness to Jacob
and faithful love to Abraham,
as you swore to our ancestors in days long ago.
</p>

## Notes

v08-12: The enemy is grammatically feminine, like a personified city. The boundary/decree term in verse 11 and Egypt/fortified-city wording in verse 12 are uncertain. The draft preserves the geographical promise without adding a modern map.

v15,18-19: The source shifts between “you” and “him,” then between “our” guilt and “their” sins. These shifts are retained rather than silently harmonized.

## Vocabulary

v14: **Inheritance**: The people are pictured as the flock belonging to God.

v18: **Who is a God like you?**: The question echoes the meaning of Micah’s name and introduces the final praise of forgiveness.
