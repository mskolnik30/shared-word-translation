## Jacob’s words to his sons

<p>
v01: Jacob called his sons together and said, “Gather around, and I will tell you what will happen to you in days to come.”
</p>

<p>
v02: “Come together and listen, sons of Jacob;
listen to Israel your father.”
</p>

<p>
v03: “Reuben, you are my firstborn,
my strength, the first fruit of my vigor,
foremost in honor and foremost in power.
v04: Uncontrolled as water, you will no longer be foremost,
for you went up to your father’s bed
and defiled it—he went up to my couch!”
</p>

<p>
v05: “Simeon and Levi are brothers;
their swords are weapons of violence.
v06: Let me never enter their council;
let my honor never join their assembly.
In their anger they killed men;
at their pleasure they hamstrung oxen.
v07: Cursed be their anger, so fierce,
and their fury, so cruel!
I will divide them within Jacob
and scatter them within Israel.”
</p>

<p>
v08: “Judah, your brothers will praise you.
Your hand will be on your enemies’ necks;
your father’s sons will bow down to you.
v09: Judah is a lion’s cub.
You have come up from the prey, my son.
He crouches and lies down like a lion,
like a lioness—who dares rouse him?
v10: The scepter will not leave Judah,
nor the ruler’s staff from between his feet,
until Shiloh comes;
the obedience of peoples will be his.
v11: He ties his young donkey to a vine,
his donkey’s colt to a choice vine.
He washes his clothes in wine,
his robe in the blood of grapes.
v12: His eyes are dark with wine,
his teeth white with milk.”
</p>

<p>
v13: “Zebulun will live by the seashore,
a haven for ships,
with his far border toward Sidon.”
</p>

<p>
v14: “Issachar is a strong-boned donkey,
lying down between the saddlebags.
v15: He sees that rest is good
and that the land is pleasant.
He bends his shoulder to the load
and becomes a forced laborer.”
</p>

<p>
v16: “Dan will judge his people
as one of the tribes of Israel.
v17: Dan will be a snake beside the road,
a viper along the path,
biting the horse’s heels
so its rider falls backward.”
</p>

<p>
v18: “I wait for your salvation, LORD.”
</p>

<p>
v19: “Gad will be raided by raiders,
but he will raid them at their heels.”
</p>

<p>
v20: “Asher’s food will be rich;
he will provide delicacies fit for a king.”
</p>

<p>
v21: “Naphtali is a doe set free;
he gives beautiful words.”
</p>

<p>
v22: “Joseph is a fruitful branch,
a fruitful branch beside a spring,
its shoots climbing over a wall.
v23: Archers bitterly attacked him;
they shot at him and harassed him.
v24: But his bow stayed firm,
and his arms remained agile,
by the hands of the Mighty One of Jacob,
by the Shepherd, the Stone of Israel,
v25: by the God of your father, who helps you,
by the Almighty, who blesses you
with blessings of heaven above,
blessings of the deep lying below,
blessings of breasts and womb.
v26: Your father’s blessings surpass the blessings of my forebears,
reaching to the bounty of the everlasting hills.
May they rest on Joseph’s head,
on the brow of the one set apart from his brothers.”
</p>

<p>
v27: “Benjamin is a ravenous wolf.
In the morning he devours prey;
in the evening he divides the plunder.”
</p>

<p>
v28: All these are the twelve tribes of Israel. This is what their father said when he blessed them, giving each one his own blessing.
</p>

## Jacob’s burial instructions and death

<p>
v29: Then he gave them these instructions: “I am about to be gathered to my people. Bury me with my ancestors in the cave in the field of Ephron the Hittite,
v30: the cave in the field of Machpelah, facing Mamre in Canaan. Abraham bought the field from Ephron the Hittite as a burial property.
</p>

<p>
v31: There they buried Abraham and his wife Sarah. There they buried Isaac and his wife Rebekah. And there I buried Leah.
v32: The field and its cave were purchased from the Hittites.”
</p>

<p>
v33: When Jacob finished instructing his sons, he drew his feet up into the bed, breathed his last, and was gathered to his people.
</p>

## Notes

v04–07: The bed violation recalls Reuben and Bilhah (35:22); the violence recalls Simeon and Levi at Shechem (chapter 34). The sudden ‘he’ in verse 4 is in the Hebrew. ‘Swords’ in verse 5 translates an uncertain word.

v08, 16, 19: Hebrew links Judah with ‘praise,’ Dan with ‘judge,’ and Gad with the repeated sounds of ‘raid’ and ‘raiders.’ These wordplays connect each name to its saying.

v10: Shiloh is left untranslated because the wording is disputed. Proposed readings include ‘until tribute comes to him’ and ‘until the one to whom it belongs comes.’ The line itself does not explicitly name a later ruler.

v12, 14: Verse 12 can also be read comparatively: eyes darker than wine and teeth whiter than milk. ‘Saddlebags’ in verse 14 is uncertain; ‘sheepfolds’ is another reading.

v21: ‘Beautiful words’ follows the Hebrew vocalization. Another interpretation continues the deer image with ‘beautiful fawns.’

v22: The Hebrew is difficult. The familiar branch reading uses ‘son’ and ‘daughters’ for a branch and shoots; another interpretation pictures a wild donkey.

v24–25: These compressed lines join several divine titles. The phrase before ‘Shepherd’ literally begins ‘from there’; its relation to the surrounding clauses is uncertain.

v26: ‘My forebears’ follows the Hebrew text used here; many translations adopt ‘ancient mountains.’ ‘Set apart’ can suggest distinction among the brothers or separation from them.

v28: The narrator calls the whole set blessings, including its rebukes and judgments.

v29–33: Being ‘gathered to his people’ is a death expression, distinct from the later burial. The repeated field and cave details connect the request with Abraham’s purchase in chapter 23.

## Vocabulary

v10: Scepter — shevet
A staff that can signify rule. The same Hebrew word also means ‘tribe,’ as in verses 16 and 28.

v18: Salvation — yeshuʿah
Rescue or deliverance. The short prayer interrupts the sayings about the sons.

v24: Stone — ʾeven
The ordinary word for a stone, used here as a divine title alongside Shepherd and Mighty One.

v25: Deep — tehom
The watery deep, also named at the opening of Genesis. Here it belongs to the imagery of blessing from above and below.
