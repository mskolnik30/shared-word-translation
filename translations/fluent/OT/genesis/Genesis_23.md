## The Death of Sarah
<p>
v01: Sarah lived one hundred twenty-seven years; these were the years of the life of Sarah.
v02: Sarah died in Kiriath-arba (that is, Hebron) in the land of Canaan, and Abraham went in to mourn for Sarah and to weep for her.
</p>

## Abraham Requests a Burial Place
<p>
v03: Then Abraham rose from before his dead and spoke to the Hittites, saying,
v04: “I am a sojourner and a foreigner among you. Give me property among you for a burial place, so that I may bury my dead out of my sight.”
</p>

<p>
v05: The Hittites answered Abraham,
v06: “Hear us, my lord. You are a prince of God among us. Bury your dead in the choicest of our tombs. None of us will withhold from you his tomb to prevent you from burying your dead.”
</p>

<p>
v07: Abraham rose and bowed to the people of the land, the Hittites.
v08: And he spoke with them, saying, “If it is your wish that I bury my dead out of my sight, hear me and intercede for me with Ephron the son of Zohar,
v09: that he may give me the cave of Machpelah, which belongs to him and is at the end of his field. Let him give it to me for its full price among you as property for a burial place.”
</p>

## The Purchase of the Cave of Machpelah
<p>
v10: Now Ephron was sitting among the Hittites. And Ephron the Hittite answered Abraham in the hearing of the Hittites, of everyone who entered the gate of his city, saying,
v11: “No, my lord, hear me. I give you the field, and I give you the cave that is in it. In the sight of the sons of my people I give it to you. Bury your dead.”
</p>

<p>
v12: Then Abraham bowed before the people of the land.
v13: And he spoke to Ephron in the hearing of the people of the land, saying, “If only you would hear me. I will give the price of the field. Take it from me, so that I may bury my dead there.”
</p>

<p>
v14: Ephron answered Abraham,
v15: “My lord, listen to me. A piece of land worth four hundred shekels of silver—what is that between me and you? Bury your dead.”
v16: Abraham listened to Ephron, and Abraham weighed out for Ephron the silver that he had named in the hearing of the Hittites, four hundred shekels of silver according to the standard weight current among the merchants.
</p>

<p>
v17: So the field of Ephron in Machpelah, which was east of Mamre, the field with the cave that was in it and all the trees that were in the field, throughout its whole boundary, was transferred
v18: to Abraham as a possession in the sight of the Hittites, before everyone who entered the gate of his city.
</p>

## Sarah Buried in Canaan
<p>
v19: After this, Abraham buried Sarah his wife in the cave of the field of Machpelah east of Mamre (that is, Hebron) in the land of Canaan.
v20: Thus the field and the cave that is in it were transferred to Abraham from the Hittites as property for a burial place.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Sarah is the first woman in Scripture whose age at death is recorded.

v02: Abraham’s mourning is described plainly and personally. The narrative gives space to grief before turning to practical concerns.

v04: Abraham identifies himself as both a sojourner and a foreigner despite the promises concerning the land.

v06: The Hittites honor Abraham publicly, calling him a “prince of God,” even though he does not yet possess the land.

v08–09: Abraham seeks legal ownership rather than temporary permission, emphasizing permanence and security.

v10–16: The negotiation follows customary public procedures. What appears as a gift becomes a formal purchase.

v15: Ephron’s mention of the price is framed politely, but the amount is substantial.

v16: The silver is weighed rather than counted, reflecting ancient commercial practice.

v17–18: The repeated legal details underscore the legitimacy of Abraham’s ownership.

v19–20: The burial site becomes the first piece of the promised land that Abraham actually possesses.

v20: The chapter ends not with conquest or inheritance but with a purchased grave, holding promise and mortality together.

## Vocabulary

v02: סָפַד (sāphaḏ)
“To mourn” or “lament.” Public and personal expressions of grief for the dead.

v04: גֵּר (gēr)
“Sojourner.” One who resides in a land without permanent native status.

v04: תּוֹשָׁב (tôšāḇ)
“Foreigner” or “resident.” A person dwelling among a people without full possession rights.

v04: אֲחֻזָּה (ʾăḥuzzâ)
“Property” or “holding.” A legally recognized possession of land.

v06: נְשִׂיא אֱלֹהִים (nĕśîʾ ʾĕlōhîm)
“Prince of God.” A title expressing honor and recognized standing.

v09: מַכְפֵּלָה (Maḵpēlāh)
“Machpelah.” The name of the cave and field purchased by Abraham.

v13: כֶּסֶף (keseph)
“Silver.” The standard medium of exchange in many ancient transactions.

v16: שֶׁקֶל (šeqel)
“Shekel.” A unit of weight commonly used in commerce.

v18: קָנָה (qānâ)
“To acquire” or “purchase.” A legal term for obtaining possession.

v19: קָבַר (qāḇar)
“To bury.” The ordinary verb for placing the dead in a grave or tomb.

v20: שָׂדֶה (śāḏeh)
“Field.” Agricultural land, often including associated structures or boundaries.

v20: אֲחֻזַּת־קָבֶר (ʾăḥuzzat-qāḇer)
“Property for a burial place.” A permanent family burial holding.
