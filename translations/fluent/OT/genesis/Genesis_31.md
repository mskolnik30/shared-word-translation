## The Command to Return

<p>
v01: Jacob heard Laban’s sons saying, “Jacob has taken everything that belonged to our father. He built all this wealth from what was our father’s.”
v02: Jacob noticed that Laban no longer regarded him as he had before.
</p>

<p>
v03: The LORD said to Jacob, “Return to the land of your ancestors and your family, and I will be with you.”
</p>

## Rachel and Leah Agree to Leave

<p>
v04: Jacob sent for Rachel and Leah and asked them to meet him in the field where his flock was.
v05: He told them, “I can see that your father no longer regards me as he did before. But my father’s God has been with me.
v06: You both know that I have worked for your father with all my strength.
</p>

<p>
v07: Yet your father cheated me and changed my wages ten times. But God did not allow him to harm me.
v08: If he said, ‘The speckled animals will be your wages,’ the whole flock bore speckled young. If he said, ‘The streaked will be your wages,’ the whole flock bore streaked young.
v09: In this way God took your father’s livestock away and gave them to me.
</p>

<p>
v10: During the flock’s mating season, I looked up in a dream and saw that the male goats mounting the flock were streaked, speckled, and mottled.
</p>

<p>
v11: God’s messenger said to me in the dream, ‘Jacob!’ ‘Here I am,’ I answered.
v12: The messenger said, ‘Look up and see: all the male goats mounting the flock are streaked, speckled, and mottled. I have seen everything Laban has been doing to you.
v13: I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed a pillar and made a vow to me. Now get up, leave this land, and return to the land of your birth.’”
</p>

<p>
v14: Rachel and Leah answered him together, “Do we still have any share or inheritance in our father’s household?
v15: Doesn’t he treat us as foreigners? He sold us and then consumed the money paid for us.
v16: All the wealth God has taken from our father belongs to us and our children. So do everything God has told you.”
</p>

## Jacob Leaves Paddan-aram

<p>
v17: Jacob got up and placed his children and wives on the camels.
v18: He drove all his livestock ahead of him and took all the property he had acquired in Paddan-aram. He set out for his father Isaac in the land of Canaan.
</p>

<p>
v19: While Laban was away shearing his sheep, Rachel stole her father’s household gods.
v20: Jacob stole away from Laban the Aramean by not telling him that he was fleeing.
v21: Jacob fled with everything he had, crossed the Euphrates, and headed toward the hill country of Gilead.
</p>

## Laban Pursues Jacob

<p>
v22: On the third day Laban was told that Jacob had fled.
v23: Laban took his relatives with him and pursued Jacob for seven days. He caught up with him in the hill country of Gilead.
</p>

<p>
v24: God came to Laban the Aramean in a dream that night and warned him, “Be careful not to make any pronouncement to Jacob, whether good or bad.”
</p>

<p>
v25: Laban overtook Jacob. Jacob had pitched his tent in the hill country, and Laban camped there with his relatives in the hill country of Gilead.
</p>

<p>
v26: Laban said to Jacob, “What have you done? You stole away without my knowledge and drove my daughters off like women captured in war.
v27: Why did you flee in secret and deceive me? Why didn’t you tell me? I would have sent you away with joy and songs, with tambourine and lyre.
v28: You did not let me kiss my grandchildren and daughters goodbye. What you have done is foolish.
</p>

<p>
v29: I have the power to harm you. But your father’s God told me last night, ‘Be careful not to make any pronouncement to Jacob, whether good or bad.’
v30: I understand that you left because you deeply longed for your father’s home. But why did you steal my gods?”
</p>

## The Search for the Household Gods

<p>
v31: Jacob answered Laban, “I left secretly because I was afraid you would take your daughters from me by force.
v32: But whoever has your gods will not live. Before our relatives, identify anything here that belongs to you and take it.” Jacob did not know that Rachel had stolen them.
</p>

<p>
v33: Laban entered Jacob’s tent, Leah’s tent, and the tent of the two slave women, but found nothing. He left Leah’s tent and entered Rachel’s.
</p>

<p>
v34: Rachel had put the household gods inside a camel saddle and was sitting on them. Laban felt through the entire tent but did not find them.
</p>

<p>
v35: Rachel said to her father, “Do not be angry, my lord, that I cannot stand before you. I am having my period.” He searched but did not find the household gods.
</p>

## Jacob Confronts Laban

<p>
v36: Jacob became angry and confronted Laban. “What is my offense?” he demanded. “What sin have I committed that you pursued me so fiercely?
v37: You have felt through all my possessions. What have you found that belongs to your household? Put it here before my relatives and yours, and let them decide between us.
</p>

<p>
v38: I was with you for twenty years. Your ewes and female goats did not miscarry, and I did not eat the rams from your flock.
v39: I did not bring you animals torn by predators; I absorbed the loss myself. You demanded repayment from me whether an animal was stolen by day or by night.
v40: By day the heat consumed me, and by night the cold. Sleep fled from my eyes.
</p>

<p>
v41: For twenty years I served in your household: fourteen years for your two daughters and six for your flock. Yet you changed my wages ten times.
v42: If my father’s God—the God of Abraham and the Fear of Isaac—had not been with me, you would have sent me away empty-handed. God saw my suffering and the labor of my hands, and last night he decided the case.”
</p>

## A Covenant and Boundary

<p>
v43: Laban answered Jacob, “These daughters are my daughters, these children my children, and these flocks my flocks. Everything you see is mine. But what can I do today to these daughters of mine or to the children they have borne?
v44: Come now, let you and me make a covenant, and let it serve as a witness between us.”
</p>

<p>
v45: Jacob took a stone and set it upright as a pillar.
</p>

<p>
v46: Then Jacob told his relatives, “Gather stones.” They piled the stones into a heap and ate there beside it.
</p>

<p>
v47: Laban called the heap Jegar-sahadutha, while Jacob called it Galeed.
</p>

<p>
v48: Laban said, “This heap is a witness between you and me today.” That is why it was named Galeed.
</p>

<p>
v49: It was also called Mizpah, because Laban said, “May the LORD watch between you and me when we are out of each other’s sight.
v50: If you mistreat my daughters or take other wives in addition to them, even though no one else is with us, remember: God is witness between you and me.”
</p>

<p>
v51: Laban continued, “Look at this heap and this pillar that I have set between you and me.
v52: This heap and pillar are witnesses: I will not cross beyond this heap toward you, and you will not cross beyond this heap and pillar toward me, to do harm.
</p>

<p>
v53: May the God of Abraham and the god of Nahor—the gods of their father—judge between us.” Jacob swore by the Fear of his father Isaac.
</p>

<p>
v54: Jacob offered a sacrifice on the mountain and invited his relatives to share the meal. They ate and spent the night on the mountain.
</p>

<p>
v55: Early the next morning Laban kissed his grandchildren and daughters and blessed them. Then he left and returned home.
</p>

## Notes

v07: ‘Ten times’ can mean ten occurrences or a full measure of repeated change. Jacob uses the same claim again in verse 41.

v10–13: Jacob’s dream attributes the marked offspring and protection from Laban to God. Compare the branch and breeding practices in 30:37–42.

v19–21: Rachel steals physical household gods. Jacob ‘steals away’ by leaving without telling Laban. The repeated theft language links two actions whose full circumstances are different.

v19: The teraphim are household religious objects. Rachel’s motive for taking them—religious, legal, economic, or personal—is not given.

v24, 29: The expression is literally “from good to bad.” Its scope is disputed. “Make any pronouncement” represents one interpretation; a broader rendering is “speak to Jacob, either good or bad.”

v32: Jacob does not know Rachel stole the household gods when he says the person possessing them will not live. The narrative does not state that his words cause Rachel’s later death.

v35: Rachel says she is menstruating. The text reports her explanation and concealment without telling readers whether the claim itself is true.

v38–42: Jacob’s defense names twenty years of labor: fourteen for Laban’s daughters and six for the flock. He describes bearing losses, heat, cold, and sleeplessness. ‘The Fear of Isaac’ is a distinctive title for Isaac’s God.

v47–53: Jegar-sahadutha is Aramaic and Galeed is related to Hebrew; both mean “heap of witness.” Mizpah means watchpost. The monument marks a boundary under divine witness.

v53: The verb ‘judge’ is plural, fitting Laban’s appeal to the God of Abraham and the god of Nahor as the gods of their father. Jacob instead swears by ‘the Fear of Isaac.’

v55: English Genesis 31:55 corresponds to Hebrew Genesis 32:1.

## Vocabulary

v13: בֵּית־אֵל (Bethel)
House of God; the place where Jacob set up a pillar and made his vow.

v19: תְּרָפִים (teraphim)
Household gods or religious images; the term is plural in form.

v24: טוֹב עַד־רָע (tov ad-ra)
From good to bad; the scope of the speech prohibited here is disputed.

v35: דֶּרֶךְ נָשִׁים (derekh nashim)
The way of women: an idiom for menstruation.

v42: פַּחַד יִצְחָק (Pachad Yitschaq)
The Fear of Isaac, a distinctive title for Isaac’s God.

v44: בְּרִית (berit)
Covenant: a binding relationship or agreement, here marking limits between estranged households.

v47: יְגַר שָׂהֲדוּתָא / גַּלְעֵד (Yegar Sahaduta / Gal-ed)
Aramaic and Hebrew-related names meaning heap of witness.

v49: מִצְפָּה (Mizpah)
Watchpost, associated with Laban’s request that the LORD watch the boundary.
