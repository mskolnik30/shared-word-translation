## Descendants beyond Counting

<p>
v01: After these events, the LORD's word came to Abram in a vision: “Do not be afraid, Abram. I am your shield. Your reward will be very great.”
</p>

<p>
v02: Abram said, “Lord GOD, what can you give me? I remain childless, and the heir of my household is Eliezer of Damascus.”
v03: Abram continued, “You have given me no descendants, so a member of my household will inherit from me.”
</p>

<p>
v04: Then the LORD's word came to him: “This man will not be your heir. Someone born from your own body will inherit from you.”
v05: God brought him outside and said, “Look up at the sky and count the stars, if you can.” Then God said, “That is how your descendants will be.”
</p>

<p>
v06: Abram trusted the LORD, and the LORD counted it to him as righteousness.
</p>

## The Covenant and the Land

<p>
v07: God said to him, “I am the LORD, who brought you out of Ur of the Chaldeans to give you this land to possess.”
</p>

<p>
v08: Abram asked, “Lord GOD, how can I know that I will possess it?”
</p>

<p>
v09: God said, “Bring me a three-year-old heifer, a three-year-old female goat, a three-year-old ram, a turtledove, and a young pigeon.”
v10: Abram brought all these, cut the animals in two, and placed each half opposite the other. He did not cut the birds in two.
v11: Birds of prey swooped down on the carcasses, and Abram drove them away.
</p>

<p>
v12: As the sun was going down, Abram fell into a deep sleep, and a great, dark terror fell over him.
</p>

<p>
v13: God said to Abram, “Know this for certain: your descendants will live as strangers in a land that is not theirs. They will be enslaved and oppressed for four hundred years.
v14: But I will judge the nation they serve, and afterward they will leave with great possessions.
v15: You yourself will go to your ancestors in peace and be buried at a good old age.
v16: In the fourth generation they will return here, because the Amorites' wrongdoing has not yet reached its full measure.”
</p>

<p>
v17: When the sun had set and darkness had come, a smoking firepot and a flaming torch passed between the pieces.
</p>

<p>
v18: That day the LORD made a covenant with Abram: “To your descendants I have given this land, from the river of Egypt to the great river, the Euphrates:
v19: the land of the Kenites, Kenizzites, Kadmonites,
v20: Hittites, Perizzites, Rephaim,
v21: Amorites, Canaanites, Girgashites, and Jebusites.”
</p>

## Notes

v01–04: Shield remains a protective image. The reward clause may also describe God as Abram’s reward. The wording about Eliezer’s household position is difficult; “heir” follows the inheritance concern of vv03–04.

v05–06: Trust and righteousness remain distinct terms. The Hebrew leaves the subject of “counted” and the recipient unnamed. The draft follows the reading that the LORD counts Abram’s trust as righteousness; the reversed assignment has also been proposed.

v09–12: The three mammals are described with a term conventionally read as three years old. The birds are not divided. Deep sleep and fearful darkness are retained without adding an explanation of Abram’s inner state.

v13–16: Four hundred years and the fourth generation are both preserved. The first duration’s relation to residence, enslavement, and oppression is not resolved by a supplied chronology.

v17–21: Making a covenant is literally cutting a covenant, beside the account of divided animals. The smoking firepot and torch pass between the pieces; Abram is not said to do so. The river of Egypt is not replaced by a more specific watercourse in the translation.
