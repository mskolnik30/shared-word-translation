## Grain in Egypt

<p>
v01: When Jacob learned that grain was available in Egypt, he said to his sons, “Why are you just looking at one another?”
v02: “I have heard there is grain in Egypt,” he continued. “Go down there and buy some for us, so we can live and not die.”
</p>

<p>
v03: So ten of Joseph’s brothers went down to Egypt to buy grain.
v04: But Jacob did not send Joseph’s brother Benjamin with the others, because he thought, “Something terrible might happen to him.”
v05: Israel’s sons came to buy grain along with the others who were coming, for there was famine in Canaan too.
</p>

## Joseph Recognizes His Brothers

<p>
v06: Joseph was the governor of the land, the one who sold grain to all its people. His brothers came and bowed before him with their faces to the ground.
v07: Joseph saw his brothers and recognized them, but he acted like a stranger and spoke harshly to them. “Where have you come from?” he asked. “From Canaan, to buy food,” they answered.
v08: Joseph recognized his brothers, but they did not recognize him.
</p>

<p>
v09: Joseph remembered the dreams he had dreamed about them. He said, “You are spies! You have come to see where the land lies exposed.”
v10: “No, my lord,” they answered. “Your servants have come to buy food.
v11: We are all sons of one man. We are honest men; your servants have never been spies.”
v12: “No,” he said. “You have come to see where the land lies exposed.”
v13: They said, “Your servants are twelve brothers, sons of one man in Canaan. The youngest is with our father now, and one is no longer with us.”
</p>

<p>
v14: Joseph replied, “It is just as I said: you are spies!
v15: Here is how you will be tested. As surely as Pharaoh lives, you will not leave this place unless your youngest brother comes here.
v16: Send one of you to bring your brother, while the rest remain in custody. Your words will be tested to see whether you are telling the truth. If not, as surely as Pharaoh lives, you are spies!”
v17: He put them all in custody for three days.
</p>

## One Brother Must Stay

<p>
v18: On the third day Joseph said, “Do this, and you will live, for I fear God.
v19: If you are honest men, let one brother remain here in custody. The rest of you may go and take grain home to your hungry households.
v20: Then bring your youngest brother to me, so your words can be confirmed and you will not die.” They did so.
</p>

<p>
v21: They said to one another, “We really are guilty because of what we did to our brother. We saw his anguish when he pleaded with us, but we would not listen. That is why this anguish has come upon us.”
v22: Reuben answered, “Didn’t I tell you not to sin against the boy? But you would not listen. Now we are being called to account for his blood.”
v23: They did not know that Joseph understood them, because an interpreter stood between them.
</p>

<p>
v24: Joseph turned away from them and wept. Then he returned and spoke to them. He took Simeon from among them and bound him before their eyes.
</p>

## Silver in the Sacks

<p>
v25: Joseph ordered their bags filled with grain, each man’s silver returned to his sack, and provisions supplied for the journey. This was done for them.
v26: They loaded the grain onto their donkeys and left.
</p>

<p>
v27: At the place where they stopped for the night, one of them opened his sack to feed his donkey and saw his silver there at the mouth of the sack.
v28: “My silver has been returned!” he told his brothers. “Look, it is here in my sack!” Their hearts sank, and they turned to one another trembling. “What has God done to us?” they asked.
</p>

## The Brothers Return to Jacob

<p>
v29: When they reached their father Jacob in Canaan, they told him everything that had happened:
v30: “The man who rules the land spoke harshly to us and treated us as spies.
v31: We told him, ‘We are honest men. We have never been spies.
v32: We are twelve brothers, sons of the same father. One is no longer with us, and the youngest is with our father in Canaan.’
v33: Then the man who rules the land said, ‘This is how I will know you are honest. Leave one brother with me, take grain for your hungry households, and go.
v34: Bring your youngest brother to me. Then I will know you are honest men, not spies. I will give your brother back, and you may trade in the land.’”
</p>

<p>
v35: As they emptied their sacks, each man found his pouch of silver inside. When they and their father saw the pouches of silver, they were afraid.
v36: Their father Jacob said, “You are robbing me of my children! Joseph is gone. Simeon is gone. Now you would take Benjamin. All of this falls on me!”
</p>

<p>
v37: Reuben said to his father, “You may put my two sons to death if I do not bring Benjamin back to you. Entrust him to me, and I will return him.”
v38: But Jacob said, “My son will not go down with you. His brother is dead, and he alone is left. If disaster strikes him on your journey, you will bring my gray head down to Sheol in grief.”
</p>

## Notes

v07–08: Joseph recognizes his brothers but conceals his identity. The repeated recognition language continues the pattern of the robe and Tamar’s identifying objects in chapters 37–38.

v09, 12: Where the land lies exposed: literally, the land’s nakedness. Joseph accuses them of looking for its vulnerable places.

v13, 32: One is no longer with us: literally, one is not. The expression can refer to death or disappearance; the brothers do not explicitly say “dead” here.

v21–23: The brothers’ recollection adds Joseph’s pleading, which chapter 37 did not describe. Their repeated failure to listen contrasts with Joseph’s ability to understand their speech.

v27, 35: One brother discovers his silver at the lodging place; all discover their pouches when they empty their sacks at home. Their retelling in 43:21 places the collective discovery at the lodging place. The accounts are left as written.

v38: He alone is left refers to Jacob’s surviving son by Rachel, not to his only living son. “My gray head” evokes Jacob’s old age, and Sheol is the realm of the dead.

## Vocabulary

v02: שֶׁבֶר (shever)
Grain available for purchase; buying grain drives the repeated journeys.

v07: נָכַר (nakhar)
To recognize; a related form here describes acting as a stranger.

v09: עֶרְוָה (ʿervah)
Nakedness; here an image of the land’s vulnerability.

v21: אָשֵׁם (ʾashem)
Guilty; the brothers apply it to their treatment of Joseph.

v23: מֵלִיץ (melits)
Interpreter or intermediary.

v38: שְׁאוֹל (Sheol)
The realm of the dead.
