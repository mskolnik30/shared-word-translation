## Joseph and His Brothers

<p>
v01: Jacob lived in the land of Canaan, where his father had lived as a resident foreigner.
</p>

<p>
v02: This is the family account of Jacob. Joseph was seventeen years old and tended the flock with his brothers. He worked as a young helper with the sons of Bilhah and Zilpah, his father’s wives, and he brought their father a bad report about them.
v03: Now Israel loved Joseph more than any of his other sons because Joseph was the son of his old age, and he made him a special long robe.
v04: When his brothers saw that their father loved him more than any of them, they hated him and could not speak a peaceful word to him.
</p>

## Joseph’s Dreams

<p>
v05: Then Joseph had a dream. When he told it to his brothers, they hated him even more.
v06: He said to them, “Listen to this dream I had.
v07: We were binding sheaves in the field when suddenly my sheaf rose and stood upright. Then your sheaves gathered around mine and bowed down to it.”
v08: His brothers said, “Are you really going to reign over us? Will you actually rule us?” So they hated him even more because of his dreams and what he said.
</p>

<p>
v09: He had another dream and told his brothers: “Listen, I have had another dream. The sun, the moon, and eleven stars were bowing down to me.”
v10: When he told his father and brothers, his father rebuked him: “What kind of dream is this? Are your mother, your brothers, and I really going to come and bow to the ground before you?”
v11: His brothers were jealous of him, but his father kept the matter in mind.
</p>

## Joseph Sent to His Brothers

<p>
v12: Now his brothers went to pasture their father’s flock near Shechem.
v13: Israel said to Joseph, “Your brothers are pasturing the flock near Shechem, aren’t they? Come, I want to send you to them.” Joseph answered, “I’m ready.”
v14: “Go and see how your brothers and the flock are doing,” Israel said, “and bring word back to me.” So he sent Joseph from the Valley of Hebron, and Joseph came to Shechem.
</p>

<p>
v15: A man found him wandering in the fields and asked, “What are you looking for?”
v16: “I’m looking for my brothers,” Joseph answered. “Please tell me where they are tending the flock.”
v17: “They have moved on,” the man said. “I heard them say, ‘Let’s go to Dothan.’” Joseph followed his brothers and found them near Dothan.
</p>

## The Brothers’ Plot

<p>
v18: They saw him in the distance, and before he reached them they plotted together to kill him.
v19: They said to one another, “Look—here comes the master of dreams!
v20: Come on, let’s kill him and throw him into one of these pits. We can say that a wild animal devoured him. Then we’ll see what becomes of his dreams.”
</p>

<p>
v21: When Reuben heard this, he intervened to rescue Joseph from their hands. “We must not take his life,” he said.
v22: “Do not shed blood,” Reuben told them. “Throw him into this pit in the wilderness, but do not lay a hand on him.” Reuben intended to rescue him from their hands and return him to his father.
</p>

<p>
v23: When Joseph reached his brothers, they stripped off his robe—the special long robe he was wearing—
v24: and they took him and threw him into the pit. The pit was empty; there was no water in it.
</p>

## Joseph Sold

<p>
v25: Then they sat down to eat. Looking up, they saw a caravan of Ishmaelites coming from Gilead. Their camels were carrying gum, balm, and resin down to Egypt.
v26: Judah said to his brothers, “What do we gain by killing our brother and covering up his blood?
v27: Come, let’s sell him to the Ishmaelites instead of laying a hand on him. After all, he is our brother, our own flesh.” His brothers agreed.
v28: When Midianite merchants passed by, Joseph was pulled and lifted out of the pit, and he was sold to the Ishmaelites for twenty pieces of silver. They took Joseph to Egypt.
</p>

## Jacob Is Deceived

<p>
v29: When Reuben returned to the pit and saw that Joseph was not there, he tore his clothes.
v30: He went back to his brothers and cried, “The boy is gone! And I—where can I go now?”
</p>

<p>
v31: They took Joseph’s robe, slaughtered a young goat, and dipped the robe in its blood.
v32: They had the special robe taken to their father with the message, “We found this. Please recognize whether it is your son’s robe or not.”
v33: He recognized it and said, “It is my son’s robe! A wild animal has devoured him. Joseph has surely been torn to pieces.”
v34: Jacob tore his clothes, wrapped sackcloth around his waist, and mourned for his son for many days.
v35: All his sons and daughters came to comfort him, but he refused their comfort. “No,” he said, “I will go down to my son in Sheol mourning.” And his father continued to weep for him.
</p>

<p>
v36: Meanwhile, the Medanites sold Joseph in Egypt to Potiphar, one of Pharaoh’s officials and captain of the guard.
</p>

## Notes

v02: The Hebrew calls Joseph’s report “bad” but does not say whether it was false; the moral ambiguity remains.

v03: The rare garment expression may describe a long robe, an ornamented robe, a robe with sleeves, or a multicolored robe. The robe clearly marks Joseph out for special status.

v10: Jacob interprets the moon as Joseph’s mother even though the preceding narrative has reported Rachel’s death. The text does not explain the tension.

v28, 36: The account names Ishmaelites and Midianites in verse 28; the Hebrew text used here names Medanites in verse 36. The relationship among these names is not explained. In verse 28 the subject of “pulled,” “lifted,” and “sold” is not explicitly identified, leaving the sellers’ identity open to interpretation.

v32: “Please recognize” is the same appeal Tamar will make to Judah in 38:25, when she presents his own identifying objects.

v35: Sheol is the realm of the dead in biblical Hebrew; Jacob expects to go there mourning.

v36: The word translated “official” can also mean “eunuch,” but it does not require that meaning in every court office. “Captain of the guard” is literally captain of the slaughterers.

## Vocabulary

v03: כְּתֹנֶת פַּסִּים (ketonet passim)
A distinctive robe; its precise design is uncertain.

v05: חֲלוֹם (ḥalom)
Dream.

v19: בַּעַל הַחֲלֹמוֹת (baʿal haḥalomot)
Master or owner of dreams, spoken mockingly.

v20: בּוֹר (bor)
Pit or cistern; later also used of Joseph’s prison.

v26: בֶּצַע (betsaʿ)
Profit or gain.

v35: שְׁאוֹל (Sheol)
The realm of the dead.
