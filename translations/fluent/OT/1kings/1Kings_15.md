## Abijam and Asa in Judah

<p>
v01: In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam son of Nebat, Abijam became king over Judah.
v02: He ruled three years in Jerusalem. His mother was Maacah daughter of Abishalom.
v03: He followed all the sins his father had committed before him. His heart was not wholly with the LORD his God, as his ancestor David’s heart had been.
</p>

<p>
v04: Yet for David’s sake the LORD his God gave him a lamp in Jerusalem, raising up his son after him and preserving Jerusalem.
v05: For David had done what was right in the LORD’s eyes and had not turned from anything he commanded him all his life—except in the matter of Uriah the Hittite.
v06: There was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam throughout his lifetime.
</p>

<p>
v07: The rest of Abijam’s history and all he did—are they not written in the Book of the Annals of Judah’s Kings? There was war between Abijam and Jeroboam.
v08: Abijam lay down with his ancestors, and they buried him in the City of David. His son Asa became king in his place.
</p>

<p>
v09: In the twentieth year of Jeroboam king of Israel, Asa became king of Judah.
v10: He ruled forty-one years in Jerusalem. His mother was Maacah daughter of Abishalom.
v11: Asa did what was right in the LORD’s eyes, as his ancestor David had.
</p>

<p>
v12: He removed the male shrine attendants from the land and removed all the idols his ancestors had made.
v13: He even removed his mother Maacah from her position as queen mother because she had made a monstrous image for Asherah. Asa cut down her image and burned it in the Kidron Valley.
v14: The high places were not removed. Yet Asa’s heart was wholly with the LORD all his days.
v15: He brought into the LORD’s house the things his father had dedicated and his own dedicated gifts: silver, gold, and utensils.
</p>

## Asa and Baasha at war

<p>
v16: There was war between Asa and Baasha king of Israel throughout their days.
v17: Baasha king of Israel attacked Judah and fortified Ramah to prevent anyone from going out or coming in to Asa king of Judah.
</p>

<p>
v18: Asa took all the silver and gold left in the treasuries of the LORD’s house and the king’s house and entrusted it to his servants. He sent them to Ben-hadad son of Tabrimmon son of Hezion, king of Aram, who lived in Damascus, with this message:
v19: “There is a covenant between me and you, as there was between my father and yours. I am sending you a gift of silver and gold. Break your covenant with Baasha king of Israel so he will withdraw from me.”
</p>

<p>
v20: Ben-hadad listened to King Asa and sent his army commanders against Israel’s towns. He struck Ijon, Dan, Abel-beth-maacah, and all Chinneroth, together with all the land of Naphtali.
v21: When Baasha heard, he stopped fortifying Ramah and stayed in Tirzah.
</p>

<p>
v22: King Asa summoned all Judah, with no one exempt. They carried away the stones and timber Baasha had used at Ramah. With them Asa fortified Geba of Benjamin and Mizpah.
</p>

<p>
v23: The rest of Asa’s history, all his mighty deeds, all he did, and the towns he built—are they not written in the Book of the Annals of Judah’s Kings? In his old age, however, he developed a disease in his feet.
v24: Asa lay down with his ancestors and was buried with them in the city of his ancestor David. His son Jehoshaphat became king in his place.
</p>

## Nadab and Baasha in Israel

<p>
v25: Nadab son of Jeroboam became king over Israel in the second year of Asa king of Judah. He ruled Israel for two years.
v26: He did evil in the LORD’s eyes, following his father’s way and the sin by which his father had led Israel into sin.
</p>

<p>
v27: Baasha son of Ahijah, from Issachar’s house, conspired against him. Baasha struck him down at Gibbethon, which belonged to the Philistines, while Nadab and all Israel were besieging it.
v28: Baasha killed him in the third year of Asa king of Judah and became king in his place.
</p>

<p>
v29: As soon as he became king, he struck down Jeroboam’s whole house. He left Jeroboam no one breathing, destroying them all, just as the LORD had said through his servant Ahijah of Shiloh.
v30: This happened because of Jeroboam’s sins, which he committed and led Israel to commit, provoking the LORD, Israel’s God, to anger.
</p>

<p>
v31: The rest of Nadab’s history and all he did—are they not written in the Book of the Annals of Israel’s Kings?
v32: There was war between Asa and Baasha king of Israel throughout their days.
</p>

<p>
v33: In the third year of Asa king of Judah, Baasha son of Ahijah became king over all Israel at Tirzah. He ruled twenty-four years.
v34: He did evil in the LORD’s eyes, following Jeroboam’s way and the sin by which Jeroboam had led Israel into sin.
</p>

## Notes

v02,10: Both Abijam and Asa are associated with Maacah daughter of Abishalom as “mother.” The term can express a broader ancestral or dynastic relation; the main text does not replace it with “grandmother.”

v06: The pinned Hebrew names Rehoboam here, even in the account of Abijam. The pronoun in “his lifetime” is not fully resolved.

v12–13: “Male shrine attendants” follows the same choice as 14:24. The precise form of Maacah’s “monstrous image” for Asherah is uncertain.

v14: Asa’s wholehearted loyalty and the survival of the high places appear side by side.

v27: Baasha’s father Ahijah is named without identifying him as Ahijah the prophet. No such relationship is supplied.

## Vocabulary

v04: Lamp
The image of a continuing royal line, also used in 11:36.

v19: Covenant
Here a political agreement. Asa asks Ben-hadad to break one agreement in favor of another.
