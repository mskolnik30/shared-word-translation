## Elijah and the drought

<p>
v01: Elijah the Tishbite, one of the inhabitants of Gilead, said to Ahab, “As the LORD, Israel’s God, lives, whom I serve, there will be neither dew nor rain these years except at my word.”
</p>

<p>
v02: The word of the LORD came to him:
v03: “Leave here, turn east, and hide by the Wadi Cherith, east of the Jordan.
v04: You will drink from the wadi. I have commanded the ravens to feed you there.”
</p>

<p>
v05: He went and did as the LORD said, settling by the Wadi Cherith east of the Jordan.
v06: The ravens brought him bread and meat in the morning and bread and meat in the evening. He drank from the wadi.
</p>

<p>
v07: After a time the wadi dried up, because no rain had fallen in the land.
</p>

## The widow of Zarephath

<p>
v08: Then the word of the LORD came to him:
v09: “Get up and go to Zarephath, which belongs to Sidon, and stay there. I have commanded a widow there to feed you.”
</p>

<p>
v10: He went to Zarephath. At the town’s entrance a widow was gathering sticks. He called to her, “Please bring me a little water in a vessel so I can drink.”
v11: As she went to get it, he called after her, “Please bring me a piece of bread too.”
</p>

<p>
v12: She said, “As the LORD your God lives, I have no baked bread—only a handful of flour in a jar and a little oil in a jug. I am gathering a couple of sticks to go and prepare it for myself and my son. We will eat it, and then we will die.”
</p>

<p>
v13: Elijah told her, “Do not be afraid. Go and do as you said. But first make me a small cake from it and bring it out to me. Afterward make something for yourself and your son.
v14: For this is what the LORD, Israel’s God, says: ‘The jar of flour will not run out, and the jug of oil will not fail, until the day the LORD sends rain on the ground.’”
</p>

<p>
v15: She went and did as Elijah said. She, he, and her household ate for many days.
v16: The jar of flour did not run out, and the jug of oil did not fail, just as the LORD had said through Elijah.
</p>

## The widow’s son

<p>
v17: After this, the son of the woman who owned the house became ill. His illness grew so severe that no breath remained in him.
v18: She said to Elijah, “What have you to do with me, man of God? Have you come to bring my guilt to mind and kill my son?”
</p>

<p>
v19: “Give me your son,” he said. He took him from her arms, carried him to the upper room where he was staying, and laid him on his bed.
v20: He cried to the LORD, “LORD my God, have you brought disaster even on this widow with whom I am staying, by killing her son?”
</p>

<p>
v21: He stretched himself over the child three times and cried to the LORD, “LORD my God, please let this child’s life return into him.”
v22: The LORD heard Elijah’s voice. The child’s life returned into him, and he lived.
</p>

<p>
v23: Elijah took the child down from the upper room into the house and gave him to his mother. “Look,” he said, “your son is alive.”
v24: The woman said to Elijah, “Now I know that you are a man of God and that the LORD’s word in your mouth is true.”
</p>

## Notes

v01: The pinned Hebrew identifies Elijah among the inhabitants of Gilead. It does not explicitly locate a town named Tishbe here.

v12–13: The widow expects one last meal before death; Elijah asks for his portion first. The draft retains this difficult sequence rather than smoothing it into a general promise of provision.

v18–20: Both the widow and Elijah address the child’s death as harm involving God. Their questions are retained as their speech.

v21–22: Nefesh can mean life or a living being. “His life returned into him” follows the bodily sequence of no breath, prayer, and renewed life without imposing an account of the soul’s nature.

## Vocabulary

v03: Wadi
A stream valley whose water may dry up.

v19: Upper room
A room above the main living space, where Elijah is staying.
