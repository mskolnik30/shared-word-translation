## A warning at Bethel

<p>
v01: A man of God came from Judah to Bethel at the LORD’s word, just as Jeroboam stood at the altar to burn offerings.
v02: At the LORD’s word he cried out against the altar: “Altar, altar! This is what the LORD says: A son named Josiah will be born to David’s house. On you he will sacrifice the high-place priests who burn offerings on you, and human bones will be burned on you.”
v03: That day he gave a sign: “This is the sign the LORD has declared: the altar will split apart, and the ashes on it will spill out.”
</p>

<p>
v04: When the king heard the man of God cry out against the altar at Bethel, Jeroboam stretched out his hand from the altar and said, “Seize him!” But the hand he stretched toward him withered, and he could not draw it back.
v05: The altar split apart and its ashes spilled out, just as the man of God had announced in the sign at the LORD’s word.
</p>

<p>
v06: The king said to the man of God, “Please seek the favor of the LORD your God. Pray for me so my hand may be restored.” The man of God sought the LORD’s favor, and the king’s hand was restored, as it had been before.
v07: The king said, “Come home with me and refresh yourself. I will give you a gift.”
</p>

<p>
v08: The man of God answered, “Even if you gave me half your house, I would not go with you. I will not eat bread or drink water in this place.
v09: For this was commanded me by the LORD’s word: ‘Do not eat bread or drink water, and do not return by the road you came.’”
v10: So he went by another road and did not return by the road he had taken to Bethel.
</p>

## The old prophet’s invitation

<p>
v11: An old prophet lived in Bethel. One of his sons came and told him all the man of God had done there that day. His sons also reported what the man had said to the king.
v12: Their father asked, “Which road did he take?” His sons had seen the road taken by the man of God who came from Judah.
v13: He told his sons, “Saddle the donkey for me.” They saddled it, and he mounted.
</p>

<p>
v14: He followed the man of God and found him sitting under a terebinth. “Are you the man of God who came from Judah?” he asked. “I am,” the man replied.
v15: The old prophet said, “Come home with me and eat bread.”
</p>

<p>
v16: He answered, “I cannot turn back and go with you. I will not eat bread or drink water with you in this place.
v17: For I was told by the LORD’s word, ‘Do not eat bread or drink water there, and do not go back by the road you came.’”
</p>

<p>
v18: The old prophet said, “I too am a prophet, like you. An angel told me by the LORD’s word, ‘Bring him back to your house so he may eat bread and drink water.’” But he was lying to him.
v19: So the man went back with him and ate bread and drank water in his house.
</p>

## Judgment on the road

<p>
v20: As they sat at the table, the LORD’s word came to the prophet who had brought him back.
v21: He cried out to the man of God from Judah, “This is what the LORD says: You defied the LORD’s command and did not keep what the LORD your God commanded you.
v22: You turned back and ate bread and drank water where he told you not to. Therefore your body will not enter your ancestors’ tomb.”
</p>

<p>
v23: After the man had eaten and drunk, the old prophet saddled the donkey for the prophet he had brought back.
v24: As he went on his way, a lion met him on the road and killed him. His body lay thrown on the road, with the donkey standing beside it and the lion standing beside the body.
</p>

<p>
v25: People passing by saw the body on the road and the lion standing beside it. They went and reported it in the town where the old prophet lived.
v26: When the prophet who had brought him back heard this, he said, “It is the man of God who defied the LORD’s command. The LORD gave him to the lion, which mauled and killed him, just as the LORD told him.”
</p>

<p>
v27: He told his sons, “Saddle the donkey for me.” They did so.
v28: He went and found the body lying on the road, with the donkey and lion standing beside it. The lion had neither eaten the body nor mauled the donkey.
</p>

<p>
v29: The prophet lifted the man of God’s body onto the donkey and brought it back to his own town to mourn and bury him.
v30: He laid the body in his own tomb, and they mourned him: “Alas, my brother!”
</p>

<p>
v31: After burying him, he told his sons, “When I die, bury me in the tomb where the man of God lies. Lay my bones beside his bones.
v32: For the word he cried out by the LORD’s command against the altar at Bethel and all the high-place shrines in Samaria’s towns will certainly come true.”
</p>

<p>
v33: Even after this, Jeroboam did not turn from his evil way. Again he appointed priests for the high places from among the people. Whoever wished, he ordained as a high-place priest.
v34: This became the sin of Jeroboam’s house that led to its being wiped out and destroyed from the face of the earth.
</p>

## Notes

v11: The pinned Hebrew begins with one son coming, then speaks of the sons reporting. The change from singular to plural is retained.

v18–20: The narrator says the old prophet lied. The same prophet subsequently receives the LORD’s word; his earlier claim and the later event remain distinct.

v23: The Hebrew is compressed about the saddling. The draft identifies the old prophet as the one who saddles the donkey for the returning man of God.

v28: The lion has killed the man but has eaten neither his body nor the donkey. These details are part of the report.

v32: “The cities of Samaria” is retained even though the founding of the city named Samaria is recounted later, in chapter 16.

## Vocabulary

v01: Man of God
A designation for a person speaking or acting with divine authority; here the prophet from Judah.

v33: Ordain
The Hebrew idiom is “fill the hand,” used for installing someone in priestly service.
