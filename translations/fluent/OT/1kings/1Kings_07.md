## Solomon’s palace buildings

<p>
v01: Solomon spent thirteen years building his own house, completing the whole complex.
v02: He built the House of the Forest of Lebanon, a hundred cubits long, fifty wide, and thirty high, on four rows of cedar pillars with cedar beams on the pillars.
v03: It was roofed with cedar above the beams resting on the pillars—forty-five beams, fifteen to a row.
v04: There were three rows of windows, facing one another in three tiers.
v05: All the doorways and their frames were rectangular, and the windows faced one another in three tiers.
</p>

<p>
v06: He made the Hall of Pillars fifty cubits long and thirty wide. A porch stood in front of it, with pillars and a canopy before them.
v07: He made the Hall of the Throne, where he would give judgment—the Hall of Justice. It was paneled with cedar from one end of the floor to the other.
</p>

<p>
v08: His own residence, in another court behind the hall, was built in the same way. He also made a house like this hall for Pharaoh’s daughter, whom he had married.
</p>

<p>
v09: All these buildings used costly stones, cut to size and sawn on their inner and outer faces, from the foundations to the coping and outside as far as the great court.
v10: The foundations were of great, costly stones, some ten cubits and some eight cubits long.
v11: Above them were costly stones cut to size, and cedar.
v12: The great court was surrounded by three courses of dressed stone and one course of cedar beams, as were the inner court of the LORD’s house and the house’s porch.
</p>

## Hiram’s bronze work

<p>
v13: King Solomon sent for Hiram from Tyre.
v14: He was the son of a widow from the tribe of Naphtali; his father was a Tyrian bronze worker. Hiram was filled with wisdom, understanding, and skill for every kind of bronze work. He came to King Solomon and did all his work.
</p>

<p>
v15: He cast the two bronze pillars. One pillar was eighteen cubits high, and a cord twelve cubits long went around the other.
v16: He made two cast-bronze capitals to set on top of the pillars. Each capital was five cubits high.
v17: For the capitals on top of the pillars he made latticework, with twisted chains: seven for one capital and seven for the other.
</p>

<p>
v18: He made the pillars and two rows around one lattice to cover the capitals above the pomegranates. He did the same for the other capital.
v19: The capitals on top of the pillars in the porch had lily-shaped work, four cubits high.
v20: On both pillars the capitals rose above the bowl-shaped part beside the lattice. Two hundred pomegranates ran in rows around the other capital.
</p>

<p>
v21: He set up the pillars at the main hall’s porch. He named the right pillar Jachin and the left pillar Boaz.
v22: The tops of the pillars were shaped like lilies. So the work on the pillars was finished.
</p>

## The Sea

<p>
v23: He made the cast-metal Sea, circular in shape, ten cubits from rim to rim and five cubits high. A cord thirty cubits long went around it.
v24: Under its rim, gourds encircled it, ten to a cubit. The gourds were in two rows, cast as one piece with the Sea.
v25: It stood on twelve cattle: three facing north, three west, three south, and three east. The Sea rested on them, and all their hindquarters faced inward.
v26: It was a handbreadth thick. Its rim was shaped like a cup’s rim, like a lily blossom. It held two thousand baths.
</p>

## The wheeled stands and basins

<p>
v27: He made ten bronze stands, each four cubits long, four wide, and three high.
v28: This was how the stands were made: they had panels fitted between crossbars.
v29: On the panels between the crossbars were lions, cattle, and cherubim. Above, on the crossbars, was a supporting ledge, and below the lions and cattle were hanging wreaths.
</p>

<p>
v30: Each stand had four bronze wheels with bronze axles. Its four corners had cast supports under the basin, with wreaths beside each support.
v31: From inside the crown, its opening rose a cubit above it. The opening was round, shaped like a pedestal, a cubit and a half across. It too had carvings. The panels, however, were square, not round.
v32: The four wheels were below the panels, with their axles attached to the stand. Each wheel was a cubit and a half high.
v33: The wheels were made like chariot wheels. Their axles, rims, spokes, and hubs were all cast metal.
</p>

<p>
v34: Each stand had four supports at its four corners, made as one piece with the stand.
v35: At the top of each stand was a round band half a cubit high. Its supports and panels were made as one piece with it.
v36: On the surfaces of its supports and panels he engraved cherubim, lions, and palm trees wherever there was room, with wreaths all around.
v37: He made all ten stands alike: the same casting, dimensions, and shape.
</p>

<p>
v38: He made ten bronze basins, each holding forty baths and measuring four cubits. There was one basin on each of the ten stands.
v39: He placed five stands on the right side of the house and five on the left. He put the Sea on the right side, toward the southeast.
</p>

## The temple furnishings

<p>
v40: Hiram made the basins, shovels, and bowls. He finished all the work he did for King Solomon on the LORD’s house:
v41: the two pillars; the two bowl-shaped capitals on top of them; the two lattices covering those capitals;
v42: the four hundred pomegranates for the two lattices, two rows for each lattice, covering the two bowl-shaped capitals at the tops of the pillars;
v43: the ten stands and the ten basins on them;
v44: the one Sea and the twelve cattle underneath it;
v45: and the pots, shovels, and bowls. All these utensils that Hiram made for King Solomon for the LORD’s house were of polished bronze.
</p>

<p>
v46: The king had them cast in the clay ground of the Jordan plain between Succoth and Zarethan.
v47: Solomon left all the utensils unweighed because there were so many. The weight of the bronze was not determined.
</p>

<p>
v48: Solomon made all the furnishings for the LORD’s house: the gold altar; the gold table for the bread of the Presence;
v49: the pure-gold lampstands, five on the right and five on the left before the inner sanctuary; the gold flowers, lamps, and tongs;
v50: the cups, snuffers, bowls, dishes, and firepans of pure gold; and the gold sockets for the doors of the innermost room, the Most Holy Place, and for the doors of the main hall.
</p>

<p>
v51: When all King Solomon’s work on the LORD’s house was finished, he brought in the things his father David had dedicated. He placed the silver, gold, and utensils in the treasuries of the LORD’s house.
</p>

## Notes

v02–03: Four rows of pillars are named, followed by forty-five beams, fifteen in a row. What the second count modifies is not wholly clear; neither number is changed.

v07: The Hebrew says “from floor to floor.” “From one end of the floor to the other” makes a possible sense of that wording; “floor to ceiling” would require a different reading.

v14: This account identifies Hiram’s mother with Naphtali and his father with Tyre. Hiram the craftsman is distinct from King Hiram.

v18–20: The Hebrew places “pillars” and “pomegranates” awkwardly in verse 18. Exchanging the two terms would produce a smoother construction description, but the draft retains their positions. Verse 42 clearly describes the rows of pomegranates.

v23–26: The Sea is described as ten cubits across, thirty around, five high, and holding two thousand baths. The translation preserves the ancient measurements as given.

v31: The stand’s opening, crown, and panels are described with compressed pronouns. Their exact arrangement remains uncertain.

v38: The Hebrew gives each basin a measurement of four cubits without identifying the dimension.

v40,45: Verse 40 names basins; verse 45 names pots. Both source terms are retained rather than making the lists identical.

v50: The final door fittings may be sockets or hinges; their precise form is uncertain.

## Vocabulary

v16: Capital
The shaped top of a pillar.

v21: Jachin and Boaz
The pillars’ names suggest “he establishes” and “in him is strength.” The text gives the names without an explanation.

v26: Bath
An ancient liquid measure. The Sea and the smaller basins have distinct capacities.
