## Ahijah’s message to Jeroboam

<p>
v01: At that time Jeroboam’s son Abijah became ill.
v02: Jeroboam told his wife, “Please get up and disguise yourself so no one recognizes you as Jeroboam’s wife. Go to Shiloh. The prophet Ahijah is there—the one who said I would be king over this people.
v03: Take ten loaves, some cakes, and a jar of honey, and go to him. He will tell you what will happen to the boy.”
</p>

<p>
v04: Jeroboam’s wife did so. She went to Shiloh and entered Ahijah’s house. Ahijah could no longer see; his eyes had grown fixed with age.
v05: The LORD had told Ahijah, “Jeroboam’s wife is coming to ask you about her son, who is ill. Tell her such and such.” When she arrived, she was disguised.
</p>

<p>
v06: When Ahijah heard her footsteps at the doorway, he said, “Come in, Jeroboam’s wife. Why are you pretending to be someone else? I have been sent to you with a harsh message.
</p>

<p>
v07: Go, tell Jeroboam, ‘This is what the LORD, Israel’s God, says: I raised you from among the people and made you leader over my people Israel.
v08: I tore the kingdom from David’s house and gave it to you. But you have not been like my servant David, who kept my commands and followed me with all his heart, doing only what was right in my eyes.
v09: You have done more evil than all who came before you. You went and made other gods and cast images for yourself, provoking me to anger. You cast me behind your back.
</p>

<p>
v10: So I am bringing disaster on Jeroboam’s house. I will cut off every one of Jeroboam’s males who urinates against a wall, confined or free in Israel. I will sweep away Jeroboam’s house as one sweeps away dung, until nothing remains.
v11: Dogs will eat anyone of Jeroboam’s house who dies in the city, and the birds of the sky will eat anyone who dies in the open country. The LORD has spoken.’
</p>

<p>
v12: “Now get up and go home. When your feet enter the city, the child will die.
v13: All Israel will mourn him and bury him. He alone of Jeroboam’s house will enter a tomb, because in him alone something good toward the LORD, Israel’s God, was found in Jeroboam’s house.
</p>

<p>
v14: The LORD will raise up for himself a king over Israel who will cut off Jeroboam’s house—this very day! And what more? Even now!
v15: The LORD will strike Israel, shaking it like a reed in water. He will uproot Israel from this good land he gave their ancestors and scatter them beyond the River, because they made their Asherah poles, provoking the LORD to anger.
v16: He will give Israel up because of Jeroboam’s sins, which he committed and led Israel to commit.”
</p>

<p>
v17: Jeroboam’s wife got up and went to Tirzah. As she crossed the threshold of the house, the boy died.
v18: They buried him, and all Israel mourned him, just as the LORD had said through his servant Ahijah the prophet.
</p>

## Jeroboam’s death

<p>
v19: The rest of Jeroboam’s history—how he waged war and how he ruled—is written in the Book of the Annals of Israel’s Kings.
v20: Jeroboam ruled for twenty-two years. He lay down with his ancestors, and his son Nadab became king in his place.
</p>

## Rehoboam’s reign

<p>
v21: Rehoboam son of Solomon ruled in Judah. He was forty-one when he became king and ruled seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city the LORD chose from all Israel’s tribes to place his name there. His mother was Naamah the Ammonite.
v22: Judah did evil in the LORD’s eyes. Their sins provoked his jealousy more than anything their ancestors had done.
v23: They too built high places, sacred pillars, and Asherah poles on every high hill and under every flourishing tree.
v24: There were also male shrine attendants in the land. The people practiced all the detestable things of the nations the LORD had driven out before the Israelites.
</p>

<p>
v25: In King Rehoboam’s fifth year, Shishak king of Egypt attacked Jerusalem.
v26: He took the treasures of the LORD’s house and the king’s house. He took everything, including all the gold shields Solomon had made.
</p>

<p>
v27: King Rehoboam made bronze shields to replace them and entrusted them to the commanders of the runners who guarded the entrance to the king’s house.
v28: Whenever the king entered the LORD’s house, the runners carried the shields, then returned them to the guardroom.
</p>

<p>
v29: The rest of Rehoboam’s history and all he did—are they not written in the Book of the Annals of Judah’s Kings?
v30: There was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam throughout their days.
v31: Rehoboam lay down with his ancestors and was buried with them in the City of David. His mother was Naamah the Ammonite. His son Abijam became king in his place.
</p>

## Notes

v10: The coarse wall-urinating expression specifically targets males. “Confined or free” renders an uncertain pair of terms that appears to include everyone in the threatened group.

v14: The final exclamations are abrupt and difficult: “this very day,” followed by “and what? Even now!” The draft preserves the broken urgency without assigning a new date.

v24: The Hebrew qadesh names male shrine attendants. “Male cult prostitutes” is a traditional rendering, but the word alone does not settle their sexual function.

v30: The account describes continuing war between the kings even though 12:24 records the abandonment of one invasion. Neither statement is removed.

## Vocabulary

v15: Asherah poles
Cult objects associated with Asherah; the form of a particular object is not always clear.

v19: Records of the kings
The account refers to royal records as sources for further deeds; this title does not identify the biblical books of Chronicles.
