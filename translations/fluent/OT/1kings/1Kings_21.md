## Naboth’s vineyard

<p>
v01: After these events, Naboth the Jezreelite had a vineyard in Jezreel beside the palace of Ahab king of Samaria.
</p>

<p>
v02: Ahab told Naboth, “Give me your vineyard for a vegetable garden, since it is close to my house. I will give you a better vineyard in exchange, or, if you prefer, pay you its value in silver.”
</p>

<p>
v03: Naboth answered, “The LORD forbid that I give you my ancestors’ inheritance!”
v04: Ahab went home sullen and angry because Naboth the Jezreelite had said, “I will not give you my ancestors’ inheritance.” He lay on his bed, turned his face away, and would not eat.
</p>

<p>
v05: His wife Jezebel came and asked, “Why are you so sullen that you will not eat?”
v06: He told her, “I said to Naboth the Jezreelite, ‘Give me your vineyard for silver, or, if you prefer, I will give you another vineyard in exchange.’ But he said, ‘I will not give you my vineyard.’”
</p>

<p>
v07: His wife Jezebel said, “Are you now the one ruling Israel? Get up, eat, and cheer up. I will give you Naboth the Jezreelite’s vineyard.”
</p>

## Jezebel’s letters

<p>
v08: She wrote letters in Ahab’s name and sealed them with his seal. She sent them to the elders and nobles living in Naboth’s town.
v09: In the letters she wrote, “Proclaim a fast and seat Naboth at the head of the people.
v10: Seat two worthless men opposite him and have them testify, ‘You cursed God and the king.’ Then take him out and stone him to death.”
</p>

<p>
v11: The men of Naboth’s town—the elders and nobles who lived there—did as Jezebel directed, just as she had written in her letters.
v12: They proclaimed a fast and seated Naboth at the head of the people.
v13: The two worthless men came and sat opposite him. Before the people they testified against Naboth: “Naboth cursed God and the king.” Then they took him outside the town and stoned him to death.
</p>

<p>
v14: They sent word to Jezebel: “Naboth has been stoned and is dead.”
</p>

<p>
v15: When Jezebel heard that Naboth had been stoned to death, she told Ahab, “Get up and take possession of the vineyard Naboth the Jezreelite refused to sell you. Naboth is no longer alive. He is dead.”
v16: When Ahab heard that Naboth was dead, he got up and went down to Naboth the Jezreelite’s vineyard to take possession of it.
</p>

## Elijah’s charge against Ahab

<p>
v17: The word of the LORD came to Elijah the Tishbite:
v18: “Get up and go down to meet Ahab king of Israel, who rules in Samaria. He is in Naboth’s vineyard, where he has gone to take possession.
</p>

<p>
v19: Tell him, ‘This is what the LORD says: Have you murdered and also taken possession?’ Then tell him, ‘This is what the LORD says: In the place where dogs licked Naboth’s blood, dogs will lick your blood—yours too.’”
</p>

<p>
v20: Ahab said to Elijah, “Have you found me, my enemy?” “I have found you,” he answered, “because you have sold yourself to do evil in the LORD’s eyes.
v21: He says, ‘I am bringing disaster on you. I will sweep away your descendants and cut off every one of Ahab’s males who urinates against a wall, confined or free in Israel.
v22: I will make your house like the house of Jeroboam son of Nebat and the house of Baasha son of Ahijah, because you provoked me to anger and led Israel into sin.’
</p>

<p>
v23: The LORD also spoke about Jezebel: ‘Dogs will eat Jezebel by the rampart of Jezreel.’
v24: Dogs will eat anyone of Ahab’s house who dies in the city, and the birds of the sky will eat anyone who dies in the open country.”
</p>

<p>
v25: There was no one like Ahab, who sold himself to do evil in the LORD’s eyes, urged on by his wife Jezebel.
v26: He acted most detestably in following idols, like everything the Amorites had done, whom the LORD drove out before the Israelites.
</p>

<p>
v27: When Ahab heard these words, he tore his clothes, put sackcloth against his skin, and fasted. He lay in sackcloth and walked about subdued.
</p>

<p>
v28: The word of the LORD came to Elijah the Tishbite:
v29: “Have you seen how Ahab has humbled himself before me? Because he has humbled himself, I will not bring the disaster in his days. I will bring it on his house in his son’s days.”
</p>

## Notes

v03–06: Naboth appeals to his ancestral inheritance. Ahab’s retelling to Jezebel leaves that reason out and reports only a refusal to give up the vineyard.

v07: Jezebel’s words about Ahab exercising kingship can be read as a taunting question or as an assertion. The draft uses a question.

v10,13: The Hebrew uses “bless” euphemistically where the accusation requires “curse.” The witnesses’ charge is part of the arranged killing, not the narrator’s finding against Naboth.

v19: The charge joins murder with taking possession. The precise place named for the blood repayment is kept without harmonizing it with later accounts.

v21: The coarse male-targeting idiom recurs from 14:10 and 16:11. “Confined or free” preserves the uncertain inclusive pair.

v23: The pinned form is rendered “rampart.” It is not silently replaced with a different word for plot or district.

v27–29: The narrative describes Ahab’s actions and the LORD’s recognition of his humbling. The disaster is deferred to his son’s days, not canceled.

## Vocabulary

v03: Inheritance
Land received through one’s family, with obligations and identity beyond its sale price.

v09: Fast
Abstaining from food; here Jezebel uses a public fast as the setting for the accusation.

v27: Sackcloth
Coarse cloth worn as an outward sign of mourning or humbling.
