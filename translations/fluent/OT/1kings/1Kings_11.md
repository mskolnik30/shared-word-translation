## Solomon’s divided heart

<p>
v01: King Solomon loved many foreign women besides Pharaoh’s daughter: Moabite, Ammonite, Edomite, Sidonian, and Hittite women.
v02: They came from nations about which the LORD had told the Israelites, “You must not enter into marriage with them, nor they with you. They will surely turn your hearts after their gods.” Yet Solomon clung to these women in love.
v03: He had seven hundred royal wives and three hundred concubines. His wives turned his heart away.
</p>

<p>
v04: As Solomon grew old, his wives turned his heart after other gods. His heart was not wholly with the LORD his God, as his father David’s heart had been.
v05: Solomon followed Ashtoreth, goddess of the Sidonians, and Milcom, the detestable god of the Ammonites.
v06: He did evil in the LORD’s eyes and did not follow the LORD fully, as his father David had.
v07: Then Solomon built a high place for Chemosh, the detestable god of Moab, on the hill facing Jerusalem, and for Molech, the detestable god of the Ammonites.
v08: He did the same for all his foreign wives, who burned offerings and sacrificed to their gods.
</p>

<p>
v09: The LORD became angry with Solomon because his heart had turned away from the LORD, Israel’s God, who had appeared to him twice.
v10: The LORD had commanded him about this very thing: he must not follow other gods. But Solomon did not keep the LORD’s command.
</p>

<p>
v11: So the LORD said to Solomon, “Since this is how you have acted, and you have not kept my covenant and the statutes I commanded you, I will surely tear the kingdom away from you and give it to your servant.
v12: But for your father David’s sake, I will not do it during your lifetime. I will tear it from your son’s hand.
v13: Still, I will not tear away the whole kingdom. I will give your son one tribe for the sake of my servant David and Jerusalem, which I chose.”
</p>

## Adversaries arise

<p>
v14: The LORD raised up an adversary against Solomon: Hadad the Edomite, of the royal line in Edom.
v15: When David was in Edom, Joab the army commander went up to bury the slain and struck down every male in Edom.
v16: Joab and all Israel stayed there six months, until he had cut off every male in Edom.
</p>

<p>
v17: Hadad escaped to Egypt with some Edomite servants of his father. He was only a young boy.
v18: They left Midian and came to Paran. Taking men from Paran with them, they went to Egypt, to Pharaoh king of Egypt. Pharaoh gave Hadad a house, assigned him food, and gave him land.
v19: Hadad found great favor with Pharaoh, who gave him his wife’s sister in marriage—the sister of Queen Tahpenes.
v20: Tahpenes’ sister bore him a son, Genubath. Tahpenes weaned him in Pharaoh’s house, and he lived there among Pharaoh’s sons.
</p>

<p>
v21: While in Egypt, Hadad heard that David had lain down with his ancestors and that Joab the army commander was dead. He told Pharaoh, “Let me go back to my own land.”
v22: Pharaoh asked him, “What do you lack here with me that makes you want to go to your own land?” “Nothing,” he answered. “But please, let me go.”
</p>

<p>
v23: God raised up another adversary against Solomon: Rezon son of Eliada, who had fled from his master Hadadezer king of Zobah.
v24: He gathered men around him and led a raiding band when David slaughtered Hadadezer’s forces. They went to Damascus, settled there, and took power in Damascus.
v25: He was Israel’s adversary throughout Solomon’s days, adding to the harm Hadad did. He loathed Israel and ruled over Aram.
</p>

## Jeroboam and the torn cloak

<p>
v26: Jeroboam son of Nebat, an Ephraimite from Zeredah, also raised his hand against the king. He was Solomon’s servant; his mother was a widow named Zeruah.
v27: This is how he came to raise his hand against the king. Solomon was building the Millo and closing the breach in the City of David his father.
v28: Jeroboam was a capable man. When Solomon saw how industrious the young man was, he put him over all the forced labor of Joseph’s house.
</p>

<p>
v29: At that time, as Jeroboam was leaving Jerusalem, the prophet Ahijah of Shiloh met him on the road. Ahijah was wearing a new cloak, and the two were alone in the open country.
v30: Ahijah seized the new cloak he was wearing and tore it into twelve pieces.
</p>

<p>
v31: He told Jeroboam, “Take ten pieces. For this is what the LORD, Israel’s God, says: ‘I am tearing the kingdom from Solomon’s hand and giving you ten tribes.
v32: But one tribe will remain his, for the sake of my servant David and Jerusalem, the city I chose from all Israel’s tribes.
v33: This is because they abandoned me and bowed to Ashtoreth, goddess of the Sidonians, Chemosh, god of Moab, and Milcom, god of the Ammonites. They have not walked in my ways, doing what is right in my eyes and keeping my statutes and judgments, as his father David did.
</p>

<p>
v34: Yet I will not take the whole kingdom from his hand. I will keep him as ruler all his life for the sake of my servant David, whom I chose and who kept my commands and statutes.
v35: I will take the kingship from his son’s hand and give you ten tribes.
v36: I will give his son one tribe, so my servant David may always have a lamp before me in Jerusalem, the city I chose for myself to place my name there.
</p>

<p>
v37: I will take you, and you will rule over all you desire. You will be king over Israel.
v38: If you listen to all I command, walk in my ways, and do what is right in my eyes, keeping my statutes and commands as my servant David did, I will be with you. I will build you an enduring house, as I built for David, and give Israel to you.
v39: For this I will afflict David’s descendants, but not forever.’”
</p>

<p>
v40: Solomon tried to kill Jeroboam. Jeroboam fled to Shishak king of Egypt and stayed in Egypt until Solomon died.
</p>

## Solomon’s death

<p>
v41: The rest of Solomon’s history, all he did, and his wisdom—are they not written in the Book of Solomon’s Acts?
v42: Solomon ruled over all Israel from Jerusalem for forty years.
v43: Solomon lay down with his ancestors and was buried in the City of David his father. His son Rehoboam became king in his place.
</p>

## Notes

v05,07: The Hebrew names Milcom in verse 5 and Molech in verse 7. These forms are preserved rather than silently made identical.

v15–17: Joab’s killing of every male in Edom and Hadad’s flight as a boy are both stated. The account’s sweeping claim is not softened.

v25: The reference to Hadad’s harm interrupts the description of Rezon. The syntax is difficult; the draft retains both adversaries and the statement that he ruled Aram.

v29–30: The context most naturally identifies Ahijah as wearing the new cloak, though the Hebrew pronouns do not name the wearer again.

v31–32: Twelve pieces are torn, ten offered to Jeroboam, and one tribe reserved for Solomon’s line. The uneven accounting is retained.

v33: The source switches to plural “they” for the apostasy but ends with “David his father.” The change of number is left visible.

v38–39: Jeroboam too receives a conditional promise of an enduring house. The affliction of David’s descendants is explicitly “not forever.”

## Vocabulary

v03: Concubine
A woman in a recognized union with lower standing than a wife; the account distinguishes the two groups.

v14: Adversary
Hebrew satan here describes an opponent raised against Solomon; it is not used as a personal name.

v36: Lamp
An image of David’s continuing royal line.
