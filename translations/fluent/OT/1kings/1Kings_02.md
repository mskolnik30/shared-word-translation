## David’s final instructions

<p>
v01: As David’s death drew near, he gave his son Solomon these instructions:
v02: “I am going the way of all the earth. Be strong. Show yourself a man.
v03: Keep the charge of the LORD your God: walk in his ways and keep his statutes, commands, judgments, and testimonies, as written in the law of Moses. Then you may act wisely in everything you do and wherever you turn,
v04: and the LORD may fulfill what he promised me: ‘If your sons watch their ways and walk faithfully before me with all their heart and all their being, you will never lack a man on Israel’s throne.’
</p>

<p>
v05: “You also know what Joab son of Zeruiah did to me—what he did to the two commanders of Israel’s armies, Abner son of Ner and Amasa son of Jether. He killed them, shedding the blood of war in peacetime and putting that blood on the belt around his waist and the sandals on his feet.
v06: Use your wisdom. Do not let his gray head go down to Sheol in peace.
</p>

<p>
v07: But show steadfast love to the sons of Barzillai the Gileadite. Let them eat at your table, because they stood by me when I fled from your brother Absalom.
</p>

<p>
v08: “You also have with you Shimei son of Gera, the Benjaminite from Bahurim. He cursed me bitterly when I went to Mahanaim. But he came down to meet me at the Jordan, and I swore to him by the LORD, ‘I will not put you to death with the sword.’
v09: Now do not leave him unpunished. You are a wise man; you will know what to do with him. Bring his gray head down to Sheol in blood.”
</p>

<p>
v10: David lay down with his ancestors and was buried in the City of David.
v11: He had ruled Israel for forty years: seven in Hebron and thirty-three in Jerusalem.
v12: Solomon took his seat on his father David’s throne, and his kingship became firmly established.
</p>

## Adonijah asks for Abishag

<p>
v13: Adonijah son of Haggith came to Bathsheba, Solomon’s mother. “Do you come in peace?” she asked. “In peace,” he replied.
v14: He said, “I have something to say to you.” “Speak,” she said.
v15: “You know the kingship was mine,” he said. “All Israel looked to me to be king. But the kingship turned away from me and went to my brother, because it came to him from the LORD.
v16: Now I have one request. Do not turn me away.” “Speak,” she told him.
v17: “Please ask King Solomon to give me Abishag the Shunammite as my wife. He will not turn you away.”
v18: “Very well,” Bathsheba said. “I will speak to the king for you.”
</p>

<p>
v19: Bathsheba went to King Solomon to speak for Adonijah. The king rose to meet her and bowed to her. Then he sat on his throne and had a throne placed for the king’s mother. She sat at his right.
v20: “I have one small request,” she said. “Do not turn me away.” The king answered, “Ask, Mother. I will not turn you away.”
v21: She said, “Let Abishag the Shunammite be given to your brother Adonijah as his wife.”
v22: King Solomon answered his mother, “Why ask for Abishag the Shunammite for Adonijah? Ask for the kingship for him too! He is my older brother—for him, for Abiathar the priest, and for Joab son of Zeruiah!”
</p>

<p>
v23: Then King Solomon swore by the LORD, “May God do this to me, and more besides, if Adonijah has not spoken these words at the cost of his life!
v24: As the LORD lives—the one who established me, seated me on my father David’s throne, and made me a house as he promised—Adonijah will be put to death today.”
v25: King Solomon sent Benaiah son of Jehoiada, who struck Adonijah down and killed him.
</p>

## Abiathar and Joab

<p>
v26: To Abiathar the priest the king said, “Go to your fields at Anathoth. You deserve death, but I will not put you to death today, because you carried the ark of the Lord GOD before my father David and shared all my father’s hardships.”
v27: So Solomon removed Abiathar from serving as the LORD’s priest, fulfilling the word the LORD had spoken at Shiloh about Eli’s house.
</p>

<p>
v28: When the news reached Joab, he fled to the LORD’s tent and took hold of the altar’s horns. Joab had sided with Adonijah, though he had not sided with Absalom.
v29: King Solomon was told, “Joab has fled to the LORD’s tent. He is there beside the altar.” Solomon sent Benaiah son of Jehoiada with the order, “Go, strike him down.”
v30: Benaiah went to the LORD’s tent and said to Joab, “The king says, ‘Come out.’” Joab answered, “No. I will die here.” Benaiah went back to the king and reported what Joab had said and how he had answered.
</p>

<p>
v31: The king told him, “Do as he says. Strike him down and bury him. Remove from me and my father’s house the guilt of the blood Joab shed without cause.
v32: The LORD will bring his blood back on his own head. He struck down two men more righteous and better than he was, killing them with the sword without my father David’s knowledge: Abner son of Ner, commander of Israel’s army, and Amasa son of Jether, commander of Judah’s army.
v33: May their blood come back on Joab’s head and on his descendants’ heads forever. But may David, his descendants, his house, and his throne have peace from the LORD forever.”
</p>

<p>
v34: Benaiah son of Jehoiada went up, struck Joab down, and killed him. Joab was buried at his own house in the wilderness.
v35: The king put Benaiah son of Jehoiada over the army in Joab’s place, and he put Zadok the priest in Abiathar’s place.
</p>

## Shimei’s confinement

<p>
v36: Then the king summoned Shimei. “Build yourself a house in Jerusalem and live there,” he said. “Do not leave it to go anywhere else.
v37: Know this: on the day you leave and cross the Kidron Valley, you will certainly die. Your blood will be on your own head.”
v38: Shimei answered the king, “Your word is good. Your servant will do as my lord the king has said.” Shimei lived in Jerusalem for a long time.
</p>

<p>
v39: After three years, two of Shimei’s slaves ran away to Achish son of Maacah, king of Gath. Shimei was told, “Your slaves are in Gath.”
v40: Shimei got up, saddled his donkey, and went to Achish at Gath to look for his slaves. He went and brought them back from Gath.
v41: Solomon was told that Shimei had gone from Jerusalem to Gath and returned.
</p>

<p>
v42: The king summoned Shimei and said, “Did I not make you swear by the LORD and warn you, ‘Know this: on the day you leave and go anywhere else, you will certainly die’? And you told me, ‘Your word is good. I will obey.’
v43: Why, then, have you failed to keep your oath by the LORD and the command I gave you?”
v44: The king also said, “You know in your heart all the evil you did to my father David. The LORD will bring your evil back on your own head.
v45: But King Solomon will be blessed, and David’s throne will stand firm before the LORD forever.”
</p>

<p>
v46: Then the king gave the order to Benaiah son of Jehoiada. He went out, struck Shimei down, and killed him. The kingdom was firmly in Solomon’s hands.
</p>

## Notes

v02: “The way of all the earth” pictures death as the journey everyone takes. David’s next commands connect wisdom with both loyalty and executions.

v08–09: David recalls his oath not to kill Shimei, then tells Solomon to bring him down to Sheol in blood. The translation leaves that tension in place.

v22: Solomon’s last words abruptly name Adonijah, Abiathar, and Joab together. The Hebrew does not spell out a new conspiracy.

v28: The pinned Hebrew says Joab had not followed Absalom. It is not changed to Solomon.

v37–40: The warning names crossing the Kidron, while Shimei later goes to Gath. Verse 36 also forbids leaving Jerusalem for any destination; the wording of each instruction is preserved.

## Vocabulary

v06: Sheol
The realm of the dead. “Gray head” keeps the image of an old man going down to death.

v24: House
Here a continuing royal family or dynasty; elsewhere in Kings the same word refers to a palace or the temple.

v28: Horns of the altar
Projections at the altar’s corners. Joab holds them while seeking refuge, as Adonijah did in chapter 1.
