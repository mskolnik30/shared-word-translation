## Two kings consider war

<p>
v01: For three years there was no war between Aram and Israel.
v02: In the third year, Jehoshaphat king of Judah went down to visit the king of Israel.
v03: Israel’s king said to his servants, “You know Ramoth-gilead belongs to us. Why are we doing nothing to take it back from the king of Aram?”
</p>

<p>
v04: He asked Jehoshaphat, “Will you join me in battle at Ramoth-gilead?” Jehoshaphat answered Israel’s king, “I am as you are, my people as your people, my horses as your horses.”
v05: But Jehoshaphat said to Israel’s king, “First, please seek the LORD’s word.”
v06: Israel’s king gathered about four hundred prophets and asked, “Should I go to war against Ramoth-gilead, or hold back?” “Go,” they said. “The Lord will give it into the king’s hand.”
</p>

<p>
v07: Jehoshaphat asked, “Is there no other prophet of the LORD here whom we can consult?”
v08: Israel’s king answered, “There is one more man through whom we can consult the LORD, Micaiah son of Imlah. But I hate him. He never prophesies good for me, only disaster.” Jehoshaphat said, “The king should not speak that way.”
v09: Then Israel’s king called a court official. “Bring Micaiah son of Imlah quickly,” he said.
</p>

<p>
v10: Israel’s king and Jehoshaphat king of Judah sat on their thrones, dressed in their robes, at the threshing floor by Samaria’s gate. All the prophets were prophesying before them.
v11: Zedekiah son of Chenaanah made himself iron horns. He declared, “This is what the LORD says: With these you will gore Aram until you finish them off.”
v12: All the prophets said the same: “Go up to Ramoth-gilead and succeed! The LORD will give it into the king’s hand.”
</p>

## Micaiah’s vision

<p>
v13: The messenger who went to summon Micaiah told him, “The prophets are speaking with one voice in the king’s favor. Please let your word agree with theirs. Speak favorably.”
v14: Micaiah answered, “As the LORD lives, I will speak whatever the LORD tells me.”
v15: When he reached the king, the king asked, “Micaiah, should we go to war at Ramoth-gilead, or hold back?” He answered, “Go and succeed! The LORD will give it into the king’s hand.”
v16: The king replied, “How many times must I put you under oath to tell me only the truth in the LORD’s name?”
</p>

<p>
v17: Then Micaiah said, “I saw all Israel scattered on the hills like sheep without a shepherd. The LORD said, ‘These have no master. Let each return home in peace.’”
v18: Israel’s king said to Jehoshaphat, “Didn’t I tell you? He prophesies nothing good for me, only disaster.”
</p>

<p>
v19: Micaiah continued, “Then hear the LORD’s word. I saw the LORD seated on his throne, with all heaven’s army standing beside him, to his right and left.
v20: The LORD asked, ‘Who will entice Ahab to go up and fall at Ramoth-gilead?’ One proposed this, another that.
v21: Then the spirit came forward and stood before the LORD. ‘I will entice him,’ it said. ‘How?’ the LORD asked.
v22: It answered, ‘I will go and become a lying spirit in the mouths of all his prophets.’ The LORD said, ‘You will entice him and succeed. Go and do it.’
v23: So now the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouths of all these prophets of yours. The LORD has pronounced disaster against you.”
</p>

<p>
v24: Zedekiah son of Chenaanah came up and struck Micaiah on the cheek. “Which way did the LORD’s spirit pass from me to speak to you?” he asked.
v25: Micaiah answered, “You will see on the day you go into an inner room to hide.”
</p>

<p>
v26: Israel’s king said, “Take Micaiah back to Amon the city governor and Joash the king’s son.
v27: Tell them, ‘The king says: Put this man in prison. Give him meager bread and water until I return in peace.’”
v28: Micaiah said, “If you really return in peace, the LORD has not spoken through me.” Then he added, “Listen, all you peoples!”
</p>

## Ahab’s last battle

<p>
v29: Israel’s king and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth-gilead.
v30: Israel’s king told Jehoshaphat, “I will enter the battle in disguise, but you wear your robes.” So Israel’s king disguised himself and went into battle.
v31: The king of Aram had ordered his thirty-two chariot commanders, “Fight no one, small or great, except the king of Israel.”
</p>

<p>
v32: When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they said, “That must be Israel’s king!” They turned to attack him, and Jehoshaphat cried out.
v33: When they saw he was not Israel’s king, they stopped pursuing him.
v34: But a man drew his bow without a particular target and struck Israel’s king between the armor’s fittings and breastplate. The king told his chariot driver, “Turn around and take me out of the fighting. I am wounded.”
</p>

<p>
v35: The battle grew fierce that day. The king was held upright in his chariot facing Aram. He died that evening, and blood from his wound ran into the chariot’s floor.
v36: At sunset a shout went through the camp: “Everyone to his town! Everyone to his own land!”
v37: The king was dead. They brought him to Samaria and buried him there.
</p>

<p>
v38: They washed the chariot at Samaria’s pool, where prostitutes bathed, and dogs licked his blood, just as the LORD had said.
v39: The rest of Ahab’s deeds, everything he did, the ivory house he built, and all the towns he built—are they not written in the annals of the kings of Israel?
v40: Ahab lay down with his ancestors, and his son Ahaziah became king in his place.
</p>

## Jehoshaphat and Ahaziah

<p>
v41: Jehoshaphat son of Asa became king over Judah in the fourth year of Ahab king of Israel.
v42: Jehoshaphat was thirty-five when he became king, and he ruled in Jerusalem for twenty-five years. His mother was Azubah daughter of Shilhi.
v43: He followed the whole course of his father Asa and did not turn aside from it, doing what was right in the LORD’s eyes. Yet the high places remained; the people still sacrificed and burned offerings there.
v44: Jehoshaphat also made peace with the king of Israel.
</p>

<p>
v45: The rest of Jehoshaphat’s deeds, his achievements, and the wars he fought—are they not written in the annals of the kings of Judah?
v46: He removed from the land the remaining male shrine attendants who had survived from his father Asa’s days.
v47: There was no king in Edom; a deputy ruled as king.
</p>

<p>
v48: Jehoshaphat built Tarshish ships to sail to Ophir for gold. But they never sailed, because they were wrecked at Ezion-geber.
v49: Then Ahaziah son of Ahab told Jehoshaphat, “Let my servants sail with yours.” But Jehoshaphat refused.
v50: Jehoshaphat lay down with his ancestors and was buried with them in the City of David his ancestor. His son Jehoram became king in his place.
</p>

<p>
v51: Ahaziah son of Ahab became king over Israel in Samaria in the seventeenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah. He ruled Israel for two years.
v52: He did evil in the LORD’s eyes, following his father, his mother, and Jeroboam son of Nebat, who led Israel into sin.
v53: He served Baal and bowed down to him, provoking the LORD, Israel’s God, just as his father had done.
</p>

## Notes

v06: The Hebrew title here is Adonai, “Lord”; verse 7 uses the divine name represented by LORD.

v19: The heavenly throne scene follows the two kings sitting on their thrones in verse 10. The vision attributes the plan to entice Ahab and the lying spirit’s commission to the LORD.

v34: The archer’s action can mean shooting innocently, unsuspectingly, or without a particular target. The exact parts of the armor named here are uncertain.

v38: The Hebrew mentions prostitutes bathing at the pool. This detail is retained, as is Samaria’s location; the account is not rewritten to make every detail match the wording of the earlier oracle.

v43: This public verse combines two Hebrew-numbered verses: Jehoshaphat’s conduct and the continuing high places. Subsequent public labels are one lower than the Hebrew labels.

v46: The term for the male shrine attendants is traditionally rendered “male cult prostitutes.” Their exact activities are disputed; the term itself does not describe a sexual act.

v48: The read tradition has Jehoshaphat “made” the ships; the written form differs. “Ships of Tarshish” can describe a class of trading vessels, without making Tarshish their destination.

## Vocabulary

v19: Army of heaven
The heavenly assembly attending the divine throne.

v43: High places
Local worship sites, often with altars; the term is not limited to mountaintops.

v48: Ophir
A source of gold named in the royal trading accounts; its location is uncertain.
