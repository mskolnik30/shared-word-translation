## The queen of Sheba

<p>
v01: The queen of Sheba heard of Solomon’s fame in connection with the LORD’s name. She came to test him with riddles.
v02: She arrived in Jerusalem with a very large retinue, camels carrying spices, a great quantity of gold, and precious stones. She came to Solomon and spoke with him about everything on her heart.
v03: Solomon answered all her questions. Nothing was hidden from the king that he could not explain to her.
</p>

<p>
v04: When the queen of Sheba saw all Solomon’s wisdom, the house he had built,
v05: the food at his table, his servants’ seating, his attendants at their posts and their clothing, his cupbearers, and the burnt offering he offered at the LORD’s house, she was left breathless.
</p>

<p>
v06: She told the king, “The report I heard in my own land about your words and wisdom was true.
v07: But I did not believe it until I came and saw with my own eyes. Not even half was told to me! Your wisdom and prosperity exceed what I heard.
v08: How fortunate your men are! How fortunate these servants of yours, who stand before you always and hear your wisdom!
</p>

<p>
v09: Blessed be the LORD your God, who delighted in you and placed you on Israel’s throne. Because the LORD loves Israel forever, he made you king to do justice and righteousness.”
</p>

<p>
v10: She gave the king a hundred twenty talents of gold, an immense quantity of spices, and precious stones. Never again did such an abundance of spices arrive as the queen of Sheba gave King Solomon.
v11: Hiram’s fleet, which brought gold from Ophir, also brought great quantities of almug wood and precious stones.
v12: The king used the almug wood for supports in the LORD’s house and the king’s house, and for lyres and harps for the singers. No such almug wood has arrived or been seen to this day.
</p>

<p>
v13: King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba everything she desired and asked for, besides what he gave her from his royal bounty. Then she and her servants returned to her land.
</p>

## Solomon’s wealth

<p>
v14: The gold that came to Solomon in one year weighed six hundred sixty-six talents,
v15: besides what came from traders, merchants’ business, all the kings of the mixed peoples, and the governors of the land.
v16: King Solomon made two hundred large shields of beaten gold, using six hundred shekels of gold for each.
v17: He also made three hundred smaller shields of beaten gold, using three minas for each, and placed them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.
</p>

<p>
v18: The king made a great ivory throne and overlaid it with refined gold.
v19: Six steps led up to it. The throne had a rounded top at the back, armrests on either side of the seat, and two lions standing beside the armrests.
v20: Twelve lions stood on the six steps, one at each end of every step. Nothing like it had been made in any kingdom.
</p>

<p>
v21: All King Solomon’s drinking vessels were gold, and all the vessels in the House of the Forest of Lebanon were pure gold. None were silver; silver counted for nothing in Solomon’s days.
v22: The king had a Tarshish fleet at sea with Hiram’s fleet. Once every three years the Tarshish fleet returned carrying gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks.
</p>

<p>
v23: King Solomon surpassed all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom.
v24: The whole earth sought an audience with Solomon to hear the wisdom God had put in his heart.
v25: Year after year each brought a gift: silver and gold vessels, garments, weapons, spices, horses, and mules.
</p>

<p>
v26: Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen. He had fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen, stationed in the chariot towns and with the king in Jerusalem.
v27: The king made silver as common in Jerusalem as stones, and cedar as plentiful as the sycamores in the lowlands.
</p>

<p>
v28: Solomon’s horses were imported from Egypt and Kue. The king’s merchants bought them from Kue at an agreed price.
v29: A chariot imported from Egypt cost six hundred silver shekels, and a horse a hundred fifty. Through these merchants they were also exported to all the Hittite kings and the kings of Aram.
</p>

## Notes

v05: “Burnt offering” follows the wording’s sacrificial sense. The phrase can also be understood as Solomon’s ascent or approach to the temple.

v08: The pinned Hebrew says “your men.” The translation retains this gendered wording.

v12: The almug wood’s identity and the exact form of the architectural supports are uncertain.

v15: The pinned vocalization suggests “mixed peoples.” “Arabia” is another way the underlying consonants have been understood.

v16,29: The weight unit shekel is supplied with the numbers where the Hebrew leaves it unstated. Verse 17 explicitly names minas.

v22: “Tarshish fleet” identifies the fleet, without necessarily specifying the destination of each voyage. The last cargo terms are uncertain; ivory, apes, and peacocks are conventional identifications.

v28: “Kue” takes the disputed term as a place name. It can also be understood in connection with a gathering or company of traders.

## Vocabulary

v01: Riddles
Testing questions or difficult sayings, not necessarily word games.

v17: Mina
A unit of weight, used here for gold.

v27: Lowland
The lower hill country contrasted with Jerusalem and the central hills.
