## A shared fate and life’s gifts

<p>
v01: I took all this to heart and examined it: the righteous, the wise, and their deeds are in God’s hand. Whether love or hatred awaits, people do not know; everything lies before them.
v02: The same thing comes to everyone: one fate for the righteous and the wicked, the good, the clean and the unclean, those who sacrifice and those who do not. The good fare like sinners; those who swear an oath like those afraid to swear.
v03: This is an evil in everything done under the sun: the same fate comes to all. Human hearts are full of evil, and madness fills them while they live. Afterward, they go to the dead.
v04: Anyone still joined to the living has hope: a living dog is better than a dead lion.
v05: The living know they will die, but the dead know nothing. They have no further reward, for even the memory of them is forgotten.
v06: Their love, hatred, and jealousy have already perished. Never again do they have a portion in anything done under the sun.
v07: Go, eat your bread with joy and drink your wine with a glad heart, for God has already accepted what you do.
v08: Let your clothes always be white, and never let your head lack oil.
v09: Enjoy life with the wife you love, through all the days of your breathlike life given you under the sun—all your breathlike days. That is your portion in life and in the work you do under the sun.
v10: Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your strength. There is no work, planning, knowledge, or wisdom in Sheol, where you are going.
</p>

## Time, chance, and overlooked wisdom

<p>
v11: Again I saw under the sun: the race does not belong to the swift, nor the battle to the strong; bread does not belong to the wise, nor wealth to the discerning, nor favor to the knowledgeable. Time and chance meet them all.
v12: People do not even know their time. Like fish caught in a cruel net or birds trapped in a snare, they are caught when an evil time suddenly falls on them.
v13: I also saw this example of wisdom under the sun, and it impressed me greatly:
v14: There was a small city with few people. A powerful king came against it, surrounded it, and built great siegeworks against it.
v15: A poor, wise man was found there, and he rescued the city by his wisdom. Yet no one remembered that poor man.
v16: I said, “Wisdom is better than strength.” Yet the poor man’s wisdom is despised, and no one listens to his words.
v17: Quiet words from the wise are better heard
than the shouting of a ruler among fools.
v18: Wisdom is better than weapons of war,
but one sinner can destroy much good.
</p>

## Notes

v04: The reading tradition has “joined”; the written consonants suggest “chosen.” The draft follows the reading tradition.

v05–06: The speaker describes the dead as excluded from what happens “under the sun.” The translation leaves his stark claims intact without adding a systematic account of life after death.

## Vocabulary

v10: **Sheol**: The realm of the dead in the poem’s Hebrew vocabulary.
