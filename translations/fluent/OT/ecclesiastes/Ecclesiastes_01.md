## Breath, cycles, and the search for wisdom

<p>
v01: The words of Qohelet, son of David, king in Jerusalem.
v02: “Breath of breaths,” says Qohelet,
“breath of breaths! Everything is a breath.”
v03: What gain does a person have from all the toil
he toils at under the sun?
v04: A generation goes, and a generation comes,
but the earth stands through the ages.
v05: The sun rises, and the sun sets;
it pants back to the place where it rises.
v06: The wind goes south and circles north;
round and round it goes,
returning on its circuits.
v07: All streams flow to the sea,
but the sea is never full.
To the place where the streams flow,
there they flow again.
v08: All things are wearisome,
more than anyone can express.
The eye is not satisfied with seeing,
nor the ear filled with hearing.
v09: What has been is what will be;
what has been done is what will be done.
There is nothing new under the sun.
v10: Is there something of which one can say, “Look, this is new”?
It already existed in ages before us.
v11: There is no remembrance of those who came before;
even those yet to come will not be remembered
by those who come afterward.
</p>

## Qohelet’s search

<p>
v12: I, Qohelet, was king over Israel in Jerusalem.
v13: I set my heart to seek and investigate with wisdom everything done under the heavens. It is a grievous task that God has given humankind to occupy them.
v14: I saw all the deeds done under the sun. Look: everything is a breath, a herding of wind.
v15: What is crooked cannot be straightened;
what is missing cannot be counted.
v16: I said to myself, “Look, I have grown in wisdom beyond everyone who was over Jerusalem before me. My heart has seen much wisdom and knowledge.”
v17: I set my heart to know wisdom and to know madness and folly. I learned that this too is a herding of wind.
v18: For with much wisdom comes much frustration;
whoever adds knowledge adds pain.
</p>

## Notes

v02,14: Hevel is literally breath or vapor. Its repeated use can suggest transience, frustration, or something that cannot be grasped; one English explanation does not cover every occurrence. “Herding wind” keeps the impossible task pictured by the Hebrew; “chasing wind” is another rendering.

v08,11–12: “Things” in verse 8 can also be “words.” The former and later ones in verse 11 may be people or events. Verse 12 begins Qohelet’s first-person royal account; the text’s identification is preserved without adding a personal name.

## Vocabulary

v01: **Qohelet**: A Hebrew title related to gathering an assembly, often rendered “Teacher” or “Preacher.” Here the title identifies the book’s questioning speaker.
