## Acting without knowing everything

<p>
v01: Send your bread out over the water;
after many days you will find it.
v02: Give a portion to seven, even to eight,
for you do not know what disaster may come on the earth.
v03: If clouds are full of rain, they empty it on the earth.
If a tree falls south or north, it stays where it falls.
v04: Keep watching the wind, and you will never sow;
keep looking at the clouds, and you will never reap.
v05: You do not know the path of the wind, or how bones take shape in a pregnant woman’s womb. Just so, you do not know the work of God, who makes everything.
v06: Sow your seed in the morning, and do not let your hand rest in the evening. You do not know which will succeed—this or that, or whether both will do well.
</p>

## Youth and the days ahead

<p>
v07: Light is sweet,
and it is good for the eyes to see the sun.
v08: If a person lives many years, let them rejoice in them all. But remember the days of darkness, for there will be many. Everything to come is breath.
v09: Rejoice, young man, in your youth. Let your heart delight in your young days. Follow your heart’s paths and what your eyes see—but know that for all this God will bring you to judgment.
v10: Remove vexation from your heart and pain from your body, for youth and the dawn of life are breath.
</p>

## Notes

v01–02: Bread sent across water and shares given to seven or eight are open images. They have been understood in terms of generosity, trade, and spreading risk; the translation does not replace the images with one explanation.

v05: “Wind” can also be “breath” or “spirit.” The line can join two unknown processes, or speak of breath entering the child whose bones form in the womb.

## Vocabulary

v10: **Dawn of life**: A rare expression associated with youth; it may evoke early life or dark hair.
