# Ecclesiastes 12

<p>
v01: Remember your Creator in the days of your youth,
before the days of trouble come
and the years draw near when you will say,
“I have no delight in them”—

v02: before the sun and the light grow dark,
and the moon and the stars,
and the clouds return after the rain;

v03: on the day when the guards of the house tremble,
and the strong men bend themselves,
and the women who grind cease because they are few,
and people who look through the windows grow dim;

v04: when the doors are shut in the street,
as the sound of the mill grows low;
when one rises at the sound of a bird,
and all the daughters of song are brought low;

v05: when they are afraid of heights,
and terrors are in the road;
when the almond tree blossoms,
the grasshopper drags itself along,
and the caper berry fails—
for a person goes to their everlasting home,
and the mourners circle in the street;

v06: before the silver cord is snapped,
and the golden bowl is crushed,
and the jar is broken at the spring,
and the wheel is crushed at the well;

v07: and the dust returns to the earth as it was,
and the spirit returns to God who gave it.

v08: “Vapor of vapors,” says the Teacher;
“all is vapor.”
</p>

<p>
v09: Beyond this, because the Teacher was wise, he continually taught the people knowledge. He weighed and searched out and set in order many proverbs.
</p>

<p>
v10: The Teacher sought to find words of delight, and to write upright words of truth.
</p>

<p>
v11: The words of the wise are like goads, and like firmly fixed nails are the masters of collections; they are given by one shepherd.
</p>

<p>
v12: And beyond these, my child, be warned: there is no end to the making of many books, and much study wearies the flesh.
</p>

<p>
v13: The end of the matter; all has been heard: fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole of humanity.
</p>

<p>
v14: For God will bring every deed into judgment, with every hidden thing, whether good or evil.
</p>

## Notes

v01: — “your Creator.” The Hebrew form is plural, literally “your Creators,” though it takes a singular sense here. The plural may heighten the majesty and fullness of God as Creator.

v07: — “the spirit.” The Hebrew *ruach* can mean spirit, breath, or wind.

v11: — “masters of collections.” The phrase may refer to those responsible for assembling and preserving sayings, or to collected sayings themselves.

v13: — “the whole of humanity.” The Hebrew expression is compact and may mean “the whole duty of humanity,” “what is fitting for every person,” or “the whole of humanity.” The translation preserves its broad claim: reverence for God and obedience to God’s commands concern human life as such.

## Vocabulary

v01: בָּרָא (bara)
“To create.” Appears here in the expression translated “your Creator.”

v07: רוּחַ (ruach)
“Spirit; breath; wind.” Here it is the life given by God that returns to God.

v08: הֶבֶל (hevel)
“Vapor.” The book’s recurring image for what is fleeting, elusive, or resistant to human control.

v13: יָרֵא (yare)
“To fear.” Here, reverent recognition of God joined with keeping the commandments.

v14: מִשְׁפָּט (mishpat)
“Judgment.” The act or process of rendering judgment.
