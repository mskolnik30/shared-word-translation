## Remember your Creator

<p>
v01: Remember your Creator in the days of your youth,
before the evil days arrive
and the years draw near when you say,
“I find no pleasure in them”;
v02: before the sun and light grow dark,
the moon and stars,
and the clouds return after the rain;
v03: when the house’s guards tremble
and the strong men bend,
the women grinding stop because they are few,
and those looking through the windows grow dim;
v04: when the doors to the street are shut
and the mill’s sound fades,
when one rises at a bird’s call
and all the daughters of song are brought low;
v05: when people fear heights
and terrors along the road;
when the almond tree blossoms,
the grasshopper drags itself along,
and the caper berry fails—
for a person is going to an enduring home,
and mourners circle the streets.
v06: Remember, before the silver cord is snapped,
the golden bowl is crushed,
the jar is broken at the spring,
and the wheel is crushed at the cistern;
v07: before the dust returns to the earth as it was,
and the spirit returns to God who gave it.
v08: “Breath of breaths,” says Qohelet.
“Everything is breath.”
</p>

## The concluding assessment

<p>
v09: Besides being wise, Qohelet taught the people knowledge. He weighed, investigated, and arranged many proverbs.
v10: Qohelet sought words that would give delight, and words of truth written with integrity.
v11: The words of the wise are like goads, and collected sayings like firmly planted nails, given by one shepherd.
v12: Beyond these, my son, be warned: there is no end to making many books, and much study wears out the body.
v13: The end of the matter, now that all has been heard: fear God and keep his commandments, for this concerns everyone.
v14: God will bring every deed into judgment, including every hidden thing, whether good or evil.
</p>

## Notes

v02–06: The aging and death imagery is left as poetry. Identifying every household object or figure with one body part would make the translation more certain than the text.

v11: The phrase may refer to collected sayings or to masters of collections. “One shepherd” is not explicitly identified in the verse.

v13: Literally, “this is all of the human.” It can express everyone’s obligation or what makes up a whole human life.

## Vocabulary

v08: **Breath**: The recurring opening image returns at the close of Qohelet’s words, before the book’s concluding assessment.
