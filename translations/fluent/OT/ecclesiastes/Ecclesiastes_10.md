## Wisdom, folly, and public life

<p>
v01: Dead flies make a perfumer’s oil stink and ferment;
a little folly outweighs wisdom and honor.
v02: A wise person’s heart leans right;
a fool’s heart leans left.
v03: Even when a fool walks along the road, he lacks sense and shows everyone he is a fool.
v04: If a ruler’s anger rises against you, do not leave your place. Calmness can lay great offenses to rest.
v05: There is an evil I have seen under the sun, like an error that comes from a ruler:
v06: folly is placed in many high positions, while the rich sit low.
v07: I have seen slaves on horses and princes walking on the ground like slaves.
v08: Whoever digs a pit may fall into it;
whoever breaks through a wall may be bitten by a snake.
v09: Quarrying stones can injure you;
splitting wood can put you in danger.
v10: If the iron is blunt and its edge is not sharpened, more strength is needed. Wisdom’s advantage is that it brings success.
v11: If a snake bites before it is charmed, the charmer’s skill brings no advantage.
v12: Words from a wise mouth bring favor;
a fool’s lips swallow him up.
v13: His words begin in folly
and end in evil madness.
v14: Yet the fool keeps talking. No one knows what will happen; who can tell anyone what will come afterward?
v15: A fool’s work wears him out;
he does not even know the way to town.
v16: How miserable for you, land whose king is a boy
and whose princes feast in the morning!
v17: How fortunate for you, land whose king is nobly born
and whose princes eat at the proper time,
for strength and not for drunkenness!
v18: Through laziness the roof sags;
through idle hands the house leaks.
v19: A feast is made for laughter,
wine makes life glad,
and money answers everything.
v20: Do not curse the king even in your thoughts, or the rich even in your bedroom. A bird of the sky may carry your voice; a winged creature may report your words.
</p>

## Notes

v03: The last clause may mean that the fool reveals his own folly to everyone, or calls everyone else a fool.

v10: The compressed final clause connects wisdom with successful action; its exact syntax is uncertain. The blunt-iron comparison remains visible.

v16: “Boy” may emphasize youth, inexperience, or low status. The contrast is with a king of noble birth in the next verse.

## Vocabulary

v07: **Slaves**: Enslaved people. The reversal of riding and walking concerns status in the speaker’s social world.
