## Wisdom and its limits

<p>
v01: A good name is better than fine oil,
and the day of death than the day of birth.
v02: Better to enter a house of mourning
than a house of feasting,
for death is everyone’s end,
and the living should take it to heart.
v03: Sorrow is better than laughter,
for a sad face can make the heart better.
v04: The wise have their hearts in the house of mourning;
fools have theirs in the house of pleasure.
v05: Better to hear a wise person’s rebuke
than to listen to the song of fools.
v06: Like thorns crackling under a pot,
so is a fool’s laughter.
This too is breath.
v07: Oppression can drive the wise mad,
and a bribe destroys the heart.
v08: Better the end of a matter than its beginning;
better a patient spirit than a proud one.
v09: Do not be quick in spirit to anger,
for anger settles in a fool’s lap.
v10: Do not ask, “Why were the old days better than these?” Wisdom does not prompt that question.
v11: Wisdom is good with an inheritance,
an advantage to those who see the sun.
v12: Wisdom offers shade, and money offers shade;
but knowledge has this advantage:
wisdom keeps its possessors alive.
v13: Look at God’s work. Who can straighten what he has bent?
v14: On a good day, enjoy the good; on a bad day, consider: God has made the one as well as the other, so that people cannot discover what comes after them.
v15: In my breathlike days I have seen everything: a righteous person dying in righteousness, and a wicked person living long in wickedness.
v16: Do not be excessively righteous or make yourself too wise. Why bring ruin on yourself?
v17: Do not be excessively wicked, and do not be a fool. Why die before your time?
v18: It is good to grasp the one warning without letting go of the other. Whoever fears God will come through both.
v19: Wisdom makes the wise stronger
than ten rulers in a city.
v20: There is no righteous person on earth
who does good and never sins.
v21: Do not take to heart every word people say, or you may hear your servant cursing you.
v22: Your own heart knows how often you too have cursed others.
</p>

## What the search found

<p>
v23: I tested all this by wisdom. I said, “I will become wise,” but wisdom was far from me.
v24: What exists is far away,
deep, so deep—who can find it?
v25: I turned my mind to knowing, exploring, and seeking wisdom and an explanation, and to knowing the wickedness of folly and the madness of foolishness.
v26: I find more bitter than death the woman who is snares, whose heart is nets and whose hands are chains. Whoever pleases God escapes her, but the sinner is caught by her.
v27: “Look, this is what I found,” says Qohelet, “adding one thing to another to find an explanation—
v28: something I kept seeking but did not find. I found one man among a thousand, but among all these I found no woman.
v29: Only this have I found: God made humanity upright, but people have sought many schemes.”
</p>

## Notes

v16–18: The text says “too righteous,” not “self-righteous.” Its warning about destructive excess stands alongside the warning against wickedness and the call to fear God.

v26–28: These are Qohelet’s gender-specific judgments within his account of searching. The translation preserves both their severity and his repeated limits on what he has found.

## Vocabulary

v27: **Qohelet**: The Hebrew designation used for the book’s speaking teacher; it is related to gathering or an assembly.
