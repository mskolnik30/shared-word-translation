## A time for every activity

<p>
v01: For everything there is a season,
a time for every activity under the heavens:
v02: a time to give birth and a time to die,
a time to plant and a time to uproot what is planted;
v03: a time to kill and a time to heal,
a time to tear down and a time to build;
v04: a time to weep and a time to laugh,
a time to mourn and a time to dance;
v05: a time to throw stones and a time to gather stones,
a time to embrace and a time to stay away from embracing;
v06: a time to seek and a time to lose,
a time to keep and a time to throw away;
v07: a time to tear and a time to sew,
a time to be silent and a time to speak;
v08: a time to love and a time to hate,
a time for war and a time for peace.
</p>

## The limits of knowing

<p>
v09: What gain does the worker have from his toil?
v10: I have seen the task God has given humankind to occupy them.
v11: He has made everything fitting in its time. He has also put eternity in their hearts, yet humans cannot discover the work God has done from beginning to end.
v12: I know there is nothing better for them than to rejoice and do good during their lives.
v13: Moreover, when anyone eats, drinks, and enjoys good in all his toil, this is God’s gift.
v14: I know that everything God does endures forever. Nothing can be added to it, and nothing taken from it. God has acted so that people will fear before him.
v15: What is has already been; what will be has already been. And God seeks what has been driven away.
</p>

## Injustice and a shared breath

<p>
v16: I saw something else under the sun: in the place of judgment, wickedness was there; in the place of righteousness, wickedness was there.
v17: I said in my heart, “God will judge the righteous and the wicked, for there is a time for every activity and for every deed there.”
v18: I said in my heart concerning human beings, “God is testing them, so they may see that they themselves are animals.”
v19: For humans have a fate and animals have a fate; one fate belongs to them both. As one dies, so does the other. They all have one breath, and humans have no advantage over animals, for everything is a breath.
v20: All go to one place. All came from dust, and all return to dust.
v21: Who knows whether the human spirit rises upward and the animal’s spirit goes downward to the earth?
v22: So I saw that there is nothing better than for a person to rejoice in his work, for that is his portion. Who can bring him to see what will come after him?
</p>

## Notes

v02–08: The poem sets fourteen pairs of times beside one another. It includes killing, hatred, and war without turning each line into a command. The stone images in verse 5 are left unexplained.

v11,15,17: “Eternity” can denote long duration or the whole stretch of time. God seeking what is “driven away” may concern the pursued or what has passed away. The final “there” in verse 17 remains difficult; no location is supplied.

v19–21: Human and animal mortality are stated together. Ruach can mean breath or spirit; verse 19 emphasizes shared breath, while verse 21 asks about their direction after death. The question is retained as a question.

## Vocabulary

v22: **Portion**: The share a person receives, here the enjoyment of work amid limits on knowing what comes afterward.
