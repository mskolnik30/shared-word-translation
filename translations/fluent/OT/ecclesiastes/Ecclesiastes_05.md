## Words, vows, and reverence

<p>
v01: Do not let your mouth rush ahead, or your heart hurry to speak before God. God is in heaven, and you are on earth, so keep your words few.
v02: Dreams come with much preoccupation;
a fool’s voice comes with many words.
v03: When you make a vow to God, do not delay paying it. He takes no pleasure in fools. Pay what you vow.
v04: Better not to vow
than to vow and leave it unpaid.
v05: Do not let your mouth lead your body into sin. Do not tell the messenger, “It was a mistake.” Why make God angry at your voice and let him destroy the work of your hands?
v06: With many dreams come much breath and many words. Fear God instead.
</p>

## Power, wealth, and enjoyment

<p>
v07: If you see the poor oppressed and justice and righteousness stolen in a province, do not be astonished: one high official watches another, with still higher officials above them.
v08: The land’s yield benefits everyone; even a king depends on cultivated fields.
v09: A lover of silver never has enough silver;
a lover of wealth never has enough income.
This too is breath.
v10: As goods multiply, so do the people who consume them. What benefit do their owners gain, beyond looking at them?
v11: A laborer sleeps sweetly, whether the meal is small or large. But a rich person’s full stomach will not let him sleep.
v12: I have seen a grievous evil under the sun: wealth kept by its owner to his own hurt.
v13: That wealth is lost in a bad venture. He has a son, but nothing remains in his hands.
v14: Naked he came from his mother’s womb, and naked he will leave, just as he came. Nothing from his hard work can he carry away in his hand.
v15: This too is a grievous evil: he goes exactly as he came. What does he gain from working for the wind?
v16: All his days he eats in darkness, with much vexation, sickness, and anger.
v17: Here is what I have seen to be good and fitting: to eat, drink, and enjoy the good in all the work one does under the sun, during the few days of life God gives. This is one’s portion.
v18: When God gives someone wealth and possessions, and the power to enjoy them, to receive a portion and delight in work, that is God’s gift.
v19: Such a person does not dwell much on life’s days, because God keeps the heart occupied with joy.
</p>

## Notes

v05: “Messenger” can also mean “angel.” The setting involves an unfulfilled vow, but the messenger’s exact role is unstated.

v08: This compressed verse is difficult. It may describe the king’s dependence on cultivated land or the advantage of a king who serves agriculture; it follows a description of officials watching officials.

v19: The verb can suggest that God keeps this person occupied with gladness, or answers through gladness. The verse does not erase the injustices described earlier.

## Vocabulary

v03: **Vow**: A promise made to God. The warning concerns fulfilling what one has freely promised.
