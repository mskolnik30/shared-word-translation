## The Beloved Comes to the Garden

<p>
v01: I have come into my garden, my sister, my bride.
I have gathered my myrrh with my spice,
eaten my honeycomb with my honey,
drunk my wine with my milk.
Eat, friends! Drink deeply, lovers!
</p>

## The Woman's Dream

<p>
v02: I was asleep, but my heart was awake.
The sound of my beloved knocking!
“Open to me, my sister, my love,
my dove, my perfect one.
My head is drenched with dew,
my hair with the drops of night.”
</p>

<p>
v03: “I have taken off my tunic—
how can I put it on again?
I have washed my feet—
how can I dirty them?”
v04: My beloved reached his hand through the opening,
and my inward parts stirred for him.
v05: I rose to open to my beloved.
My hands dripped myrrh,
my fingers flowing myrrh
on the handles of the bolt.
</p>

<p>
v06: I opened to my beloved,
but my beloved had slipped away, gone.
My very life went out when he spoke.
I sought him but did not find him;
I called, but he did not answer.
</p>

<p>
v07: The watchmen found me
as they made their rounds in the city.
They struck me; they wounded me.
The guards of the walls
took my wrap from me.
</p>

<p>
v08: I put you under oath, daughters of Jerusalem:
if you find my beloved, what will you tell him?
That I am sick with love.
</p>

## The Daughters of Jerusalem Respond

<p>
v09: How is your beloved more than another beloved,
most beautiful of women?
How is your beloved more than another beloved,
that you put us under such an oath?
</p>

## The Woman Praises Her Beloved

<p>
v10: My beloved is radiant and ruddy,
standing out among ten thousand.
v11: His head is finest gold;
his hair falls in curls,
black as a raven.
</p>

<p>
v12: His eyes are like doves beside streams of water,
bathed in milk,
securely set.
v13: His cheeks are beds of spice,
mounds of fragrant herbs.
His lips are lilies
dripping flowing myrrh.
</p>

<p>
v14: His arms are rods of gold
set with beryl.
His abdomen is polished ivory
overlaid with sapphires.
v15: His legs are pillars of marble
set on bases of fine gold.
His appearance is like Lebanon,
choice as its cedars.
</p>

<p>
v16: His mouth is sweetness itself;
every part of him is desirable.
This is my beloved, this is my friend,
daughters of Jerusalem.
</p>

## Notes

v01: The invitation at the end changes its addressees to a group. The text does not identify a separate speaker for it.

v06: The Hebrew says her life went out “when he spoke,” even though the narrative now finds him gone. The draft preserves this difficult sequence.

v07: The guards beat and wound her and take her wrap. Their violence remains explicit within the searching scene.

v12: The final phrase literally evokes being set in fullness; it is often understood as eyes fitting securely in their settings.

## Vocabulary

v14: **Beryl**: An approximate name for the Hebrew gemstone; ancient stone names do not map securely to modern mineral categories.
