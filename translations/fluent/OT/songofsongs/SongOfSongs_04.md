## The Beloved Praises the Woman

<p>
v01: How beautiful you are, my love!
How beautiful!
Your eyes are doves behind your veil.
Your hair is like a flock of goats
streaming down Mount Gilead.
</p>

<p>
v02: Your teeth are like a flock of shorn sheep
coming up from washing,
each bearing twins,
none bereaved among them.
v03: Your lips are a scarlet thread;
your mouth is lovely.
Your temples are slices of pomegranate
behind your veil.
</p>

<p>
v04: Your neck is like David’s tower,
built in courses;
a thousand shields hang on it,
all the shields of warriors.
v05: Your two breasts are like two fawns,
twins of a gazelle,
grazing among the lilies.
</p>

<p>
v06: Until the day breathes and the shadows flee,
I will go to the mountain of myrrh
and the hill of frankincense.
v07: You are beautiful in every part, my love;
there is no blemish in you.
</p>

## Come with Me from Lebanon

<p>
v08: Come with me from Lebanon, bride;
come with me from Lebanon.
Look down from Amana’s summit,
from the peaks of Senir and Hermon,
from lions’ dens,
from mountains of leopards.
</p>

<p>
v09: You have captured my heart, my sister, my bride;
you have captured my heart
with one glance of your eyes,
with one jewel of your necklace.
v10: How lovely your caresses, my sister, my bride!
How much better than wine!
The fragrance of your oils surpasses every spice.
</p>

<p>
v11: Your lips drip nectar, bride;
honey and milk are beneath your tongue.
Your clothes smell like Lebanon.
</p>

## A Garden Locked

<p>
v12: A locked garden is my sister, my bride,
a locked spring, a sealed fountain.
v13: Your shoots are an orchard of pomegranates
with choice fruits,
henna with nard,
v14: nard and saffron, sweet cane and cinnamon,
with every frankincense tree,
myrrh and aloes,
with all the finest spices.
</p>

<p>
v15: You are a garden fountain,
a well of living water,
streams flowing from Lebanon.
</p>

## The Woman's Invitation

<p>
v16: Wake, north wind! Come, south wind!
Blow through my garden; let its spices flow.
Let my beloved come into his garden
and eat its choice fruits.
</p>

## Notes

v04: The description of the tower’s construction is uncertain; “built in courses” is one understanding of the rare expression.

v08: The verb can suggest looking down from the heights or coming away from them. Lebanon and the named peaks remain part of the poem’s landscape.

v12: The Hebrew repeats the closed or locked image with a rare word commonly understood as a spring; some witnesses repeat “garden.”

## Vocabulary

v09: **My sister, my bride**: A term of intimacy in this love poetry. The endearment itself does not establish that the lovers are biological siblings.
