## The Daughters of Jerusalem Speak

<p>
v01: Where has your beloved gone,
most beautiful of women?
Which way did your beloved turn?
We will seek him with you.
</p>

## The Woman Responds

<p>
v02: My beloved has gone down to his garden,
to the beds of spice,
to graze in the gardens
and gather lilies.
v03: I am my beloved’s, and my beloved is mine;
he grazes among the lilies.
</p>

## The Beloved Praises the Woman

<p>
v04: You are beautiful as Tirzah, my love,
lovely as Jerusalem,
awesome as troops with banners.
v05: Turn your eyes away from me;
they overwhelm me.
Your hair is like a flock of goats
streaming down from Gilead.
</p>

<p>
v06: Your teeth are like a flock of ewes
coming up from washing,
each bearing twins,
none bereaved among them.
v07: Your temples are slices of pomegranate
behind your veil.
</p>

<p>
v08: There may be sixty queens,
eighty concubines,
and young women beyond counting,
v09: but my dove, my perfect one, is unique,
her mother’s only one,
the cherished one of her who bore her.
Young women saw her and called her fortunate;
queens and concubines praised her.
</p>

<p>
v10: “Who is this looking out like the dawn,
beautiful as the moon,
clear as the sun,
awesome as troops with banners?”
</p>

## The Woman in the Garden

<p>
v11: I went down to the nut orchard
to see the valley’s new growth,
to see whether the vines had budded
and the pomegranates were in bloom.
v12: Before I knew it, my desire had set me
among the chariots of my noble people.
</p>

## Return, Shulammite

<p>
v13: Return, return, Shulammite!
Return, return, so we may look at you!
Why would you look at the Shulammite
as at a dance of two camps?
</p>

## Notes

v12: This is one of the book’s most difficult lines. It may describe being carried among chariots of noble people, or contain a personal name, Amminadib. The draft preserves the chariot image and marks the uncertainty.

v13: This verse is 7:1 in the Hebrew source. The next thirteen public verses correspond to Hebrew 7:2–14. “Two camps” can also evoke the place name Mahanaim.

## Vocabulary

v13: **Shulammite**: A designation whose relation to a place or to Solomon is uncertain; the poem does not explain it.
