## The people want to return

<p>
v01: The whole community cried out, and the people wept that night.
v02: All the Israelites complained against Moses and Aaron. The whole community said to them, “If only we had died in Egypt! If only we had died in this wilderness!
v03: Why is the LORD bringing us into this land to fall by the sword? Our wives and little ones will be taken as plunder. Wouldn’t we be better off going back to Egypt?”
</p>

<p>
v04: They said to one another, “Let’s choose a leader and go back to Egypt.”
</p>

<p>
v05: Moses and Aaron fell facedown before the assembled Israelite community.
v06: Joshua son of Nun and Caleb son of Jephunneh, who had scouted the land, tore their clothes.
</p>

<p>
v07: They told the whole Israelite community, “The land we went through to scout is very, very good.
v08: If the LORD delights in us, he will bring us into this land and give it to us. It is a land flowing with milk and honey.
v09: Only do not rebel against the LORD. Do not fear the people of the land—they are bread for us! Their shade has left them, and the LORD is with us. Do not fear them!”
</p>

<p>
v10: The whole community called for them to be stoned. Then the LORD’s glory appeared at the tent of meeting to all the Israelites.
</p>

## Moses pleads for the people

<p>
v11: The LORD said to Moses, “How long will these people despise me? How long will they refuse to trust me, despite all the signs I have done among them?
v12: I will strike them with plague and disinherit them. Then I will make you a greater and stronger nation than they are.”
</p>

<p>
v13: Moses said to the LORD, “The Egyptians will hear about it. You brought these people out from among them by your power,
v14: and they will tell the inhabitants of this land. They have heard that you, LORD, are among these people, that you, LORD, appear eye to eye, that your cloud stands over them, and that you go ahead of them in a pillar of cloud by day and a pillar of fire by night.
v15: If you kill this people as one, the nations that have heard of you will say,
v16: ‘The LORD could not bring these people into the land he swore to give them, so he slaughtered them in the wilderness.’
</p>

<p>
v17: Now, please, let my Lord’s power be great, just as you said:
v18: ‘The LORD is slow to anger,
abounding in faithful love,
forgiving guilt and rebellion,
yet certainly not clearing the guilty,
bringing the fathers’ guilt upon their children,
to the third and fourth generations.’
</p>

<p>
v19: Please forgive these people’s wrongdoing in keeping with your great faithful love, just as you have borne with them from Egypt until now.”
</p>

<p>
v20: The LORD replied, “I have forgiven them, as you asked.
v21: But as surely as I live, and as surely as the LORD’s glory fills the whole earth,
v22: all those who have seen my glory and the signs I did in Egypt and the wilderness, yet have tested me these ten times and have not listened to my voice—
v23: they will never see the land I swore to give their ancestors. None who despise me will see it.
</p>

<p>
v24: But my servant Caleb has a different spirit and has followed me fully. I will bring him into the land he entered, and his descendants will possess it.
</p>

<p>
v25: The Amalekites and Canaanites live in the valley. Tomorrow, turn back and set out into the wilderness by the road toward the Sea of Reeds.”
</p>

## Forty years in the wilderness

<p>
v26: The LORD then spoke to Moses and Aaron:
v27: “How long—this evil community, complaining against me! I have heard the Israelites’ complaints against me.
</p>

<p>
v28: Tell them, ‘As surely as I live, declares the LORD, I will do to you exactly what you have said in my ears.
v29: Your corpses will fall in this wilderness—all of you who were counted, your full number from twenty years old and up, who complained against me.
v30: You will certainly not enter the land where I raised my hand and swore to settle you, except Caleb son of Jephunneh and Joshua son of Nun.
</p>

<p>
v31: But your little ones, whom you said would become plunder, I will bring in. They will know the land you rejected.
v32: As for you, your corpses will fall in this wilderness.
v33: Your children will live as shepherds in the wilderness for forty years, bearing the cost of your prostitution, until the last of your corpses lies in the wilderness.
v34: For the forty days you scouted the land, you will bear your wrongdoing for forty years—a year for each day, a year for each day. You will know my opposition.
v35: I, the LORD, have spoken. I will certainly do this to the whole evil community that has gathered against me. They will come to their end in this wilderness; there they will die.’”
</p>

<p>
v36: The men Moses had sent to scout the land came back and stirred the whole community to complain against him by spreading a bad report about the land.
v37: Those men who spread the bad report died by a plague before the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v38: Of the men who went to scout the land, Joshua son of Nun and Caleb son of Jephunneh remained alive.
</p>

## An ascent against the warning

<p>
v39: When Moses told all this to the Israelites, the people mourned deeply.
</p>

<p>
v40: Early the next morning they went up toward the heights of the hill country. “Here we are!” they said. “We will go up to the place the LORD spoke of. We have sinned.”
</p>

<p>
v41: But Moses said, “Why are you disobeying the LORD’s command? This will not succeed.
v42: Do not go up. The LORD is not among you, and your enemies will defeat you.
v43: Amalekites and Canaanites are there ahead of you, and you will fall by the sword. You have turned away from following the LORD, so the LORD will not be with you.”
</p>

<p>
v44: Still, they presumed to go up into the heights of the hill country. Neither Moses nor the ark of the LORD’s covenant left the camp.
v45: The Amalekites and Canaanites who lived in that hill country came down, attacked them and battered them all the way to Hormah.
</p>

## Notes

v9: “Bread for us” pictures the enemy as easily consumed. Their departed “shade” pictures lost protection; the text does not explicitly identify that protection.

v14: “Eye to eye” is the Hebrew image for direct appearance or encounter. It differs from the “mouth to mouth” language in 12:8.

v18–20: Moses recalls Exodus 34:6–7, retaining both forgiveness and the statement about guilt across generations. This quotation omits some clauses found there. The LORD’s forgiveness in verse 20 does not cancel the consequences narrated afterward.

v19: The verb rendered “borne with” can also mean “forgiven.” It carries the image of bearing or lifting.

v21: The glory clause can be read as part of the oath, as here, or as a declaration that the whole earth will be filled with the LORD’s glory.

v29: The judgment names those counted from twenty years old and up. Its census language should not be silently expanded to every person in the camp.

v33–34: “Prostitution” is a metaphor for the people’s unfaithfulness, not a report of a particular sexual act. “My opposition” translates a difficult expression also understood in terms of rejection, estrangement or thwarted purpose. The children bear consequences across the stated forty years.

## Vocabulary

v18: ḥesed — faithful love
Steadfast commitment expressed in mercy and faithfulness; the wording connects this appeal with Exodus 34.

v25: Sea of Reeds
Hebrew Yam Suph, traditionally called the Red Sea. The name does not by itself locate a crossing or fix a route.

v30: Raised hand
The gesture of an oath. Here it recalls the LORD’s sworn promise.

v33: Shepherds
The Hebrew names the children’s life tending flocks in the wilderness, rather than only saying they will wander.
