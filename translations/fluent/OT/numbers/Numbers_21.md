## Arad attacks Israel

<p>
v01: The Canaanite king of Arad, who lived in the Negev, heard that Israel was coming along the road of Atharim. He fought Israel and took some of them captive.
v02: Israel made a vow to the LORD: “If you will indeed give these people into our hands, we will devote their cities to destruction.”
v03: The LORD heard Israel’s voice and gave them the Canaanites. Israel devoted them and their cities to destruction, and the place was named Hormah.
</p>

## The snakes and the bronze snake

<p>
v04: They set out from Mount Hor by the road toward the Sea of Reeds to go around Edom. On the journey, the people’s patience ran out.
v05: They spoke against God and Moses: “Why did you bring us up from Egypt to die in the wilderness? There is no bread and no water, and we are sick of this worthless food!”
v06: The LORD sent fiery snakes among the people. They bit the people, and many Israelites died.
</p>

<p>
v07: The people came to Moses. “We have sinned by speaking against the LORD and against you,” they said. “Pray to the LORD to take the snakes away from us.” So Moses prayed for the people.
</p>

<p>
v08: The LORD told Moses, “Make a fiery snake and put it on a pole. Anyone who has been bitten and looks at it will live.”
</p>

<p>
v09: Moses made a bronze snake and put it on the pole. When a snake bit someone, that person looked at the bronze snake and lived.
</p>

## Stages of the journey

<p>
v10: The Israelites traveled on and camped at Oboth.
v11: From Oboth they traveled to Iye-abarim and camped in the wilderness facing Moab, toward the sunrise.
v12: From there they moved on and camped in the Zered Valley.
v13: Then they moved on and camped beyond the Arnon, in the wilderness extending from Amorite territory. The Arnon is Moab’s boundary, between Moab and the Amorites.
</p>

<p>

</p>

<p>

</p>

## Sihon and Og

<p>
v21: Israel sent messengers to Sihon king of the Amorites:
v22: “Let us pass through your land. We will not turn into fields or vineyards or drink water from wells. We will follow the King’s Highway until we have crossed your territory.”
</p>

<p>
v23: Sihon would not let Israel pass through his territory. He gathered all his people and went out into the wilderness against Israel. At Jahaz he fought them.
v24: Israel struck him with the sword and took possession of his land from the Arnon to the Jabbok, as far as the Ammonites. The Ammonite boundary was strong.
</p>

<p>
v25: Israel captured all these towns and settled in the Amorite towns, in Heshbon and all its surrounding villages.
v26: Heshbon had been the city of Sihon king of the Amorites. He had fought the former king of Moab and taken all his land as far as the Arnon.
</p>

<p>
v27: That is why the poets say:
“Come to Heshbon!
Let Sihon’s city be built and firmly established.
v28: Fire went out from Heshbon,
a flame from Sihon’s city.
It consumed Ar of Moab
and the lords of the Arnon’s heights.
v29: Woe to you, Moab!
You are lost, people of Chemosh!
He made his sons fugitives
and his daughters captives
of Sihon king of the Amorites.
v30: We shot them down;
Heshbon perished as far as Dibon.

</p>

<p>
v31: So Israel settled in Amorite territory.
v32: Moses sent men to spy out Jazer. They captured its surrounding villages, and Moses drove out the Amorites there.
</p>

v34: The LORD said to Moses, “Do not fear him. I have given him, all his people and his land into your hand. Do to him what you did to Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon.”
v35: They struck down Og, his sons and all his people, leaving him no survivor. Then they took possession of his land.
</p>

## Notes

v2–3: Devoting the towns to destruction is the content of Israel’s vow. Verse 3 includes both the Canaanites and their towns. Hormah echoes this language of devotion or destruction.

v6–9: “Fiery” may describe the effect of the snakes’ bites; it does not establish a species. The Hebrew words for snake and bronze sound alike. The narrative attributes the instruction to the LORD and explicitly connects looking at the bronze snake with living.

v14–15: This quotation from the Book of the Wars of the LORD is a surviving fragment with uncertain place names. Waheb and Suphah are retained. The draft does not invent the missing setting or an opening verb.

v17–20: The well song ends within verse 18. The itinerary resumes in that same verse and continues through verse 20. “A ruler’s staff” in the song can also be understood as the ruler or lawgiver himself.

v24: The pinned Hebrew says the Ammonite boundary was strong. An alternative textual reading names Jazer; it is not substituted into the draft.

v27–30: The poem recalls Sihon’s victory over Moab and ends with a new “we.” Its final lines are difficult. Verse 30 is read here as “we shot them down”; other readings concern offspring or a light. The marked Hebrew word rendered “which” also has a textual alternative, “fire.” These alternatives change how the last line is connected.

v32: After the plural verb for capturing the villages, the Hebrew returns to a singular verb. The draft identifies Moses as that subject. Both the written and read forms are singular; the read form makes the action causative: driving out or dispossessing.

## Vocabulary

v1: Atharim
The route name is retained. An older interpretation connects it with the scouts, but that is not certain.

v4: Sea of Reeds
Hebrew Yam Suph, traditionally called the Red Sea. The route here goes around Edom.

v12–15: Valley
The Hebrew naḥal can mean a valley, wadi or its stream; it need not imply water flowing all year.

v16–18: Beer
The name means well. The repeated well language connects the place, the promise and the song.

v29: Chemosh
The god named in the poem’s address to Moab. The poem depicts his sons as fugitives and his daughters as captives.
