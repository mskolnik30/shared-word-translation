## The red cow and the purification water

<p>
v01: The LORD gave Moses and Aaron these instructions:
v02: “This is the requirement of the instruction the LORD has commanded: Tell the Israelites to bring you a red cow, sound and without defect, that has never carried a yoke.
</p>

<p>
v03: Give her to Eleazar the priest. She is to be taken outside the camp and slaughtered in his presence.
v04: Eleazar the priest must take some of her blood on his finger and sprinkle it seven times toward the front of the tent of meeting.
v05: The cow must be burned before his eyes—her hide, flesh, blood and dung together.
v06: The priest must take cedarwood, hyssop and scarlet yarn and throw them into the fire burning the cow.
</p>

<p>
v07: Then the priest must wash his clothes and bathe his body in water. Afterward he may enter the camp, but he remains unclean until evening.
v08: The person who burns her must also wash their clothes in water and bathe their body in water, and will remain unclean until evening.
</p>

<p>
v09: A clean man must gather the cow’s ashes and place them outside the camp in a clean place. They are to be kept for the Israelite community to make water for removing impurity. It is a purification offering.
v10: The person who gathers the cow’s ashes must wash his clothes and remain unclean until evening. This is a lasting rule for the Israelites and the foreigner living among them.
</p>

## Contact with the dead

<p>
v11: Anyone who touches a human corpse will be unclean for seven days.
v12: They must purify themselves with this water on the third day, and on the seventh day they will be clean. If they do not purify themselves on the third day, they will not be clean on the seventh day.
v13: Anyone who touches a dead human body and does not purify themselves defiles the LORD’s tabernacle. That person must be cut off from Israel. Because the water for removing impurity was not sprinkled on them, they remain unclean; their impurity is still on them.
</p>

<p>
v14: This is the instruction when someone dies in a tent: everyone who enters the tent and everyone already in it will be unclean for seven days.
v15: Every open container without a cover fastened over it will be unclean.
v16: Anyone in the open country who touches someone killed by a sword, another dead body, a human bone or a grave will be unclean for seven days.
</p>

<p>
v17: For an unclean person, take some ashes from the burned purification offering and add fresh water to them in a container.
v18: A clean man must take hyssop, dip it in the water, and sprinkle the tent, all its containers and the people who were there. He must also sprinkle anyone who touched a human bone, someone killed, another dead body or a grave.
v19: The clean person must sprinkle the unclean person on the third and seventh days, purifying them on the seventh day. The person being purified must wash their clothes and bathe in water; at evening they will be clean.
</p>

<p>
v20: Anyone who becomes unclean and does not purify themselves must be cut off from the assembly for defiling the LORD’s sanctuary. The water for removing impurity has not been sprinkled on them; they are unclean.
</p>

<p>
v21: This is a lasting rule for them. The person who sprinkles the water for removing impurity must wash their clothes, and anyone who touches that water will be unclean until evening.
v22: Whatever the unclean person touches becomes unclean, and anyone who touches it will be unclean until evening.”
</p>

## Notes

v2–10: The animal is a red cow that has never borne a yoke; the text does not separately state whether she has calved. The priest oversees the slaughter and burning. Those preparing the ashes become unclean, even though the ashes provide purification.

v9, 17: The Hebrew term ḥaṭṭāʾt commonly names the sin offering. Here “purification offering” highlights its use for corpse impurity, which is not itself described as a personal wrongdoing.

v12, 19: Verse 12’s accents support the sequence used here: purification on the third day, cleanness on the seventh. Its clauses can also be joined to require purification on both days. Verse 19 explicitly prescribes sprinkling on the third and seventh days, followed by washing and cleanness at evening.

v13, 20: “Cut off” names a severe removal from the people without specifying the means here. The offense is remaining unpurified and thereby defiling the sanctuary.

v21: The person sprinkling the water must wash clothes. The separate clause about touching the water explicitly adds uncleanness until evening; the draft preserves that distinction.

## Vocabulary

v6: Hyssop
A plant used in sprinkling; the ancient plant’s exact botanical identification is uncertain.

v9: Water for removing impurity
Water prepared with the ashes and used for cleansing after contact with death.

v17: Living water
The Hebrew expression here describes fresh water, associated with a flowing source, rather than stagnant water.

v22: Unclean
A ritual condition affecting contact and access. Its use here does not mean that every affected person has committed a moral offense.
