## The Nazirite Vow

<p>
v01: The LORD said to Moses,
v02: “Tell the Israelites: If a man or woman makes a special vow, a Nazirite vow, to set themselves apart for the LORD,
v03: that person must abstain from wine and other strong drink. They must not drink vinegar made from either, or any drink made by soaking grapes, and they must not eat fresh or dried grapes.
v04: Throughout their time of separation, they must eat nothing that comes from the grapevine, from the seeds to the skins.
</p>

<p>
v05: Throughout the vow of separation, no razor is to pass over their head. Until the time they have set apart for the LORD is complete, they are to be holy and let the hair of their head grow freely.
</p>

<p>
v06: Throughout their separation to the LORD, they must not go near a dead person.
v07: They must not make themselves unclean when their father, mother, brother or sister dies, because their consecration to God is on their head.
v08: Throughout their separation, they are holy to the LORD.
</p>

## When a Sudden Death Interrupts the Vow

<p>
v09: If someone suddenly dies beside them, unexpectedly, and defiles their consecrated head, they are to shave their head on the day of their cleansing. They must shave it on the seventh day.
</p>

<p>
v10: On the eighth day they are to bring two turtledoves or two young pigeons to the priest at the entrance of the tent of meeting.
v11: The priest is to offer one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering, making atonement for the person’s sin in connection with the dead person. That same day their head is to be made holy again.
v12: They must set apart their time of separation for the LORD again and bring a year-old male lamb as a guilt offering. The earlier days will not count, because their separation was defiled.
</p>

## Completing the Vow

<p>
v13: This is the instruction for the Nazirite when the time of separation is complete: the person is to be brought to the entrance of the tent of meeting.
v14: They are to bring their offering to the LORD: one year-old male lamb without defect as a burnt offering, one year-old female lamb without defect as a sin offering, and one ram without defect as a peace offering,
v15: with a basket of unleavened bread made from fine flour—loaves mixed with oil and unleavened wafers spread with oil—along with the grain offerings and drink offerings.
</p>

<p>
v16: The priest is to present these before the LORD and offer the person’s sin offering and burnt offering.
v17: He is to offer the ram to the LORD as a peace-offering sacrifice, together with the basket of unleavened bread. The priest is also to present its grain offering and drink offering.
</p>

<p>
v18: At the entrance of the tent of meeting, the Nazirite is to shave their consecrated head. They are to take the hair from that head and put it on the fire beneath the peace-offering sacrifice.
v19: After the Nazirite has shaved their consecrated head, the priest is to take the ram’s boiled shoulder, one unleavened loaf from the basket and one unleavened wafer, and place them in the Nazirite’s hands.
v20: The priest is to wave them as a wave offering before the LORD. They are holy and belong to the priest, in addition to the breast of the wave offering and the contributed thigh. After this, the Nazirite may drink wine.
</p>

<p>
v21: This is the instruction for the Nazirite who vows an offering to the LORD for their separation, besides anything else they can afford. They must do what they vowed, in keeping with the instruction for their separation.”
</p>

## The Priestly Blessing

<p>
v22: The LORD said to Moses,
v23: “Tell Aaron and his sons: This is how you are to bless the Israelites. Say to them:
v24: ‘May the LORD bless you and keep you;
v25: may the LORD make his face shine on you
and be gracious to you;
v26: may the LORD lift his face toward you
and give you peace.’
</p>

<p>
v27: So they will place my name on the Israelites, and I myself will bless them.”
</p>

## Notes

v02: The vow is explicitly open to a man or a woman. “They” in the following instructions refers to the individual taking it. Nazirite means someone set apart; it is not the same word as Nazarene.

v03–04: The grape restriction extends beyond wine to vinegar, a grape infusion or juice, and fresh and dried grapes. “Seeds to skins” expresses the whole grape; the precise senses of these rare terms are uncertain.

v07: The consecration “on their head” refers to the uncut hair as its visible sign. The noun nezer can also mean a crown or sacred diadem.

v09–12: The death is sudden and unplanned. The text nevertheless uses sin, atonement and guilt-offering language for the resulting violation of the vow’s conditions. The earlier days no longer count.

v13: The Hebrew says someone is to “bring” the Nazirite but does not name that person. The passive wording leaves the agent unspecified.

v24–26: The blessing addresses Israel with singular “you.” It repeats the LORD’s name three times and preserves the images of a shining and lifted face. The language expresses gracious attention, ending with peace.

v27: The priests place God’s name on Israel; God is the emphatic “I” who promises to bless.

## Vocabulary

v02: נָזִיר (nazir)
Nazirite: a person set apart by vow to the LORD.

v11: חַטָּאת (ḥattat)
Sin offering; also called a purification offering. Here it addresses the defilement caused by the unexpected death.

v20: תְּנוּפָה (tenufah)
Wave offering: a presentation before the LORD. The term connects this rite with the offerings in Leviticus.

v26: שָׁלוֹם (shalom)
Peace, including well-being and wholeness; more than the absence of conflict.
