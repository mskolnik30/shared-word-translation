## Offerings in the land

<p>
v01: The LORD gave Moses these instructions:
v02: “Tell the Israelites: When you enter the land I am giving you to live in,
v03: and you prepare an offering by fire for the LORD from the herd or flock—a burnt offering or a sacrifice to fulfill a vow, as a voluntary gift, or at your appointed festivals—to make a pleasing aroma for the LORD,
v04: the person bringing the offering must also present to the LORD a grain offering of one-tenth of an ephah of fine flour mixed with one-quarter of a hin of oil.
</p>

<p>
v05: For each lamb, prepare one-quarter of a hin of wine as a drink offering with the burnt offering or sacrifice.
</p>

<p>
v06: For a ram, prepare a grain offering of two-tenths of an ephah of fine flour mixed with one-third of a hin of oil.
v07: Also present one-third of a hin of wine as a drink offering, a pleasing aroma for the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v08: When you prepare a bull as a burnt offering or a sacrifice to fulfill a vow or as a peace offering to the LORD,
v09: bring with the bull a grain offering of three-tenths of an ephah of fine flour mixed with half a hin of oil.
v10: Bring half a hin of wine as a drink offering, an offering by fire with a pleasing aroma for the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v11: Do this for each bull, each ram, and each lamb or young goat.
v12: However many animals you prepare, do this for each one.
</p>

<p>
v13: Every native-born Israelite must follow these instructions when presenting an offering by fire with a pleasing aroma for the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v14: If a foreigner lives among you, now or in generations to come, and prepares an offering by fire with a pleasing aroma for the LORD, they must do as you do.
</p>

<p>
v15: The community must have one rule for you and for the foreigner living among you, a lasting rule throughout your generations. You and the foreigner stand alike before the LORD.
v16: You and the foreigner living among you must have the same instruction and the same ruling.”
</p>

## The first dough

<p>
v17: The LORD also instructed Moses:
v18: “Tell the Israelites: When you come into the land where I am bringing you,
v19: and you eat the bread of that land, set aside a contribution for the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v20: Set aside a cake from the first of your dough as a contribution, just as you set aside a contribution from the threshing floor.
v21: Throughout your generations, give the LORD a contribution from the first of your dough.
</p>

## Unintentional sin and defiance

<p>
v22: If you go astray unintentionally and fail to carry out all these commands that the LORD has spoken to Moses—
v23: everything the LORD has commanded you through Moses, from the day the LORD gave the command onward through your generations—
v24: and this happens unintentionally, without the community’s knowledge, the whole community must prepare one young bull as a burnt offering, a pleasing aroma for the LORD, with its prescribed grain and drink offerings, and one male goat as a sin offering.
</p>

<p>
v25: The priest will make atonement for the whole Israelite community, and they will be forgiven. It was unintentional, and they have brought their offering, an offering by fire for the LORD, and their sin offering before the LORD for their error.
v26: The whole Israelite community and the foreigner living among them will be forgiven, because all the people were involved in the unintentional error.
</p>

<p>
v27: If one person sins unintentionally, they must bring a year-old female goat as a sin offering.
v28: The priest will make atonement before the LORD for the person who sinned unintentionally. When atonement is made for them, they will be forgiven.
</p>

<p>
v29: The same instruction applies to anyone who acts unintentionally, whether a native-born Israelite or a foreigner living among you.
</p>

<p>
v30: But anyone who acts with a raised hand, whether native-born or a foreigner, insults the LORD. That person must be cut off from their people.
v31: They have despised the LORD’s word and broken his command. That person must certainly be cut off; their guilt remains on them.”
</p>

## A man gathers wood on the Sabbath

<p>
v32: While the Israelites were in the wilderness, they found a man gathering wood on the Sabbath.
v33: Those who found him gathering wood brought him to Moses, Aaron and the whole community.
</p>

<p>
v34: They kept him in custody because it had not been made clear what should be done to him.
</p>

<p>
v35: The LORD said to Moses, “The man must be put to death. The whole community must stone him outside the camp.”
</p>

<p>
v36: The whole community took him outside the camp and stoned him to death, as the LORD had commanded Moses.
</p>

## Tassels as reminders

<p>
v37: The LORD said to Moses:
v38: “Tell the Israelites to make tassels on the corners of their garments throughout their generations, and to put a blue cord on each corner’s tassel.
v39: These will be your tassels: when you look at them, remember all the LORD’s commands and carry them out. Do not go scouting after your own heart and eyes, prostituting yourselves by following them.
v40: Then you will remember and carry out all my commands and be holy to your God.
v41: I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of Egypt to be your God. I am the LORD your God.”
</p>

## Notes

v4–10: The flour measures rise with the animal: one-tenth of an ephah for a lamb, two-tenths for a ram, three-tenths for a bull. Oil and wine are each one-quarter, one-third and one-half of a hin respectively. The ephah is understood in the Hebrew’s “tenths.”

v14–16: The resident foreigner and native-born Israelite share the same instructions for these offerings and the same standing before the LORD in this setting.

v20–21: The rare word rendered “dough” has also been understood as coarse meal. The first portion is set aside; no percentage is specified here.

v22–31: The text contrasts unintentional failure with defiance. It does not label every intentional act with this same sanction. “Cut off” is a severe removal from the people; the phrase alone does not specify how it is carried out.

v34–36: The earlier uncertainty about what to do is left as stated. The account then explicitly attributes the death sentence to the LORD and its execution to the community.

v39: The verb translated “go scouting” is also used of exploring the land in chapters 13–14. The warning links looking, remembering and acting. Its prostitution language pictures unfaithfulness.

## Vocabulary

v3: ʾiššeh — offering by fire
Also understood as a food offering. The established label is retained across the sacrificial instructions.

v4: Ephah and hin
An ephah measures dry goods; a hin measures liquids. The ratios are kept in ancient units rather than converted to falsely exact modern amounts.

v8: Peace offering
Also called a well-being offering; see the sacrificial instructions in Leviticus 3 and 7.

v24: Sin offering
Also called a purification offering. It is distinct from the accompanying burnt offering.

v30: Raised hand
An image of open defiance here, unlike the oath gesture in 14:30.

v38: ṣîṣit — tassel
Fringe or tassel at a garment’s corner, with a blue cord. The passage does not prescribe later knot patterns.
