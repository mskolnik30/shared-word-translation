## Men sent to scout Canaan

<p>
v01: The LORD gave Moses this instruction:
v02: “Send men to scout the land of Canaan, which I am giving the Israelites. Send one man from each ancestral tribe, each a leader among his people.”
v03: At the LORD’s command, Moses sent them out from the wilderness of Paran. Every man was a leader of the Israelites.
</p>

<p>
v04: These were their names. Reuben’s tribe sent Shammua son of Zaccur;
v05: Simeon’s tribe, Shaphat son of Hori;
v06: Judah’s tribe, Caleb son of Jephunneh;
v07: Issachar’s tribe, Igal son of Joseph;
v08: Ephraim’s tribe, Hoshea son of Nun;
v09: Benjamin’s tribe, Palti son of Raphu;
v10: Zebulun’s tribe, Gaddiel son of Sodi;
v11: Joseph’s tribe—that is, Manasseh’s tribe—Gaddi son of Susi;
v12: Dan’s tribe, Ammiel son of Gemalli;
v13: Asher’s tribe, Sethur son of Michael;
v14: Naphtali’s tribe, Nahbi son of Vophsi;
v15: and Gad’s tribe, Geuel son of Machi.
</p>

<p>
v16: Those were the men Moses sent to scout the land. Moses gave Hoshea son of Nun the name Joshua.
</p>

## The land and its people

<p>
v17: When Moses sent them to scout Canaan, he told them, “Go up this way through the Negev, then into the hill country.
v18: Find out what the land is like. Are its people strong or weak? Are there few of them or many?
v19: Is the land they live in good or bad? What are their towns like—open camps or fortified places?
v20: Is the soil rich or poor? Are there trees or not? Be courageous, and bring back some of the land’s fruit.” It was the season of the first ripe grapes.
</p>

<p>
v21: They went up and scouted the land from the wilderness of Zin to Rehob, toward Lebo-hamath.
v22: They went up through the Negev and reached Hebron. Ahiman, Sheshai and Talmai, descendants of Anak, were there. Hebron had been built seven years before Zoan in Egypt.
</p>

<p>
v23: At the Valley of Eshcol, they cut a branch with a single cluster of grapes. Two men carried it on a pole. They also took pomegranates and figs.
v24: That place was named the Valley of Eshcol because of the cluster the Israelites cut there.
</p>

<p>
v25: After forty days of scouting the land, they returned.
</p>

## The scouts report

<p>
v26: They came to Moses, Aaron and the whole Israelite community at Kadesh in the wilderness of Paran. They gave their report to them and the whole community, and showed them the land’s fruit.
v27: They told Moses, “We went into the land you sent us to. It really does flow with milk and honey! Here is its fruit.
v28: But the people living there are powerful, and their cities are fortified and very large. We even saw descendants of Anak there.
v29: Amalekites live in the Negev. Hittites, Jebusites and Amorites live in the hill country. Canaanites live beside the sea and along the Jordan.”
</p>

<p>
v30: Caleb quieted the people before Moses. “We must go up and take possession of it!” he said. “We certainly can!”
</p>

<p>
v31: But the men who had gone with him said, “We cannot go up against these people. They are stronger than we are.”
v32: They spread a bad report among the Israelites about the land they had scouted: “The land we went through to scout devours its inhabitants. Every person we saw there was huge.
v33: We saw the Nephilim there—the descendants of Anak come from the Nephilim. In our own eyes we were like grasshoppers, and that is how we looked to them too.”
</p>

## Notes

v16: Hoshea and Joshua both contain language of saving; Joshua names the LORD in that connection. The notice does not say this was the first time the name was used; see Exodus 17:9.

v21: Lebo-hamath may be read as a place name or as “the entrance to Hamath.” The wording describes the northern reach of the expedition.

v22: The Hebrew shifts from “they went up” to “he came.” The draft reads the expedition collectively. The singular could focus on an individual, but the verse does not name that person. Some ancient witnesses use the plural.

v32–33: The devouring land, enormous inhabitants and grasshopper comparison belong to the scouts’ report. The translation preserves their claims without turning them into independent measurements or explaining how they knew what the inhabitants thought.

## Vocabulary

v2: tûr — scout
To explore or inspect. The same verb appears in the warning about following the heart and eyes in 15:39.

v17: Negev
The dry southern region of the land; the word can also mean south.

v23–24: ʾeškōl — cluster
The valley’s name, Eshcol, repeats the Hebrew word for a cluster of grapes. The valley or wadi need not have a permanent stream.

v33: Nephilim
Also named in Genesis 6:4. Here the scouts connect Anak’s descendants with them. The word is retained rather than deciding their identity or origin.
