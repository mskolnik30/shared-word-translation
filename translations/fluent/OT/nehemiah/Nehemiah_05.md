## Debt and the enslavement of families

<p>
v01: A great outcry arose from the people and their wives against their fellow Jews.
v02: Some said, “There are many of us, with our sons and daughters. We need grain so we can eat and stay alive.”
v03: Others said, “We are mortgaging our fields, vineyards, and houses to get grain during the famine.”
v04: Still others said, “We have borrowed silver against our fields and vineyards to pay the king’s tax.
v05: Our flesh is like our brothers’ flesh, and our children are like their children. Yet we are forcing our sons and daughters into slavery. Some of our daughters are already enslaved, and we are powerless: our fields and vineyards belong to others.”
</p>

<p>
v06: I was very angry when I heard their outcry and these reports.
v07: After thinking it through, I confronted the nobles and officials. “You are charging interest to your own brothers!” I told them. Then I called a large assembly against them.
v08: I said, “As far as we could, we have bought back our fellow Jews who were sold to the nations. Now you are selling your own brothers, so they must be sold back to us!” They fell silent and could find nothing to say.
</p>

<p>
v09: I went on, “What you are doing is not good. Should you not walk in the fear of our God, to avoid the insults of the nations who are our enemies?
v10: I, my brothers, and my men are also lending them silver and grain. Let us stop charging this interest.
v11: Give back their fields, vineyards, olive groves, and houses today, along with the hundredth part of the silver, grain, new wine, and oil that you are charging them.”
</p>

<p>
v12: They answered, “We will give it back and demand nothing from them. We will do as you say.” I summoned the priests and made the creditors swear to carry out this promise.
v13: I also shook out the fold of my garment and said, “May God shake out of his house and his possessions everyone who fails to keep this promise. May he be shaken out and left empty like this!” The whole assembly said, “Amen,” and praised the LORD. The people kept their promise.
</p>

## Nehemiah’s conduct as governor

<p>
v14: From the day I was appointed governor in Judah—from the twentieth to the thirty-second year of King Artaxerxes, twelve years—neither I nor my brothers ate the governor’s food allowance.
v15: The earlier governors had burdened the people, taking food and wine from them, besides forty shekels of silver. Even their servants lorded it over the people. I did not act that way, because I feared God.
v16: I also devoted myself to work on this wall. We acquired no fields, and all my men were gathered there for the work.
</p>

<p>
v17: At my table were 150 Jews and officials, besides those who came to us from the surrounding nations.
v18: Each day one ox, six choice sheep, and birds were prepared for me. Every ten days there was an abundant supply of all kinds of wine. Even so, I did not demand the governor’s food allowance, because the people’s burden of service was heavy.
v19: Remember for my good, my God, all that I have done for this people.
</p>

## Notes

v05: The families describe their sons and daughters being enslaved for debt, with some daughters already enslaved. Their complaint is not softened to ordinary employment.

v07–11: The vocabulary can refer to lending, debt claims, or interest. The “hundredth” in verse 11 is one percent of the listed goods, but the text gives no time interval for the charge.

v15: The verse states forty shekels of silver in addition to food and wine; it does not specify a daily interval for this amount.

## Vocabulary

v11: Shekel
A unit of weight used for silver payments.

v14: The governor’s food allowance
Provision claimed by a governor from the governed population. Nehemiah says he declined it.
