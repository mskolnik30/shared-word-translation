## Mockery and a prayer

<p>
v01: When Sanballat heard that we were rebuilding the wall, he became furious. He mocked the Jews
v02: before his associates and the army of Samaria: “What are these feeble Jews doing? Will they restore it for themselves? Will they sacrifice? Will they finish in a day? Can they bring stones back to life from these heaps of rubble—burned stones?”
v03: Tobiah the Ammonite, standing beside him, added, “Even a fox climbing on what they are building would break down their stone wall!”
</p>

<p>
v04: Hear us, our God, for we are despised! Turn their insults back on their own heads. Hand them over as plunder in a land of captivity.
v05: Do not cover their wrongdoing or wipe their sin away from your sight, for they have provoked you in front of the builders.
v06: So we rebuilt the wall, joining it all together up to half its height. The people had their hearts in the work.
</p>

## Threats against the work

<p>
v07: When Sanballat, Tobiah, the Arabs, the Ammonites, and the Ashdodites heard that Jerusalem’s walls were being repaired and the gaps were beginning to close, they became very angry.
v08: They all plotted together to come and fight against Jerusalem and throw it into confusion.
v09: We prayed to our God and posted a guard against them day and night.
</p>

<p>
v10: Then the people of Judah said,
“The carriers’ strength is failing,
and there is so much rubble.
We cannot build the wall.”
v11: Our enemies said, “They will not know or see us until we are among them. Then we will kill them and stop the work.”
</p>

<p>
v12: The Jews who lived near them came and told us ten times, “You must return to us from every place.”
v13: So I stationed people by families in the low, exposed places behind the wall, with their swords, spears, and bows.
v14: After looking things over, I stood up and told the nobles, officials, and the rest of the people, “Do not be afraid of them. Remember the great and awe-inspiring Lord, and fight for your brothers, sons, daughters, wives, and homes.”
</p>

## Building with weapons ready

<p>
v15: When our enemies heard that their plan was known to us and that God had frustrated it, we all returned to the wall, each to his work.
v16: From that day on, half my men worked while the other half held spears, shields, bows, and armor. The leaders stood behind all the people of Judah
v17: who were building the wall. Those carrying loads worked with one hand and held a weapon with the other.
v18: Each builder worked with his sword strapped at his side. The man who sounded the ram’s horn stayed beside me.
</p>

<p>
v19: I told the nobles, officials, and the rest of the people, “The work is spread over a wide area, and we are far apart along the wall.
v20: Wherever you hear the ram’s horn, gather to us there. Our God will fight for us.”
</p>

<p>
v21: We kept working, with half the men holding spears, from daybreak until the stars appeared.
v22: At that time I also told the people, “Each man and his servant must spend the night inside Jerusalem, so they can guard us by night and work by day.”
v23: Neither I, my brothers, my men, nor the guards who followed me took off our clothes. Each kept his weapon even when going for water.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Public 4:1–6 corresponds to Hebrew 3:33–38; public 4:7–23 corresponds to Hebrew 4:1–17. The source records are preserved without omission.

v02: The question about restoring the wall is difficult. Proposals include leaving the builders to themselves or allowing them to restore it.

v04–05: The prayer asks for retaliation and for the opponents’ guilt not to be erased. Its force is retained as part of the narrated conflict.

v12: The warning’s Hebrew wording is difficult. It can be understood as a repeated appeal for the workers to return home, or as a warning that enemies would attack from every direction.

v23: The final words are exceptionally compressed: literally, “each his weapon, the water.” Keeping weapons while going for water is one possible reading; the exact action is uncertain.

## Vocabulary

v10: Burden bearers
Workers who carried building materials and cleared debris.

v20: Our God will fight for us
Nehemiah’s assurance to the workers; it is part of his speech in the threatened city.
