## Confession and worship

<p>
v01: On the twenty-fourth day of that month, the Israelites gathered, fasting, wearing sackcloth, and with earth on their heads.
v02: The descendants of Israel separated themselves from all foreigners. They stood and confessed their sins and their ancestors’ wrongs.
v03: They stood in their places and read from the Book of the Instruction of the LORD their God for a quarter of the day. For another quarter they confessed and bowed in worship to the LORD their God.
</p>

<p>
v04: On the Levites’ platform stood Jeshua, Bani, Kadmiel, Shebaniah, Bunni, Sherebiah, another Bani, and Chenani. They cried out loudly to the LORD their God.
</p>

## A prayer recalling God’s faithfulness

<p>
v05: The Levites Jeshua, Kadmiel, Bani, Hashabneiah, Sherebiah, Hodiah, Shebaniah, and Pethahiah said, “Stand up and bless the LORD your God from everlasting to everlasting!”
“May your glorious name be blessed,
exalted above all blessing and praise.
</p>

<p>
v06: You alone are the LORD.
You made heaven, the highest heaven and all its host,
the earth and everything on it,
the seas and everything in them.
You give life to them all,
and the host of heaven bows before you.
v07: You are the LORD God who chose Abram,
brought him out of Ur of the Chaldeans,
and gave him the name Abraham.
v08: You found his heart faithful before you
and made a covenant with him
to give his descendants the land of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Jebusites, and Girgashites.
You kept your word, for you are righteous.
</p>

<p>
v09: You saw our ancestors’ suffering in Egypt
and heard their cry at the Sea of Reeds.
v10: You performed signs and wonders against Pharaoh,
all his servants, and all the people of his land,
for you knew how arrogantly they treated our ancestors.
You made a name for yourself that remains to this day.
v11: You split the sea before them,
and they passed through it on dry ground.
You hurled their pursuers into the depths,
like a stone into mighty waters.
v12: By a pillar of cloud you led them by day,
and by a pillar of fire at night,
to light the way they should go.
v13: You came down on Mount Sinai
and spoke with them from heaven.
You gave them just judgments, trustworthy instructions,
and good statutes and commands.
v14: You made your holy Sabbath known to them
and gave them commands, statutes, and instruction
through your servant Moses.
v15: You gave them bread from heaven for their hunger
and brought water from a rock for their thirst.
You told them to enter and possess the land
you had raised your hand in oath to give them.
</p>

<p>
v16: But they, our ancestors, acted arrogantly.
They stiffened their necks
and did not listen to your commands.
v17: They refused to listen
and did not remember the wonders you had done among them.
They stiffened their necks and appointed a leader
to return to slavery in their rebellion.
But you are a God of forgiveness,
gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and rich in faithful love.
You did not abandon them.
v18: Even when they made themselves a cast-metal calf
and said, ‘This is your God who brought you up from Egypt,’
and committed great blasphemies,
v19: you, in your great compassion, did not abandon them in the wilderness.
The pillar of cloud did not leave them by day, to guide them on the way,
nor the pillar of fire by night, to light the way they should go.
v20: You gave your good Spirit to instruct them.
You did not withhold your manna from their mouths,
and you gave them water for their thirst.
v21: For forty years you sustained them in the wilderness.
They lacked nothing; their clothes did not wear out,
and their feet did not swell.
</p>

<p>
v22: You gave them kingdoms and peoples,
allotting them territory.
They took possession of Sihon’s land, the land of the king of Heshbon,
and the land of Og king of Bashan.
v23: You made their children as numerous as the stars of heaven
and brought them into the land
you had told their ancestors to enter and possess.
v24: Their children entered and possessed the land.
You subdued its inhabitants, the Canaanites, before them
and handed over their kings and the peoples of the land
for them to do with as they pleased.
v25: They captured fortified cities and fertile land,
houses full of good things, cisterns already cut,
vineyards, olive groves, and fruit trees in abundance.
They ate, were satisfied, and grew fat,
delighting in your great goodness.
</p>

<p>
v26: But they defied you and rebelled against you.
They threw your instruction behind their backs
and killed your prophets, who warned them to return to you.
They committed great blasphemies.
v27: So you handed them over to their enemies, who oppressed them.
In their distress they cried to you,
and you heard from heaven.
In your great compassion you gave them deliverers
who saved them from their enemies’ hands.
v28: But as soon as they had rest, they did evil before you again.
You abandoned them into their enemies’ hands, and their enemies ruled over them.
Again they cried to you, and you heard from heaven.
Time after time you rescued them in your compassion.
v29: You warned them to return to your instruction,
but they acted arrogantly and did not listen to your commands.
They sinned against your judgments, which give life to the person who does them.
They turned a stubborn shoulder, stiffened their necks,
and would not listen.
v30: For many years you were patient with them.
By your Spirit you warned them through your prophets,
but they would not listen.
So you handed them over to the peoples of the lands.
v31: Yet in your great compassion you did not finish them off or abandon them,
for you are a gracious and compassionate God.
</p>

<p>
v32: Now, our God, great, mighty, and awe-inspiring God,
you keep covenant and faithful love.
Do not regard as small all the hardship that has come upon us—
our kings, officials, priests, prophets, ancestors, and all your people—
from the days of Assyria’s kings until today.
v33: You have been righteous in all that has come upon us.
You have acted faithfully; we have done wrong.
v34: Our kings, officials, priests, and ancestors did not carry out your instruction.
They did not attend to your commands
or the warnings you gave them.
v35: Even in their own kingdom,
amid the great goodness you gave them
and the broad, fertile land you set before them,
they did not serve you or turn from their evil deeds.
</p>

<p>
v36: Look at us today: we are slaves!
In the land you gave our ancestors
to eat its fruit and its good things—
here we are, slaves in it.
v37: Its rich produce goes to the kings you have set over us because of our sins.
They rule over our bodies and our livestock as they please.
We are in great distress.”
</p>

## A written commitment

<p>
v38: Because of all this, we are making a firm agreement and putting it in writing. Our officials, Levites, and priests place their seals on it.
</p>

## Notes

v04: Bani occurs twice in the list as written. The names have not been silently consolidated.

v09: “Sea of Reeds” renders the Hebrew name traditionally called the Red Sea. The name alone does not settle the crossing’s location.

v17: “In their rebellion” follows the pinned Hebrew. Some readings instead have “in Egypt”; that change is not adopted here.

v18: The prayer’s quotation has the singular “This is your God,” although Exodus 32:4 uses a plural formulation. The wording here is preserved.

v22: The wording about the allotted territories is compressed and uncertain. The two named Amorite kingdoms remain distinct.

v38: This verse is Hebrew 10:1. The word for the written commitment is not the usual Hebrew word for covenant, though the undertaking has a solemn communal character.

## Vocabulary

v17: Faithful love
Enduring, committed kindness. The prayer repeatedly contrasts God’s mercy with the people’s rebellion.

v36–37: Slaves
The speakers describe foreign rule over their land, produce, bodies, and livestock as servitude.
