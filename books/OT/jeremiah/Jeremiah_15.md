# Jeremiah 15

v01:
<p>
The LORD said to me: “Even if Moses and Samuel stood before me, my whole being would not turn toward this people. Send them away from before me; let them go out.
</p>

v02:
<p>
When they ask you, “Where are we to go?”  
you are to say to them, “Thus says the LORD:

Those destined for death, to death;  
those destined for the sword, to the sword;  
those destined for famine, to famine;  
those destined for captivity, to captivity.”
</p>

v03:
<p>
“I will appoint over them four kinds,” declares the LORD: “the sword to kill, the dogs to drag away, and the birds of the heavens and the beasts of the earth to devour and to destroy.
</p>

v04:
<p>
I will make them an object of horror to all the kingdoms of the earth because of Manasseh son of Hezekiah, king of Judah, for what he did in Jerusalem.
</p>

v05:
<p>
For who will have compassion on you, Jerusalem? Who will mourn for you? Who will turn aside to ask after your well-being?
</p>

v06:
<p>
“You have forsaken me,” declares the LORD. “You keep going backward. So I have stretched out my hand against you and destroyed you; I am weary of relenting.
</p>

v07:
<p>
I have winnowed them with a winnowing fork at the gates of the land. I have bereaved; I have destroyed my people. They did not turn from their ways.
</p>

v08:
<p>
Their widows have become more numerous before me than the sand of the seas. Against the mother of a young man I have brought a destroyer at noon; suddenly I have made anguish and terrors fall upon her.
</p>

v09:
<p>
She who bore seven has languished; she has breathed out her life. Her sun has gone down while it was still day; she is ashamed and humiliated. And their remnant I will give to the sword before their enemies,” declares the LORD.
</p>

v10:
<p>
Woe is me, my mother, that you bore me, a man of strife and a man of contention to all the land! I have neither lent, nor have they lent to me; yet every one of them curses me.
</p>

v11:
<p>
The LORD said: “Surely I have set you free for good; surely I have made the enemy entreat you in a time of evil and in a time of distress.
</p>

v12:
<p>
Can iron break iron from the north, and bronze?
</p>

v13:
<p>
Your wealth and your treasuries I will give as plunder, without price, because of all your sins throughout all your borders.
</p>

v14:
<p>
I will make you pass over to your enemies into a land you do not know, for a fire has been kindled in my anger; it will burn against you.
</p>

v15:
<p>
You know, LORD; remember me and attend to me, and avenge me against my persecutors. Do not take me away because of your patience. Know that for your sake I bear reproach.
</p>

v16:
<p>
Your words were found, and I ate them; your word became for me joy and the delight of my heart, for your name has been called upon me, LORD, God of armies.
</p>

v17:
<p>
I did not sit in the company of revelers and rejoice. Because of your hand I sat alone, for you filled me with indignation.
</p>

v18:
<p>
Why has my pain become unending, and my wound grievous, refusing to be healed? Will you indeed be to me like a deceptive brook, like waters that are not reliable?
</p>

v19:
<p>
Therefore thus says the LORD: “If you return, I will restore you, and you will stand before me. If you bring out what is precious from what is worthless, you will be as my mouth. Let them turn to you, but you must not turn to them.
</p>

v20:
<p>
I will make you to this people a fortified wall of bronze. They will fight against you, but they will not overcome you, for I am with you to save you and to deliver you,” declares the LORD.
</p>

v21:
<p>
“I will deliver you from the hand of the wicked, and redeem you from the grasp of the ruthless.”
</p>

## Notes

- **15:1** Moses and Samuel are remembered as powerful intercessors for Israel. Their imagined presence intensifies the declaration that judgment upon this people will not be turned aside.

- **15:2** The repeated pattern—“to death … to the sword … to famine … to captivity”—has the force of an irrevocable assignment. The verse does not distinguish these outcomes as different degrees of guilt.

- **15:6** “Relenting” translates language that can describe being moved from a course of judgment. The LORD’s weariness here underscores the long history of refusal described in the surrounding verses.

- **15:11** The Hebrew of the first clause is difficult, and ancient witnesses and translations reflect differing understandings. The rendering “set you free” understands the line as the LORD’s assurance that Jeremiah has been preserved for his good.

- **15:16** To “eat” God’s words portrays their complete reception and inward appropriation. Yet the joy of receiving the word stands alongside the prophet’s isolation and pain in the verses that follow.

- **15:18** A “deceptive brook” is a seasonal stream that promises water but fails when it is needed. Jeremiah’s question gives direct voice to his experience of disappointment without resolving the tension before the LORD answers.

- **15:19** “What is precious from what is worthless” calls Jeremiah to speech disciplined by the LORD’s values. He is not to be drawn back into the people’s resistance; instead, they must turn toward his God-given word.

## Vocabulary

- **15:1 — נֶפֶשׁ (*nephesh*):** “whole being.” The LORD’s *nephesh* expresses personal aversion toward the people, not merely an impersonal legal judgment.

- **15:6 — נָחַם (*naḥam*):** “relent.” In this passage the term concerns the LORD’s turning from announced judgment; the statement that God is weary of relenting heightens the finality of the warning.

- **15:16 — דָּבָר (*davar*):** “word.” God’s word is found and eaten by Jeremiah, becoming both joy and the defining claim upon his life.

- **15:17 — זַעַם (*zaʿam*):** “indignation.” Jeremiah’s separation from celebratory company is linked to the indignation with which the LORD has filled him.

- **15:19 — שׁוּב (*shuv*):** “return.” The word is repeated: Jeremiah must return, the LORD will restore him, and the people must turn to him rather than Jeremiah turning toward them.

- **15:21 — פָּדָה (*padah*):** “redeem.” The LORD’s promised rescue of Jeremiah is described with language of release from a claim or power that holds someone captive.
