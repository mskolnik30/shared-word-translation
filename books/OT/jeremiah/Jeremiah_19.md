# Jeremiah 19

v01:
<p>
Thus says the LORD: Go, buy a potter’s earthenware flask. Take some of the elders of the people and some of the elders of the priests,
</p>

v02:
<p>
and go out to the Valley of Ben-hinnom, at the entrance of the Potsherd Gate. There proclaim the words that I will speak to you.
</p>

v03:
<p>
Say: Hear the word of the LORD, kings of Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem. Thus says the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel: Look, I am bringing such disaster on this place that the ears of everyone who hears of it will ring.
</p>

v04:
<p>
Because they have forsaken me and made this place foreign, and have burned incense in it to other gods whom neither they nor their ancestors nor the kings of Judah have known, and because they have filled this place with innocent blood.
</p>

v05:
<p>
They have built the high places of Baal to burn their children in the fire as whole offerings to Baal—something I did not command, did not speak of, and that never entered my heart.
</p>

v06:
<p>
Therefore, look, days are coming, declares the LORD, when this place will no longer be called Topheth or the Valley of Ben-hinnom, but the Valley of Slaughter.
</p>

v07:
<p>
I will empty out the counsel of Judah and Jerusalem in this place. I will make them fall by the sword before their enemies and by the hand of those seeking their lives. I will give their corpses as food to the birds of the sky and the wild animals of the earth.
</p>

v08:
<p>
I will make this city a horror and an object of hissing. Everyone who passes by it will be appalled and will hiss because of all its wounds.
</p>

v09:
<p>
I will make them eat the flesh of their sons and the flesh of their daughters; each one will eat the flesh of a neighbor in the siege and distress with which their enemies and those seeking their lives will press them.
</p>

v10:
<p>
Then break the flask before the eyes of the people who go with you,
</p>

v11:
<p>
and say to them: Thus says the LORD of hosts: In this way I will break this people and this city, as one breaks a potter’s vessel that cannot be repaired again. They will bury in Topheth because there is no place left to bury.
</p>

v12:
<p>
Thus I will do to this place, declares the LORD, and to its inhabitants, making this city like Topheth.
</p>

v13:
<p>
The houses of Jerusalem and the houses of the kings of Judah will become unclean like the place of Topheth—all the houses on whose roofs they burned incense to all the host of the heavens and poured out drink offerings to other gods.
</p>

v14:
<p>
Then Jeremiah came from Topheth, where the LORD had sent him to prophesy. He stood in the court of the house of the LORD and said to all the people,
</p>

v15:
<p>
Thus says the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel: Look, I am bringing on this city and on all its towns all the disaster that I have spoken against it, because they stiffened their neck so as not to listen to my words.
</p>

## Notes

- **v2:** The name of the gate is difficult. The Hebrew is traditionally read as “Potsherd Gate,” which fits the earthenware flask and the potter imagery in the chapter. Another reading may suggest a gate associated with the sun.
- **v3:** “The ears … will ring” describes the physical shock of hearing terrible news. The judgment is presented as something so horrifying that its report itself overwhelms the hearer.
- **v4:** To “make this place foreign” means to treat a place belonging to the LORD as though it belonged to strangers. The charge joins idolatry with the shedding of innocent blood.
- **v5:** The threefold denial—“I did not command, did not speak of, and that never entered my heart”—intensifies the rejection of child sacrifice. It is not merely an unauthorized form of worship but wholly opposed to the LORD’s will.
- **v7:** “I will empty out” echoes the sound of the Hebrew word for the flask in v1 and v10. The enacted breaking of the vessel and the emptying of Judah’s counsel together portray the collapse of Jerusalem’s plans.
- **v9:** The language of siege is stark and literal. The chapter does not soften the horror of famine within a besieged city.
- **v11:** The broken pot signifies ruin beyond repair. Yet the prophetic image does not answer every question about the future of the people; it declares the irreversible severity of the judgment being announced here.
- **v13:** Rooftops served as accessible domestic spaces and could be used for religious rites. The city’s houses, including royal houses, are implicated in worship directed to the “host of the heavens” and to other gods.
- **v15:** “Stiffened their neck” is an embodied image of refusal: rather than bowing or turning to listen, the people set themselves rigidly against the LORD’s words.

## Vocabulary

- **v1: בַּקְבֻּק (*baqbuq*)** — an earthenware flask or bottle. Its narrow shape and fragile material make it suited to Jeremiah’s public sign-act; its sound may also echo the verb “empty out” in v7.
- **v4: נָכַר (*nakhar*)** — “to make foreign” or “to treat as alien.” Jerusalem’s sacred space is portrayed as estranged from the LORD through idolatry.
- **v5: עֹלָה (*ʿolah*)** — a “whole offering,” an offering that goes up in smoke. Here the term is horrifyingly applied to children offered to Baal.
- **v6: תֹּפֶת (*Topheth*)** — the place in the Valley of Ben-hinnom associated in this passage with child sacrifice, death, and burial. Its name becomes transformed into a sign of judgment.
- **v7: בָּקַק (*baqaq*)** — “to empty out.” The LORD will empty Judah and Jerusalem of their counsel, a wordplay with the flask (*baqbuq*) Jeremiah carries.
- **v8: שַׁמָּה (*shammah*)** — “horror” or “desolation.” The city’s devastation will make it an object of appalled public attention.
- **v15: עֹרֶף (*ʿoreph*)** — “neck” or “nape.” To stiffen the neck is a recurring image for stubborn refusal to hear and obey the LORD.
