## A Word to Baruch

<p>
v01: The word that Jeremiah the prophet spoke to Baruch son of Neriah when he wrote these words in a book from the mouth of Jeremiah, in the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah, saying:

v02: “Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel, concerning you, Baruch:

v03: You said,
‘Woe to me!
For the LORD has added grief to my pain.
I am weary with my groaning,
and I find no rest.’
</p>

## Do Not Seek Great Things for Yourself

<p>
v04: “Thus you shall say to him: Thus says the LORD:

Look, what I built
I am tearing down,
and what I planted
I am uprooting—
that is, this whole land.

v05: And you—do you seek great things for yourself?
Do not seek them.
For look, I am bringing disaster
upon all flesh,” says the LORD,
“but I will give you your life as plunder
in every place where you go.”
</p>
## Notes
v01: The chapter returns chronologically to the fourth year of Jehoiakim and is closely connected with Baruch’s writing work in Jeremiah 36.
v03: Baruch’s lament is preserved without rebuke for feeling grief. The divine response addresses the scale of his expectations amid a collapsing world.
v04: “build” and “plant,” normally hopeful verbs in Jeremiah, are reversed by “tear down” and “uproot.” The whole land is undergoing the destructive side of Jeremiah’s prophetic commission.
v05: The nature of the “great things” Baruch seeks is not specified. TSW preserves the open wording rather than guessing whether the concern was status, security, prophetic recognition, or something else.
v05: Baruch is not promised escape from upheaval but preservation of life. The recurring phrase “life as plunder” describes survival when other gains cannot be secured.

##Vocabulary

v03: יָגוֹן (yagon)
“Grief” or “sorrow.” Deep emotional pain.

v03: אֲנָחָה (anachah)
“Groaning” or “sighing.” Audible expression of distress.

v04: בָּנָה (banah)
“To build.” Here the LORD tears down what had been built.

v04: נָטַע (nata)
“To plant.” Here paired with uprooting.

v05: גְּדֹלוֹת (gedolot)
“Great things.” Things of importance, status, or magnitude; the phrase remains deliberately unspecified.

v05: שָׁלָל (shalal)
“Plunder.” Baruch’s life itself is promised as the spoil he will carry away.
