# Jeremiah 5

v01:
<p>
Roam through the streets of Jerusalem; look, please, and take note. Search her squares: if you can find anyone, if there is one who does justice, who seeks faithfulness, then I will forgive her.
</p>

v02:
<p>
Though they say, “As Yahweh lives,” they swear falsely.
</p>

v03:
<p>
Yahweh, are not your eyes upon faithfulness? You struck them, but they did not grieve; you brought them to an end, but they refused to receive discipline. They made their faces harder than rock; they refused to turn back.
</p>

v04:
<p>
Then I said, “Surely these are only the poor; they have acted foolishly, for they do not know the way of Yahweh, the justice of their God.
</p>

v05:
<p>
I will go to the great and speak with them, for they know the way of Yahweh, the justice of their God.” But they too, together, had broken the yoke; they had torn off the bonds.
</p>

v06:
<p>
Therefore a lion from the forest will strike them; a wolf of the desert plains will devastate them. A leopard watches over their cities; everyone who goes out from them will be torn apart. For their rebellions are many; their turnings away are great.
</p>

v07:
<p>
“Why should I forgive you? Your children have forsaken me and have sworn by what is not God. I satisfied them, and they committed adultery; they crowd into the house of a prostitute.
</p>

v08:
<p>
They are well-fed, lustful stallions; each one neighs after the wife of a neighbor.
</p>

v09:
<p>
Should I not punish for these things?” declares Yahweh. “Should I not take vengeance on a nation like this?”
</p>

v10:
<p>
“Go up among her vine rows and destroy, but do not make a full end. Strip away her branches, for they do not belong to Yahweh.
</p>

v11:
<p>
For the house of Israel and the house of Judah have utterly betrayed me,” declares Yahweh.
</p>

v12:
<p>
They have denied Yahweh and said, “He is nothing. No evil will come upon us; we will not see sword or famine.
</p>

v13:
<p>
The prophets will become wind, and the word is not in them. Thus it will be done to them.”
</p>

v14:
<p>
Therefore, thus says Yahweh, God of hosts: “Because you speak this word, look, I am making my words fire in your mouth, and this people wood; the fire will consume them.
</p>

v15:
<p>
Look, I am bringing against you a nation from far away, house of Israel,” declares Yahweh. “It is an enduring nation; it is a nation from ancient time. It is a nation whose language you do not know, and you will not understand what it speaks.
</p>

v16:
<p>
Its quiver is like an open grave; all of them are warriors.
</p>

v17:
<p>
It will consume your harvest and your bread; they will consume your sons and your daughters. It will consume your flock and your herd; it will consume your vine and your fig tree. With the sword it will crush your fortified cities in which you trust.
</p>

v18:
<p>
“But even in those days,” declares Yahweh, “I will not make a full end of you.
</p>

v19:
<p>
And when you ask, ‘Why has Yahweh our God done all these things to us?’ then you shall say to them, ‘As you forsook me and served foreign gods in your land, so you will serve strangers in a land that is not yours.’
</p>

v20:
<p>
Declare this in the house of Jacob; proclaim it in Judah, saying:
</p>

v21:
<p>
“Hear this, please, foolish people without understanding, who have eyes but do not see, who have ears but do not hear.
</p>

v22:
<p>
Will you not fear me?” declares Yahweh. “Will you not tremble before me—I who set the sand as a boundary for the sea, an enduring decree that it cannot cross? Though its waves rage, they cannot prevail; though they roar, they cannot cross it.
</p>

v23:
<p>
But this people has a stubborn and rebellious heart. They have turned aside and gone away.
</p>

v24:
<p>
They do not say in their heart, ‘Let us fear Yahweh our God, who gives rain—the early rain and the latter rain—in its season, who keeps for us the appointed weeks of harvest.’
</p>

v25:
<p>
Your iniquities have turned these away, and your sins have withheld good from you.
</p>

v26:
<p>
For wicked people are found among my people. They watch, crouching like bird-catchers; they set a destructive trap; they catch people.
</p>

v27:
<p>
Like a cage full of birds, so their houses are full of deceit. Therefore they have become great and rich.
</p>

v28:
<p>
They have grown fat; they are sleek. They have gone beyond deeds of evil. They do not judge the case—the case of the orphan—yet they prosper; and they do not uphold the justice of the needy.
</p>

v29:
<p>
Should I not punish for these things?” declares Yahweh. “Should I not take vengeance on a nation like this?”
</p>

v30:
<p>
A shocking and horrible thing has happened in the land:
</p>

v31:
<p>
The prophets prophesy falsely, and the priests rule by their own authority; and my people love it so. But what will you do when its end comes?
</p>

## Notes

- v1: The search for even one person who “does justice” and “seeks faithfulness” recalls Abraham’s intercession for Sodom. The possibility of forgiveness remains open, yet the chapter’s unfolding testimony exposes the city’s deep failure.
- v3: “Faithfulness” echoes the word sought in v1. The people’s refusal to receive discipline is described not merely as ignorance but as a hardened refusal to return.
- v10: The city is portrayed as a vineyard. The command to destroy is qualified: “do not make a full end,” a restraint repeated in v18.
- v12–13: The people dismiss prophetic warning by calling the prophets “wind,” without a true word in them. In v14, Yahweh reverses their claim: Jeremiah’s words become fire, and the people become wood for its consuming force.
- v15–17: The invading nation is described chiefly through its distance, antiquity, incomprehensible speech, and relentless consumption. The repeated “it will consume” gives the warning a cumulative, devastating rhythm.
- v22–24: The sea, despite its roaring waves, remains within the boundary Yahweh sets; Judah, by contrast, has a “stubborn and rebellious heart.” The passage joins reverence for the Creator with gratitude for rain and harvest.
- v26–28: Social injustice is not secondary to the chapter’s indictment. Deceit and exploitation fill the houses of the powerful, while the orphan and the needy are denied justice.
- v31: “The priests rule by their own authority” translates a difficult expression that may also suggest rule carried out under the prophets’ direction. The verse condemns the shared acceptance of false prophecy and corrupt leadership.

## Vocabulary

- v1: **אֱמוּנָה — *’emunah***: “faithfulness.” In this chapter it is what Yahweh seeks in Jerusalem and what Yahweh’s eyes are directed toward in v3; it denotes reliable covenantal integrity, not merely inward belief.
- v3: **מוּסָר — *musar***: “discipline.” The people refuse to receive the corrective instruction conveyed through suffering, deepening their hardened resistance.
- v5: **עֹל — *‘ol***: “yoke.” Together with “bonds,” it depicts the people’s rejection of Yahweh’s ordering claim upon their life.
- v6: **מְשׁוּבָה — *meshubah***: “turning away” or “apostasy.” The word emphasizes repeated departure from Yahweh rather than a single failure.
- v28: **מִשְׁפָּט — *mishpat***: “justice” or “judgment.” Here it concerns concrete legal protection for those without power, especially the orphan and the needy.
