<p>
v01:
Hear the word that the LORD has spoken concerning you, house of Israel.
</p>

<p>
v02:
Thus says the LORD:

Do not learn the way of the nations,
and do not be dismayed by the signs of the heavens,
though the nations are dismayed by them.
</p>

<p>
v03:
For the customs of the peoples are emptiness:
a tree is cut from the forest,
the work of a craftsperson’s hands with an axe.
</p>

<p>
v04:
They adorn it with silver and gold;
they fasten it with nails and hammers
so that it will not topple.
</p>

<p>
v05:
Like a scarecrow in a cucumber field, they cannot speak;
they must be carried, for they cannot walk.
Do not fear them,
for they cannot do harm,
nor is it in them to do good.
</p>

<p>
v06:
There is none like you, LORD;
you are great,
and your name is great in might.
</p>

<p>
v07:
Who would not fear you,
King of the nations?
For this is your due.

For among all the wise of the nations
and in all their kingdoms,
there is none like you.
</p>

<p>
v08:
Together they are dull and foolish;
the instruction of idols is wood.
</p>

<p>
v09:
Hammered silver is brought from Tarshish,
and gold from Uphaz—
the work of a craftsperson and of a goldsmith’s hands.
Blue and purple are their clothing;
all of them are the work of skilled artisans.
</p>

<p>
v10:
But the LORD is the true God;
he is the living God and the everlasting King.
At his wrath the earth quakes,
and the nations cannot endure his indignation.
</p>

<p>
v11:
Thus you shall say to them:

“The gods that did not make the heavens and the earth
shall perish from the earth
and from beneath these heavens.”
</p>

<p>
v12:
He made the earth by his power,
established the world by his wisdom,
and stretched out the heavens by his understanding.
</p>

<p>
v13:
When he gives his voice,
there is a roar of waters in the heavens;
he causes clouds to rise from the ends of the earth.
He makes lightning for the rain
and brings out the wind from his storehouses.
</p>

<p>
v14:
Every person is dull, without knowledge;
every goldsmith is put to shame by an idol.
For the image he has cast is a lie,
and there is no breath in them.
</p>

<p>
v15:
They are emptiness, a work of mockery;
at the time of their punishment, they will perish.
</p>

<p>
v16:
The Portion of Jacob is not like these,
for he is the Maker of all things,
and Israel is the tribe of his inheritance;
the LORD of hosts is his name.
</p>

<p>
v17:
Gather up your bundle from the land,
you who live under siege.
</p>

<p>
v18:
For thus says the LORD:

Look, I am slinging out the inhabitants of the land
at this time,
and I will bring distress upon them,
so that they may find it.
</p>

<p>
v19:
Woe is me because of my brokenness!
My wound is grievous.
But I said,
“Surely this is my sickness,
and I must bear it.”
</p>

<p>
v20:
My tent has been ravaged,
and all my cords have been torn away.
My children have gone from me, and they are no more;
there is no one left to spread my tent again
or to set up my tent curtains.
</p>

<p>
v21:
For the shepherds have become dull
and have not sought the LORD;
therefore they have not acted wisely,
and all their flock has been scattered.
</p>

<p>
v22:
The sound of a report—look, it is coming!
A great shaking from the land of the north,
to make the cities of Judah a desolation,
a dwelling place of jackals.
</p>

<p>
v23:
I know, LORD, that a person’s way is not their own;
it is not for one who walks
to direct their steps.
</p>

<p>
v24:
Correct me, LORD, but with justice;
not in your anger,
lest you make me few.
</p>

<p>
v25:
Pour out your wrath on the nations that do not know you,
and on the families that do not call on your name.
For they have devoured Jacob;
they have devoured him and brought him to an end,
and they have made his dwelling place desolate.
</p>

## Notes

- **10:2.** “Signs of the heavens” refers to celestial phenomena that the nations treated as grounds for fear or divination. The command does not deny that the heavens are meaningful within God’s creation; it rejects being ruled by the fear attached to them.
- **10:5.** The image is literally like a “palm tree of a cucumber field,” an expression whose precise picture is uncertain. The comparison emphasizes the idol’s fixed, speechless, and dependent condition.
- **10:11.** This verse is in Aramaic rather than Hebrew. Its direct address, “Thus you shall say to them,” may present Israel’s response to surrounding peoples and their gods.
- **10:16.** “Portion of Jacob” names the LORD as Israel’s allotted possession, while “the tribe of his inheritance” reverses the relation: Israel is also the LORD’s own inheritance. The mutual language stands against the lifeless products of human craft.
- **10:18.** The phrase “so that they may find it” is abrupt: the object is unstated. It may mean that the inhabitants will finally encounter the distress announced against them, or that they will come to recognize the LORD’s action through it.
- **10:23–24.** The prayer holds together human limitation, divine correction, and the plea that judgment not become annihilation. “With justice” asks not for the absence of discipline but for discipline governed by right measure.

## Vocabulary

- **10:3 — הֶבֶל (*hevel*):** “Emptiness” describes the customs of the peoples as insubstantial and futile. The word returns in verse 15, framing idols as unable to bear the weight placed upon them.
- **10:10 — אֱמֶת (*’emet*):** “True” distinguishes the LORD from manufactured gods. Here it conveys reliability and reality: the LORD is truly God, living and enduring.
- **10:13 — רוּחַ (*ruach*):** “Wind” comes from God’s storehouses. The term can also mean breath or spirit; its use here contributes to the contrast with idols, in which “there is no breath” (v. 14).
- **10:16 — חֵלֶק (*cheleq*):** “Portion” expresses covenant belonging. The LORD is Jacob’s portion, not an object possessed or fashioned by Israel.
- **10:18 — קָלַע (*qala‘*):** “Slinging out” evokes throwing a stone from a sling. The image makes the coming removal from the land forceful and inescapable.
- **10:24 — מִשְׁפָּט (*mishpat*):** “Justice” or “judgment” asks for correction according to what is right, in contrast to anger that would reduce the speaker to nothing.
