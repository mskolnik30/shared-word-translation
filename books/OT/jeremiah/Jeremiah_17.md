He will live in parched places in the desert,
in a salt land where no one lives.

v07:

whose trust is the LORD.

v08:

sending out its roots beside a stream.
It does not fear when heat comes;
its leaves remain green.

and it does not cease to bear fruit.

v09:

and desperately sick—
who can understand it?

v10:

I test the inward parts,
to give to each according to their way,

v11:

v12:

## Notes

- **17:1.** Judah’s sin is pictured as permanently engraved both on the heart—the center of thought, desire, and resolve—and on the horns of the altars, the projecting corners associated with sacrificial worship. The image joins inward disposition and public worship in a single indictment.

- **17:5–8.** The contrast is not between receiving help from other people and trusting the LORD, but between making mortal flesh one’s strength and making the LORD one’s trust. The paired images of desert shrub and well-watered tree portray the consequences of these different allegiances.

- **17:9–10.** “Heart” includes more than emotion; it refers to the inner person, including will and understanding. The LORD’s searching of the heart does not deny its obscurity but answers it: what humans cannot finally know, God examines and judges.

- **17:11.** The saying about the partridge uses an image whose precise natural-history reference is uncertain. Its point is clear: wealth gained without justice is not securely possessed and ends in exposure and folly.

- **17:13.** The Hebrew textual tradition preserves two closely related readings: “those who turn away from me” and “those who turn away from you.” The translation follows the first-person reading, which fits the LORD’s concluding identification as “the fountain of living water.”

- **17:21–27.** Sabbath keeping is expressed concretely through the refusal to carry commercial loads through Jerusalem’s gates. The command concerns worship, rest, and the public ordering of the city’s life; the gates become the place where covenant faithfulness is made visible.

## Vocabulary

- **17:1 חַטָּאת (*ḥaṭṭāʾt*)** — “sin.” Here Judah’s sin is not fleeting failure but an enduring inscription, engraved on heart and altar alike.

- **17:5 בָּטַח (*bāṭaḥ*)** — “trust.” The passage contrasts reliance on mortal strength with reliance on the LORD, making trust the central issue in the paired curse and blessing.

- **17:9 לֵב (*lēb*)** — “heart.” In this passage the heart is the hidden center of human life, capable of deception and fully known only to the LORD.

- **17:10 כִּלְיָה (*kilyāh*)** — “inward parts,” literally “kidneys.” Alongside the heart, the term expresses the deepest and least accessible dimensions of a person before God.

- **17:13 מִקְוֶה (*miqweh*)** — “hope.” The LORD is called “Hope of Israel,” a title set against the abandonment of the LORD as the source of living water.

- **17:13 מָקוֹר (*māqôr*)** — “fountain” or “spring.” The LORD is the “fountain of living water,” the life-giving source Israel has forsaken.

- **17:22 קָדַשׁ (*qādaš*)** — “make holy,” “set apart.” Keeping the Sabbath holy means treating the day as belonging distinctively to the LORD.

- **17:23 מוּסָר (*mûsār*)** — “instruction” or “discipline.” The ancestors’ refusal is not merely failure to hear but refusal to receive correction that might form them.
