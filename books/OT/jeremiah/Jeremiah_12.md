# Jeremiah 12

v01:
Righteous are you, LORD,  
when I bring a case against you;  
yet I would speak with you about matters of justice:  
Why does the way of the wicked prosper?  
Why are all who deal treacherously at ease—  
those who deal in treachery?

v02:
You planted them; they have also taken root.  
They go on; they also bear fruit.  
You are near in their mouth,  
but far from their kidneys.

v03:
But you, LORD, know me;  
you see me and test my heart toward you.  
Pull them away like sheep for slaughter;  
set them apart for a day of killing.

v04:
How long will the land mourn  
and all the vegetation of the field wither?  
Because of the evil of those who live in it,  
livestock and birds are swept away,  
for they say, “He will not see our end.”

v05:
If you have run with foot soldiers  
and they have wearied you,  
how will you compete with horses?  
And in a land of peace you are secure—  
but what will you do in the swelling of the Jordan?

v06:
For even your brothers and your father’s house—  
even they have dealt treacherously with you;  
even they have called after you at full voice.  
Do not trust them  
when they speak good things to you.

v07:
I have forsaken my house;  
I have abandoned my inheritance.  
I have given the beloved of my soul  
into the hand of her enemies.

v08:
My inheritance has become to me  
like a lion in the forest;  
she has raised her voice against me;  
therefore I hate her.

v09:
Is my inheritance to me a speckled bird of prey,  
with birds of prey all around her?  
Go, gather all the wild animals;  
bring them to devour.

v10:
Many shepherds have ruined my vineyard;  
they have trampled my portion.  
They have made my delightful portion  
a desolate wilderness.

v11:
He has made it a desolation;  
desolate, it mourns to me.  
All the land is made desolate,  
because no one takes it to heart.

v12:
Upon every bare height in the wilderness  
destroyers have come,  
for the sword of the LORD devours  
from one end of the land to the other end of the land;  
there is no peace for any flesh.

v13:
They have sown wheat  
and harvested thorns.  
They have worn themselves out;  
they will gain nothing.  
Be ashamed of your yields  
because of the burning anger of the LORD.

v14:
<p>
Thus says the LORD concerning all my evil neighbors who touch the inheritance that I caused my people Israel to inherit: See, I am uprooting them from upon their land, and I will uproot the house of Judah from among them.
</p>

v15:
<p>
And after I uproot them, I will again have compassion on them, and I will restore each one to their inheritance and each one to their land.
</p>

v16:
<p>
And if they truly learn the ways of my people, to swear by my name, “As the LORD lives,” just as they taught my people to swear by Baal, then they will be built up in the midst of my people.
</p>

v17:
<p>
But if they will not listen, I will uproot that nation, uprooting and destroying it, declares the LORD.
</p>

## Notes

- **1:** Jeremiah begins by affirming that the LORD is righteous even while bringing a complaint concerning justice. The verse does not resolve the tension between God’s righteousness and the prosperity of those who act treacherously.

- **2:** “Kidneys” represents the hidden, inward self in Hebrew thought. The contrast is between speech that names God and an inner life far from God.

- **3:** “Set them apart” uses language associated with consecration. Here it is used with fearful irony: the wicked are set apart not for worship but for slaughter.

- **5:** The “swelling of the Jordan” may refer to the river’s floodwaters or to its luxuriant, dangerous riverbank growth. The image intensifies the comparison: if the present trial has exhausted Jeremiah, a far greater danger lies ahead.

- **7–9:** The LORD’s inheritance, Israel, is spoken of with grief and hostility together: “the beloved of my soul” is handed over, becomes like a lion against the LORD, and is portrayed as a bird of prey surrounded by other predators. The shifting images preserve the emotional force of betrayal, judgment, and vulnerability.

- **14–17:** The oracle widens judgment beyond Judah to its surrounding nations. Yet uprooting is not the final word: those peoples may be restored if they learn the ways of the LORD rather than teaching Israel allegiance to Baal.

## Vocabulary

- **1: בָּגַד (*bagad*)** — “to deal treacherously.” The verse intensifies the word by pairing it with a related noun: those at ease are not merely mistaken but actively characterized by betrayal.

- **3: קָדַשׁ (*qadash*)** — “to set apart” or “consecrate.” In this passage the term is turned toward judgment, marking people out for the day of killing.

- **5: תַּחָרָה (*taḥarah*)** — “to compete” or “contend.” Jeremiah’s struggle is pictured as a race that moves from foot soldiers to horses, exposing the greater endurance required ahead.

- **7: נַחֲלָה (*naḥalah*)** — “inheritance” or “allotted possession.” Israel is repeatedly called the LORD’s inheritance, expressing both covenant belonging and the pain of Israel’s rejection.

- **9: עַיִט (*ʿayiṭ*)** — “bird of prey.” The image portrays the LORD’s inheritance as exposed to attack from surrounding predators.

- **14: נָתַשׁ (*natash*)** — “to uproot” or “tear away.” The repeated word joins judgment upon Judah’s neighbors with Judah’s own displacement.

- **16: לָמַד (*lamad*)** — “to learn” or “teach.” The nations once taught Israel to swear by Baal; restoration is imagined as their learning Israel’s covenantal way instead.
