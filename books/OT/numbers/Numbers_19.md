## The Red Heifer

<p>
v01: The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron, saying,  
v02: “This is the statute of the instruction that the LORD has commanded: Speak to the Israelites and have them bring you a red heifer without defect, in which there is no blemish and upon which no yoke has ever come.  
v03: You shall give it to Eleazar the priest, and it shall be taken outside the camp and slaughtered before him.  
v04: Eleazar the priest shall take some of its blood with his finger and sprinkle some of its blood toward the front of the Tent of Meeting seven times.  
v05: Then the heifer shall be burned before his eyes—its hide, flesh, blood, and dung shall be burned.  
</p>

<p>
v06: The priest shall take cedar wood, hyssop, and scarlet material and throw them into the burning of the heifer.  
v07: Then the priest shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in water; afterward he may enter the camp, but the priest shall be unclean until evening.  
v08: The one who burns it shall wash his clothes in water and bathe his body in water and shall be unclean until evening.  
v09: A man who is clean shall gather up the ashes of the heifer and place them outside the camp in a clean place. They shall be kept for the congregation of the Israelites for water of purification; it is for purification from sin.  
v10: The one who gathers the ashes of the heifer shall wash his clothes and be unclean until evening. This shall be a perpetual statute for the Israelites and for the resident alien who lives among them.  
</p>

## Purification from Contact with Death

<p>
v11: Whoever touches the dead body of any person shall be unclean seven days.  
v12: That person shall purify themselves with the water on the third day and on the seventh day and shall be clean. But if the person does not purify themselves on the third day and on the seventh day, the person shall not be clean.  
v13: Whoever touches a dead body, the body of a person who has died, and does not purify themselves defiles the tabernacle of the LORD. That person shall be cut off from Israel. Because the water of purification was not sprinkled on them, they remain unclean; their uncleanness is still upon them.  
</p>

<p>
v14: This is the instruction when a person dies in a tent: everyone who enters the tent and everyone who is in the tent shall be unclean seven days.  
v15: Every open vessel that has no covering fastened over it shall be unclean.  
v16: Whoever in the open field touches someone killed by the sword, a dead body, a human bone, or a grave shall be unclean seven days.  
</p>

## The Water of Purification

<p>
v17: For the unclean person they shall take some of the ashes from the burning of the purification offering and put fresh water with them in a vessel.  
v18: A clean person shall take hyssop, dip it in the water, and sprinkle it on the tent, all the vessels, the people who were there, and the one who touched the bone, the slain person, the dead body, or the grave.  
v19: The clean person shall sprinkle the unclean person on the third day and on the seventh day. On the seventh day the unclean person shall purify themselves, wash their clothes, and bathe in water, and at evening shall be clean.  
</p>

<p>
v20: But anyone who is unclean and does not purify themselves—that person shall be cut off from the midst of the assembly, because that person has defiled the sanctuary of the LORD. The water of purification has not been sprinkled on them; they are unclean.  
v21: It shall be a perpetual statute for them. The one who sprinkles the water of purification shall wash their clothes, and whoever touches the water of purification shall be unclean until evening.  
v22: Whatever the unclean person touches shall be unclean, and the person who touches it shall be unclean until evening.  
</p>

## Notes
v2: The heifer must be entirely suitable for the rite and never used for ordinary labor. The text calls this regulation a “statute of the instruction,” joining fixed ordinance and Torah.  
v3: The animal is slaughtered outside the camp rather than at the altar, yet its blood is oriented toward the Tent of Meeting.  
v7–10: Those who prepare the purifying ashes themselves become temporarily unclean. The rite holds together cleansing power and contact with the sphere of death.  
v9: “water of purification” — The Hebrew phrase literally means “water of impurity,” referring to water used to remove impurity, not water that is itself morally polluted.  
v11–22: Contact with human death creates a seven-day ritual impurity that must be resolved before ordinary access to the sanctuary community is restored.  

## Vocabulary
v2: חֻקַּת הַתּוֹרָה (chuqqat hattorah)  
“Statute of the instruction.” A fixed ordinance within the Torah.  
v2: פָּרָה אֲדֻמָּה (parah adummah)  
“Red heifer.” The animal required for the purification rite.  
v6: אֵזוֹב (ezov)  
“Hyssop.” A small plant used in purification rituals.  
v9: מֵי נִדָּה (mei niddah)  
“Water of purification.” Literally “water of impurity,” water prepared to remove corpse impurity.  
v12: חָטָא (chata)  
“To purify from sin/impurity” or “to sin,” depending on stem and context. Here ritual purification is in view.
