<p>
For the director. A Maskil of the sons of Korah.
</p>

## Thirst for the Living God

<p>
v01: As a deer longs
for channels of water,
so my soul longs for you, O God.

v02: My soul thirsts for God,
for the living God.
When shall I come
and appear before the face of God?

v03: My tears have been my bread
day and night,
while they say to me all day,
“Where is your God?”
</p>

## Remembering the House of God

<p>
v04: These things I remember
as I pour out my soul within me:
how I passed with the throng,
leading them in procession
to the house of God,
with the sound of rejoicing and thanksgiving,
a multitude keeping festival.

v05: Why are you bowed down, my soul,
and why are you in turmoil within me?
Hope in God,
for I shall again praise God,
the saving help of God’s face.
</p>

## Deep Calls to Deep

<p>
v06: My God,
my soul is bowed down within me;
therefore I remember you
from the land of Jordan and Hermon,
from Mount Mizar.

v07: Deep calls to deep
at the sound of your waterfalls;
all your breakers and your waves
have passed over me.

v08: By day the LORD commands
steadfast love,
and at night the LORD’s song is with me—
a prayer to the God of my life.
</p>

## Why Have You Forgotten Me?

<p>
v09: I say to God, my rock,
“Why have you forgotten me?
Why do I go mourning
under the oppression of the enemy?”

v10: As with a crushing in my bones,
my adversaries reproach me,
while they say to me all day,
“Where is your God?”

v11: Why are you bowed down, my soul,
and why are you in turmoil within me?
Hope in God,
for I shall again praise God,
the saving help of my face
and my God.
</p>

## Notes

v01–02: “longs … thirsts” — Physical need for water becomes the controlling image for longing for God. The metaphor is allowed to retain its bodily force.

v02: “appear before the face of God” — The expression is associated with coming into divine presence, particularly in worship. The concrete language of God’s “face” is retained.

v04: “leading them in procession” — The Hebrew verb is rare and difficult. The context suggests movement with a festival crowd toward the house of God.

v05: “the saving help of God’s face” — The Hebrew wording differs slightly from the refrain in v11. The translation preserves that difference rather than harmonizing the two refrains.

v06: “Mount Mizar” — The location is uncertain. The name is retained without identifying it with a known site.

v07: “Deep calls to deep” — The language moves from thirst to overwhelming water. The psalmist who longed for streams now experiences the imagery of waters passing over them.

v10: “As with a crushing in my bones” — The Hebrew construction is difficult and intensely physical. The translation preserves the image of inward bodily injury produced by the enemies’ reproach.

v11: The repeated refrain does not eliminate the psalmist’s distress. Hope and turmoil remain together at the conclusion of the psalm.

## Vocabulary

v01: עָרַג (arag)
“To long for” or “pant after.” Intense desire expressed through the image of an animal seeking water.

v02: צָמֵא (tsame)
“To thirst.” Bodily thirst becomes an image for desire for the living God.

v02: נֶפֶשׁ (nefesh)
“Soul,” “life,” or “self.” The living self in its desire, distress, and longing.

v05: יָחַל (yachal)
“To hope” or “wait expectantly.” Trust directed toward what God will yet do.

v07: תְּהוֹם (tehom)
“Deep.” The great or primeval waters; here one depth answers another.

v08: חֶסֶד (ḥesed)
“Steadfast love.” Faithful and enduring love expressed within relationship.

v11: יְשׁוּעָה (yeshuah)
“Saving help,” “deliverance,” or “salvation.” Rescue or restoration associated here with the presence of God.
