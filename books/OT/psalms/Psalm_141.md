<p>
A psalm of David.
</p>

## Let My Prayer Be Incense before You

<p>
v01: O LORD, I call to you; hasten to me.
Give ear to my voice when I call to you.
v02: Let my prayer be set before you as incense,
the lifting of my hands as the evening offering.
v03: Set a guard, O LORD, over my mouth;
keep watch over the door of my lips.
v04: Do not incline my heart toward an evil thing,
to practice wicked deeds
with those who do evil;
let me not eat their delicacies.
</p>

## Let the Righteous Strike Me in Steadfast Love

<p>
v05: Let the righteous strike me—it is steadfast love;
let one rebuke me—it is oil for my head;
let my head not refuse it.
Yet my prayer is still against their evil deeds.
v06: When their judges are thrown down beside the rock,
then they will hear my words, for they are pleasant.
v07: As when one plows and breaks open the earth,
so our bones are scattered at the mouth of Sheol.
</p>

## My Eyes Are toward You

<p>
v08: But my eyes are toward you, O LORD my Lord;
in you I take refuge.
Do not pour out my life.
v09: Keep me from the trap they have laid for me
and from the snares of those who do evil.
v10: Let the wicked fall into their own nets together,
while I pass by safely.
</p>
##Notes

v05–07: The Hebrew is difficult, especially the transition from welcomed righteous rebuke to the fate of hostile judges. The translation follows the Masoretic wording and sequence rather than rearranging the lines to create a smoother argument.

v06: “Thrown down beside the rock” is a stark and uncertain image.

##Vocabulary

v02: מִנְחָה (minchah)
“Offering,” often a grain or tribute offering; here the evening offering provides the liturgical image for lifted hands.

v07: שְׁאוֹל (sheʾol)
“The realm of the dead, pictured here as having a mouth at which scattered bones lie exposed.”
