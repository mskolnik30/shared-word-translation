<v01:1>For the leader. Of David. A psalm. A song.

v02: Let God arise, let his enemies be scattered;
let those who hate him flee before him.
v03: As smoke is driven away, drive them away;
as wax melts before the fire, let the wicked perish before God.
v04: But let the righteous be glad;
let them rejoice before God, yes, let them delight with joy.
v05: Sing to God, sing praises to his name;
exalt him who rides on the clouds;
by his name Yah, and rejoice before him.
v06: A father of the fatherless and a defender of widows
is God in his holy dwelling.
v07: God sets the solitary in families;
he brings out those who are bound with chains;
but the rebellious dwell in a scorched land.
v08: O God, when you went out before your people,
when you marched through the wilderness—Selah—
v09: the earth shook,
even the heavens poured down rain at the presence of God;
Sinai itself was moved at the presence of God, the God of Israel.
v10: You, O God, sent a plentiful rain,
whereby you confirmed your inheritance, when it was weary.
v11: Your creatures dwell in it;
you prepare in your goodness for the poor, O God.

v12: The Lord gives the word;
great is the company of those who proclaim it:
v13: “Kings of armies flee, they flee,
and she who remains at home divides the spoil.
v14: Though you lie down among the sheepfolds,
you will be like the wings of a dove covered with silver,
and her feathers with shining gold.”
v15: When the Almighty scattered kings in it,
it snowed in Zalmon.

v16: The mountain of God is the mountain of Bashan;
a mountain of many peaks is the mountain of Bashan.
v17: Why do you gaze with envy, you many-peaked mountain,
at the mountain which God desired for his dwelling place?
The Lord will dwell in it forever.
v18: The chariots of God are twice ten thousand, thousands upon thousands;
the Lord is among them, as at Sinai, in the sanctuary.
v19: You have ascended on high, you have led captivity captive;
you have received gifts among people,
even among the rebellious, that Yah God might dwell there.

v20: Blessed is the Lord, who daily bears us up;
the God of our salvation—Selah—
v21: our God is a God of salvation;
and to God, the Lord, belongs escape from death.
v22: Surely God will crush the heads of his enemies,
the hairy scalp of one who still goes on in his trespasses.
v23: The Lord said, “I will bring them back from Bashan,
I will bring them back from the depths of the sea,
v24: that your foot may crush them in blood,
and the tongues of your dogs may have their portion from your enemies.”

v25: They have seen your procession, O God,
the procession of my God, my King, into the sanctuary.
v26: The singers went before, the players on instruments followed after;
among them were the maidens playing timbrels.
v27: Bless God in the congregations,
the Lord, you who are of the fountain of Israel.
v28: There is little Benjamin, their ruler,
the princes of Judah and their company,
the princes of Zebulun and the princes of Naphtali.

v29: Your God has commanded your strength;
strengthen, O God, what you have done for us.
v30: Because of your temple at Jerusalem
kings will bring presents to you.
v31: Rebuke the beasts of the reeds,
the herd of bulls with the calves of the peoples,
trample underfoot those who lust after silver;
scatter the peoples who delight in war.
v32: Envoys will come out of Egypt;
Cush will quickly stretch out her hands to God.

v33: Sing to God, you kingdoms of the earth;
oh, sing praises to the Lord—Selah—
v34: to him who rides on the heaven of heavens, which are of old!
Behold, he sends out his voice, a mighty voice.
v35: Ascribe strength to God;
his majesty is over Israel, and his strength is in the skies.
v36: O God, you are awesome from your holy places,
the God of Israel is he who gives strength and power to his people.
Blessed be God.

## Notes

- **v01:1**: This verse serves as the superscription for the psalm, acknowledging its author and purpose as a liturgical leader's song of praise.
- **v18**: "You have ascended on high" is a depiction of God's victory and sovereignty, traditionally seen as a descent for the receiving of gifts or spoils, signifying a triumphal presence.
- **v35**: The repetition of the call to recognize God's strength illustrates the theme of divine majesty and power throughout the psalm.

## Vocabulary

- **v18 רֶכֶב (re.khev)**: "Chariots". Symbolic for divine power and military might, often associated with God's sovereignty and intervention in human affairs.
- **v19 מַתָּנוֹת (mat.ta.nah)**: "Gifts". These signify both offerings given to God and God's generous provisions and empowerment of people.
- **v35 עֹז (oz)**: "Strength". A key thematic element, representing God's might in sustaining and defending Israel and highlighting the importance of acknowledging this divine strength.
