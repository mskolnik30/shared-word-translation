<p>
v01: For the director, according to Jeduthun. A psalm of David.
</p>
<p>
v02: Only in God is my soul at rest;  
from him is my salvation.  
v03: Only he is my rock and my salvation,  
my fortress; I shall not be greatly shaken.  
v04: How long will all of you attack a man  
to batter him,  
a leaning wall, a tottering fence?  
v05: Only from his exaltation they have counseled to thrust him down;  
they delight in falsehood.  
With their mouth they bless,  
but inwardly they curse. Selah  
v06: Only in God, O my soul, wait in silence,  
for my hope is from him.  
v07: Only he is my rock and my salvation,  
my fortress; I shall not be shaken.  
v08: On God rests my salvation and my glory;  
my mighty rock, my refuge is God.  
v09: Trust in him at all times, O people;  
pour out your heart before him;  
God is a refuge for us. Selah  
v10: Surely men of low degree are vanity,  
and men of high degree are a lie;  
in the balances they go up;  
together they are lighter than a breath.  
v11: Trust not in oppression,  
and do not put vain hope in robbery;  
if riches increase,  
do not set your heart upon them.  
v12: One thing God has spoken,  
two things I have heard:  
that power belongs to God,  
v13: and to you, O Lord, belongs steadfast love,  
for you will repay everyone  
according to their work.  
</p>

## Notes

v02 Omitting repetitions of "only" after their third instance retains the source's liturgical rhythm, emphasizing trust in God alone.

v04 "Leaning wall" and "tottering fence" describe vulnerability and instability, playing into the psalm's theme of personal assault and divine defense.

v05 The contrast between outward blessing and inward cursing further highlights the duplicity the psalmist faces.

v12-13 God's sovereignty and steadfast love form an inclusio with human action and divine recompense, emphasizing moral and theological balance.

## Vocabulary

v02 אֱלֹהִים (e.lo.him) — Here, God is the sole source of salvation and strength, a theme central to the psalm's trust motif. The term complements other divine titles within the text, enhancing the focus on monotheistic faithfulness.

v13 חֶסֶד (che.sed) — This enduring love or covenant loyalty underpins divine justice as it connects with divine recompense for human actions. It forms a theological bridge between God's nature and the ethical order.
