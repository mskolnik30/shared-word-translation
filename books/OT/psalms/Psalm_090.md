## A Prayer of Moses, the Man of God
<p>
v01: Lord, you have been our dwelling place
in all generations.
v02: Before the mountains were born,
or you brought forth the earth and the world,
from everlasting to everlasting you are God.
</p>

## The Frailty of Humanity
<p>
v03: You return humanity to dust
and say, “Return, children of Adam.”
v04: For a thousand years in your sight
are like yesterday when it passes,
or like a watch in the night.
</p>

<p>
v05: You sweep them away—they are like a dream,
like grass that is renewed in the morning:
v06: in the morning it flourishes and is renewed;
in the evening it fades and withers.
</p>

## Life Under Divine Judgment
<p>
v07: For we are brought to an end by your anger;
by your wrath we are dismayed.
v08: You have set our iniquities before you,
our hidden sins in the light of your presence.
</p>

<p>
v09: For all our days pass away under your wrath;
we bring our years to an end like a sigh.
v10: The years of our life are seventy,
or even by strength eighty;
yet their span is toil and trouble;
they are soon gone, and we fly away.
</p>

<p>
v11: Who considers the power of your anger,
and your wrath according to the fear of you?
</p>

## A Prayer for Wisdom and Favor
<p>
v12: So teach us to number our days,
that we may gain a heart of wisdom.
</p>

<p>
v13: Return, O LORD! How long?
Have compassion on your servants!
v14: Satisfy us in the morning with your steadfast love,
that we may rejoice and be glad all our days.
</p>

<p>
v15: Make us glad for as many days as you have afflicted us,
for as many years as we have seen evil.
v16: Let your work be shown to your servants,
and your splendor to their children.
</p>

<p>
v17: Let the favor of the Lord our God be upon us,
and establish the work of our hands upon us—
yes, establish the work of our hands.
</p>
## Notes
v01–02: God is eternal and stable; humanity finds dwelling in him.  
v03–06: Human life is brief and cyclical, contrasted with divine permanence.  
v07–11: Mortality is interpreted through divine judgment, not randomness.  
v10: Life expectancy is acknowledged without idealization.  
v12: Wisdom arises from awareness of finitude.  
v13–15: Petition seeks reversal—not removal of suffering, but balance and restoration.  
v16–17: Human work gains meaning only through divine establishment.  

## Vocabulary

v01: מָעוֹן (ma‘on)  
“Dwelling place.” Refuge or habitation.  

v02: עוֹלָם (olam)  
“Everlasting.” Unbounded time.  

v03: דַּכָּא (dakka)  
“Dust.” Crushed matter, mortality.  

v04: אֶלֶף (eleph)  
“Thousand.” Large measure of time.  

v05: זֶרֶם (zerem)  
“Flood.” Sweeping force.  

v05: חָצִיר (chatsir)  
“Grass.” Fleeting life.  

v08: עָלַם (alam)  
“To hide.” Conceal.  

v12: מָנָה (manah)  
“To number.” Count or reckon.  

v14: שָׂבַע (sava)  
“To satisfy.” Fill fully.  

v17: כּוּן (kun)  
“To establish.” Make firm.
