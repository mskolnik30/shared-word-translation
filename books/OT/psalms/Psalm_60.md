<p>
v01: For the leader. To the tune of "The Lily of the Testimony." A Miktam of David, for instruction
v01: when he fought Aram Naharaim and Aram Zobah, and Joab returned and struck down twelve thousand Edomites in the Valley of Salt.
</p>

<p>
v02: O God, you have rejected us, broken through us; you were angry—restore us.
v03: You have made the earth tremble; you have split it open. Heal its fractures, for it is shaking.
</p>

<p>
v04: You have shown your people harsh things; you have given us wine to drink that makes us stagger.
v05: You have set up a banner for those who fear you, to be lifted up because of truth. Selah.
</p>

<p>
v06: That your beloved ones may be delivered, save with your right hand, and answer us.
</p>

<p>
v07: God has spoken in his holiness: "I will exult, I will divide Shechem, and measure out the Valley of Succoth.
v08: Gilead is mine, and Manasseh is mine; Ephraim is my helmet, Judah my scepter.
v09: Moab is my washbasin; upon Edom I throw my shoe; over Philistia I shout in triumph."
</p>

<p>
v10: Who will bring me into the fortified city? Who will lead me to Edom?
v11: Have you not, O God, cast us off? And you do not go out with our armies, O God?
</p>

<p>
v12: Grant us help against the foe, for human deliverance is worthless.
v13: In God we shall do valiantly; it is he who will tread down our foes.
</p>

## Notes

- **v01**: The title "The Lily of the Testimony" might refer to a musical or poetic style.
- **v05**: "Selah" likely indicates a pause for reflection or musical interlude.
- **v07-08**: The geographic territories mentioned (Shechem, Succoth, Gilead, Manasseh, Ephraim, Judah, Moab, Edom, Philistia) highlight the extent of God's sovereign claim.

## Vocabulary

- **v02 זָנַח (za.nach)**: "Rejected" suggests a temporary distancing from God as a reaction to Israel's condition.
- **v05 יַ֫יִן תַּרְעֵלָה (ya.yin tar.e.lah)**: "Wine to stagger" symbolizes the overwhelming experience of divine judgment.
- **v10 מוֹאָב סִ֬יר רַחְצִ֗י (mo.av sir ra.chats)**: "Moab is my washbasin" portrays Moab as a servant or subjugated territory.
