## Send Out Your Light and Truth

<p>
v01: Vindicate me, O God,
and contend my case
against an unfaithful nation;
from a deceitful and unjust person
rescue me.

v02: For you are God, my stronghold.
Why have you rejected me?
Why do I go mourning
under the oppression of the enemy?

v03: Send out your light and your truth;
let them lead me.
Let them bring me
to your holy mountain
and to your dwelling places.
</p>

## I Will Come to the Altar of God

<p>
v04: Then I will come
to the altar of God,
to God, my exceeding joy;
and I will praise you with the lyre,
O God, my God.
</p>

## Hope in God

<p>
v05: Why are you bowed down, my soul,
and why are you in turmoil within me?
Hope in God,
for I shall again praise God,
the saving help of my face
and my God.
</p>

## Notes

v01: “Vindicate me” — The Hebrew verb is the ordinary verb for judging. In context the psalmist asks God to judge the case and establish what is right, so “vindicate” conveys the direction of the appeal without eliminating its judicial character.

v01: “an unfaithful nation” — The Hebrew describes a nation lacking faithfulness or covenant loyalty. The expression is broader than a claim about ethnicity or political identity.

v03: “your light and your truth” — Light and truth are personified as guides capable of leading the psalmist back into God’s presence.

v03: “dwelling places” — The Hebrew is plural. The translation retains the plural rather than reducing it to a single sanctuary reference.

v04: “my exceeding joy” — The Hebrew intensifies the language of joy, literally approaching the sense of “the joy of my rejoicing.”

v05: Psalm 43 has no superscription and repeats the refrain found in Psalm 42. The close literary relationship is preserved without collapsing the two canonical psalms into a single chapter.

## Vocabulary

v01: שָׁפַט (shafat)
“To judge.” To render judgment or establish what is right; here expressed as an appeal for vindication.

v01: רִיב (riv)
“To contend,” “plead,” or “conduct a case.” Language drawn from dispute or legal advocacy.

v03: אוֹר (or)
“Light.” Illumination that guides and makes the way visible.

v03: אֱמֶת (emet)
“Truth,” “faithfulness,” or “reliability.” That which is firm, dependable, and true.

v04: שִׂמְחָה (simchah)
“Joy” or “gladness.” Rejoicing associated here with approaching God in worship.

v05: יָחַל (yachal)
“To hope” or “wait expectantly.” The repeated command of Psalms 42–43 directs the distressed self toward God.
