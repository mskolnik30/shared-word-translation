v01: Of David.
<p>
v01: To you, Yahweh, I call;
my rock, do not be silent to me,  
lest, if you are silent to me,  
I become like those who go down to the pit.  
v02: Hear the voice of my pleas when I cry to you,
when I lift up my hands toward your holy sanctuary.  
v03: Do not drag me away with the wicked
and with the workers of iniquity,  
those who speak peace to their neighbors  
while evil is in their hearts.  
v04: Give them according to their deeds
and according to the evil of their acts;  
give them according to the work of their hands;  
return to them their recompense.  
v05: Because they do not regard the deeds of Yahweh
or the work of his hands,  
he will tear them down and not build them up.  
</p>

<p>
v06: Blessed be Yahweh,
for he has heard the voice of my pleas.  
v07: Yahweh is my strength and my shield;
my heart trusts in him, and I am helped.  
Therefore my heart exults,  
and with my song I will thank him.  
</p>

<p>
v08: Yahweh is their strength,
and the stronghold of salvation for his anointed.  
v09: Save your people and bless your inheritance;
shepherd them and carry them forever.  
</p>

## Notes

- **Yahweh**: The personal name of God, often rendered as "LORD" in English translations. It signifies the covenantal and relational character of God with his people.
- **Pit** (v1): A metaphor for the realm of the dead or a state of despair.
- **Sanctuary** (v2): Refers to the inner place of the temple, symbolizing God's presence and holiness.
- **Wicked and workers of iniquity** (v3): Evildoers who deceive others with their words while harboring malice in their hearts.
- **Tear them down and not build them up** (v5): A depiction of judgment and the finality of God's justice against those who disregard his works.

## Vocabulary

- **צור (tsur)** (v1): Often translated as "rock," symbolizing strength, stability, and refuge.
- **תַּחֲנוּן (taḥanun)** (v2, v6): Meaning "pleas" or "supplications," indicating earnest prayer for mercy or favor.
- **יִשׁוּעָה (yeshuah)** (v8): Translated as "salvation," conveying the concept of deliverance from danger or distress.
- **מָשִׁיח (mashiach)** (v8): Translated as "anointed," referring to a king or priest consecrated for a divine purpose, often associated with the coming of a messianic figure.
