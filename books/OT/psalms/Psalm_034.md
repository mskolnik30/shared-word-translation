<p>
v01:  
Of David, when he changed his behavior before Abimelech, who drove him away, and he went.  
</p>

<p>
v02:  
I will bless the LORD at all times;  
his praise will continually be in my mouth.  
v03:  
My whole being will boast in the LORD;  
the humble will hear and rejoice.  
v04:  
Magnify the LORD with me,  
and let us exalt his name together.  
</p>

<p>
v05:  
I sought the LORD, and he answered me;  
he delivered me from all my fears.  
v06:  
They looked to him and were radiant,  
and their faces will not be ashamed.  
v07:  
This poor one cried, and the LORD heard;  
he saved him from all his troubles.  
v08:  
The angel of the LORD encamps around those who fear him,  
and rescues them.  
</p>

<p>
v09:  
Taste and see that the LORD is good;  
blessed is the one who takes refuge in him.  
v10:  
Fear the LORD, you his holy ones,  
for those who fear him have no lack.  
v11:  
Young lions suffer want and hunger,  
but those who seek the LORD lack no good thing.  
</p>

<p>
v12:  
Come, children, listen to me;  
I will teach you the fear of the LORD.  
v13:  
Who is the person who desires life,  
who loves days in order to see good?  
v14:  
Guard your tongue from evil  
and your lips from speaking deceit.  
v15:  
Turn away from evil and do good;  
seek peace and pursue it.  
</p>

<p>
v16:  
The eyes of the LORD are toward the righteous,  
and his ears toward their cry.  
v17:  
The face of the LORD is against those who do evil,  
to cut off the memory of them from the earth.  
v18:  
They cry out, and the LORD hears;  
from all their troubles he delivers them.  
v19:  
The LORD is near to the brokenhearted,  
and he saves those crushed in spirit.  
</p>

<p>
v20:  
Many are the troubles of the righteous,  
but the LORD delivers him from them all.  
v21:  
He guards all his bones;  
not one of them is broken.  
v22:  
Evil will put the wicked to death,  
and those who hate the righteous will be held guilty.  
v23:  
The LORD redeems the life of his servants,  
and none of those who take refuge in him will be held guilty.  
</p>

## Notes

v1: The superscription connects this psalm with David’s escape from a Philistine ruler. The narrative in 1 Samuel 21 names that ruler Achish; “Abimelech” may be a royal title, or the heading may preserve a different tradition.  

v6: “They looked to him” has no explicit subject in Hebrew. It may refer broadly to those who, like the psalmist, turn to the LORD in need.  

v8: “The angel of the LORD” presents the LORD’s protection in personal, encircling imagery. The verse does not explain the precise identity of this messenger.  

v20–21: The promise of deliverance does not deny the righteous person’s many troubles. The language of unbroken bones intensifies the claim of God’s preserving care.  

v22–23: The same Hebrew verb translated “will be held guilty” closes both verses. Evil brings guilt upon the wicked, while those who take refuge in the LORD are not held guilty.  

## Vocabulary

v5: **דָּרַשׁ — *darash***: “I sought.” Here seeking the LORD is an active turning toward God for answer and deliverance.  

v8: **חָנָה — *chanah***: “encamps.” The verb evokes a military camp set around those who fear the LORD, portraying protective presence.  

v9: **חָסָה — *chasah***: “takes refuge.” This recurring psalm-language describes entrusting oneself to the LORD for shelter and safety.  

v12: **יִרְאָה — *yirah***: “fear.” The fear of the LORD is taught as a life-shaping reverence expressed through speech, conduct, and the pursuit of peace.  

v19: **דַּכָּא — *dakka***: “crushed.” Paired with the brokenhearted, it names those inwardly crushed or brought low; the LORD is near to them.
