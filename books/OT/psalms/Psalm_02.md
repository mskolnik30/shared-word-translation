<p>
v01: Why do the nations rage
    and the peoples plot in vain?
v02: The kings of the earth set themselves,
    and the rulers take counsel together,
    against Yahweh and against his Anointed, saying,
v03: "Let us break their bonds apart
    and cast away their cords from us."
</p>

<p>
v04: He who sits in the heavens laughs;
    the Lord holds them in derision.
v05: Then he will speak to them in his anger,
    and terrify them in his wrath, saying,
v06: "As for me, I have set my king
    on Zion, my holy hill."
</p>

<p>
v07: I will tell of the decree:
    Yahweh said to me,
    "You are my son;
    today I have begotten you.
v08: Ask of me, and I will make the nations your inheritance,
    and the ends of the earth your possession.
v09: You shall break them with a rod of iron
    and dash them in pieces like a potter's vessel."
</p>

<p>
v10: Now therefore, O kings, be wise;
    be warned, O rulers of the earth.
v11: Serve Yahweh with fear,
    and rejoice with trembling.
v12: Kiss the son, lest he be angry and you perish in the way,
    for his wrath is quickly kindled.
    Blessed are all who take refuge in him.
</p>

## Notes

1. **v2**: "Anointed" - The Hebrew term "Mashiach" refers to one set apart by God, typically a king or priest, and has been traditionally understood as pointing to the Messiah.
2. **v4**: "He who sits in the heavens laughs" - This portrays God's supreme position and unassailable authority in the face of human rebellion, offering a contrast between divine power and human impotence.
3. **v7**: "You are my son; today I have begotten you" - This phrase signifies a special relationship and designation, often understood in both royal and messianic contexts, ultimately echoing in New Testament Christology.
4. **v12**: "Kiss the son" - This act of homage or submission suggests loyalty and allegiance, underscoring the importance of acknowledging divine authority.

## Vocabulary

- **v2**: מָשִׁיחַ (ma.shi.ach) - "Anointed": Used for one consecrated as king or priest, anticipating Messiah, who embodies ultimate divine kingship.
- **v4**: שָׂחַק (sa.chaq) - "Laughs": Represents divine derision, capturing the futility of human schemes against God's sovereign plan.
- **v7**: יָלַד (ya.lad) - "Begotten": Implies a special creation or designation day, often related to covenantal promises or divine adoption in a royal context.
