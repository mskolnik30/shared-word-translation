v01: For the leader. Do not destroy. A Miktam of David, when he fled from Saul, into the cave.

<p>
v02: Be gracious to me, O God, be gracious to me,
    for in you my soul takes refuge;
in the shadow of your wings I will take refuge,
    until destruction passes by.
v03: I call to God Most High,
    to God who fulfills his purpose for me.
v04: He will send from heaven and save me;
    he will put to shame those who trample on me. Selah
God will send forth his steadfast love and his faithfulness.
</p>

<p>
v05: My soul is in the midst of lions;
    I lie down among those who breathe out fire—
people whose teeth are spears and arrows,
    whose tongues are sharp swords.
</p>

v06: Be exalted, O God, above the heavens;
    let your glory be over all the earth.

<p>
v07: They set a net for my steps;
    my soul was bowed down.
They dug a pit in my path,
    but they have fallen into it themselves. Selah
</p>

<p>
v08: My heart is steadfast, O God,
    my heart is steadfast.
I will sing and make melody.
v09: Awake, my glory!
    Awake, O harp and lyre!
    I will awake the dawn.
v10: I will thank you, Lord, among the peoples;
    I will sing praises to you among the nations.
v11: For your steadfast love is great to the heavens,
    and your faithfulness to the clouds.
</p>

v12: Be exalted, O God, above the heavens;
    let your glory be over all the earth.

## Notes

**v01 "Do not destroy"**: This phrase might refer to a tune or an instruction, linking to other psalms with a similar heading (Psalms 58, 59, and 75).

**v02 "shadow of your wings"**: This metaphor evokes a sense of protection and care, akin to a bird sheltering its young.

**v03 "Most High"**: This title emphasizes the supreme sovereignty of God over all creation.

**v06, 12 "Be exalted, O God"**: These refrains call for God's glory to be recognized both in the heavens and on earth, framing the psalm with a desire for divine majesty to be revealed.

**v05 "lions...fire...spears...swords"**: This vivid imagery portrays the psalmist's adversaries as fierce and destructive, yet simultaneously highlights the psalmist's vulnerability.

## Vocabulary

**v01 מִכְתָּם (mikh.tam)**: Likely a literary or musical term, specific meaning uncertain. Used in this inscription and in similar Psalms (16, 58-60) often during times of distress.

**v04 יִשְׁלַח (sha.lach)**: "He will send." In this context, it conveys God's active deliverance from threat, aligned with His attributes of love and truth.
