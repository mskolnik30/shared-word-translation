<p>
v01:  
A psalm. A song for the dedication of the house. Of David.  
</p>

<p>
v02:  
I will exalt you, LORD,  
for you have drawn me up  
and have not made my enemies rejoice over me.  
v03:  
LORD my God,  
I cried out to you,  
and you healed me.  
v04:  
LORD, you brought up my life from Sheol;  
you kept me alive from going down to the Pit.  
</p>

<p>
v05:  
Sing praises to the LORD, you faithful ones,  
and give thanks at the remembrance of his holiness.  
v06:  
For his anger is for a moment;  
his favor is for a lifetime.  
Weeping may lodge for the evening,  
but rejoicing comes in the morning.  
</p>

<p>
v07:  
As for me, I said in my security,  
“I will never be shaken.”  
v08:  
LORD, by your favor  
you made my mountain stand strong.  
You hid your face;  
I was dismayed.  
</p>

<p>
v09:  
To you, LORD, I called;  
to the Lord I pleaded for grace:  
v10:  
“What gain is there in my blood,  
in my going down to the Pit?  
Will dust give you thanks?  
Will it declare your faithfulness?  
v11:  
Hear, LORD, and be gracious to me;  
LORD, be my helper.”  
</p>

<p>
v12:  
You turned my mourning into dancing for me;  
you loosened my sackcloth  
and clothed me with joy,  
v13:  
so that glory may sing praise to you and not be silent.  
LORD my God,  
I will give thanks to you forever.  
</p>

## Notes

v1: The superscription links this psalm with the dedication of “the house.” The phrase may refer to a sanctuary or royal house; the psalm itself does not identify the occasion.  

v4: “Sheol” and “the Pit” evoke the realm of death and the grave. The speaker describes deliverance as restoration from the edge of death.  

v6: The briefness of anger and the endurance of favor are set beside the movement from nighttime weeping to morning rejoicing. The verse does not deny suffering; it places it within a larger confession of God’s restoring favor.  

v8: “My mountain” is an image of established security and strength. When God hides his face, that apparent stability is exposed as dependent on God’s favor.  

v10: The questions appeal to the loss of praise that death would bring. They express the psalmist’s urgent plea for life, rather than offering a full account of life after death.  

v13: “Glory” may refer to the speaker’s honor, inner self, or whole being. Its open wording allows the final praise to gather the psalmist’s restored life into thanksgiving.  

## Vocabulary

v4: **שְׁאוֹל — *she’ol***: Sheol is the realm of the dead, named here with “the Pit” to portray the speaker’s nearness to death and God’s rescue.  

v5: **חָסִיד — *ḥasid***: “Faithful ones” names those bound to the LORD in covenant loyalty; they are summoned to join the individual’s thanksgiving.  

v6: **רָצוֹן — *ratson***: “Favor” denotes God’s goodwill or acceptance. In this verse it stands in deliberate contrast to divine anger.  

v8: **פָּנִים — *panim***: Literally “face,” God’s face expresses attentive, life-giving presence. Its concealment leaves the speaker dismayed.  

v12: **מָחוֹל — *maḥol***: “Dancing” is the embodied counterpart to mourning, expressing the reversal God has brought about.
