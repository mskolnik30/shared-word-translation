v01: For the director, with stringed instruments. A psalm of David.

v02: 
<p>
When I call, answer me,  
O God of my righteousness.  
You have given me relief when I was in distress.  
Be gracious to me and hear my prayer.  
</p>  
v03: 
<p>
You people, how long will my honor be turned into shame?  
How long will you love vain words and seek after lies?    
Selah  
</p>  
v04: 
<p>
Know that the Lord has set apart the faithful for himself;  
the Lord hears when I call to him.  
</p>  
v05:
<p>
Be angry, but do not sin;  
ponder in your own heart upon your bed and be still.  
Selah  
</p>  
v06: 
<p>
Offer the sacrifices of righteousness,  
and put your trust in the Lord.  
</p>  
v07: 
<p>
Many say, "Who will show us any good?"  
Lift up the light of your face upon us, O Lord.  
</p>  
v08: 
<p>
You have put gladness in my heart,  
more than when their grain and new wine abound.  
</p>  
v09: 
<p>
In peace I will both lie down and sleep;  
for you alone, O Lord, make me dwell in safety.  
</p>  

## Notes

**4:1** "God of my righteousness" reflects a relationship in which God justifies or vindicates the psalmist's moral standing or cause, indicating a trust in divine justice and mercy.

**4:2** "Relief when I was in distress" may recall past experiences of divine deliverance, encouraging present confidence.

**4:4** "Be angry, but do not sin" acknowledges the reality and potential risk of anger, urging reflection and restraint.

**4:6** "Sacrifices of righteousness" implies offerings made with sincere hearts and just intent, beyond mere ritualistic practice.

**4:9** "In peace I will both lie down and sleep" conveys a deep sense of security and trust in God's protection, contrasting external turmoil.

## Vocabulary

**4:1** אֱלֹהִים (e.lo.him) — "God" is used here as the deity invoked for justice and protection, representing the divine presence actively involved in the life of the psalmist.

**4:2** צֶדֶק (tse.deq) — often translated as "righteousness," here reflects adherence to justice, implying moral integrity upheld by divine intervention.

**4:4** חָסִיד (cha.sid) — "faithful" or "devout one," suggesting those who are dedicated to God and whom God favors. Such set-apart faithfulness is highlighted as a community identity. 

**4:5** רָגַז (ra.gaz) — translated as "be angry," this term captures emotional agitation and its potential moral implications, demanding careful response.
