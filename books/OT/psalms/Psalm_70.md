v01: For the director. Of David. To bring remembrance.

v02: 
<p> 
Elohim, to deliver me,  
Yahweh, to my help, hasten!
</p>

v03: 
<p> 
Let them be put to shame and humiliated,  
those who seek my life.  
Let them turn back,  
and be dishonored, those who delight in my hurt.
</p>

v04: 
<p> 
Let them turn back because of their shame  
who say, "Aha! Aha!"
</p>

v05: 
<p> 
Let all who seek you rejoice  
and be glad in you.  
Let those who love your salvation say continually,  
"Great is Elohim!"
</p>

v06: 
<p> 
But I am poor and needy;  
Elohim, hasten to me.  
My help and my deliverer are you;  
Yahweh, do not delay.
</p>

## Notes

- Psalm 70, a brief psalm of distress and urgent appeal, highlights the speaker's dependence on God's immediate action.
- v1: "To bring remembrance" may indicate a liturgical function or call to God's attention.
- v2: "Elohim" and "Yahweh" are invoked in parallel for deliverance, emphasizing covenantal relationship.
- v3-4: Repetition with variations invites both shame upon adversaries and a witness to their failure.
- v5: Corporate joy contrasts individual distress, broadening the scope of salvation's praise.
- v6: The speaker is depicted as "poor and needy," underscoring vulnerability and reliance on God alone.

## Vocabulary

- v1: לְהַזְכִּיר (le'hazkir) - "To bring remembrance" likely designates the psalm as a reminder or memorial, perhaps for God or the worshipping community.
- v2: אֱלֹהִים (Elohim) - A title for God, used here with immediacy and relational expectation in covenant context.
- v2: יְהוָה (Yahweh) - The personal, covenant name of God, signifying intimacy and loyalty in the plea.
