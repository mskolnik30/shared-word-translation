<p>
v01: Of David. A psalm.  
The earth is the Lord's and everything in it,  
  the world and those who dwell in it.
v02: For he founded it upon the seas  
  and established it upon the rivers.
v03: Who shall ascend the hill of the Lord?  
  And who shall stand in his holy place?
v04: Those with clean hands and pure hearts,  
  who do not lift up their soul to what is false  
  and do not swear deceitfully.
v05: They will receive blessing from the Lord  
  and righteousness from the God of their salvation.
v06: Such is the generation of those who seek him,  
  who seek your face, Jacob. Selah.
</p>
<p>
v07: Lift up your heads, O gates;  
  and be lifted up, O ancient doors,  
  that the King of glory may come in.
v08: Who is this King of glory?  
  The Lord, strong and mighty,  
  the Lord, mighty in battle.
v09: Lift up your heads, O gates;  
  and lift them up, O ancient doors,  
  that the King of glory may come in.
v10: Who is he, this King of glory?  
  The Lord of hosts,  
  he is the King of glory. Selah.
</p>

## Notes

- **v01-02**: The psalm begins by affirming God's sovereign ownership over the earth and its inhabitants, grounding this claim in His creative act of establishing the world.
- **v03-04**: A rhetorical question is posed about who can approach God's presence, emphasizing the moral and spiritual qualifications required, such as purity and sincerity.
- **v06**: This verse describes the people who seek God, and notably uses "Jacob," highlighting a covenantal and ancestral identity.
- **v07-10**: A liturgical entry involving the repetition of a call-and-response format emphasizes the majesty and grandeur of the "King of glory," identified as the Lord of hosts, suggesting a procession or ritual welcoming God's presence.

## Vocabulary

- **v01 אָרֶץ (erets)**: Typically refers to the land or earth. Here, it signifies the entirety of creation under God’s dominion.
- **v07 מֶלֶךְ כָּבוֹד (melek kavod)**: "King of glory" underscores God's majesty, honor, and divine kingship, particularly celebrated in liturgical contexts.
