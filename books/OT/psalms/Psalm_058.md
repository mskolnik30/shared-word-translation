<p>
v01: For the director. <br>  
“Do not destroy.” Of David. A Miktam.  
</p>
v02: Indeed, are you silent, you gods? <br>  
Do you judge fairly, you children of humankind? <br>  
v03: Even from the heart you work injustice; <br>  
your hands weigh out violence on the earth.  
v04: The wicked go astray from the womb; <br>  
they are liars from birth.  
v05: Their venom is like the venom of a snake; <br>  
like a deaf adder that closes its ears,  
v06: unable to hear the sound of charmers, <br>  
of a skilled enchanter.  
v07: O God, break the teeth in their mouths; <br>  
tear out the fangs of the young lions, O Lord!  
v08: Let them vanish like water that runs away; <br>  
when he aims his arrows, let them be cut off.  
v09: Like a snail that melts as it moves, <br>  
like a stillborn child who never sees the sun,  
v10: before your pots can feel the heat of thorns— <br>  
whether green or burning—he will sweep them away.  
v11: The righteous will be glad when they behold vengeance; <br>  
they will wash their feet in the blood of the wicked.  
v12: And people will say, "Surely there is a reward for the righteous; <br>  
surely there is a God who judges on earth."  
```  

## Notes

v1: Miktam: The term "Miktam" likely denotes a particular type of psalm or musical setting. Its exact meaning is uncertain, but it appears in several psalm titles associated with David.  

v10: Green or burning: The imagery here suggests swift and inevitable judgment, indicating that God's justice can come regardless of the readiness or state of the circumstances (whether the thorns are green or dried and kindling).  

v12: Reward for the righteous: This verse underscores a central theological affirmation of the psalm: that there is a divine moral order and that righteousness will ultimately be vindicated by God.  

## Vocabulary

v2: אֵ֣לֶם (e.lem)  
Here translated as “silent,” suggesting inactivity or failure to act, in contrast with the expectation of just verdicts from those expected to judge righteously.  

v11: צַ֭דִּיק (tsad.diq)  
"The righteous" in this passage refers to those who align with God’s ways and justice, contrasting with the wicked whose actions bring divine retribution.  

v12: אֱ֝לֹהִ֗ים (e.lo.him)  
Emphasizes God’s role as ultimate judge, reinforcing divine sovereignty over earthly justice.
