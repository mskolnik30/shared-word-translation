## God Is Our Refuge

<p>
v01: God is our refuge and strength,
  a help in troubles, very near at hand.

v02: Therefore we will not fear though the earth gives way,
  though the mountains topple into the heart of the seas;

v03: though its waters roar and foam,
  though the mountains tremble at its surging. *Selah*
</p>
## A River for the City of God

<p>
v04: There is a river whose streams gladden the city of God,
  the holy dwelling of the Most High.

v05: God is in her midst; she will not be shaken;
  God will help her at the break of morning.

v06: The nations were in uproar, the kingdoms tottered;
  he lifted his voice—the earth melts.

v07: The LORD of Hosts is with us;
  the God of Jacob is our stronghold. *Selah*
</p>
## Come and See

<p>
v08: Come, behold the works of the LORD,
  who has set desolations on the earth—

v09: making wars cease to the end of the earth,
  breaking the bow and shattering the spear,
  burning the chariots with fire.

v10: “Be still, and know that I am God;
  I will be exalted among the nations,
  I will be exalted in the earth.”

v11: The LORD of Hosts is with us;
  the God of Jacob is our stronghold. *Selah*
</p>

## Notes

v01: Three nouns are stacked—refuge (maḥseh), strength (ʿōz), help (ʿezrâ)—and the closing phrase says God is "very" found, a help easily met. The opening is not measured; it is abundant.

v02–03: The first stanza imagines the unmaking of the world: earth giving way, mountains falling into the sea, waters in chaos. These are the elements that the ancient Near Eastern imagination feared most. The psalm names them and refuses to fear them.

v04: A river appears in the midst of an inland city. Jerusalem has no river. The river is theological geography: a reference to Eden, to Ezekiel's vision, to the streams that the LORD's presence makes flow. Where God dwells, water comes.

v05: "At the break of morning" (liphnôt bōqer) — help comes when the night ends. The psalm does not deny the night.

v06: The verbs of v.6 are charged: nations "in uproar" (the same root as the waters' "roar" in v.3), kingdoms "totter" (the same verb as the mountains in v.2). The psalm is mapping cosmic upheaval onto political upheaval—and answering both with one voice.

v07, v11: The refrain. The LORD of hosts (yhwh ṣəvāʾôt) is a military title; the God of Jacob is a covenant title. Both are ours.

v08–09: "Behold the works of the LORD" reverses the gaze. The works named are not creation but the unmaking of weapons. Bows broken, spears shattered, chariots burned.

v10: "Be still" (harpû) is more than quiet. The verb means "let go," "desist," "drop your hands." It is addressed first, in context, to the raging nations, but the ages have heard it as addressed to all who tremble. Both hearings stand. The knowing that follows is participatory: stop striving long enough to recognize who God is.

v11: The refrain returns identical, but it now arrives after the silencing. What the song affirmed in cosmic terms is now the truth that the still soul confesses.

## Vocabulary

v01: מַחֲסֶה (maḥseh) — Refuge. A place to which one runs and is received. The word recurs across the Psalter as an image of the LORD himself.

v01: עֹז (ʿōz) — Strength. Not raw power, but firm steadiness; the strength of one who does not fall.

v01: עֶזְרָה (ʿezrâ) — Help. Practical, embodied aid—not abstract sympathy.

v04: פֶּלֶג (peleg) — A stream, a watercourse. The plural names a river of many channels, a generosity of waters.

v05: בַּל־תִּמּוֹט (bal-timmôṭ) — "Will not be shaken." The very verb used of the mountains in v.2 is now denied of the city. What threatens the world cannot threaten her.

v07: יהוה צְבָאוֹת (yhwh ṣəvāʾôt) — LORD of hosts. The title evokes the armies of heaven; in this psalm the warrior is also the disarmer.

v07: מִשְׂגָּב (miśgāv) — Stronghold, high refuge. A high place of safety, lifted out of the reach of harm.

v10: רָפָה (rāphâ) — To slacken, to let go, to be still. The Hiphil imperative here means "drop your hands"—both the warrior's hands and the anxious hands.

v10: יָדַע (yādaʿ) — To know, to recognize. Not to inform oneself, but to acknowledge.
