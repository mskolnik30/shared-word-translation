<p>
v01: For the director; to the tune of "Doe of the Dawn." A psalm of David.
</p>

<p>
v02: My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?  
    Far from my salvation, the words of my groaning.
</p>
<p>
v03: My God, I cry by day, and you do not answer;  
    and by night, yet there is no rest for me.
</p>
<p>
v04: Yet you are holy,  
    enthroned on the praises of Israel.
</p>
<p>
v05: In you our ancestors trusted;  
    they trusted and you rescued them.
</p>
<p>
v06: To you they cried and were delivered;  
    in you they trusted and were not ashamed.
</p>

<p>
v07: But I am a worm and not a man,  
    scorned by humanity and despised by the people.
</p>
<p>
v08: All who see me mock me;  
    they hurl insults, shaking their heads:
</p>
<p>
v09: "He trusts in the Lord; let him deliver him;  
    let him rescue him, for he delights in him."
</p>

<p>
v10: Yet you are the one who brought me from the womb;  
    you made me secure on my mother's breasts.
</p>
<p>
v11: On you I was cast from the womb;  
    from my mother's belly you have been my God.
</p>
<p>
v12: Do not be far from me, for trouble is near;  
    for there is none to help.
</p>

<p>
v13: Many bulls encompass me;  
    mighty bulls of Bashan surround me.
</p>
<p>
v14: They open wide their mouths at me,  
    like a ravenous and roaring lion.
</p>

<p>
v15: I am poured out like water,  
    and all my bones are out of joint;  
    my heart is like wax; it is melted within my chest.
</p>
<p>
v16: My strength is dried up like a potsherd,  
    and my tongue sticks to my jaws;  
    you lay me in the dust of death.
</p>

<p>
v17: For dogs surround me;  
    a company of evildoers encircles me;  
    like a lion, my hands and feet.
</p>
<p>
v18: I can count all my bones—  
    they stare and gloat over me;  
</p>
<p>
v19: they divide my garments among them,  
    and for my clothing they cast lots.
</p>

<p>
v20: But you, Lord, do not be far off!  
    My strength, come quickly to my aid!
</p>
<p>
v21: Deliver my soul from the sword,  
    my only life from the power of the dog.
</p>
<p>
v22: Save me from the mouth of the lion;  
    from the horns of the wild oxen!  
    You have answered me.
</p>

<p>
v23: I will declare your name to my brothers;  
    in the midst of the congregation I will praise you.
</p>
<p>
v24: You who fear the Lord, praise him!  
    All you offspring of Jacob, glorify him;  
    and stand in awe of him, all you offspring of Israel!
</p>

<p>
v25: For he has not despised or abhorred  
    the affliction of the afflicted,  
    and he has not hidden his face from him,  
    but has heard, when he cried to him.
</p>
<p>
v26: From you comes my praise in the great congregation;  
    my vows I will perform before those who fear him.
</p>

<p>
v27: The afflicted shall eat and be satisfied;  
    those who seek the Lord shall praise him!  
    May your hearts live forever!
</p>
<p>
v28: All the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the Lord,  
    and all the families of the nations will bow down before you,
</p>
<p>
v29: for the kingdom is the Lord's,  
    and he rules over the nations.
</p>

<p>
v30: All the prosperous of the earth shall eat and worship;  
    before him shall bow all who go down to the dust,  
    even the one who could not keep himself alive.
</p>
<p>
v31: Posterity will serve him;  
    it will be told of the Lord to the coming generation,
</p>
<p>
v32: they will come and proclaim his righteousness  
    to a people yet unborn, that he has done it.
</p>

## Notes

v2: "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" - This lament echoes through the ages as an expression of profound suffering and abandonment. The opening cry sets the tone for the psalm, inviting readers into a deep engagement with the anguish and subsequent deliverance.

v4: "Yet you are holy, enthroned on the praises of Israel." - The psalmist shifts attention from despair to a foundational trust in God's holiness and authority, suggesting that despite current suffering, God's nature and presence remains unshakeable.

v17: "like a lion, my hands and feet." - The Hebrew here is complex and debated, leading to varying translations; the imagery evokes vulnerability to fierce and predatory threats, emphasizing the depth of the psalmist's distress.

## Vocabulary

v2: אֵלִי (el) - "My God" - A term of intimate relationship with the divine, heightening the poignancy of the psalmist's sense of abandonment.

v21: יְהוָה (YHWH) - "Lord" - The covenant name of God, reflecting deep personal commitment and relationship.
