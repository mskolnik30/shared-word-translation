<p>
v01:  
For the director. Of David, the servant of the LORD.  
</p>

<p>
v02:  
An utterance of transgression to the wicked is within my heart:  
there is no fear of God before his eyes.  
v03:  
For in his own eyes he flatters himself,  
too much to find his guilt and hate it.  
v04:  
The words of his mouth are trouble and deceit;  
he has ceased to act wisely, to do good.  
v05:  
He plans trouble on his bed;  
he sets himself on a way that is not good;  
evil he does not reject.  
</p>

<p>
v06:  
LORD, your steadfast love is in the heavens,  
your faithfulness reaches to the clouds.  
v07:  
Your righteousness is like the mountains of God;  
your judgments are a great deep.  
Human and beast you save, LORD.  
v08:  
How precious is your steadfast love, O God!  
The children of humankind take refuge  
in the shadow of your wings.  
v09:  
They are filled from the abundance of your house,  
and you give them drink from the river of your delights.  
</p>

<p>
v10:  
For with you is the fountain of life;  
in your light we see light.  
v11:  
Draw out your steadfast love to those who know you,  
and your righteousness to the upright in heart.  
v12:  
Do not let the foot of pride come against me;  
do not let the hand of the wicked drive me away.  
v13:  
There the workers of trouble have fallen;  
they are thrust down and cannot rise.  
</p>

## Notes

v2: The Hebrew reads “within my heart,” though the surrounding references are to the wicked person. The line may present the psalmist’s inward discernment of transgression’s message, while its grammar also creates a striking closeness between the speaker and the evil being described.  

v3: The wording is compressed and ambiguous. The verse portrays self-flattery as preventing the wicked from discovering and hating their own guilt.  

v6–7: The psalm answers the confined world of the wicked with God’s immeasurable steadfast love, faithfulness, righteousness, and judgments. God’s judgments are compared not only to mountains but also to the unfathomable deep.  

v8–9: The refuge of God’s wings joins temple-like abundance, nourishment, and flowing water. The imagery describes creaturely safety and joy without limiting them to a single setting.  

v12–13: The prayer closes with both a plea for protection and a declaration of the evildoers’ downfall. “There” points dramatically to the place of their collapse without specifying it.  

## Vocabulary

v2: **פֶּשַׁע — *peshaʿ***: “transgression” is personified as giving an utterance or oracle. Its voice stands in direct contrast to the fear of God.  

v6: **חֶסֶד — *ḥesed***: “steadfast love” names God’s faithful, covenantal goodness. In this psalm it is expansive, protective, and life-giving.  

v7: **מִשְׁפָּט — *mishpat***: “judgments” can refer to God’s acts of justice and governing decisions. Their comparison to the great deep emphasizes both their depth and their vastness.  

v9: **עֵדֶן — *ʿeden***: “delights” evokes luxuriant pleasure and abundance. The plural form intensifies the river imagery.  

v10: **מְקוֹר חַיִּים — *meqor ḥayyim***: “fountain of life” presents life as flowing from God. The following line links this life-giving source with divine light.
