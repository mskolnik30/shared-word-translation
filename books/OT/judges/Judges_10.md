## Tola and Jair

<p>
v01: After Abimelech, Tola son of Puah, son of Dodo, a man of Issachar, arose to save Israel. He lived at Shamir in the hill country of Ephraim.

v02: He judged Israel twenty-three years. Then he died and was buried at Shamir.

v03: After him Jair the Gileadite arose and judged Israel twenty-two years.

v04: He had thirty sons who rode on thirty donkeys, and they had thirty towns, called Havvoth-Jair to this day, which are in the land of Gilead.

v05: Jair died and was buried at Kamon.
</p>

## Israel Again Serves Other Gods

<p>
v06: The children of Israel again did evil in the eyes of the LORD and served the Baals, the Ashtaroth, the gods of Aram, the gods of Sidon, the gods of Moab, the gods of the children of Ammon, and the gods of the Philistines. They abandoned the LORD and did not serve him.

v07: The anger of the LORD burned against Israel, and he sold them into the hand of the Philistines and into the hand of the children of Ammon.

v08: They crushed and oppressed the children of Israel that year. For eighteen years they oppressed all the children of Israel who were beyond the Jordan in the land of the Amorites, which is in Gilead.

v09: The children of Ammon crossed the Jordan to fight also against Judah, Benjamin, and the house of Ephraim, so Israel was greatly distressed.
</p>

## Israel Cries Out

<p>
v10: The children of Israel cried out to the LORD, saying, “We have sinned against you, for we have abandoned our God and served the Baals.”

v11: The LORD said to the children of Israel, “Did I not save you from Egypt, from the Amorites, from the children of Ammon, and from the Philistines?

v12: And when the Sidonians, Amalekites, and Maonites oppressed you and you cried out to me, I saved you from their hand.

v13: Yet you have abandoned me and served other gods. Therefore I will save you no more.

v14: Go and cry out to the gods whom you have chosen. Let them save you in the time of your distress.”
</p>

## Put Away the Foreign Gods

<p>
v15: The children of Israel said to the LORD, “We have sinned. Do to us whatever is good in your eyes; only please deliver us this day.”

v16: So they put away the foreign gods from among them and served the LORD, and his soul became impatient over the misery of Israel.

v17: Then the children of Ammon were gathered together and camped in Gilead. The children of Israel gathered together and camped at Mizpah.

v18: The people, the leaders of Gilead, said to one another, “Who is the man who will begin to fight against the children of Ammon? He shall become head over all the inhabitants of Gilead.”
</p>
## Notes
v01–05: Tola and Jair receive only brief notices, yet both are said to judge Israel. The quiet summaries contrast with the violent instability surrounding Abimelech and Jephthah.
v04: Jair’s thirty sons, donkeys, and towns communicate wealth and social standing. The Hebrew name *Havvoth-Jair* means “villages of Jair.”
v06: The list of gods is unusually broad, portraying Israel’s unfaithfulness as comprehensive rather than limited to a single cult.
v10–14: Israel’s confession initially receives a severe response. The LORD points to repeated deliverances followed by repeated abandonment.
v16: “his soul became impatient over the misery of Israel” — The Hebrew is strikingly anthropomorphic and literally describes the LORD’s *nefesh* becoming short or unable to endure Israel’s suffering. TSW preserves the emotional force.
v18: The chapter ends with a leadership vacuum. The question is not yet who the LORD will raise up, but who will begin the fight and thereby become head over Gilead.

##Vocabulary

v01: יָשַׁע (yasha)
“To save” or “deliver.” Used of Tola’s role in rescuing Israel.

v04: חַוֹּת יָעִיר (Havvot Ya'ir)
“Villages of Jair.” A regional name associated with Jair’s sons.

v07: מָכַר (makhar)
“To sell.” Used figuratively for the LORD handing Israel over to oppressors.

v08: רָצַץ (ratsats)
“To crush” or “oppress.” A forceful term for Ammonite oppression.

v10: חָטָא (chata)
“To sin.” Israel explicitly confesses wrongdoing against the LORD.

v16: קָצַר נֶפֶשׁ (qatsar nefesh)
“To become impatient in soul” or “unable to bear.” Used of the LORD’s response to Israel’s misery.

v18: רֹאשׁ (rosh)
“Head.” A leader or chief over the people.
