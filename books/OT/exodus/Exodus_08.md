## Notes

v5: **8:5** “Take the honor over me” renders an unusual expression that gives Pharaoh the choice of when Moses should pray. The wording may carry an edge of irony: Pharaoh is invited to name the time, while the LORD’s power—not Pharaoh’s—will be made known.  

v13: **8:13** The precise identity of the *kinnim* is uncertain. It may refer to gnats, lice, or another small biting insect. “Gnats” conveys the pervasive nuisance described here without claiming certainty.  

v17: **8:17** The Hebrew word translated “the swarm” does not identify the creatures. It may refer to a mixture of insects or to a particular kind of biting fly; the text emphasizes its overwhelming presence rather than its precise species.  

v19: **8:19** “A redemption” is the Hebrew wording. Some ancient versions understand a related word meaning “distinction” or “separation.” The received Hebrew text joins the protection of the LORD’s people with the language of deliverance.  

v22: **8:22** “What is an abomination to the Egyptians” may refer to animals that Egyptians regarded as unsuitable for sacrifice or sacred. Moses does not specify the offerings, but he stresses that worship of the LORD cannot be confined within Pharaoh’s terms.  

## Vocabulary

v8: 13 — כִּנִּים (*kinnim*)  
v13: Small insects that arise from the dust and cover humans and livestock. The term’s uncertain identification contributes to the plague’s unsettling, all-encompassing character.  

v8: 15 — אֶצְבַּע אֱלֹהִים (*’etsbaʿ ’elohim*)  
“The finger of God.” The magicians’ confession recognizes a power beyond their secret arts, even though Pharaoh does not yield.  

v8: 17 — עָרֹב (*ʿarov*)  
“Swarm.” The word leaves the plague’s exact nature open while portraying an invading mass that fills houses and ruins the land.  

v8: 19 — פְּדוּת (*peduth*)  
“Redemption” or “ransom.” Here the LORD establishes *peduth* between Israel and Egypt, marking the preservation of his people as an act of deliverance.
