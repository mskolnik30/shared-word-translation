## Notes

- **6:3** “By my name Yahweh I was not known to them” can mean that the ancestors had not come to know Yahweh through the full lived disclosure of this name. The verse does not necessarily deny that the name appears in earlier ancestral narratives; here the name is bound to God’s covenant faithfulness and coming deliverance.

- **6:6–8** The repeated “I will” forms a concentrated declaration of deliverance: Yahweh will bring out, deliver, redeem, take, be God to the people, bring them in, and give them the land. Liberation from Egypt is inseparable from covenant relationship with God.

- **6:9** “Their spirit was broken” renders a phrase literally involving “shortness of spirit.” Israel’s inability to listen is attributed not to indifference but to the crushing effects of harsh slavery.

- **6:12, 30** “Uncircumcised of lips” is Moses’ own image for an impediment or unfitness in speech. The text leaves open whether he means a physical difficulty, fear, lack of eloquence, or a broader sense of inadequacy.

- **6:14–27** The genealogy interrupts the confrontation with Pharaoh in order to identify Moses and Aaron within Israel, particularly within Levi’s clan. The narrative returns emphatically to them in verses 26–27: these are the very people commissioned to lead Israel out of Egypt.

- **6:26** “According to their companies” uses an organized, military image for Israel. The enslaved people are being portrayed as a people whom Yahweh will bring out in ordered divisions.

## Vocabulary

- **6:3 — אֵל שַׁדַּי (*El Shaddai*)**: “God Almighty.” This divine designation recalls God’s appearances to the ancestors and stands alongside the name Yahweh as the passage connects ancestral promise with present deliverance.

- **6:4 — בְּרִית (*berit*)**: “covenant.” Yahweh’s commitment to give the land is presented as an enduring bond with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, now remembered in Israel’s oppression.

- **6:6 — גָּאַל (*ga'al*)**: “redeem.” The verb evokes the action of a family protector who acts to recover or defend a relative. Yahweh claims Israel from slavery as their deliverer.

- **6:6 — זְרוֹעַ נְטוּיָה (*zeroa netuyah*)**: “outstretched arm.” This image expresses Yahweh’s active and powerful intervention against Egypt.

- **6:7 — עַם (*'am*)**: “people.” Yahweh’s promise, “I will take you for my people,” describes belonging and covenant identity, not merely release from forced labor.

- **6:8 — מוֹרָשָׁה (*morashah*)**: “possession.” The land is not simply a destination but a granted inheritance, given by Yahweh in fulfillment of the promise to the ancestors.

- **6:9 — קֹצֶר רוּחַ (*qotser ruach*)**: “shortness of spirit.” The phrase portrays the inward constriction and exhaustion produced by Israel’s oppression.

- **6:12 — עֲרַל שְׂפָתַיִם (*'aral sefatayim*)**: “uncircumcised of lips.” Moses uses the language of uncircumcision figuratively to describe lips not ready or able to speak effectively.

- **6:26 — צָבָא (*tsava'*)**: “company” or “division.” The term gives Israel an ordered, collective identity as Yahweh brings them out of Egypt.
