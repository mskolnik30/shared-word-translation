## Jethro Comes to Moses

<p>
v01: Jethro, priest of Midian, Moses’ father-in-law, heard all that God had done for Moses and for Israel his people, that the LORD had brought Israel out from Egypt.

v02: So Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law, took Zipporah, Moses’ wife, after Moses had sent her away,

v03: and her two sons. The name of one was Gershom, for Moses had said, “I have been a resident alien in a foreign land.”

v04: The name of the other was Eliezer, for he had said, “The God of my father was my help and rescued me from Pharaoh’s sword.”

v05: Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law, came with Moses’ sons and wife to Moses in the wilderness, where he was camped at the mountain of God.

v06: He said to Moses, “I, your father-in-law Jethro, am coming to you, and your wife and her two sons with her.”

v07: Moses went out to meet his father-in-law, bowed down, and kissed him. They asked each other about their well-being and came into the tent.
</p>

## Jethro Rejoices over the LORD’s Deliverance

<p>
v08: Moses told his father-in-law all that the LORD had done to Pharaoh and to Egypt for Israel’s sake, all the hardship that had found them on the way, and how the LORD had rescued them.

v09: Jethro rejoiced over all the good that the LORD had done for Israel, rescuing them from the hand of Egypt.

v10: Jethro said, “Blessed be the LORD, who rescued you from the hand of Egypt and from the hand of Pharaoh, who rescued the people from under the hand of Egypt.

v11: Now I know that the LORD is greater than all gods, for in the very thing in which they acted arrogantly, he was above them.”

v12: Then Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law, took a burnt offering and sacrifices for God. Aaron and all the elders of Israel came to eat bread with Moses’ father-in-law before God.
</p>

## Jethro Advises Moses

<p>
v13: The next day Moses sat to judge the people, and the people stood around Moses from morning until evening.

v14: When Moses’ father-in-law saw all that he was doing for the people, he said, “What is this thing you are doing for the people? Why do you sit alone while all the people stand around you from morning until evening?”

v15: Moses said to his father-in-law, “Because the people come to me to seek God.

v16: When they have a matter, it comes to me, and I judge between one person and another and make known the statutes of God and his instructions.”

v17: Moses’ father-in-law said to him, “The thing you are doing is not good.

v18: You will surely wear yourself out, both you and this people who are with you, for the thing is too heavy for you. You are not able to do it alone.
</p>

## Choose Capable People

<p>
v19: “Now listen to my voice; I will counsel you, and may God be with you. You be for the people before God, and you shall bring the matters to God.

v20: Teach them the statutes and instructions, and make known to them the way in which they shall walk and the work they shall do.

v21: But you shall seek out from all the people capable men who fear God, people of truth who hate unjust gain. Place such over them as leaders of thousands, leaders of hundreds, leaders of fifties, and leaders of tens.

v22: Let them judge the people at all times. Every great matter they shall bring to you, but every small matter they shall judge themselves. Make it lighter for yourself, and let them carry the burden with you.

v23: If you do this thing and God commands you, then you will be able to endure, and all this people also will go to their place in peace.”
</p>

## Moses Listens to Jethro

<p>
v24: Moses listened to the voice of his father-in-law and did all that he had said.

v25: Moses chose capable men from all Israel and made them heads over the people: leaders of thousands, leaders of hundreds, leaders of fifties, and leaders of tens.

v26: They judged the people at all times. The difficult matter they brought to Moses, but every small matter they judged themselves.

v27: Then Moses sent his father-in-law away, and he went to his own land.
</p>
## Notes
v02: “after Moses had sent her away” does not explain when or why Zipporah had been sent back. The narrative simply records her reunion with Moses through Jethro.
v03–04: The names Gershom and Eliezer interpret major parts of Moses’ story: alien residence and divine help.
v11: Jethro’s confession follows the exodus report. The final clause is compressed; the sense is that Egypt’s arrogant treatment of Israel became the arena of the LORD’s superiority.
v12: Jethro participates in sacrificial worship and a meal “before God” with Aaron and Israel’s elders.
v15: “seek God” can include seeking divine guidance, decision, or judgment through Moses.
v17–18: The criticism is not of Moses’ concern for justice but of a structure that makes one person carry every case.
v21: The qualifications combine competence, reverence, truthfulness, and resistance to corrupt gain.
v23: Jethro’s counsel remains subject to divine command: “if … God commands you.” Administrative wisdom does not replace discernment.

##Vocabulary

v03: גֵּר (ger)
“Resident alien.” A person living outside ancestral land or kinship territory.

v04: עֵזֶר (ezer)
“Help.” The word behind the name Eliezer.

v16: תּוֹרָה (torah)
“Instruction.” Teaching that directs covenant life.

v18: נָבֹל תִּבֹּל (navol tibbol)
“You will surely wear yourself out.” An emphatic idiom of exhaustion.
