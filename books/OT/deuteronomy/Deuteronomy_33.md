## Moses Blesses Israel before His Death

<p>
v01: This is the blessing with which Moses, the man of God, blessed the children of Israel before his death.  

v02: He said:  

“The LORD came from Sinai  
and dawned upon them from Seir;  
he shone forth from Mount Paran  
and came from myriads of holy ones.  
At his right hand  
was fiery instruction for them.  

v03: Indeed, he loves the peoples;  
all his holy ones are in your hand.  
They sat down at your feet;  
each receives  
from your words.  

v04: Moses commanded us instruction,  
an inheritance  
for the assembly of Jacob.  

v05: And he became king in Jeshurun  
when the heads of the people gathered,  
together with the tribes of Israel.  
</p>

## Reuben

<p>
v06: May Reuben live and not die,  
though his men  
be few.  
</p>

## Judah

<p>
v07: And this concerning Judah. He said:  

“Hear, O LORD, the voice of Judah,  
and bring him  
to his people.  
With his hands he contends for himself;  
be a help  
against his adversaries.”  
</p>

## Levi

<p>
v08: Concerning Levi he said:  

“Your Thummim and your Urim  
belong to your faithful one,  
whom you tested at Massah,  
with whom you contended  
at the waters of Meribah;  

v09: who said of his father and mother,  
‘I do not regard them,’  
and did not recognize his brothers  
or acknowledge his sons,  
for they kept your word  
and guarded your covenant.  

v10: They shall teach your judgments to Jacob  
and your instruction to Israel.  
They shall put incense before your face  
and whole burnt offering  
upon your altar.  

v11: Bless, O LORD, his strength,  
and accept the work of his hands.  
Shatter the loins  
of those who rise against him  
and those who hate him,  
so that they rise no more.”  
</p>

## Benjamin

<p>
v12: Concerning Benjamin he said:  

“The beloved of the LORD  
shall dwell securely beside him.  
He shelters him all the day,  
and between his shoulders  
he dwells.”  
</p>

## Joseph

<p>
v13: Concerning Joseph he said:  

“Blessed by the LORD is his land,  
with the choice gifts of the heavens,  
with dew,  
and with the deep  
crouching beneath;  

v14: with the choice produce  
of the sun  
and the choice yield  
of the months;  

v15: with the best  
of the ancient mountains  
and the choice gifts  
of the everlasting hills;  

v16: with the choice gifts of the earth  
and its fullness,  
and the favor  
of the one who dwelt in the bush.  
May these come upon the head of Joseph  
and upon the crown of the one  
set apart from his brothers.  

v17: His firstborn bull has majesty,  
and his horns are the horns of a wild ox.  
With them he will gore peoples,  
all of them,  
to the ends of the earth.  
They are the ten thousands of Ephraim,  
and they are the thousands  
of Manasseh.”  
</p>

## Zebulun and Issachar

<p>
v18: Concerning Zebulun he said:  

“Rejoice, Zebulun, in your going out,  
and Issachar,  
in your tents.  

v19: They will call peoples  
to the mountain;  
there they will sacrifice  
righteous sacrifices.  
For they will draw  
the abundance of the seas  
and hidden treasures  
of the sand.”  
</p>

## Gad

<p>
v20: Concerning Gad he said:  

“Blessed is the one  
who enlarges Gad.  
He dwells like a lion  
and tears arm  
and crown of head.  

v21: He saw the first portion for himself,  
for there a commander’s portion  
was reserved.  
He came with the heads of the people;  
he carried out  
the righteousness of the LORD  
and his judgments  
with Israel.”  
</p>

## Dan

<p>
v22: Concerning Dan he said:  

“Dan is a lion’s cub  
that leaps out  
from Bashan.”  
</p>

## Naphtali

<p>
v23: Concerning Naphtali he said:  

“Naphtali, satisfied with favor  
and full of the blessing of the LORD,  
possess the west  
and the south.”  
</p>

## Asher

<p>
v24: Concerning Asher he said:  

“Blessed above sons is Asher;  
may he be favored  
by his brothers,  
and may he dip his foot  
in oil.  

v25: Iron and bronze  
shall be your bars,  
and as your days,  
so shall your strength be.”  
</p>

## There Is None Like the God of Jeshurun

<p>
v26: “There is none like the God of Jeshurun,  
who rides through the heavens  
to your help  
and through the clouds  
in his majesty.  

v27: The eternal God is a dwelling place,  
and underneath  
are everlasting arms.  
He drove out the enemy before you  
and said,  
‘Destroy!’  

v28: So Israel dwells securely,  
the fountain of Jacob alone,  
in a land of grain and new wine;  
indeed, his heavens  
drop dew.  

v29: Happy are you, Israel!  
Who is like you,  
a people saved by the LORD,  
the shield of your help  
and the sword of your majesty?  
Your enemies will cringe before you,  
and you will tread  
upon their heights.”  
</p>

## Notes

v1: Moses is called “the man of God,” a title identifying him by prophetic intimacy and service rather than royal office.  

v2: Sinai, Seir, and Paran place the divine appearance in the southern wilderness. The imagery echoes older poetic traditions of the LORD’s coming.  

v2: “fiery instruction” translates a very difficult Hebrew phrase. The consonantal text is unusual, and ancient versions differ. TSW preserves the close association of fire and divine instruction while noting the uncertainty.  

v3: “peoples” may refer broadly to Israel’s tribes or to peoples/nations more generally. The next lines focus on the holy ones receiving words at God’s feet.  

v5: The subject of “became king in Jeshurun” is debated. It may refer to the LORD or, less likely, Moses. TSW leaves the grammatical subject implicit in English as much as possible while the wider poem points toward divine kingship.  

v6: Reuben receives a brief blessing for survival despite small numbers.  

v8–11: Levi’s blessing links priestly discernment, loyalty, teaching, incense, sacrifice, and defense.  

v9: The language of disregarding father, mother, brothers, and sons recalls Levite loyalty in covenant crises. Priestly allegiance to the LORD takes precedence over kinship.  

v12: “between his shoulders he dwells” may picture the LORD dwelling in Benjamin’s territory, traditionally associated with Jerusalem’s temple region, or Benjamin sheltered between divine shoulders. TSW preserves the poetic ambiguity.  

v13–17: Joseph’s blessing is the most agriculturally abundant, gathering dew, deep waters, sun, months, mountains, hills, earth, and divine favor.  

v16: “the one who dwelt in the bush” recalls Moses’ encounter at the burning bush and applies that divine favor to Joseph.  

v17: Ephraim receives “ten thousands” and Manasseh “thousands,” reflecting Ephraim’s later prominence despite Manasseh’s firstborn status.  

v19: “hidden treasures of the sand” may refer to coastal resources, trade, shellfish products, or other maritime wealth. The poem does not specify.  

v21: Gad’s “commander’s portion” likely refers to the eastern territory secured before the crossing and to Gad’s participation with Israel’s leaders.  

v23: The geographic terms translated “west and south” have been understood in other ways, including sea and south. TSW follows the common territorial sense.  

v25: “as your days, so shall your strength be” translates a rare final word whose exact meaning is uncertain. Alternatives include security or rest. TSW preserves the traditional blessing sense.  

v27: “dwelling place” can also mean refuge. The image of everlasting arms underneath Israel is one of enduring divine support.  

v28: “fountain of Jacob” may refer to Jacob’s offspring as a source or to Israel dwelling apart. TSW preserves the compressed poetic syntax.  

v29: The blessing ends with the LORD as shield and sword, making Israel’s safety depend upon divine deliverance rather than military self-sufficiency.  

## Vocabulary

v1: אִישׁ הָאֱלֹהִים (ish ha-Elohim)  
“Man of God.” A title for Moses.  

v2: זָרַח (zarach)  
“To dawn” or “shine forth.” Divine appearance like sunrise.  

v2: אֵשְׁדָּת (eshdat)  
“Fiery instruction.” A difficult textual expression in the divine appearance.  

v4: מוֹרָשָׁה (morashah)  
“Inheritance” or “possession.” Torah as the assembly of Jacob’s heritage.  

v5: יְשֻׁרוּן (Yeshurun)  
“Jeshurun.” A poetic name for Israel.  

v8: תֻּמִּים וְאוּרִים (Tummim ve-Urim)  
“Thummim and Urim.” Priestly instruments of discernment.  

v12: יְדִיד (yedid)  
“Beloved.” Benjamin as beloved of the LORD.  

v16: רְצוֹן (ratson)  
“Favor” or “goodwill.” The blessing of the one who dwelt in the bush.  

v17: רְאֵם (re'em)  
“Wild ox.” A powerful horned animal.  

v27: מְעוֹנָה (me'onah)  
“Dwelling place” or “refuge.” The eternal God as Israel’s habitation.  

v27: זְרֹעֹת עוֹלָם (zero'ot olam)  
“Everlasting arms.” The image of divine support beneath Israel.  

v29: אַשְׁרֶיךָ (ashrekha)  
“Happy are you” or “blessed are you.” The final acclamation over Israel.
