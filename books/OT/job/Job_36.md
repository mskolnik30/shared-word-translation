## Elihu: I Have More to Say for God

<p>
v01: Elihu continued and said:

v02: “Wait for me a little, and I will show you,
for there are still words for God.

v03: I will bring my knowledge from far away
and give righteousness to my Maker.

v04: For truly my words are not false;
one complete in knowledge is with you.
</p>

## God Is Mighty and Does Not Reject

<p>
v05: Look, God is mighty and does not reject;
he is mighty in strength of heart.

v06: He does not preserve the wicked alive,
but gives justice to the afflicted.

v07: He does not withdraw his eyes from the righteous,
but with kings on the throne
he seats them forever, and they are exalted.

v08: And if they are bound in chains
and caught in cords of affliction,

v09: then he declares to them their work
and their transgressions, that they acted arrogantly.

v10: He opens their ear to discipline
and commands that they turn back from harm.

v11: If they listen and serve him,
they complete their days in prosperity
and their years in pleasantness.

v12: But if they do not listen,
they pass away by the weapon
and expire without knowledge.
</p>

## God Delivers the Afflicted Through Affliction

<p>
v13: The godless in heart store up anger;
they do not cry for help when he binds them.

v14: Their life dies in youth,
and their life among the cult prostitutes.

v15: He delivers the afflicted through their affliction
and opens their ear through oppression.

v16: He also entices you from the mouth of distress
to a broad place with no constraint,
and what is set upon your table will be full of richness.

v17: But you are filled with the judgment of the wicked;
judgment and justice take hold of you.
</p>

## Beware That Wrath Does Not Entice You

<p>
v18: Beware lest wrath entice you into scoffing,
and do not let the greatness of a ransom turn you aside.

v19: Will your wealth keep you from distress,
or all the forces of your strength?

v20: Do not long for the night,
when peoples are removed from their place.

v21: Take care; do not turn toward harm,
for you have chosen this rather than affliction.
</p>

## God Is Exalted in His Power

<p>
v22: Look, God is exalted in his power.
Who is a teacher like him?

v23: Who has appointed his way for him?
Or who can say, ‘You have done injustice’?

v24: Remember to magnify his work,
which people have sung.

v25: All humanity has seen it;
a mortal beholds it from far away.

v26: Look, God is great, and we do not know him;
the number of his years cannot be searched out.
</p>

## He Draws Up the Drops of Water

<p>
v27: For he draws up drops of water;
they distill rain from his mist,

v28: which the clouds pour down
and drip upon humanity abundantly.

v29: Can anyone understand the spreading of the clouds,
the thunderings of his pavilion?

v30: Look, he spreads his lightning around him
and covers the roots of the sea.

v31: For by these he judges peoples;
he gives food in abundance.

v32: He covers his hands with lightning
and commands it concerning the target.

v33: His thunder declares his presence;
the cattle also declare what is coming.
</p>

## Notes

v03: Elihu’s stated purpose is to “give righteousness” to his Maker—to defend God’s justice. This self-understanding distinguishes his speech from Job’s attempt to vindicate himself.

v05: “does not reject” is a striking claim after Job repeatedly experienced rejection. Elihu describes divine might as compatible with attention rather than indifference.

v08–12: Elihu interprets suffering as a means through which God exposes arrogance and opens the ear to discipline. The framework remains corrective and pedagogical.

v15: “delivers the afflicted through their affliction” is Elihu’s most concise theology of suffering: suffering can become the very medium of deliverance rather than only punishment.

v18–21: The Hebrew of these lines is difficult. The general warning is against allowing anger, wealth, or longing for escape to draw Job away from faithful endurance.

v27–33: Elihu turns from moral argument toward weather and divine power. This transition prepares literarily for the LORD’s coming speech from the storm.

v33: The final Hebrew line is especially obscure. TSW follows the sense that animals perceive or announce the approaching storm.

## Vocabulary

v03: צֶדֶק (tsedeq)
“Righteousness.” Elihu seeks to attribute righteousness to his Maker.

v09: גָּבַר (gavar)
“To act arrogantly” or “show oneself mighty.” Human self-exaltation exposed through affliction.

v10: מוּסָר (musar)
“Discipline” or “instruction.” God opens the sufferer’s ear to it.

v15: עֳנִי (oni)
“Affliction.” The very condition through which Elihu says God can deliver.

v22: מוֹרֶה (moreh)
“Teacher.” Elihu asks who teaches like God.

v29: סֻכָּה (sukkah)
“Pavilion” or “booth.” The clouded dwelling from which thunder sounds.
