## The LORD Answers Job from the Storm

<p>
v01: Then the LORD answered Job from the storm and said:

v02: “Who is this who darkens counsel
with words without knowledge?

v03: Gird up your waist like a man;
I will question you,
and you make me know.
</p>

## Where Were You When I Founded the Earth?

<p>
v04: Where were you when I founded the earth?
Declare it, if you know understanding.

v05: Who set its measurements—surely you know!
Or who stretched a measuring line upon it?

v06: Upon what were its bases sunk,
or who laid its cornerstone,

v07: when the morning stars sang together
and all the sons of God shouted for joy?
</p>

## Who Shut the Sea Behind Doors?

<p>
v08: And who shut in the sea with doors
when it burst out, coming from the womb,

v09: when I made cloud its garment
and thick darkness its swaddling cloth,

v10: and I prescribed my boundary for it
and set bars and doors,

v11: and said, ‘Thus far you may come, and no farther;
here your proud waves shall stop’?
</p>

## Have You Commanded the Morning?

<p>
v12: Have you ever in your days commanded the morning
or made the dawn know its place,

v13: that it might take hold of the edges of the earth
and shake the wicked out of it?

v14: It is changed like clay under a seal,
and things stand out like a garment.

v15: Their light is withheld from the wicked,
and the uplifted arm is broken.
</p>

## Have You Entered the Springs of the Sea?

<p>
v16: Have you entered the springs of the sea
or walked in the searchings of the deep?

v17: Have the gates of Death been revealed to you?
Have you seen the gates of death-shadow?

v18: Have you understood the breadths of the earth?
Declare it, if you know all this.
</p>

## Where Is the Way to Light?

<p>
v19: Where is the way to the dwelling of light,
and darkness—where is its place,

v20: that you may take it to its territory
and understand the paths to its house?

v21: Surely you know, for you were born then,
and the number of your days is great!
</p>

## Have You Entered the Storehouses of Snow?

<p>
v22: Have you entered the storehouses of snow
or seen the storehouses of hail,

v23: which I have reserved for a time of distress,
for a day of battle and war?

v24: What is the way by which light is distributed,
or the east wind scattered over the earth?
</p>

## Who Cut a Channel for the Flood?

<p>
v25: Who cut a channel for the flood
and a way for the thunderbolt,

v26: to make it rain upon a land where no one lives,
upon a wilderness in which there is no human,

v27: to satisfy the waste and desolate land
and make the growth of grass sprout?

v28: Does the rain have a father?
Or who fathered the drops of dew?

v29: From whose womb came the ice,
and the frost of heaven—who gave it birth?

v30: The waters hide themselves like stone,
and the face of the deep becomes firm.
</p>

## Can You Bind the Pleiades?

<p>
v31: Can you bind the bonds of the Pleiades
or loosen the cords of Orion?

v32: Can you bring out the constellations in their season
or guide the Bear with her children?

v33: Do you know the statutes of the heavens?
Can you establish their rule on the earth?
</p>

## Can You Raise Your Voice to the Clouds?

<p>
v34: Can you raise your voice to the clouds
so that an abundance of water covers you?

v35: Can you send lightning so that it goes
and says to you, ‘Here we are’?

v36: Who put wisdom in the inward parts
or gave understanding to the mind?

v37: Who can number the clouds by wisdom,
or tip over the jars of heaven,

v38: when dust is cast into a mass
and clods cling together?
</p>

## Can You Hunt Prey for the Lion?

<p>
v39: Can you hunt prey for the lioness
or satisfy the appetite of young lions,

v40: when they crouch in their dens
and lie in wait in the thicket?

v41: Who provides for the raven its prey
when its young cry out to God
and wander for lack of food?
</p>
## Notes
v01: The LORD answers “from the storm,” the first direct divine speech since the prose opening. The answer is encounter and interrogation rather than a causal explanation of Job’s suffering.
v02: “words without knowledge” challenges the scope of Job’s speech, but the verse does not repeat the friends’ accusations of hidden moral crimes.
v04–11: Creation is portrayed as architecture and birth. Earth is founded like a building while sea bursts from a womb and is swaddled before receiving boundaries.
v07: “sons of God” recalls the heavenly assembly of chapters 1–2, now portrayed rejoicing at creation rather than debating Job.
v12–15: Dawn is imagined as taking the earth by its edges and shaking wicked people loose, while light gives shape to the world like a seal impressing clay.
v16–21: Job is questioned about domains beyond human access: sea depths, Death, the breadth of earth, and the dwellings of light and darkness.
v26–27: Rain falls even on uninhabited wilderness. Divine care therefore exceeds human usefulness and includes creation with no immediate human audience.
v31–33: The constellation names are traditional identifications of ancient Hebrew star groups; precise correspondence is not certain in every case.
v36: The Hebrew words rendered “inward parts” and “mind” are rare and disputed; proposed referents include human intellect or phenomena of the sky. TSW follows the common human-wisdom interpretation while preserving the uncertainty in this note.
v39–41: The speech turns from cosmic order to wild animals, asking Job to consider creatures sustained outside human control.

##Vocabulary

v01: סְעָרָה (se'arah)
“Storm” or “whirlwind.” The setting from which the LORD speaks.

v03: אָזַר (azar)
“To gird.” To prepare oneself by fastening clothing for action.

v04: יָסַד (yasad)
“To found” or “lay a foundation.” Creation pictured as architecture.

v08: סָכַךְ (sakhakh)
“To shut in” or “enclose.” God restrains the sea.

v17: שַׁעֲרֵי־מָוֶת (sha'arei-mavet)
“Gates of Death.” Poetic boundary of the realm of death.
