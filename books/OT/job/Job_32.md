## Elihu Becomes Angry

<p>
v01: Then these three men stopped answering Job because he was righteous in his own eyes.
v02: But the anger of Elihu son of Barachel the Buzite, from the clan of Ram, burned against Job because he declared himself righteous rather than God.
v03: His anger also burned against his three friends because they had found no answer, yet they had condemned Job.
v04: Now Elihu had waited for Job with words because they were older than he.
v05: When Elihu saw that there was no answer in the mouth of the three men, his anger burned.
</p>

## I Am Young, and You Are Aged

<p>
v06: Elihu son of Barachel the Buzite answered and said:

“I am young in days,
and you are aged;
therefore I was timid and afraid
to declare my knowledge to you.

v07: I said, ‘Let days speak,
and many years teach wisdom.’

v08: But truly it is the spirit in a human,
the breath of the Almighty,
that gives them understanding.

v09: It is not the great who are wise,
nor do the aged always understand justice.

v10: Therefore I say, ‘Listen to me;
I also will declare my knowledge.’
</p>

## I Waited for Your Words

<p>
v11: Look, I waited for your words;
I gave ear to your understandings
until you searched out what to say.

v12: I gave you my attention,
and look, there was no one who corrected Job,
no one among you who answered his words.

v13: Do not say, ‘We have found wisdom;
God will drive him away, not a man.’

v14: He did not arrange his words against me,
and I will not answer him with your speeches.
</p>

## I Am Full of Words

<p>
v15: They are dismayed; they answer no more;
words have departed from them.

v16: And shall I wait because they do not speak,
because they stand there and answer no more?

v17: I also will answer my share;
I also will declare my knowledge.

v18: For I am full of words;
the spirit within me constrains me.

v19: Look, my belly is like wine with no vent;
like new wineskins it is ready to burst.

v20: I will speak so that I may find relief;
I will open my lips and answer.

v21: Please let me not show partiality to anyone,
and let me not flatter any human.

v22: For I do not know how to flatter;
if I did, my Maker would soon carry me away.”
</p>
## Notes
v01–05: Elihu enters only after the three friends stop speaking. The narrator records his anger both at Job and at the friends: Job for justifying himself, the friends for condemning without an answer.
v02: “righteous rather than God” — The Hebrew can mean Job justified himself more than God or at God’s expense. Elihu frames the dispute as involving the character of God as well as Job.
v08: Elihu qualifies the wisdom of age by appealing to the divine spirit and breath that give understanding. Wisdom is not simply accumulated years.
v13: Elihu warns the friends not to retreat into the claim that only God could answer Job after their own arguments have failed.
v18–20: The imagery of fermenting wine in sealed skins depicts Elihu’s intense compulsion to speak, but the narrative does not itself declare his coming speech inspired.
v21–22: Elihu claims impartiality, a significant concern in a book dominated by legal and judicial imagery.

##Vocabulary

v02: חָרָה אַף (charah af)
“Anger burned.” The repeated idiom describing Elihu’s reaction.

v08: רוּחַ (ruach)
“Spirit,” “wind,” or “breath.” The spirit in a human associated with understanding.

v09: מִשְׁפָּט (mishpat)
“Justice” or “judgment.” Not automatically understood by age alone.

v21: נָשָׂא פָנִים (nasa panim)
“To show partiality.” Literally to lift a person’s face in favoritism.
