## Do You Know When the Mountain Goats Give Birth?

<p>
v01: “Do you know the time when the mountain goats give birth?  
Do you watch when the deer is in labor?

v02: Can you count the months they fulfill?  
Do you know the time when they give birth,

v03: when they crouch and bring forth their young  
and release their labor pains?

v04: Their young become strong and grow in the open field;  
they go out and do not return to them.
</p>

## Who Set the Wild Donkey Free?

<p>
v05: Who set the wild donkey free?  
Who loosened the bonds of the wild ass,

v06: whose house I made the Arabah  
and whose dwelling the salt land?

v07: He laughs at the tumult of the city;  
he does not hear the shouts of the driver.

v08: He searches the mountains for his pasture  
and seeks after every green thing.
</p>

## Will the Wild Ox Serve You?

<p>
v09: Will the wild ox be willing to serve you?  
Will it spend the night beside your manger?

v10: Can you bind the wild ox in a furrow with a rope?  
Will it harrow the valleys after you?

v11: Will you trust it because its strength is great  
and leave your labor to it?

v12: Will you trust it to bring back your seed  
and gather it to your threshing floor?
</p>

## The Wings of the Ostrich Flutter

<p>
v13: The wings of the ostrich flutter joyfully,  
but are they the pinions and feathers of the stork?

v14: For she leaves her eggs on the ground  
and warms them in the dust,

v15: forgetting that a foot may crush them  
or a wild animal trample them.

v16: She treats her young harshly, as though they were not hers;  
she does not fear that her labor may be in vain,

v17: because God made her forget wisdom  
and did not give her a share in understanding.

v18: But when she lifts herself to run,  
she laughs at horse and rider.
</p>

## Do You Give the Horse Its Might?

<p>
v19: Do you give the horse its might?  
Do you clothe its neck with a mane?

v20: Do you make it leap like a locust?  
The majesty of its snorting is terror.

v21: It paws in the valley and rejoices in strength;  
it goes out to meet weapons.

v22: It laughs at fear and is not terrified;  
it does not turn back from the sword.

v23: Upon it the quiver rattles,  
the flashing spear and javelin.

v24: With shaking and rage it swallows the ground;  
it cannot stand still when the horn sounds.

v25: At every blast of the horn it says, ‘Aha!’  
It smells the battle from far away,  
the thunder of commanders and the war cry.
</p>

## Does the Hawk Fly by Your Wisdom?

<p>
v26: Is it by your wisdom that the hawk soars  
and spreads its wings toward the south?

v27: Is it at your command that the eagle rises  
and makes its nest high?

v28: On the rock it dwells and spends the night,  
on the crag of the rock, a stronghold.

v29: From there it searches out food;  
its eyes see from far away.

v30: Its young suck blood,  
and where the slain are, there it is.”
</p>

## Notes

v01–04: The LORD directs Job toward hidden processes of wild birth that occur without human supervision. Knowledge and care exist outside human observation.

v05–12: Wild donkey and wild ox are valuable precisely because they are not domesticated. The speech celebrates creaturely freedom that does not serve human economic purposes.

v09: The “wild ox” is Hebrew *re'em*, probably the extinct aurochs rather than the mythical unicorn found in some older translations.

v13–18: The ostrich passage is playful and paradoxical: limited apparent wisdom coexists with remarkable speed. Creaturely value is not reduced to one standard of intelligence.

v19–25: The warhorse’s fearlessness is described with admiration even though warfare itself is dangerous. The animal embodies a power Job did not create.

v26–30: Hawk and eagle act through capacities not derived from human wisdom or command, extending the theme of creation’s independence from human governance.

## Vocabulary

v01: יָעֵל (ya'el)
“Mountain goat” or “ibex.” A wild goat living in rocky terrain.

v05: פֶּרֶא (pere)
“Wild donkey.” A free-ranging desert animal.

v09: רְאֵם (re'em)
“Wild ox,” likely the aurochs. A powerful undomesticated bovine.

v13: רְנָנִים (renanim)
“Ostriches,” traditionally understood from a rare poetic term associated with ringing cries or wings.

v19: גְּבוּרָה (gevurah)
“Might” or “strength.” The power of the warhorse.

v26: נֵץ (nets)
“Hawk.” A bird of prey whose migration or flight exceeds human direction.
