## I Will Speak in the Bitterness of My Soul

<p>
v01: “My soul is weary of my life.  
I will give free course to my complaint;  
I will speak in the bitterness of my soul.

v02: I will say to God, ‘Do not condemn me;  
make me know why you contend with me.

v03: Is it good for you to oppress,  
to reject the work of your hands,  
while you shine upon the counsel of the wicked?

v04: Do you have eyes of flesh?  
Do you see as a human sees?

v05: Are your days like the days of a mortal,  
or your years like the years of a man,

v06: that you seek out my guilt  
and search for my sin,

v07: though you know that I am not wicked,  
and there is no one who can deliver from your hand?
</p>

## Your Hands Shaped Me

<p>
v08: Your hands shaped me and made me,  
together all around—yet you swallow me up.

v09: Remember, please, that you made me like clay.  
Will you return me to dust?

v10: Did you not pour me out like milk  
and curdle me like cheese,

v11: clothe me with skin and flesh,  
and weave me together with bones and sinews?

v12: You gave me life and steadfast love,  
and your care guarded my spirit.
</p>

## Yet These Things You Hid in Your Heart

<p>
v13: Yet these things you hid in your heart;  
I know that this was with you:

v14: if I sin, you watch me  
and do not acquit me of my guilt.

v15: If I am wicked, woe to me!  
And if I am righteous, I cannot lift my head.  
I am filled with disgrace  
and look upon my affliction.

v16: If my head rises, you hunt me like a lion  
and again show yourself wondrous against me.

v17: You renew your witnesses against me  
and increase your anger with me;  
changes and warfare are against me.
</p>

## Why Did You Bring Me from the Womb?

<p>
v18: Why then did you bring me out from the womb?  
I should have expired,  
and no eye would have seen me.

v19: I would have been as though I had not been,  
carried from womb to grave.

v20: Are not my days few?  
Stop, and leave me alone,  
that I may brighten a little

v21: before I go—and do not return—  
to the land of darkness and death-shadow,

v22: a land of deepest gloom,  
like death-shadow without order,  
where light is like darkness.”
</p>

## Notes

v02: Job asks God directly to disclose the charge against him. His lament is increasingly framed as a legal dispute in which he does not know the accusation.

v03: Job asks whether God takes pleasure in oppressing the work of God’s own hands while favoring wicked counsel. The question is accusatory, not a settled theological proposition.

v08–12: Job’s body is described through craft and gestation imagery: shaped like clay, poured like milk, curdled like cheese, clothed, and woven. The God he experiences as destroyer is also the God who carefully made him.

v12: “steadfast love” — Job acknowledges *hesed* in the gift and preservation of life even while arguing that present suffering contradicts that care.

v13: The verse marks Job’s fear that divine care itself concealed a hidden intention to watch for guilt and punish him.

v21–22: Sheol is imagined as darkness without order, where even light is dark. Job asks only for a brief easing before entering that realm.

## Vocabulary

v01: נָקַט (naqat)
“To be weary” or “loathe.” Job is exhausted with his life.

v02: רָשַׁע (rasha)
“To condemn as wicked.” Job asks God not to pronounce him guilty without revealing the charge.

v08: עָצַב (atsav)
“To shape” or “form.” God’s hands carefully form Job.

v09: חֹמֶר (chomer)
“Clay.” Human creatureliness and mortality.

v12: חֶסֶד (ḥesed)
“Steadfast love.” Faithful care Job recognizes in the gift of life.

v21: צַלְמָוֶת (tsalmavet)
“Death-shadow” or “deep darkness.” Job’s image for the realm of death.
