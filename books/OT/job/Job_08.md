## Bildad: Does God Pervert Justice?

<p>
v01: Then Bildad the Shuhite answered and said:  

v02: “How long will you speak these things,  
and the words of your mouth be a mighty wind?  

v03: Does God pervert justice?  
Does the Almighty pervert righteousness?  

v04: If your children sinned against him,  
he gave them into the hand of their transgression.  

v05: If you would earnestly seek God  
and plead with the Almighty,  

v06: if you were pure and upright,  
surely now he would awaken for you  
and restore your righteous dwelling.  

v07: Though your beginning was small,  
your latter days would become very great.  
</p>

## Ask the Former Generation

<p>
v08: For please ask the former generation,  
and establish yourself upon what their ancestors searched out.  

v09: For we are of yesterday and know nothing,  
for our days on earth are a shadow.  

v10: Will they not teach you and speak to you  
and bring out words from their heart?  
</p>

## Can Papyrus Grow Without a Marsh?

<p>
v11: Can papyrus grow without a marsh?  
Can reeds flourish without water?  

v12: While still green and not cut,  
they dry up before any other plant.  

v13: So are the paths of all who forget God;  
the hope of the godless will perish.  

v14: His confidence is cut off,  
and what he trusts is a spider’s house.  

v15: He leans upon his house, but it does not stand;  
he holds fast to it, but it does not endure.  
</p>

## God Will Not Reject the Blameless

<p>
v16: He is green before the sun,  
and his shoots spread over his garden.  

v17: His roots wrap around a stone heap;  
he sees a house of stones.  

v18: If he is uprooted from his place,  
it denies him, saying,  
‘I have never seen you.’  

v19: Look, this is the joy of his way,  
and from the dust others sprout.  

v20: Look, God will not reject a blameless person,  
and he will not strengthen the hand of evildoers.  

v21: He will yet fill your mouth with laughter  
and your lips with a shout of joy.  

v22: Those who hate you will be clothed with shame,  
and the tent of the wicked will be no more.”  
</p>

## Notes

v3: Bildad begins from a conviction the book itself does not deny: God does not pervert justice. His error develops in assuming he can infer the precise moral cause of Job’s suffering from that truth.  

v4: Bildad directly attributes the deaths of Job’s children to their sin, despite the prologue giving no such explanation. This is one of the friends’ harshest applications of retribution theology.  

v8–10: Bildad appeals to inherited wisdom and the accumulated insight of prior generations rather than personal revelation.  

v11–15: Papyrus without water and a spider’s web portray the instability of hope detached from God. The images themselves are powerful even though Bildad misapplies the principle to Job.  

v16–19: The plant imagery is textually difficult and may describe apparent flourishing that is quickly erased. The broader point remains the instability of the wicked.  

v20: Bildad’s statement that God does not reject the blameless ironically places him close to Job’s own claim, but he assumes Job’s suffering proves that Job cannot be blameless.  

## Vocabulary

v3: מִשְׁפָּט (mishpat)  
“Justice” or “judgment.” Bildad insists God does not distort it.  

v3: צֶדֶק (tsedeq)  
“Righteousness” or “right order.” Parallel with justice.  

v5: שָׁחַר (shachar)  
“To seek earnestly.” To seek God early or diligently.  

v11: גֹּמֶא (gome)  
“Papyrus.” A marsh plant dependent upon water.  

v13: חָנֵף (chanef)  
“Godless” or “profane.” One whose hope Bildad says will perish.  

v14: עַכָּבִישׁ (akkavish)  
“Spider.” Its web becomes an image of fragile confidence.
