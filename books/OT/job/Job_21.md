## Listen Carefully to My Words

<p>
v01: Then Job answered and said:

v02: “Listen carefully to my words,
and let this be your comfort to me.

v03: Bear with me, and I will speak;
and after I have spoken, mock on.

v04: Is my complaint directed to a human?
Why then should my spirit not be impatient?

v05: Turn toward me and be appalled;
put your hand over your mouth.

v06: When I remember, I am terrified,
and trembling seizes my flesh.
</p>

## Why Do the Wicked Live?

<p>
v07: Why do the wicked live,
grow old,
and even become mighty in power?

v08: Their offspring are established with them in their presence,
and their descendants before their eyes.

v09: Their houses are safe from fear,
and the rod of God is not upon them.

v10: Their bull breeds and does not fail;
their cow calves and does not miscarry.

v11: They send out their little ones like a flock,
and their children dance.

v12: They lift their voice with tambourine and lyre
and rejoice to the sound of the pipe.

v13: They spend their days in prosperity
and in a moment go down to Sheol.
</p>

## They Say to God, Depart from Us

<p>
v14: Yet they say to God, ‘Depart from us;
we do not desire knowledge of your ways.

v15: What is the Almighty, that we should serve him?
And what would we gain if we plead with him?’

v16: Look, their prosperity is not in their own hand.
The counsel of the wicked is far from me.
</p>

## How Often Is the Lamp of the Wicked Put Out?

<p>
v17: How often is the lamp of the wicked extinguished,
or their calamity come upon them?
How often does he distribute pains in his anger?

v18: Are they like straw before the wind,
or chaff that a storm steals away?

v19: You say, ‘God stores up a man’s guilt for his children.’
Let him repay the man himself, so that he may know it.

v20: Let his own eyes see his destruction,
and let him drink from the wrath of the Almighty.

v21: For what does he care for his household after him
when the number of his months is cut off?
</p>

## Can Anyone Teach God Knowledge?

<p>
v22: Can anyone teach God knowledge,
since he judges those on high?

v23: One dies in the fullness of his strength,
completely at ease and secure,

v24: his sides full of fat
and the marrow of his bones moist.

v25: Another dies with bitterness of soul,
never having tasted good.

v26: Together they lie down in the dust,
and worms cover them.
</p>

## Your Answers Remain False

<p>
v27: Look, I know your thoughts
and the schemes by which you wrong me.

v28: For you say, ‘Where is the house of the noble?
And where is the tent where the wicked lived?’

v29: Have you not asked those who travel the roads?
Do you not recognize their signs,

v30: that the evil person is spared in the day of calamity,
and carried away from the day of wrath?

v31: Who declares his way to his face?
And who repays him for what he has done?

v32: When he is carried to the grave,
a watch is kept over his tomb.

v33: The clods of the valley are sweet to him;
all humanity follows after him,
and those before him are without number.

v34: How then will you comfort me with emptiness,
when falsehood remains in your answers?”
</p>
## Notes
v07: Job directly contradicts Zophar’s claim that wicked joy is only momentary. His argument is observational: wicked people may live long, become powerful, and see their families flourish.
v09–13: The prosperity Job describes includes security, fertility, children, music, and peaceful death—precisely many blessings the friends associate exclusively with righteousness.
v14–15: The prosperous wicked openly reject God, eliminating the explanation that their prosperity reflects hidden piety.
v17: Job’s repeated “How often?” challenges the friends’ portrayal of judgment as immediate and predictable.
v19–21: Job questions deferred punishment falling on children. If judgment is to demonstrate moral order, he argues, the wrongdoer himself must perceive it.
v23–26: Death levels radically different lives. Prosperity and bitterness both end in dust and worms, complicating attempts to read moral status from outward condition.
v29–33: Job appeals to the testimony of travelers and public experience. The wicked may receive honor even in burial rather than public disgrace.

##Vocabulary

v04: שִׂיחַ (siach)
“Complaint,” “meditation,” or “speech.” Job’s case is ultimately directed beyond the friends.

v07: עָתַק (ataq)
“To grow old” or “advance.” The wicked can live into old age.

v13: טוֹב (tov)
“Good,” “prosperity,” or “well-being.” The wicked spend their days in good circumstances.

v17: נֵר (ner)
“Lamp.” An image of life and flourishing whose extinction the friends predict.

v25: מַר־נֶפֶשׁ (mar-nefesh)
“Bitter in soul.” One whose life has been marked by deep suffering.

v34: מָעַל (ma'al)
“Falsehood,” “treachery,” or “unfaithfulness.” Job’s judgment upon the friends’ answers.
