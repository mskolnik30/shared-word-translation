## Elihu: Listen to My Words

<p>
v01: “But now, Job, please hear my speech,  
and give ear to all my words.  

v02: Look, now I open my mouth;  
my tongue speaks in my palate.  

v03: My words come from the uprightness of my heart,  
and my lips speak knowledge clearly.  

v04: The Spirit of God made me,  
and the breath of the Almighty gives me life.  

v05: If you can, answer me;  
arrange yourself before me; take your stand.  

v06: Look, I am like you before God;  
I also was pinched from clay.  

v07: Look, no terror of me should frighten you,  
and my pressure will not be heavy upon you.  
</p>

## You Have Said, I Am Pure

<p>
v08: Surely you have spoken in my hearing,  
and I have heard the sound of your words:  

v09: ‘I am pure, without transgression;  
I am clean, and there is no guilt in me.  

v10: Look, he finds occasions against me;  
he considers me his enemy.  

v11: He puts my feet in the stocks;  
he watches all my paths.’  

v12: Look, in this you are not right, I answer you,  
for God is greater than a human.  

v13: Why do you contend against him  
because he does not answer all a person’s words?  
</p>

## God Speaks in More Than One Way

<p>
v14: For God speaks in one way,  
and in two, though a person does not perceive it:  

v15: in a dream, a vision of the night,  
when deep sleep falls upon people,  
in slumber upon the bed.  

v16: Then he opens the ears of people  
and seals their instruction,  

v17: to turn a person from his deed  
and hide pride from a man,  

v18: to hold back his life from the pit  
and his life from passing over by the weapon.  
</p>

## A Person Is Corrected with Pain

<p>
v19: A person is corrected with pain upon his bed  
and with continual strife in his bones,  

v20: so that his life loathes bread  
and his soul desirable food.  

v21: His flesh wastes away from sight,  
and his bones once unseen stick out.  

v22: His life draws near to the pit,  
and his life to those who bring death.  
</p>

## If There Is a Messenger for Him

<p>
v23: If there is a messenger for him,  
an interpreter, one among a thousand,  
to declare to a human what is upright for him,  

v24: then he is gracious to him and says,  
‘Deliver him from going down to the pit;  
I have found a ransom.’  

v25: His flesh becomes fresher than in youth;  
he returns to the days of his youthful strength.  

v26: He prays to God, and God accepts him;  
he sees God’s face with a joyful shout,  
and God restores to a human his righteousness.  

v27: He sings before people and says,  
‘I sinned and twisted what was right,  
and it was not repaid to me.  

v28: He redeemed my life from passing into the pit,  
and my life will see the light.’  
</p>

## God Does These Things Again and Again

<p>
v29: Look, God does all these things  
twice, three times with a person,  

v30: to bring back his life from the pit,  
that he may be enlightened with the light of life.  

v31: Pay attention, Job; listen to me.  
Be silent, and I will speak.  

v32: If you have words, answer me;  
speak, for I desire to justify you.  

v33: If not, listen to me;  
be silent, and I will teach you wisdom.”  
</p>

## Notes

v6–7: Elihu presents himself as the kind of human disputant Job desired: made from the same clay and therefore not overwhelming him with divine terror.  

v9: Elihu summarizes Job more absolutely than Job usually speaks. Job has claimed integrity and denied the accusations against him, not consistently claimed total sinlessness.  

v14–18: Elihu argues that divine silence may actually be unrecognized communication, especially through dreams intended to redirect human pride.  

v19–22: Elihu also interprets suffering as corrective communication. Unlike the three friends, he emphasizes prevention and restoration rather than simple punishment for past wrongdoing.  

v23: “messenger … interpreter” — The figure’s identity is not defined. It may be a heavenly messenger, human mediator, or poetic representative who explains what is right.  

v24: “ransom” — Hebrew *kofer* can mean a price of release or covering payment. Elihu introduces it without describing its source.  

v26–28: Restoration includes prayer, renewed access to God’s face, confession, righteousness, and return from the pit to light.  

## Vocabulary

v4: נְשָׁמָה (neshamah)  
“Breath.” The breath of the Almighty gives Elihu life.  

v6: חֹמֶר (chomer)  
“Clay.” Elihu emphasizes shared creatureliness with Job.  

v16: מוּסָר (musar)  
“Instruction” or “discipline.” God can seal instruction through dreams.  

v19: יָכַח (yakhach)  
“To correct” or “reprove.” Pain is interpreted by Elihu as correction.  

v23: מֵלִיץ (melits)  
“Interpreter,” “mediator,” or “spokesman.” One who explains what is upright.  

v24: כֹּפֶר (kofer)  
“Ransom.” A payment or means of release from the pit.
