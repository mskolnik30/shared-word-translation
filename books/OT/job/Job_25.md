## Bildad: How Can a Human Be Righteous Before God?

<p>
v01: Then Bildad the Shuhite answered and said:

v02: “Dominion and dread are with him;
he makes peace in his heights.

v03: Is there any number to his armies?
And upon whom does his light not rise?

v04: How then can a human be righteous before God?
How can one born of woman be pure?

v05: Look, even the moon is not bright,
and the stars are not pure in his eyes.

v06: How much less a human, a maggot,
and a son of man, a worm!”
</p>

## Notes

v02: Bildad’s final speech is brief and concentrates on divine majesty and human smallness rather than addressing Job’s observations about injustice.

v03: God’s “armies” may include heavenly hosts or the innumerable forces under divine command.

v04: Bildad repeats the friends’ central question about human righteousness before God. The book does not deny human creaturely limitation, but Job’s case concerns whether his suffering proves special guilt.

v05: Moon and stars are invoked to intensify the contrast between divine holiness and created brilliance.

v06: The imagery of maggot and worm is deliberately degrading. Bildad’s theology of transcendence leaves little room for the dignity of the suffering person before God.

## Vocabulary

v02: מָשַׁל (mashal)
“Dominion” or “rule.” Sovereign authority belonging to God.

v02: פַּחַד (pachad)
“Dread” or “terror.” Awe-inspiring fear before divine majesty.

v03: גְּדוּד (gedud)
“Troop” or “army.” The forces under God’s authority.

v04: זָכָה (zakhah)
“To be pure” or “clean.” Bildad questions human purity before God.

v06: רִמָּה (rimmah)
“Maggot.” An image of human frailty.

v06: תּוֹלֵעָה (tole'ah)
“Worm.” A parallel image of creaturely smallness.
