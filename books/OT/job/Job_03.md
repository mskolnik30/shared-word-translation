## Job Curses the Day of His Birth

<p>
v01: After this Job opened his mouth and cursed his day.
v02: Job answered and said:

v03: “Let the day perish on which I was born,
and the night that said,
‘A male has been conceived.’

v04: Let that day be darkness.
Let God above not seek it,
and let no light shine upon it.

v05: Let darkness and death-shadow reclaim it;
let a cloud settle upon it;
let whatever darkens the day terrify it.

v06: That night—let thick darkness seize it.
Let it not rejoice among the days of the year;
let it not enter the number of the months.

v07: Look, let that night be barren;
let no joyful cry enter it.

v08: Let those who curse days curse it,
those ready to rouse Leviathan.

v09: Let the stars of its twilight grow dark.
Let it wait for light and find none;
let it not see the eyelids of dawn,

v10: because it did not shut
the doors of my mother’s womb
or hide trouble from my eyes.
</p>

## Why Did I Not Die at Birth?

<p>
v11: Why did I not die from the womb,
come out from the belly and expire?

v12: Why did knees receive me?
And why breasts, that I should nurse?

v13: For now I would have lain down and been quiet;
I would have slept; then I would have been at rest,

v14: with kings and counselors of the earth,
who built ruins for themselves,

v15: or with princes who had gold,
who filled their houses with silver.

v16: Or why was I not like a hidden miscarriage,
like infants who never saw light?

v17: There the wicked cease from raging,
and there the weary are at rest.

v18: Prisoners are at ease together;
they do not hear the voice of the taskmaster.

v19: Small and great are there,
and the servant is free from his master.
</p>

## Why Is Light Given to the Suffering?

<p>
v20: Why is light given to one in misery,
and life to the bitter in soul,

v21: who wait for death, but it does not come,
and dig for it more than for hidden treasures,

v22: who rejoice exceedingly
and are glad when they find the grave?

v23: Why is light given to a man whose way is hidden,
whom God has hedged in?

v24: For my sighing comes before my bread,
and my groanings pour out like water.

v25: For the terror I feared has come upon me,
and what I dreaded has happened to me.

v26: I have no ease,
and I have no quiet,
and I have no rest—
yet trouble comes.”
</p>
##Notes

v05: “death-shadow” — Hebrew *tsalmavet* can mean deep darkness or the shadow of death.

##Vocabulary

v01: קָלַל (qalal)
“To curse” or “treat as accursed.” Job directs the curse toward his birth day.

v05: צַלְמָוֶת (tsalmavet)
“Death-shadow” or “deep darkness.” Thick, deathlike darkness.

v07: גַּלְמוּד (galmud)
“Barren” or “desolate.” A night emptied of fertility and joy.

v08: לִוְיָתָן (Livyatan)
“Leviathan.” A great chaos creature in Hebrew poetic imagery.
