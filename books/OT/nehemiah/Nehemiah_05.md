## A Cry Against Economic Oppression

<p>
v01: There was a great outcry from the people and their wives against their fellow Jews.
v02: Some were saying, “We, our sons, and our daughters are many. We need grain so that we may eat and live.”
v03: Others were saying, “We are pledging our fields, vineyards, and houses so that we may get grain during the famine.”
v04: Others were saying, “We have borrowed money for the king’s tax on our fields and vineyards.
v05: Now our flesh is like the flesh of our relatives, our children like their children. Yet we are bringing our sons and daughters into servitude, and some of our daughters are already enslaved. We are powerless, because our fields and vineyards belong to others.”
</p>

## Nehemiah Rebukes the Nobles

<p>
v06: I became very angry when I heard their outcry and these words.
v07: I considered the matter within myself and contended with the nobles and officials. I said to them, “Each of you is charging interest to your own relatives.” Then I held a great assembly against them.
v08: I said to them, “As far as we were able, we bought back our fellow Jews who had been sold to the nations. And now you are selling your own relatives so that they may be sold back to us?” They were silent and could not find a word.
v09: So I said, “What you are doing is not good. Should you not walk in the fear of our God because of the reproach of the nations, our enemies?
v10: I also, along with my relatives and servants, have been lending them money and grain. Please, let us leave off this interest.
v11: Restore to them today their fields, vineyards, olive groves, and houses, along with the hundredth part of the money, grain, new wine, and oil that you have been charging them.”
</p>

<p>
v12: They said, “We will restore it and require nothing from them. We will do as you say.” Then I called the priests and made them swear to do according to this word.
v13: I also shook out the fold of my garment and said, “So may God shake out from house and possession everyone who does not fulfill this word. So may that person be shaken out and emptied.” All the assembly said, “Amen,” and praised the LORD. And the people did according to this word.
</p>

## Nehemiah Refuses the Governor’s Allowance

<p>
v14: Moreover, from the day I was appointed governor in the land of Judah, from the twentieth year to the thirty-second year of King Artaxerxes—twelve years—I and my relatives did not eat the governor’s food allowance.
v15: The former governors who were before me laid heavy burdens on the people and took from them bread and wine, besides forty shekels of silver. Even their servants ruled harshly over the people. But I did not do so, because of the fear of God.
v16: I also devoted myself to the work on this wall, and we acquired no land. All my servants were gathered there for the work.
v17: At my table were one hundred fifty Jews and officials, besides those who came to us from the nations around us.
v18: What was prepared each day was one ox and six choice sheep; birds were also prepared for me, and every ten days an abundance of every kind of wine. Yet with all this I did not demand the governor’s allowance, because the service was heavy on this people.
v19: Remember me, my God, for good, according to all that I have done for this people.
</p>

##Notes
v01: The crisis inside the community interrupts the wall narrative. External opposition is not the only threat to restoration; economic exploitation among fellow Jews becomes equally urgent.
v05: Debt has moved from mortgaged property to the loss of children into debt servitude. The complaint emphasizes shared kinship: “our flesh is like the flesh of our relatives.”
v07: “interest” — The Hebrew term can describe interest or usurious increase. The issue is lending that deepens the vulnerability of fellow community members.
v08: Nehemiah contrasts redeeming Jews from foreign slavery with a system that effectively sells them again through internal debt.
v11: “the hundredth part” likely refers to a recurring interest charge, often understood as approximately one percent for the relevant period of assessment.
v13: Shaking out the garment enacts a symbolic curse: covenant-breaking is pictured as being emptied from household and property.
v15: Nehemiah presents his refusal of customary governor support as an act shaped by fear of God and the people’s heavy burden.
v19: The prayer “Remember me” recurs later in the book and places Nehemiah’s public conduct before God’s judgment.

##Vocabulary
v01: צְעָקָה (tse'aqah)
“Outcry.” A cry arising from oppression or urgent distress.
v03: עָרַב (arav)
“To pledge” or “give as security.” To place property under obligation for debt.
v07: נָשָׁא (nasha)
“To lend at interest” or “act as creditor.” Used in the economic complaint.
v08: פָּדָה (padah)
“To redeem.” To buy back or secure release from bondage.
v09: יִרְאָה (yirah)
“Fear” or “reverence.” Here the fear of God becomes an economic ethic.
v13: חֹצֶן (chotsen)
“Fold” or “bosom of a garment.” The part Nehemiah shakes out in the symbolic act.
v19: זָכַר (zakhar)
“To remember.” In prayer, to attend to and act with regard to someone or something.
