## David Organizes the Army

<p>
v01: David counted the people who were with him and set over them commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds.

v02: David sent out the people, one third under the hand of Joab, one third under the hand of Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab’s brother, and one third under the hand of Ittai the Gittite. The king said to the people, “I myself will surely go out with you also.”

v03: But the people said, “You shall not go out, for if we flee, they will not set their heart upon us, and if half of us die, they will not set their heart upon us. But you are worth ten thousand of us. So now it is better that you be ready to help us from the city.”

v04: The king said to them, “Whatever is good in your eyes I will do.” So the king stood beside the gate, and all the people went out by hundreds and by thousands.
</p>

## Deal Gently with Absalom

<p>
v05: The king commanded Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, saying, “Deal gently with the young man Absalom for my sake.” And all the people heard when the king commanded all the commanders concerning Absalom.
</p>

## Absalom’s Army Is Defeated

<p>
v06: The people went out into the field against Israel, and the battle was in the forest of Ephraim.

v07: There the people of Israel were struck before the servants of David, and the slaughter there was great on that day, twenty thousand.

v08: The battle spread there over the face of all the land, and the forest devoured more people that day than the sword devoured.
</p>

## Absalom Is Caught in the Tree

<p>
v09: Absalom happened to meet the servants of David. Absalom was riding on his mule, and the mule went under the thick branches of a great terebinth, and his head caught fast in the terebinth. He was left hanging between the heavens and the earth, while the mule that was under him went on.

v10: One man saw it and told Joab and said, “Look, I saw Absalom hanging in a terebinth.”

v11: Joab said to the man who told him, “And look, you saw him? Why then did you not strike him there to the ground? I would have given you ten pieces of silver and a belt.”

v12: The man said to Joab, “Even if I weighed in my hand a thousand pieces of silver, I would not stretch out my hand against the king’s son, for in our hearing the king commanded you and Abishai and Ittai, saying, ‘Guard whoever touches the young man Absalom.’

v13: Otherwise I would have dealt falsely against my own life—and no matter is hidden from the king—and you yourself would have stood aloof.”

v14: Joab said, “I will not wait like this before you.” He took three staffs in his hand and thrust them into the heart of Absalom while he was still alive in the heart of the terebinth.

v15: Then ten young men who carried Joab’s armor surrounded Absalom and struck him and put him to death.
</p>

## Absalom Is Buried

<p>
v16: Joab blew the ram’s horn, and the people returned from pursuing Israel, for Joab held back the people.

v17: They took Absalom and threw him into a great pit in the forest and raised over him a very great heap of stones. And all Israel fled, each to his tent.

v18: Now Absalom in his lifetime had taken and set up for himself a pillar that is in the King’s Valley, for he said, “I have no son to cause my name to be remembered.” He called the pillar after his own name, and it is called Absalom’s Monument to this day.
</p>

## Ahimaaz Wants to Carry the News

<p>
v19: Then Ahimaaz son of Zadok said, “Please let me run and bring the king good news that the LORD has judged him from the hand of his enemies.”

v20: Joab said to him, “You shall not be a man of good news this day. You may bring good news another day, but this day you shall not bring good news, because the king’s son is dead.”

v21: Then Joab said to the Cushite, “Go, tell the king what you have seen.” The Cushite bowed down to Joab and ran.

v22: Ahimaaz son of Zadok again said to Joab, “Whatever happens, please let me also run after the Cushite.” Joab said, “Why should you run, my son, when there is no reward for the news?”

v23: “Whatever happens,” he said, “I will run.” So he said to him, “Run.” Then Ahimaaz ran by the way of the plain and passed the Cushite.
</p>

## David Waits for News

<p>
v24: David was sitting between the two gates. The watchman went up to the roof of the gate by the wall, lifted his eyes, and looked, and behold, a man running alone.

v25: The watchman called and told the king. The king said, “If he is alone, there is news in his mouth.” He kept coming nearer and nearer.

v26: Then the watchman saw another man running. The watchman called to the gatekeeper and said, “Look, another man is running alone.” The king said, “This one also is bringing news.”

v27: The watchman said, “I see the running of the first like the running of Ahimaaz son of Zadok.” The king said, “He is a good man and comes with good news.”
</p>

## Ahimaaz Reports Victory but Avoids Absalom’s Death

<p>
v28: Ahimaaz called and said to the king, “Peace!” He bowed down before the king with his face to the ground and said, “Blessed be the LORD your God, who has delivered up the men who lifted their hand against my lord the king.”

v29: The king said, “Is it well with the young man Absalom?” Ahimaaz said, “I saw a great tumult when Joab sent the king’s servant and me your servant, but I do not know what it was.”

v30: The king said, “Turn aside and stand here.” So he turned aside and stood.
</p>

## The Cushite Tells David Absalom Is Dead

<p>
v31: And behold, the Cushite came. The Cushite said, “Let my lord the king receive good news, for the LORD has judged for you this day from the hand of all who rose against you.”

v32: The king said to the Cushite, “Is it well with the young man Absalom?” The Cushite said, “May the enemies of my lord the king and all who rise against you for evil be like the young man!”
</p>

## David Weeps for Absalom

<p>
v33: The king trembled. He went up to the upper room over the gate and wept. As he went, thus he said:

“My son Absalom!
My son, my son Absalom!
If only I had died instead of you,
Absalom, my son,
my son!”
</p>
## Notes
v02: Ittai the Gittite commands one third of David’s army despite being a recent foreign ally, showing the trust David now places in him.
v03: The troops recognize David as the strategic target and refuse to risk him in open battle.
v05: David’s public command to “deal gently” with Absalom creates the moral tension of the chapter. Every commander and soldier knows the king wants his son spared.
v08: “the forest devoured more” may refer to terrain, pits, branches, confusion, and flight casualties. The forest itself is personified as an agent of death.
v09: The text says Absalom’s “head” caught in the tree, not specifically his hair. His famous hair from chapter 14 may invite association, but TSW does not insert it.
v09: Hanging “between the heavens and the earth” visually expresses Absalom’s suspended political and familial status: neither safely mounted nor grounded.
v12–13: The unnamed soldier refuses a large reward because David’s command was public and because he does not trust Joab to protect him afterward.
v14: The objects Joab thrusts into Absalom are literally “staffs” or “rods,” traditionally rendered darts. The following ten men complete the killing.
v17: Absalom receives burial beneath a stone heap in the forest, contrasting with the monument he had built to preserve his name.
v18: This verse says Absalom had no son, while 14:27 says three sons were born to him. They may have died before the monument was made, or the traditions may reflect different stages. TSW preserves both statements without harmonization.
v19: Ahimaaz interprets victory as the LORD’s judicial deliverance, but Joab knows the king will not experience the news as simply good.
v29: Ahimaaz evades David’s direct question about Absalom, whether from ignorance or unwillingness to speak the death.
v32: The Cushite conveys Absalom’s death indirectly through a wish that all David’s enemies share his fate.
v33: David’s lament is stripped of royal language and political analysis. He repeats only “my son” and wishes for substitutionary death.

##Vocabulary

v03: שִׂים לֵב (sim lev)
“To set the heart.” To regard as the decisive target.

v05: אַט (at)
“Gently.” David’s command concerning Absalom.

v19: בָּשַׂר (basar)
“To bring good news.”

v28: שָׁלוֹם (shalom)
“Peace” or “well-being.” Ahimaaz’s opening announcement.
