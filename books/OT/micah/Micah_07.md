## The Corruption of the Nation
<p>
v01: Woe is me!
For I have become
as when the summer fruit has been gathered,
as when the grapes have been gleaned:
there is no cluster to eat,
no first-ripe fig that my soul desires.
</p>

<p>
v02: The faithful have perished from the land,
and there is no upright person among mankind.
They all lie in wait for blood,
and each hunts his brother with a net.
v03: Their hands are skilled in doing evil.
The ruler asks,
and the judge takes a bribe,
and the great man speaks
the desire of his soul,
and together they weave it.
</p>

<p>
v04: The best of them is like a brier,
the most upright of them
worse than a thorn hedge.
The day of your watchmen,
of your punishment, has come.
Now shall be their confusion.
</p>

<p>
v05: Put no trust in a neighbor;
have no confidence in a friend.
Guard the doors of your mouth
from her who lies in your arms.
v06: For son treats father with contempt,
daughter rises against her mother,
daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law;
a man's enemies
are the men of his own house.
</p>

## Hope in the LORD
<p>
v07: But as for me,
I will look to the LORD.
I will wait for the God of my salvation.
My God will hear me.
</p>

<p>
v08: Rejoice not over me, my enemy.
Though I have fallen,
I shall rise.
Though I sit in darkness,
the LORD will be a light to me.
v09: I will bear the indignation of the LORD
because I have sinned against him,
until he pleads my cause
and executes justice for me.
He will bring me out into the light.
I shall look upon his righteousness.
</p>

<p>
v10: Then my enemy will see,
and shame will cover her who said to me,
“Where is the LORD your God?”
My eyes will look upon her.
Now she will be trampled down
like mud in the streets.
</p>

## The Future Restoration of Zion
<p>
v11: A day for building your walls!
In that day
the boundary shall be far extended.
v12: In that day
they shall come to you
from Assyria and the cities of Egypt,
from Egypt to the River,
from sea to sea,
and from mountain to mountain.
v13: But the earth shall be desolate
because of its inhabitants,
for the fruit of their deeds.
</p>

## A Prayer for Mercy
<p>
v14: Shepherd your people with your staff,
the flock of your inheritance,
who dwell alone in a forest
in the midst of a fruitful land.
Let them graze in Bashan and Gilead
as in the days of old.
</p>

<p>
v15: “As in the days
when you came out of the land of Egypt,
I will show them marvelous things.”
v16: The nations shall see and be ashamed
of all their might.
They shall lay their hands on their mouths;
their ears shall be deaf.
v17: They shall lick the dust like a serpent,
like crawling things of the earth.
They shall come trembling from their strongholds.
They shall turn in fear to the LORD our God,
and they shall stand in awe of you.
</p>

## The LORD's Unmatched Mercy
<p>
v18: Who is a God like you,
forgiving iniquity
and passing over transgression
for the remnant of his inheritance?
He does not retain his anger forever,
because he delights in steadfast love.
</p>

<p>
v19: He will again have compassion upon us.
He will tread our iniquities underfoot.
You will cast all their sins
into the depths of the sea.
v20: You will show faithfulness to Jacob
and steadfast love to Abraham,
as you swore to our fathers
from the days of old.
</p>

## Notes

v01–06: Micah laments the moral collapse of society, where corruption affects every level of human relationships.  

v02: The disappearance of the faithful echoes prophetic themes of widespread covenant unfaithfulness.  

v03: Political, judicial, and social leaders cooperate in injustice rather than restraining it.  

v05–06: Sin has become so pervasive that even family relationships are fractured.  

v07: The prophet responds to societal collapse not with despair but with trust in God.  

v08–09: The speaker acknowledges guilt while remaining confident in God's eventual vindication.  

v09: Divine discipline and divine salvation are held together within the covenant relationship.  

v11–12: The restoration of Zion includes the gathering of people from distant lands.  

v14: The imagery of shepherding recalls God's covenant care for Israel throughout its history.  

v15: The future deliverance is compared to the exodus from Egypt.  

v16–17: The nations are humbled before God's power and saving acts.  

v18: The book concludes by focusing on God's character rather than Israel's failures.  

v18: Micah's name means “Who is like the LORD?” and the closing question echoes that theme.  

v19: The casting of sins into the depths of the sea portrays complete forgiveness and removal of guilt.  

v20: The final verses root future hope in God's covenant promises to Abraham and Jacob.  

## Vocabulary

v01: בִּכּוּרָה (bikkûrâ)  
“First-ripe fig.” The earliest and most desired fruit of the harvest.  

v02: חָסִיד (ḥāsîḏ)  
“Faithful one.” A person marked by covenant loyalty.  

v03: שֹׁחַד (šōḥaḏ)  
“Bribe.” Payment that corrupts justice.  

v04: סִבּוּךְ קוֹץ (sibbûḵ qôṣ)  
“Thorn hedge.” An image of harmful and troublesome people.  

v07: יֶשַׁע (yešaʿ)  
“Salvation.” Deliverance and rescue provided by God.  

v08: אוֹר (ʾôr)  
“Light.” A symbol of life, guidance, and divine favor.  

v09: רִיב (rîḇ)  
“To plead a cause.” To act as an advocate in a legal dispute.  

v14: שֵׁבֶט (šēḇeṭ)  
“Staff.” A shepherd's rod symbolizing guidance and authority.  

v18: נָשָׂא עָוֹן (nāśāʾ ʿāwōn)
“To forgive iniquity.” Literally “to bear” or “carry away” guilt.

v18: חֶסֶד (ḥeseḏ)  
“Steadfast love.” Loyal covenant mercy and faithfulness.  

v19: רָחַם (rāḥam)  
“To have compassion.” To show tender mercy and care.  

v20: אֱמֶת (ʾĕmeṯ)  
“Faithfulness” or “truth.” Reliability and covenant dependability.
