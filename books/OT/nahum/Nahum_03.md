## Woe to the City of Blood

<p>
v01: Woe to the city of blood,  
full of lies,  
full of plunder—  
its prey never departs!

v02: The crack of the whip,  
the rumble of the wheel,  
galloping horse  
and bounding chariot!

v03: Horsemen charging,  
flashing sword,  
glittering spear,  
a multitude of slain,  
heaps of corpses,  
without end to the bodies—  
they stumble over their bodies.
</p>

## Because of Her Many Betrayals

<p>
v04: Because of the many prostitutions  
of the prostitute,  
gracious in appearance,  
mistress of sorceries,  
who sells nations through her prostitutions  
and clans through her sorceries:

v05: “Look, I am against you,”  
says the LORD of Hosts.

“I will lift your skirts over your face;  
I will show nations your nakedness  
and kingdoms your shame.

v06: I will throw filth upon you;  
I will treat you with contempt  
and make you a spectacle.

v07: Then everyone who sees you  
will flee from you and say,  
‘Nineveh is devastated!  
Who will mourn for her?’

Where will I seek  
comforters for you?”
</p>

## Are You Better Than Thebes?

<p>
v08: Are you better than No-Amon,  
which sat among the Nile channels,  
with water surrounding her,  
whose rampart was the sea  
and whose wall was water?

v09: Cush was her strength,  
and Egypt without limit;  
Put and the Libyans  
were among her helpers.

v10: Yet she also went into exile,  
she went into captivity.  
Her infants were dashed to pieces  
at the head of every street.  
They cast lots for her honored ones,  
and all her great ones  
were bound in chains.
</p>

## You Also Will Become Drunk

<p>
v11: You also will become drunk;  
you will hide yourself.  
You also will seek refuge  
from the enemy.

v12: All your fortresses  
are like fig trees with first-ripe figs;  
when they are shaken,  
they fall into the mouth of the eater.

v13: Look, your troops within you  
are like women.  
The gates of your land  
are opened wide to your enemies;  
fire consumes your bars.
</p>

## Prepare for the Siege

<p>
v14: Draw water for the siege!  
Strengthen your forts!  
Go into the clay  
and tread the mortar;  
take hold of the brick mold!

v15: There the fire will consume you;  
the sword will cut you down.  
It will consume you like the locust.

Multiply yourself like the locust!  
Multiply like the grasshopper!

v16: You multiplied your merchants  
more than the stars of heaven.  
The locust strips the land  
and flies away.
</p>

## Your Leaders Have Vanished

<p>
v17: Your guards are like grasshoppers,  
and your officials like swarms of locusts  
that settle on walls  
on a cold day.  
When the sun rises, they fly away,  
and no one knows  
where they are.

v18: Your shepherds are asleep,  
O king of Assyria;  
your nobles lie still.  
Your people are scattered  
on the mountains,  
and there is no one  
to gather them.
</p>

## There Is No Healing for Your Wound

<p>
v19: There is no relief  
for your fracture;  
your wound is grievous.

All who hear the news about you  
clap their hands over you,  
for upon whom has your evil  
not passed continually?
</p>

## Notes

v01: “city of blood” — Nineveh is condemned not for one isolated act of violence but as a city characterized by bloodshed, deception, and plunder.

v02–03: The Hebrew piles sound and movement upon one another. The short clauses imitate the pace and confusion of battle.

v04: “prostitutions” — The metaphor portrays imperial seduction, exploitation, and betrayal. It is directed against Nineveh's political practices and is retained because the prophetic rhetoric depends upon the image.

v05–06: The exposure imagery is intentionally humiliating and violent. TSW preserves the prophetic metaphor without presenting humiliation or sexualized violence as morally exemplary human behavior.

v08: “No-Amon” — The Hebrew name refers to Thebes in Egypt. The city's waterways and international allies did not prevent its fall, making it a warning to Nineveh.

v09: “Cush … Put … Libyans” — These names identify regions and peoples allied with or connected to Egypt. The verse stresses the breadth of Thebes' resources rather than providing a complete political map.

v10: The destruction of infants reflects the brutal realities of ancient conquest. The text reports this violence as part of Thebes' devastation; it does not soften the horror.

v13: “your troops … are like women” — This is an ancient military taunt using gendered assumptions about combat strength. TSW retains the source imagery as written rather than replacing or endorsing the cultural stereotype.

v15–17: Locust imagery shifts rapidly. Nineveh is told to multiply like locusts, yet its merchants and officials also disappear like insects that strip a place and fly away.

v18: “shepherds” — The term refers figuratively to Assyria's leaders. Their sleep is the stillness of failed or dead leadership while the people scatter without anyone to gather them.

v19: The book closes with the nations applauding Assyria's fall because its violence had reached them repeatedly. The final question leaves Nineveh's judgment within the memory of widespread imperial harm.

## Vocabulary

v01: עִיר דָּמִים (ir damim)  
“City of blood.” A city characterized by bloodshed and violent guilt.

v04: זְנוּנִים (zenunim)  
“Prostitutions” or “acts of unfaithfulness.” Used metaphorically for seductive and exploitative relationships.

v04: כְּשָׁפִים (keshafim)  
“Sorceries.” Practices associated with magical manipulation or enchantment.

v08: נֹא אָמוֹן (No-Amon)  
“No-Amon,” the biblical name for Thebes in Egypt.

v10: גּוֹרָל (goral)  
“Lot.” An object used in casting lots to distribute persons, property, or decisions.

v12: בִּכּוּרָה (bikkurah)  
“First-ripe fig.” An early fig easily shaken from the tree and quickly eaten.

v15: אַרְבֶּה (arbeh)  
“Locust.” A swarming insect used here as an image of multiplication and devastating disappearance.

v18: רֹעִים (ro'im)  
“Shepherds.” Literally those who tend flocks; figuratively the leaders of Assyria.

v19: שֶׁבֶר (shever)  
“Fracture,” “breaking,” or “ruin.” A wound or collapse for which the verse says there is no relief.
