v03: Opposite the twenty cubits belonging to the inner court, and opposite the pavement belonging to the outer court, gallery faced gallery on the third level.

v04: In front of the chambers was a passage, ten cubits wide, leading inward; a path one cubit wide; and their entrances faced north.

</p>

v08: For the length of the chambers toward the outer court was fifty cubits, but there before the temple it was one hundred cubits.

v09: Below these chambers was an entrance from the east, for one entering them from the outer court.
</p>

v10: In the width of the court wall, toward the east, opposite the separate area and opposite the building, were chambers.

v11: A path lay before them, like the appearance of the chambers that faced north: their length was the same as their width, and so were all their exits, their arrangements, and their entrances.

v12: As were the entrances of the chambers that faced south, there was an entrance at the head of the path—the path before the wall, the wall facing east—for those entering them.
</p>

v14: “When the priests enter, they shall not go out from the holy place into the outer court. There they shall leave the garments in which they minister, for they are holy; they shall clothe themselves and put on other garments, and then they may approach what belongs to the people.”

##Notes

v03: “gallery faced gallery”: The repeated word describes a structural feature whose precise form is uncertain.

##Vocabulary

v01: לִשְׁכָּה (lishkah)
“A chamber or designated room within the temple complex.” In this chapter these chambers are not incidental storage spaces; they order priestly movement, food, garments, and holy offerings.

v13: קֹדֶשׁ (qodesh)
“Holiness, or that which is set apart for God.” The word recurs throughout the chapter for chambers, offerings, garments, and space, binding the architectural details to Israel’s worship.

v13: קָדְשֵׁי הַקֳּדָשִׁים (qodshei haqqodashim)
“Most holy offerings.” This intensified phrase marks offerings with the highest degree of sanctity and explains why they are eaten and kept within holy chambers.

v13: מִנְחָה (minchah)
“Grain offering.” Here it is named with the sin offering and guilt offering as food or sacred material to be handled within the holy place.
