## Notes

- **38:2–3** “Chief prince of Meshech and Tubal” follows the traditional reading of *rosh* as “chief.” The Hebrew may also be read as a place-name, “prince of Rosh, Meshech, and Tubal.” The text does not identify Gog with a contemporary nation; it gathers distant peoples into a symbolic hostile force.

- **38:4, 16** God both draws Gog forward and judges his assault. The hooks in Gog’s jaws and the declaration, “I will bring you,” do not soften Gog’s evil intention in verses 10–12; the chapter holds divine purpose and human violence together without explaining their relation.

- **38:8, 11, 14** Israel’s security is stressed repeatedly. The image of unwalled villages exposes vulnerability, yet the passage presents this security within God’s restoration of a people gathered from the nations.

- **38:13** “Young lions” may refer literally to young lions, perhaps as an image for aggressive trading powers, or figuratively to subordinate settlements or rulers. The question exposes the invaders’ desire for plunder.

- **38:16, 23** God’s being “shown holy” is not withdrawal from the nations but public self-disclosure through judgment. The nations’ knowledge of the LORD is a central purpose of the chapter.

- **38:17** The question links Gog with earlier prophetic expectation, though it does not name a particular prior prophecy. The verse invites readers to hear this vision within Israel’s wider prophetic witness to judgment on hostile powers.

- **38:21–22** The defeat of Gog comes through internal violence, pestilence, bloodshed, storm, hail, fire, and sulfur. The piling up of images gives the judgment cosmic scale rather than describing a conventional battle alone.

## Vocabulary

- **38:2 — גּוֹג (*Gog*)**: The name designates the leader addressed throughout the chapter. Gog functions as the concentrated figure of a vast coalition opposed to restored Israel.

- **38:8 — אַחֲרִית (*’aḥarit*)**: “Latter” or “final” period. Here, in “the latter years” and again in verse 16 as “the latter days,” the term places Gog’s assault within God’s decisive future purpose without specifying a timetable.

- **38:8 — בֶּטַח (*beṭaḥ*)**: “Security” or “safety.” Its repeated use emphasizes Israel’s unguarded rest and heightens the contrast between apparent vulnerability and God’s protection.

- **38:16 — קָדַשׁ (*qadash*)**: “To be holy” or “show oneself holy.” God’s holiness is made visible before the nations through the judgment of Gog, revealing God’s distinct and sovereign character.

- **38:19 — קִנְאָה (*qin’ah*)**: “Jealousy” or “zeal.” In this passage it expresses God’s intense, covenantal opposition to the assault upon the land and people claimed as God’s own.
