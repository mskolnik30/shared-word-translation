<p>He brought me into the temple hall and measured the pilasters: six cubits wide on this side and six cubits wide on that side—the width of the tent.</p>

<p>The width of the doorway was ten cubits, and the sides of the doorway were five cubits on this side and five cubits on that side. He measured its length: forty cubits; and its width: twenty cubits.</p>

v03:
<p>Then he went inward and measured the pilaster of the doorway: two cubits. The doorway was six cubits, and the width of the doorway was seven cubits.</p>

v04:
<p>He measured its length: twenty cubits; and its width: twenty cubits, before the temple hall. Then he said to me, “This is the most holy place.”</p>

v05:
<p>He measured the wall of the house: six cubits. The width of each side chamber was four cubits, all around the house, all around.</p>

v06:
<p>The side chambers were chamber beside chamber, thirty times over in three levels. They entered into the wall belonging to the house, around the side chambers, all around, so that they might be supported, but they were not supported by the wall of the house.</p>

v07:
<p>The side chambers widened and went upward, story by story, because the structure of the house went upward, story by story, all around the house. Therefore the house became wider upward; and from the lowest level one went up to the highest through the middle level.</p>

v08:
<p>I saw that the house had a raised base all around. The foundations of the side chambers were a full rod, six cubits to the joint.</p>

<p>The width of the outer wall of the side chamber was five cubits. The open space beside the side chambers belonging to the house—</p>

v10:

v11:
<p>The doorway of the side chamber opened onto the open space: one doorway facing north and one doorway facing south. The width of the open space was five cubits, all around.</p>

v12:
<p>The building that faced the separate area on the western side was seventy cubits wide. The wall of the building was five cubits wide all around, and its length was ninety cubits.</p>

v13:

v14:

v15:

## Notes

- v4: “Most holy place” translates *qōdeš haqqodāšîm*, literally “holy of holies.” The doubled form marks this inner room as the most intensely holy space within the temple.
- v6: The wording distinguishes the side chambers’ supports from the main wall of the house. Their structure is carefully attached around the building without being inserted into or weakening its principal wall.
- v7: The architectural description is difficult at points. The Hebrew emphasizes the upward widening and circling movement of the chambers, while the exact route of the stairway remains somewhat unclear.
- v16: “Shuttered windows” renders a term that can suggest closed, fixed, or screened windows. The repeated references to covering and wood paneling produce an enclosed, ordered interior.
- v18–20: Unlike the four-faced living creatures in Ezekiel’s earlier vision, each cherub in this temple decoration has two visible faces: human and young lion. The imagery joins living creatures and palm trees throughout the sacred space.
- v22: The wooden altar is called “the table that is before YHWH.” The two descriptions hold together altar and table, sacrifice and the presence of God, without explaining their precise relationship.
- v20: The verse ends abruptly with “and the wall of the temple hall.” The Hebrew may continue the description of the carvings, but its compressed grammar leaves the final connection unstated.

## Vocabulary

- v4: קֹדֶשׁ קָדָשִׁים (*qōdeš qodāšîm*) — “most holy place.” The superlative construction identifies the innermost chamber as sacred beyond the surrounding temple spaces.
- v6: צֵלָע (*ṣēlāʿ*) — “side chamber.” The word describes the chambers arranged around the temple house, contributing to the layered structure of the vision.
- v12: גִּזְרָה (*gizrâ*) — “separate area.” This designated zone lies alongside the temple complex and is repeatedly measured as part of its ordered holiness.
- v15: אַתִּיק (*ʾattîq*) — “gallery.” The term refers to architectural projections or galleries; its precise form is uncertain, but it belongs to the complex surrounding structure.
- v18: כְּרוּב (*kĕrûb*) — “cherub.” These heavenly beings appear here as carved temple imagery, marking the house as a place associated with God’s holy presence.
- v18: תִּמֹרָה (*timôrâ*) — “palm tree.” The repeated palm-tree carvings interweave with the cherubim across the walls and doors.
- v22: שֻׁלְחָן (*šulḥān*) — “table.” The altar is identified as a table before YHWH, giving this furnishing a relational orientation toward God’s presence.
