## Notes

- v02: “Shepherds” is a political and spiritual image for Israel’s leaders. The accusation turns on the repeated verb “shepherd”: those entrusted with the flock have instead used the flock to sustain themselves.
- v04: The sequence of neglected sheep—the weak, sick, injured, straying, and lost—is answered in reverse by the LORD’s own actions in v16. Leadership is measured here by care for those most exposed to harm.
- v12: “A day of cloud and deep darkness” evokes catastrophe and disorientation. The LORD’s search reaches the flock precisely in the conditions that have scattered it.
- v16: The final clause can refer back to the flock as a whole: “I will shepherd it with justice.” The promise of tender restoration is not separated from judgment on those within the flock who are fat and strong at others’ expense.
- v23: “My servant David” speaks of a Davidic ruler who will embody the shepherding Israel’s leaders failed to provide. The passage holds together the LORD’s direct shepherding in vv11–16 and the raising up of this one shepherd.
- v25: The “covenant of peace” is not merely an inward state. It brings safety, fruitful land, freedom from predation, and release from oppressive labor.
- v29: “A planting for renown” may describe a notable or abundant planting, and may also suggest a planting that gives Israel a restored name rather than hunger and disgrace.

## Vocabulary

- v02: רֹעִים (*roʿim*) — “shepherds.” The word names both literal shepherds and Israel’s leaders; the chapter presses the contrast between shepherding the flock and feeding oneself from it.
- v04: פֶּרֶךְ (*perek*) — “harshness.” The term describes crushing, ruthless domination, sharpening the charge that the shepherds have treated the flock as subjects to exploit.
- v12: עֲרָפֶל (*ʿarafel*) — “deep darkness.” Alongside cloud, it portrays the obscurity and danger of the day in which the flock was scattered.
- v16: מִשְׁפָּט (*mishpat*) — “justice.” The LORD’s shepherding includes both restoration of the vulnerable and right judgment among the flock.
- v23: נָשִׂיא (*nasiʾ*) — “prince.” In v24 David is called a prince among the people, while the LORD alone remains their God.
- v25: בְּרִית שָׁלוֹם (*berit shalom*) — “covenant of peace.” This covenant establishes comprehensive well-being: security, freedom from threat, and life in a restored land.
- v29: מַטָּע (*mattaʿ*) — “planting.” The promised planting counters famine and public disgrace, joining the chapter’s images of pasture, fruitfulness, and secure dwelling.
