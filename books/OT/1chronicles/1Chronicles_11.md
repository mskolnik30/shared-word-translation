# 1 Chronicles 11

v01:  
<p>Then all Israel gathered to David at Hebron, saying, “Look, we are your bone and your flesh.</p>  

v02:  
<p>In times past, even when Saul was king, you were the one leading Israel out and bringing them in. And YHWH your God said to you, ‘You will shepherd my people Israel, and you will be leader over my people Israel.’”</p>  

v03:  
<p>So all the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron, and David made a covenant with them at Hebron before YHWH. Then they anointed David king over Israel, according to the word of YHWH through Samuel.</p>  

v04:  
<p>David and all Israel went to Jerusalem—that is, Jebus—where the Jebusites, the inhabitants of the land, were.</p>  

v05:  
<p>The inhabitants of Jebus said to David, “You will not come in here.” But David captured the stronghold of Zion, which is the City of David.</p>  

v06:  
<p>David said, “Whoever strikes down a Jebusite first will become chief and commander.” Joab son of Zeruiah went up first, and he became chief.</p>  

v07:  
<p>David lived in the stronghold; therefore they called it the City of David.</p>  

v08:  
<p>He built up the city all around, from the Millo and all around it; and Joab restored the rest of the city.</p>  

v09:  
<p>David became greater and greater, for YHWH of Armies was with him.</p>  

v10:  
<p>These are the chiefs of David’s warriors, who gave him strong support in his kingdom, together with all Israel, to make him king, according to the word of YHWH concerning Israel.</p>  

v11:  
<p>This is the number of David’s warriors: Jashobeam son of Hachmoni, chief of the Thirty. He raised his spear against three hundred slain at one time.</p>  

v12:  
<p>After him was Eleazar son of Dodo the Ahohite; he was among the three warriors.</p>  

v13:  
<p>He was with David at Pas-dammim, where the Philistines had gathered for battle. There was a portion of the field full of barley, and the people fled from before the Philistines.</p>  

v14:  
<p>But they took their stand in the middle of the field and defended it, and struck down the Philistines. And YHWH brought about a great deliverance.</p>  

v15:  
<p>Three of the thirty chiefs went down to David at the rock, at the cave of Adullam, while a Philistine camp was encamped in the Valley of Rephaim.</p>  

v16:  
<p>David was then in the stronghold, and the Philistine garrison was then in Bethlehem.</p>  

v17:  
<p>David longed and said, “Who will give me water to drink from the well of Bethlehem that is by the gate?”</p>  

v18:  
<p>So the three broke through the Philistine camp, drew water from the well of Bethlehem that is by the gate, carried it, and brought it to David. But David would not drink it; he poured it out to YHWH.</p>  

v19:  
<p>He said, “Far be it from me, before my God, that I should do this! Should I drink the blood of these men—their lives? For they brought it at the risk of their lives.” Therefore he would not drink it. These things the three warriors did.</p>  

v20:  
<p>Abishai, Joab’s brother, was chief of the three. He raised his spear against three hundred slain, and he had a name among the three.</p>  

v21:  
<p>Among the three, he was more honored than the two, and he became their commander; but he did not attain to the three.</p>  

v22:  
<p>Benaiah son of Jehoiada, son of a valiant man from Kabzeel, a man of many deeds—he struck down the two Ariels of Moab. He also went down and struck down a lion in the middle of a pit on a day of snow.</p>  

v23:  
<p>He struck down an Egyptian, a man five cubits tall. The Egyptian had a spear in his hand like a weaver’s beam, but Benaiah went down to him with a staff, snatched the spear from the Egyptian’s hand, and killed him with his own spear.</p>  

v24:  
<p>These things Benaiah son of Jehoiada did, and he had a name among the three warriors.</p>  

v25:  
<p>He was honored among the Thirty, but he did not attain to the three. David put him over his guard.</p>

## Notes

- **11:3** The covenant is made “before YHWH.” David’s kingship is not presented merely as a political agreement between tribal leaders and a military ruler; it is established in the presence of Israel’s God.  
- **11:8** The verb rendered “restored” can also carry the sense of preserving alive or reviving. Joab’s work is portrayed as rebuilding and renewing the parts of the city beyond David’s construction.  
- **11:10–25** The chapter honors David’s warriors while repeatedly locating David’s kingship within YHWH’s word and crediting YHWH with deliverance (v. 14). Human courage and divine saving action are held together without being collapsed into one another.  
- **11:19** David calls the water “the blood” and “the lives” of the men because they obtained it at the risk of their lives. Pouring it out to YHWH refuses to treat their costly loyalty as something for his own gratification.  
- **11:21, 25** “The three” and “the Thirty” appear to be recognized circles of elite warriors. The wording preserves a distinction between exceptional honor and membership in the highest-ranked group.  
- **11:22** The expression translated “the two Ariels of Moab” is uncertain. *Ariel* may be a personal or clan designation, or it may evoke “lion-like men”; the text does not explain the phrase.  

## Vocabulary

- **11:3 — בְּרִית (*berit*)**: “covenant.” David’s agreement with Israel’s elders is made before YHWH, framing the transfer of kingship as a binding relationship accountable to God.  
- **11:9 — צְבָאוֹת (*tseva’ot*)**: “armies” or “hosts.” In “YHWH of Armies,” the title presents YHWH as ruler over vast forces and accompanies David’s growing strength.  
- **11:10 — גִּבּוֹרִים (*gibborim*)**: “warriors” or “mighty ones.” The term introduces the distinguished fighters whose deeds fill the remainder of the chapter.  
- **11:14 — תְּשׁוּעָה (*teshu‘ah*)**: “deliverance.” Although warriors defend the field, the text attributes the great rescue to YHWH.  
- **11:19 — נֶפֶשׁ (*nephesh*)**: “life,” “self,” or “person.” David identifies the water with the lives of the men who brought it, emphasizing the cost at which it was obtained.
