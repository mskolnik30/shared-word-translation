## Notes

- **v1:** The term rendered “fortress” can refer to a fortified royal complex or citadel. Here it describes the temple as a great and weighty undertaking belonging to God rather than merely to a human ruler.  
- **v5:** “Fill their hand” is an idiom associated with consecration and commissioning. David’s invitation concerns both a voluntary offering and a willing dedication to the LORD’s work.  
- **v15:** “Sojourners” and “residents” express human transience before God. The statement that “there is no hope” likely speaks of no lasting security or permanence on earth, not necessarily a denial of hope in God.  
- **v20:** The people bow before both the LORD and the king, but the narrative distinguishes their worshipful response to the LORD from their public honor and submission to the king.  
- **v23:** Calling Solomon’s seat “the throne of the LORD” presents Israel’s monarchy as exercised under the LORD’s kingship; it does not erase the distinction between Solomon and God.  
- **v22:** “A second time” may refer to the public confirmation of Solomon’s kingship following an earlier anointing and accession (see 1 Kings 1).  

## Vocabulary

- **v1: בִּירָה (*bîrâ*)** — “fortress.” The word portrays the temple project as an imposing royal and sacred complex, emphasizing its scale and its belonging to the LORD.  
- **v5: מִתְנַדֵּב (*mitnaddev*)** — “one who gives willingly.” Voluntary generosity is repeated throughout this chapter as the fitting response to God’s prior provision.  
- **v9: לֵב שָׁלֵם (*lēv shālēm*)** — “whole heart.” The phrase describes undivided inward commitment, joining generosity, joy, and integrity before God.  
- **v11: מַמְלָכָה (*mamlākhâ*)** — “kingdom.” David’s praise locates every human kingdom, including Israel’s monarchy, within the LORD’s universal rule.  
- **v14: הִתְנַדֵּב (*hitnaddev*)** — “to give willingly.” The verb underscores David’s confession that even the people’s capacity to offer freely comes from God.  
- **v15: גֵּרִים (*gērîm*)** — “sojourners.” Israel’s gathered community confesses that it lives before God without ultimate possession or permanence.  
- **v17: בֹּחֵן לֵבָב (*bōḥēn lēvāv*)** — “one who tests the heart.” God’s concern is not only the abundance of the offering but the truthfulness and uprightness of those who give.  
- **v23: כִּסֵּא יְהוָה (*kissēʾ YHWH*)** — “the throne of the LORD.” Solomon’s royal authority is framed as derivative and accountable to the LORD’s reign.
