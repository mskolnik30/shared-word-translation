# 1 Chronicles 18

v01:  
<p>
After this, David struck the Philistines and subdued them. He took Gath and its surrounding towns from the hand of the Philistines.  
</p>

v02:  
<p>
He struck Moab, and the Moabites became David’s servants, bringing tribute.  
</p>

v03:  
<p>
David struck Hadadezer king of Zobah toward Hamath, as he went to establish his power at the Euphrates River.  
</p>

v04:  
<p>
David captured from him one thousand chariots, seven thousand horsemen, and twenty thousand foot soldiers. David hamstrung all the chariot horses, but left from them one hundred chariot horses.  
</p>

v05:  
<p>
When the Arameans of Damascus came to help Hadadezer king of Zobah, David struck twenty-two thousand men of the Arameans.  
</p>

v06:  
<p>
Then David placed garrisons among the Arameans of Damascus, and the Arameans became David’s servants, bringing tribute. The LORD gave David victory wherever he went.  
</p>

v07:  
<p>
David took the gold shields that had belonged to Hadadezer’s servants and brought them to Jerusalem.  
</p>

v08:  
<p>
From Tibhath and Cun, cities of Hadadezer, David took a very great quantity of bronze. With it Solomon made the bronze Sea, the pillars, and the bronze vessels.  
</p>

v09:  
<p>
When Toi king of Hamath heard that David had struck all the army of Hadadezer king of Zobah,  
</p>

v10:  
<p>
he sent his son Hadoram to King David, to ask after his welfare and to bless him because he had fought against Hadadezer and struck him; for Hadadezer had been at war with Toi. Hadoram brought articles of gold, silver, and bronze.  
</p>

v11:  
<p>
King David also dedicated these to the LORD, along with the silver and gold that he had carried off from all the nations: from Edom, Moab, the Ammonites, the Philistines, and Amalek.  
</p>

v12:  
<p>
Abishai son of Zeruiah struck Edom in the Valley of Salt—eighteen thousand.  
</p>

v13:  
<p>
He placed garrisons in Edom, and all the Edomites became David’s servants. The LORD gave David victory wherever he went.  
</p>

v14:  
<p>
So David reigned over all Israel, administering justice and righteousness for all his people.  
</p>

v15:  
<p>
Joab son of Zeruiah was over the army; Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was recorder;  
</p>

v16:  
<p>
Zadok son of Ahitub and Abimelech son of Abiathar were priests; Shavsha was secretary;  
</p>

v17:  
<p>
and Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Kerethites and the Pelethites. David’s sons were foremost at the king’s side.  
</p>

## Notes

- **v3 — “establish his power”:** Literally, Hadadezer went “to set up his hand” at the Euphrates. The expression can describe establishing control, authority, or a military position in that region.  
- **v4 — “hamstrung”:** David disables the horses belonging to the captured chariot force. The text does not explain his motive; it records both the disabling of the larger force and the preservation of one hundred chariot horses.  
- **v6, 13 — “gave David victory”:** The same statement frames David’s campaigns in Aram and Edom. David acts decisively, yet the account attributes his success to the LORD.  
- **v8 — “the bronze Sea”:** This is the large bronze basin later made for the temple. The verse links David’s military acquisitions with Solomon’s future temple construction.  
- **v10 — “to bless him”:** In this diplomatic setting, “bless” expresses an act of honor or congratulation after David’s victory over a common enemy.  
- **v12 — “eighteen thousand”:** The Hebrew gives the number without explicitly repeating “men.” The context indicates those struck in battle.  
- **v14 — “justice and righteousness”:** The paired terms describe rule that is both legally just and faithful to what is right. The military account closes by emphasizing David’s governance of his own people.  
- **v16 — “Abimelech”:** The Hebrew text here names Abimelech son of Abiathar. A parallel list in 2 Samuel 8:17 has a different form of the name and lineage.  
- **v17 — “foremost at the king’s side”:** The Hebrew calls David’s sons “the first ones at the hand of the king.” It likely denotes a position of high service or rank near the king, though the precise office is not stated.  

## Vocabulary

- **v1 — כָּנַע (*kanaʿ*):** “to subdue, bring low.” David’s victory over the Philistines is described not merely as a battle won but as their being brought under his power.  
- **v2 — מִנְחָה (*minḥah*):** “gift, offering, tribute.” Here it is the payment brought by a defeated people to their ruler, carrying both political and economic significance.  
- **v6 — יָשַׁע (*yashaʿ*):** “save, deliver, give victory.” The verb credits the LORD with David’s deliverance and success wherever he goes.  
- **v11 — קָדַשׁ (*qadash*):** “make holy, dedicate.” David sets apart the captured wealth for the LORD, redirecting royal spoils toward sacred use.  
- **v14 — מִשְׁפָּט (*mishpat*):** “justice, judgment.” David’s rule includes the rendering of just decisions for the people.  
- **v14 — צְדָקָה (*tsedaqah*):** “righteousness, rightness.” Paired with *mishpat*, it portrays governance ordered toward what is right and faithful.  
- **v17 — כְּרֵתִי (*kereti*) / פְּלֵתִי (*peleti*):** “Kerethites / Pelethites.” These groups serve under Benaiah in a distinct royal military body; their exact ethnic origins and role are not fully explained here.
