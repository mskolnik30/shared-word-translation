## Wisdom Calls

<p>
v01: Does not Wisdom call?
Does not Understanding
raise her voice?

v02: On the heights beside the way,
at the crossroads,
she takes her stand.

v03: Beside the gates,
at the entrance to the city,
at the entryways
she cries aloud:

v04: “To you, human beings, I call,
and my voice is directed
to all humanity.

v05: O simple ones, learn prudence;
O fools, gain an understanding heart.
</p>

## My Mouth Speaks Truth

<p>
v06: Listen,
for I will speak noble things,
and the opening of my lips
will bring what is right.

v07: For my mouth speaks truth,
and wickedness is an abomination
to my lips.

v08: All the words of my mouth
are righteous;
there is nothing twisted
or crooked in them.

v09: They are all straightforward
to the discerning
and right to those
who find knowledge.

v10: Receive my discipline
rather than silver,
and knowledge
rather than choice gold,

v11: for wisdom is better than jewels,
and nothing you desire
can compare with her.
</p>

## Wisdom and Prudence

<p>
v12: I, Wisdom, dwell with prudence,
and I find knowledge
and discretion.

v13: The fear of the LORD
is hatred of evil.

Pride and arrogance,
the evil way,
and a perverse mouth
I hate.

v14: Counsel and sound wisdom are mine;
I am understanding;
strength is mine.

v15: By me kings reign,
and rulers decree
what is just.

v16: By me princes rule,
and nobles—
all who judge with righteousness.
</p>

<p>
v17: I love those who love me,
and those who diligently seek me
will find me.

v18: Riches and honor are with me,
enduring wealth
and righteousness.

v19: My fruit is better than gold,
even fine gold,
and my yield
than choice silver.

v20: I walk in the path of righteousness,
in the midst
of the paths of justice,

v21: giving an inheritance
to those who love me
and filling their treasuries.
</p>

## Wisdom Before the World

<p>
v22: The LORD possessed me
at the beginning of the LORD’s way,
before the works of old.

v23: From ancient times I was established,
from the beginning,
before the earth.

v24: When there were no depths,
I was brought forth,
when there were no springs
abounding with water.

v25: Before the mountains were settled,
before the hills,
I was brought forth,

v26: before the LORD had made the earth
and the open fields,
or the first dust
of the world.
</p>

<p>
v27: When the LORD established the heavens,
I was there;
when the LORD inscribed a circle
on the face of the deep;

v28: when the LORD made firm
the skies above,
when the springs of the deep
were established;

v29: when the LORD set a boundary for the sea
so that the waters would not cross the command,
when the LORD marked out
the foundations of the earth—

v30: then I was beside the LORD,
like a master worker;
I was a delight day after day,
rejoicing before the LORD always,

v31: rejoicing in the LORD’s inhabited world
and delighting
in humanity.
</p>

## Whoever Finds Me Finds Life

<p>
v32: And now, children, listen to me:
blessed are those
who keep my ways.

v33: Hear discipline
and become wise;
do not neglect it.

v34: Blessed is the one
who listens to me,
watching daily at my doors,
waiting beside my entrances.

v35: For whoever finds me
finds life
and obtains favor
from the LORD.

v36: But whoever sins against me
harms their own life;
all who hate me
love death.”
</p>
## Notes
v01: Wisdom continues the feminine personification already present in Proverbs 1. She speaks publicly rather than revealing knowledge only to an elite group.
v02–03: Wisdom takes her place at roads, crossroads, gates, and entrances—the spaces where people make choices and where public life occurs.
v04: Wisdom’s invitation extends broadly to humanity. The teaching is not presented as secret knowledge reserved for a few.
v05: “understanding heart” — The heart is again the center of judgment and discernment rather than merely emotion.
v10–11: Wisdom is repeatedly valued above precious metals and jewels. Material wealth is not denied value, but it cannot substitute for the capacity to live wisely.
v13: “fear of the LORD” is defined ethically: reverence for the LORD includes hatred of evil, pride, arrogance, destructive conduct, and distorted speech.
v15–16: Political authority is accountable to wisdom and justice. Rule is not portrayed as self-legitimating merely because power has been obtained.
v18: Wealth associated with Wisdom is joined to righteousness. The text does not commend wealth detached from just conduct.
v22: “possessed me” — The Hebrew verb *qanah* can mean acquire, possess, or, in some contexts, create or bring forth. The verse has played a significant role in later theological debate. TSW uses “possessed” while leaving the wider lexical range visible in the note rather than resolving the text through later doctrinal concerns.
v23: “established” — The Hebrew verb is unusual in this context and can carry meanings such as being installed, set up, or poured out. The translation preserves the sense of Wisdom being present from before creation.
v24–25: “brought forth” — Birth imagery is used twice for Wisdom’s existence before the ordered world. The metaphor is retained without defining its metaphysical implications.
v27: “a circle on the face of the deep” — The image reflects ancient poetic description of God establishing order over the waters. The text does not function as a technical account of cosmology.
v29: “would not cross the command” — The sea is assigned a boundary within creation. Creation is pictured as ordered through divine command rather than uncontrolled water.
v30: “master worker” — The Hebrew *amon* is exceptionally difficult. It has been understood as master worker, artisan, confidant, or child/nursling. TSW uses “master worker” while recognizing that the precise sense remains uncertain.
v30–31: Creation is accompanied by delight. Wisdom rejoices before the LORD and particularly delights in the inhabited world and humanity.
v35–36: The chapter concludes by placing life and death in relation to Wisdom. Finding Wisdom means finding life; rejecting her is ultimately self-destructive.

## Vocabulary

v01: חָכְמָה (chokmah)
“Wisdom.” Skill in understanding and living rightly, here personified as a woman who speaks.

v05: עָרְמָה (ormah)
“Prudence” or “shrewdness.” The capacity to perceive circumstances and respond wisely.

v07: אֱמֶת (emet)
“Truth,” “faithfulness,” or “reliability.” Speech that is firm and trustworthy.

v12: מְזִמָּה (mezimmah)
“Discretion” or “purposeful planning.” Thoughtful capacity to plan and discern.

v13: גֵּאָה (ge'ah)
“Pride” or “exaltation.” Self-elevation condemned as incompatible with fear of the LORD.

v14: תּוּשִׁיָּה (tushiyyah)
“Sound wisdom,” “effective wisdom,” or “resourcefulness.” Wisdom capable of producing faithful and stable action.

v20: צְדָקָה (tsedaqah)
“Righteousness.” Rightness in conduct and relationships.

v22: קָנָה (qanah)
“To acquire,” “possess,” or “bring forth.” The verb’s range makes the precise nuance of Wisdom’s relationship to the LORD in v22 difficult to reduce to one English word.

v24: חוּל (chul)
“To be brought forth,” “writhe,” or “give birth.” Used here in birth imagery concerning Wisdom before creation.

v27: חוּג (chug)
“Circle,” “horizon,” or “vault.” A boundary or circular line drawn upon the face of the deep.

v30: אָמוֹן (amon)
“Master worker,” possibly “artisan,” “confidant,” or “nursling.” A rare and disputed term describing Wisdom beside the LORD during creation.

v31: שַׁעֲשֻׁעִים (sha'ashuim)
“Delight” or “rejoicing.” Deep pleasure or enjoyment.

v35: רָצוֹן (ratson)
“Favor,” “acceptance,” or “goodwill.” Favor received here from the LORD by the one who finds Wisdom.
