## Better a Dry Morsel with Peace

<p>
v01: Better is a dry morsel
with quiet
than a house full of feasting
with conflict.

v02: A servant who acts wisely
will rule over a child who brings shame
and will share an inheritance
among the family.

v03: The crucible is for silver
and the furnace for gold,
but the LORD
tests hearts.

v04: An evildoer listens
to wicked lips,
and a liar pays attention
to a destructive tongue.

v05: Whoever mocks the poor
insults their Maker,
and whoever rejoices at disaster
will not go unpunished.
</p>

<p>
v06: Children’s children
are the crown of the aged,
and parents
are the glory of their children.

v07: Fine speech
does not suit a fool,
much less lying lips
a noble person.

v08: A gift is like a precious stone
in the eyes of its owner;
wherever it turns,
it seems to prosper.

v09: Whoever covers an offense
seeks love,
but whoever repeats a matter
separates close friends.

v10: A rebuke goes deeper
into a discerning person
than a hundred blows
into a fool.
</p>

## Friendship and Conflict

<p>
v11: An evil person seeks only rebellion,
so a cruel messenger
will be sent against them.

v12: Better to meet a bear
robbed of her cubs
than a fool
in the midst of folly.

v13: Whoever returns evil for good,
evil will not depart
from their house.

v14: The beginning of conflict
is like releasing water;
so leave a quarrel
before it breaks out.

v15: Whoever declares the wicked righteous
and whoever condemns the righteous—
both are an abomination
to the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v16: Why should a fool have money in hand
to buy wisdom
when there is no heart
to understand?

v17: A friend loves at all times,
and a brother or sister
is born for adversity.

v18: One lacking sense
strikes hands
and becomes surety
before a neighbor.

v19: Whoever loves transgression
loves conflict;
whoever makes their doorway high
seeks destruction.

v20: One with a crooked heart
finds no good,
and one with a twisted tongue
falls into trouble.
</p>

## A Joyful Heart

<p>
v21: The one who fathers a fool
has sorrow,
and the parent of a senseless child
has no joy.

v22: A joyful heart
is good medicine,
but a crushed spirit
dries the bones.

v23: The wicked take a bribe in secret
to bend
the paths of justice.

v24: Wisdom is before
the face of the discerning,
but the eyes of a fool
are at the ends of the earth.
</p>

<p>
v25: A foolish child
is grief to a father
and bitterness
to the one who gave birth.

v26: It is not good
to punish the righteous,
or to strike honorable people
for their uprightness.

v27: One who restrains words
has knowledge,
and one of understanding
has a calm spirit.

v28: Even a fool who keeps silent
is considered wise;
when they close their lips,
they are considered discerning.
</p>
## Notes
v01: Peace within scarcity is preferred to abundance consumed by conflict.
v03: As metal is tested by heat, inward character is tested by the LORD.
v05: Mockery of poverty is interpreted as an insult against the Creator of the poor person.
v08: The proverb observes the perceived social power of gifts without necessarily endorsing bribery, which is explicitly condemned elsewhere in the chapter.
v09: Repeatedly publicizing an offense can destroy relationship even when the original wrong might have been covered in love.
v14: Conflict is compared with breaching a dam. Once released, it becomes increasingly difficult to contain.
v17: Friendship is characterized by enduring love; kinship becomes particularly visible when adversity arrives.
v22: Emotional and bodily life are interconnected. Joy can be medicinal, while a crushed spirit can diminish physical vitality.

## Vocabulary

v01: שָׁלוָה (shalvah)
“Quiet” or “peace.” A condition of undisturbed well-being.

v03: בָּחַן (bachan)
“To test” or “examine.” To determine quality or character.

v09: כָּסָה (kasah)
“To cover.” Here describing restraint in exposing another person’s offense.

v14: מָדוֹן (madon)
“Conflict” or “strife.” Sustained contention between people.

v17: רֵעַ (rea)
“Friend” or “neighbor.” A person in close relational proximity.

v18: עָרַב (arav)
“To become surety.” To guarantee another person’s obligation.

v22: גֵּהָה (gehah)
“Medicine” or “healing.” Something that brings bodily restoration.

v27: קַר־רוּחַ (qar ruach)
“Calm spirit.” Literally a “cool spirit,” contrasted with heated reaction.
