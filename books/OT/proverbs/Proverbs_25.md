## Proverbs Copied in the Days of Hezekiah

<p>
v01: These also are proverbs of Solomon,
which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah
copied.

v02: It is the glory of God
to conceal a matter,
and the glory of kings
to search a matter out.

v03: As the heavens are high
and the earth is deep,
so the heart of kings
cannot be searched out.

v04: Remove the dross from silver,
and material comes forth
for the silversmith.

v05: Remove the wicked
from the king’s presence,
and the throne
will be established in righteousness.
</p>

## Do Not Exalt Yourself

<p>
v06: Do not exalt yourself
before the king
or stand in the place
of the great,

v07: for it is better
to be told, “Come up here,”
than to be put lower
before a noble
whom your eyes have seen.

v08: Do not rush out
to bring a dispute,
for what will you do in the end
when your neighbor humiliates you?

v09: Argue your case
with your neighbor,
but do not reveal
another person’s secret,

v10: lest the one who hears
put you to shame
and your bad reputation
never leave.
</p>

## Words Fitly Spoken

<p>
v11: Like apples of gold
in settings of silver
is a word spoken
at the right time.

v12: Like a gold ring
or an ornament of fine gold
is a wise correction
to a listening ear.

v13: Like the coolness of snow
in the time of harvest
is a faithful messenger
to those who send them,
for they refresh
the life of their master.

v14: Like clouds and wind
without rain
is one who boasts
of a gift never given.

v15: Through patience
a ruler may be persuaded,
and a gentle tongue
can break a bone.
</p>

## Practice Restraint

<p>
v16: If you find honey,
eat only what is enough for you,
lest you become full of it
and vomit.

v17: Let your foot
be seldom in your neighbor’s house,
lest they become weary of you
and hate you.

v18: Like a club, a sword,
and a sharp arrow
is one who gives false testimony
against a neighbor.

v19: Like a broken tooth
or an unsteady foot
is trust in an unfaithful person
in a day of trouble.

v20: Like taking off a garment
on a cold day,
or vinegar poured on soda,
is one who sings songs
to a troubled heart.
</p>

## Care for an Enemy

<p>
v21: If your enemy is hungry,
give them bread to eat;
if they are thirsty,
give them water to drink,

v22: for you will heap burning coals
upon their head,
and the LORD
will reward you.

v23: A north wind brings rain,
and a secretive tongue
brings angry faces.

v24: Better to live
on a corner of a roof
than share a house
with a quarrelsome spouse.
</p>

<p>
v25: Like cold water
to a weary soul
is good news
from a distant land.

v26: Like a muddied spring
or a polluted fountain
is a righteous person
who gives way before the wicked.

v27: It is not good
to eat too much honey,
nor is seeking one’s own glory
glorious.

v28: Like a city broken into
and without walls
is a person
without self-control.
</p>
## Notes
v01: The heading identifies a later stage in the preservation of Solomonic wisdom during the reign of Hezekiah.  
v02: Concealment and investigation are not opposites in value. God’s mystery and a ruler’s responsibility to investigate are each given their own glory.  
v11: The exact objects described by “apples of gold” are uncertain; the image clearly emphasizes beauty, craftsmanship, and appropriateness.  
v11: The exact objects described by “apples of gold” are uncertain.
v13: “snow” likely refers to coolness brought from snow-covered regions rather than snow falling during harvest.  
v15: The “gentle tongue” breaking bone is deliberate paradox: soft, persistent speech can accomplish what force cannot.  
v20: The Hebrew comparison involving vinegar and soda describes a reaction that is inappropriate and disruptive, matching insensitive cheerfulness in the presence of grief.  
v21–22: Care for an enemy replaces retaliation. The “burning coals” image has been understood as shame, conviction, or judgment; TSW leaves the metaphor intact.  
v28: Self-control functions like a city wall: it protects inward life from uncontrolled intrusion and destruction.  

## Vocabulary

v01: עָתַק (ataq)  
“To copy” or “transcribe.” Here describing preservation of the sayings by Hezekiah’s scribes.  

v02: חָקַר (chaqar)  
“To search out.” To investigate what is concealed.  

v04: סִיג (sig)  
“Dross.” Impurity removed during metal refining.  

v11: אֹפֶן (ofen)  
“Setting” or “framework.” The surrounding craftsmanship in which the golden object is placed.  

v13: צִיר נֶאֱמָן (tsir neeman)
“Faithful messenger.” A reliable representative who fulfills the sender’s purpose.

v15: אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם (erekh appayim)
“Patience.” Literally being “long of anger.”

v19: בָּטַח (batach)
“To trust.” To rely upon another for security.

v28: מַעְצָר (maatsar)  
“Restraint” or “control.” The capacity to govern oneself.
