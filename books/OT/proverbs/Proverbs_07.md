## Keep My Words

<p>
v01: My child,
keep my words
and treasure my commandments
within you.

v02: Keep my commandments and live,
and keep my instruction
like the pupil of your eye.

v03: Bind them on your fingers;
write them
on the tablet of your heart.

v04: Say to wisdom,
“You are my sister,”
and call understanding
your close relative,

v05: so that they may guard you
from the strange woman,
from the foreign woman
who makes her words smooth.
</p>

## A Young Man Without Sense

<p>
v06: For at the window of my house
I looked down through my lattice,

v07: and I saw among the simple;
I noticed among the young people
a young man lacking sense.

v08: He was passing through the street
near her corner,
walking along the way
toward her house,

v09: in the twilight,
in the evening of the day,
in the middle of night
and darkness.
</p>

## She Came Out to Meet Him

<p>
v10: And look, a woman came out to meet him,
dressed like a prostitute
and crafty in heart.

v11: She is loud and rebellious;
her feet do not remain
in her house.

v12: Now in the street,
now in the public squares,
and at every corner
she lies in wait.

v13: She seized him and kissed him;
with a bold face
she said to him:

v14: “I had sacrifices of well-being to offer;
today I have fulfilled my vows.

v15: So I came out to meet you,
diligently seeking your face,
and I have found you.
</p>

<p>
v16: I have spread coverings over my bed,
colored linens from Egypt.

v17: I have perfumed my bed
with myrrh, aloes, and cinnamon.

v18: Come, let us drink our fill of love
until morning;
let us delight ourselves
with love.

v19: For the man is not at home;
he has gone
on a distant journey.

v20: He took a bag of silver with him;
he will return home
at the full moon.”
</p>

## He Followed Her

<p>
v21: With much persuasion
she turned him aside;
with smooth lips
she compelled him.

v22: Suddenly he followed her,
like an ox going to slaughter,
like a fool going
to the restraint of discipline,

v23: until an arrow pierces his liver,
like a bird rushing into a snare,
not knowing
that it will cost his life.
</p>

## Do Not Wander into Her Ways

<p>
v24: And now, children, listen to me;
pay attention
to the words of my mouth.

v25: Do not let your heart turn aside
to her ways;
do not wander
into her paths.

v26: For many are the wounded
she has brought down,
and numerous are all
whom she has killed.

v27: Her house is the way to Sheol,
descending to the chambers of death.
</p>
## Notes
v02: “pupil of your eye” — The Hebrew literally refers to the little or central part of the eye, something instinctively guarded because of its vulnerability and value.
v03: “tablet of your heart” — Wisdom is to become internal rather than remaining an external set of instructions.
v04: “sister … close relative” — Wisdom and understanding are pictured as intimate family relationships. The learner is to cultivate closeness with wisdom rather than with the seductive stranger.
v07: “lacking sense” — The Hebrew literally says “lacking heart.” Because the heart includes judgment and discernment, the expression describes deficient practical wisdom.
v09: Darkness is described in accumulating terms. The physical setting mirrors the young man’s lack of perception.
v10: “crafty in heart” — The Hebrew phrase is difficult and can suggest a guarded, concealed, or cunning heart. The emphasis is that her intentions are not transparent.
v14: “sacrifices of well-being” — Such offerings could involve a meal eaten from part of the sacrifice. The statement may imply that food is available for the encounter, while giving the invitation a religious surface.
v19: “the man” — The woman refers to her husband indirectly rather than using relational language such as “my husband.”
v20: “full moon” — The Hebrew expression refers to a fixed or full-moon time, suggesting that the husband’s absence is expected to last long enough for secrecy.
v22: The second comparison in the Hebrew is difficult. The text clearly portrays the young man as moving willingly toward restraint and destruction without comprehending the outcome.
v23: “liver” — The image is deliberately bodily and fatal. The young man discovers too late that what appeared to promise pleasure threatens life itself.
v26: The warning widens beyond one inexperienced young man. The narrative is presented as one example within a repeated human pattern.
v27: The house that promised pleasure is finally identified as a road descending toward death.

## Vocabulary

v01: צָפַן (tsafan)
“To treasure” or “store.” To keep something carefully within oneself.

v02: אִישׁוֹן עַיִן (ishon ayin)
“Pupil of the eye.” The vulnerable center of the eye, used as an image of something carefully guarded.

v04: מוֹדָע (moda)
“Close relative” or “intimate acquaintance.” Someone known within a close relationship.

v07: חֲסַר־לֵב (chasar lev)
“Lacking sense.” Literally “lacking heart,” meaning deficient in judgment or discernment.

v10: נָצַר (natsar)
“To guard” or “conceal.” The related form in v10 contributes to the difficult description of the woman’s inward character.

v14: זֶבַח שְׁלָמִים (zevach shelamim)
“Sacrifice of well-being.” A sacrificial offering associated with peace, wholeness, and a shared meal.

v18: דֹּדִים (dodim)
“Love” or “lovemaking.” Expressions of erotic affection.

v21: פָּתָה (patah)
“To entice,” “persuade,” or “make receptive.” To draw someone toward a course of action.

v23: מוֹקֵשׁ (moqesh)
“Snare.” A trap used for catching prey.

v27: שְׁאוֹל (Sheol)
“Sheol.” The realm of the dead, here the destination of the path being warned against.
