## The Fool and Folly

<p>
v01: Like snow in summer
and rain at harvest,
so honor
is not fitting for a fool.

v02: Like a sparrow flitting
and a swallow flying,
so a curse without cause
does not come to rest.

v03: A whip for the horse,
a bridle for the donkey,
and a rod
for the back of fools.

v04: Do not answer a fool
according to their folly,
lest you also become
like them.

v05: Answer a fool
according to their folly,
lest they become wise
in their own eyes.
</p>

<p>
v06: Whoever sends a message
by the hand of a fool
cuts off their own feet
and drinks violence.

v07: Like the legs of the lame
hanging loose
is a proverb
in the mouth of fools.

v08: Like tying a stone
into a sling
is giving honor
to a fool.

v09: Like a thorn
that enters the hand of a drunkard
is a proverb
in the mouth of fools.

v10: Like an archer
who wounds everyone
is one who hires a fool
or whoever happens to pass by.
</p>

<p>
v11: Like a dog
that returns to its vomit
is a fool
who repeats folly.

v12: Do you see someone
wise in their own eyes?
There is more hope
for a fool than for them.
</p>

## The Lazy Person

<p>
v13: The lazy person says,
“There is a lion in the road!
A lion
in the public square!”

v14: As a door turns
on its hinges,
so the lazy person
turns on the bed.

v15: The lazy person
buries a hand in the dish
and is too weary
to bring it back to the mouth.

v16: The lazy person
is wiser in their own eyes
than seven people
who can answer sensibly.
</p>

## Conflict and Deception

<p>
v17: Like one who grabs a dog
by the ears
is a passerby
who meddles in someone else’s quarrel.

v18: Like a madman
who shoots firebrands,
arrows, and death,

v19: so is one
who deceives a neighbor
and says,
“I was only joking!”

v20: Without wood
the fire goes out;
without a gossip
conflict quiets down.

v21: As charcoal is for hot embers
and wood for fire,
so a contentious person
is for kindling conflict.

v22: The words of a gossip
are like delicious morsels;
they go down
to the innermost parts.
</p>

## Hatred Hidden by Speech

<p>
v23: Like silver glaze
covering an earthen vessel
are smooth lips
with an evil heart.

v24: One who hates
disguises it with their lips,
but within themselves
they store deception.

v25: When they make their voice gracious,
do not trust them,
for seven abominations
are in their heart.

v26: Though hatred
is covered with deception,
its wickedness
will be exposed in the assembly.

v27: Whoever digs a pit
will fall into it,
and whoever rolls a stone
will have it roll back upon them.

v28: A lying tongue
hates those it crushes,
and a flattering mouth
brings ruin.
</p>
## Notes
v4–5: The paired sayings intentionally give opposite instructions. Wisdom requires discerning whether answering would reproduce folly or expose it.  
v8: The Hebrew image is unusual; a stone fixed improperly in a sling defeats the instrument’s purpose. Honor given to a fool is similarly misplaced.  
v10: The Hebrew is difficult and has produced several translations. The central image is indiscriminate damage caused by entrusting responsibility without discernment.  
v11: Repeated folly is depicted in deliberately repulsive imagery.  
v12: Self-assured pseudo-wisdom is presented as even more resistant to correction than ordinary folly.  
v17: Meddling in a conflict that does not belong to you is compared with grabbing an unfamiliar dog by the ears: once begun, danger becomes difficult to escape.  
v18–19: Harm does not disappear because the person causing it later calls it humor.  
v23: Attractive speech can function like a thin layer of precious metal hiding a cheap or fragile interior.  

##Vocabulary

v01: כְּסִיל (kesil)
“Fool.” One characterized by resistance to wisdom.

v02: קְלָלָה (qelalah)
“Curse.” Spoken harm or condemnation.

v07: מָשָׁל (mashal)
“Proverb.” Wisdom speech that becomes ineffective in the mouth of one unwilling to live it.

v13: עָצֵל (atsel)
“Lazy person” or “sluggard.”
