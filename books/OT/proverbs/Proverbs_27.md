## Do Not Boast About Tomorrow

<p>
v01: Do not boast
about tomorrow,
for you do not know
what a day may bring.

v02: Let another praise you
and not your own mouth,
a stranger
and not your own lips.

v03: A stone is heavy
and sand is weighty,
but the provocation of a fool
is heavier than both.

v04: Wrath is cruel
and anger is a flood,
but who can stand
before jealousy?

v05: Better is open correction
than love kept hidden.
</p>

<p>
v06: Faithful are the wounds
of a friend,
but the kisses of an enemy
are excessive.

v07: A satisfied person
tramples honey,
but to a hungry person
even bitterness is sweet.

v08: Like a bird
wandering from its nest
is a person
wandering from home.

v09: Oil and incense
make the heart glad,
and the sweetness of a friend
comes from heartfelt counsel.

v10: Do not abandon
your friend or your parent’s friend;
do not enter your sibling’s house
in the day of your disaster.
Better a nearby neighbor
than a distant sibling.
</p>

## Friends Sharpen One Another

<p>
v11: Be wise, my child,
and make my heart glad,
so that I may answer
the one who reproaches me.

v12: The prudent see danger
and hide themselves;
the simple pass on
and suffer for it.

v13: Take the garment
of one who becomes surety for a stranger;
hold it as security
for an outsider.

v14: Whoever blesses a neighbor
with a loud voice early in the morning
may have it counted
as a curse.

v15: A continual dripping
on a rainy day
and a quarrelsome spouse
are alike.

v16: Whoever would restrain such a person
restrains the wind
and grasps oil
with the hand.
</p>

<p>
v17: Iron sharpens iron,
and one person
sharpens the face
of another.

v18: Whoever tends a fig tree
will eat its fruit,
and whoever guards a master
will be honored.

v19: As water reflects
a face to a face,
so a human heart
reflects the person.

v20: Sheol and Abaddon
are never satisfied,
and the eyes of humanity
are never satisfied.

v21: The crucible is for silver
and the furnace for gold,
and a person is tested
by the praise they receive.
</p>

## Know the Condition of Your Flocks

<p>
v22: Even if you crush a fool
in a mortar with a pestle
along with crushed grain,
their folly
will not depart.

v23: Know well
the condition of your flocks;
give careful attention
to your herds,

v24: for riches
do not last forever,
nor does a crown endure
from generation to generation.

v25: When the hay is removed
and new grass appears
and the vegetation of the hills
is gathered,

v26: lambs will provide
your clothing,
and goats
the price of a field,

v27: and there will be enough goat’s milk
for your food,
for the food of your household,
and provision
for your servants.
</p>
## Notes
v01: The proverb challenges confidence in a future that has not yet arrived.
v05–06: Faithful friendship includes correction capable of causing pain. The contrast is with affection that hides what needs to be spoken and hostility disguised as affection.
v10: Relational nearness can matter more in crisis than biological closeness combined with distance.
v14: Even an apparently positive action can become offensive when timing and manner are inconsiderate.
v17: Mutual relationship forms character. “Sharpening” can include challenge as well as encouragement.
v19: The Hebrew line is compressed. TSW preserves the comparison between reflection in water and the way the heart reveals the person.
v21: Praise itself becomes a test: what a person does with honor can reveal character just as heat reveals metal.
v23–27: Wisdom includes careful attention to ordinary material realities. Wealth is unstable; sustainable provision requires knowing and tending what one has.

## Vocabulary

v01: הָלַל (halal)
“To boast” or “praise.” Here self-confident speech about tomorrow.

v04: קִנְאָה (qin'ah)
“Jealousy” or “zeal.” Intense passion that can become overwhelming.

v06: נֶאֱמָן (neeman)
“Faithful” or “reliable.” Describing wounds given from genuine friendship.

v09: עֵצָה (etsah)
“Counsel.” Thoughtful advice arising from relationship.

v17: חָדַד (chadad)
“To sharpen.” To make keen, here used relationally.

v20: אֲבַדּוֹן (Abaddon)
“Destruction.” Paired with Sheol as an insatiable realm of death.

v21: מַהֲלָל (mahalal)
“Praise.” Commendation that itself tests character.

v23: פָּנִים (panim)
“Face” or “condition.” In the idiom, to know the “face” of the flock is to know its actual condition.
