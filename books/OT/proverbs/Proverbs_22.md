## A Good Name

<p>
v01: A good name is to be chosen  
rather than great riches,  
and favor  
rather than silver and gold.

v02: Rich and poor meet together;  
the LORD is the Maker  
of them all.

v03: The prudent see danger  
and hide themselves,  
but the simple pass on  
and suffer for it.

v04: The reward of humility  
and the fear of the LORD  
is riches, honor,  
and life.

v05: Thorns and snares  
are in the way of the crooked;  
whoever guards their life  
stays far from them.
</p>

## Form the Young in Wisdom

<p>
v06: Train a child  
according to the way they should go;  
even when old,  
they will not depart from it.

v07: The rich rule over the poor,  
and the borrower  
is servant to the lender.

v08: Whoever sows injustice  
will reap trouble,  
and the rod of their fury  
will fail.

v09: One with a generous eye  
will be blessed,  
for they share their bread  
with the poor.

v10: Drive out the scoffer,  
and conflict will go out;  
quarreling and disgrace  
will cease.
</p>

<p>
v11: Whoever loves purity of heart  
and has gracious speech,  
the king  
will be their friend.

v12: The eyes of the LORD  
guard knowledge,  
but the LORD overturns  
the words of the treacherous.

v13: The lazy person says,  
“There is a lion outside!  
I will be killed  
in the street!”

v14: The mouth of forbidden relationships  
is a deep pit;  
one under the LORD’s indignation  
falls into it.

v15: Folly is bound  
to the heart of a child,  
but the rod of discipline  
drives it far away.

v16: Whoever oppresses the poor  
to increase their own wealth,  
or gives to the rich,  
comes only to poverty.
</p>

## Words of the Wise

<p>
v17: Incline your ear  
and hear the words of the wise;  
apply your heart  
to my knowledge.

v18: For it will be pleasant  
if you keep them within you,  
if all of them are ready  
upon your lips.

v19: So that your trust  
may be in the LORD,  
I teach them to you today—  
yes, to you.

v20: Have I not written for you  
thirty sayings  
of counsel and knowledge,

v21: to make you know  
the certainty of truthful words,  
so that you may return truthful words  
to those who sent you?
</p>

## Do Not Exploit the Vulnerable

<p>
v22: Do not rob the poor  
because they are poor,  
and do not crush the afflicted  
at the gate,

v23: for the LORD will plead their case  
and will take the life  
of those who take from them.

v24: Do not become a companion  
of an angry person,  
and do not go  
with one given to rage,

v25: lest you learn their ways  
and take a snare  
upon your life.

v26: Do not be among those  
who strike hands in pledge,  
among those who become surety  
for debts.

v27: If you have nothing with which to pay,  
why should your bed  
be taken  
from under you?

v28: Do not move  
the ancient boundary  
that your ancestors established.

v29: Do you see someone  
skilled in their work?  
They will stand before kings;  
they will not stand before obscure people.
</p>

## Notes

v01: Reputation and relational favor are treated as more valuable than accumulated wealth.

v06: The Hebrew phrase “according to his way” can refer to the path appropriate for the child, the direction in which the child is being formed, or the child’s characteristic way. The proverb states the formative power of early training without functioning as a mechanical guarantee.

v07: The proverb observes the power imbalance created by debt.

v09: The Hebrew literally speaks of a “good eye,” an idiom for generosity.

v16: Oppression of the poor and strategic giving to the rich are both presented as paths that ultimately impoverish.

v20: The Hebrew may mean “thirty sayings” or “excellent things.” The structure of Proverbs 22:17–24:22 supports the traditional understanding of thirty instructional units.

v22–23: The LORD is presented as legal advocate for those who lack social power at the city gate.

v28: Boundary markers protected inherited land. Moving one could amount to quietly stealing another family’s property.

## Vocabulary

v01: שֵׁם (shem)
“Name.” Reputation or identity as known within the community.

v03: עָרוּם (arum)
“Prudent.” Able to perceive danger before entering it.

v06: חָנַךְ (chanakh)
“To train,” “dedicate,” or “initiate.” To begin someone within a particular way.

v09: טוֹב־עַיִן (tov ayin)
“Generous eye.” Literally “good of eye,” an idiom for generosity.

v17: חָכָם (chakham)
“Wise.” One formed in skillful understanding.

v23: רִיב (riv)
“To plead a case” or “contend.” Judicial advocacy.

v28: גְּבוּל (gevul)
“Boundary.” A territorial marker defining property or inheritance.

v29: מָהִיר (mahir)
“Skilled” or “quick.” Competent and accomplished in work.
