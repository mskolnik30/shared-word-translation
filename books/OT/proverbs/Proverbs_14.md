## Wisdom Builds

<p>
v01: A wise woman builds her house,  
but Folly tears it down  
with her own hands.

v02: Whoever walks in uprightness fears the LORD,  
but one whose ways are crooked  
despises the LORD.

v03: In the mouth of a fool  
is a rod for pride,  
but the lips of the wise  
will guard them.

v04: Where there are no oxen,  
the manger is clean,  
but abundant produce comes  
through the strength of an ox.

v05: A faithful witness does not lie,  
but a false witness  
breathes out lies.
</p>

<p>
v06: A scoffer seeks wisdom  
and finds none,  
but knowledge comes easily  
to the discerning.

v07: Leave the presence of a fool,  
for there you will not find  
lips of knowledge.

v08: The wisdom of the prudent  
is to understand their way,  
but the folly of fools  
is deception.

v09: Fools mock at guilt,  
but among the upright  
there is favor.

v10: The heart knows its own bitterness,  
and no stranger shares fully  
in its joy.
</p>

## The Way That Seems Right

<p>
v11: The house of the wicked  
will be destroyed,  
but the tent of the upright  
will flourish.

v12: There is a way  
that seems right to a person,  
but its end  
is the way of death.

v13: Even in laughter  
the heart may ache,  
and the end of joy  
may be grief.

v14: The one who turns away in heart  
will be filled with the fruit of their ways,  
but a good person  
with the fruit of their deeds.

v15: The simple believe every word,  
but the prudent  
consider their steps.
</p>

<p>
v16: The wise fear and turn away from evil,  
but a fool is reckless  
and overconfident.

v17: One who is quick-tempered  
acts foolishly,  
and one who devises evil  
is hated.

v18: The simple inherit folly,  
but the prudent  
are crowned with knowledge.

v19: The evil bow before the good,  
and the wicked  
at the gates of the righteous.

v20: The poor are disliked  
even by their neighbor,  
but the rich have many friends.
</p>

## Compassion for the Poor

<p>
v21: Whoever despises a neighbor sins,  
but blessed is the one  
who shows favor to the poor.

v22: Do not those who devise evil go astray?  
But steadfast love and faithfulness  
belong to those who devise good.

v23: In all labor there is profit,  
but mere talk  
leads only to poverty.

v24: The crown of the wise is their wealth,  
but the folly of fools  
remains folly.

v25: A truthful witness saves lives,  
but one who breathes out lies  
deceives.
</p>

## The Fear of the LORD

<p>
v26: In the fear of the LORD  
there is strong confidence,  
and there will be refuge  
for one’s children.

v27: The fear of the LORD  
is a fountain of life,  
turning a person away  
from the snares of death.

v28: In a multitude of people  
is a king’s glory,  
but without people  
a ruler is ruined.

v29: Whoever is slow to anger  
has great understanding,  
but one who is quick-tempered  
exalts folly.

v30: A tranquil heart  
gives life to the body,  
but jealousy  
rots the bones.
</p>

<p>
v31: Whoever oppresses the poor  
insults their Maker,  
but whoever honors God  
shows favor to the needy.

v32: The wicked are overthrown  
by their own evil,  
but the righteous find refuge  
even in death.

v33: Wisdom rests  
in the heart of the discerning,  
but what is among fools  
becomes known.

v34: Righteousness exalts a nation,  
but sin is a disgrace  
to any people.

v35: A king’s favor is toward  
a servant who acts wisely,  
but wrath falls upon  
one who brings shame.
</p>

## Notes

v04: The clean manger presents a real advantage, but it comes at the cost of the productive strength of the ox. Wisdom recognizes that fruitful work often includes inconvenience and disorder.

v09: The Hebrew is compact and can refer either to fools mocking guilt or to guilt itself mocking fools. TSW follows the former while retaining the contrast with favor among the upright.

v10: The proverb recognizes the limits of another person’s access to inward experience. Both bitterness and joy retain a personal depth that cannot be completely shared.

v12: Sincerity does not guarantee wisdom. A path can appear correct from within a person’s own judgment while nevertheless leading toward death.

v20: The saying observes a painful social reality rather than endorsing it: wealth attracts relationships while poverty can produce isolation.

v27: Fear of the LORD is again life-giving rather than merely frightening. Reverence turns the learner away from paths leading toward death.

v31: Treatment of the poor is directly related to treatment of their Creator. Social conduct therefore becomes theological conduct.

v34: Righteousness and sin have communal consequences. Proverbs does not restrict morality to individuals.

## Vocabulary

v01: חָכְמוֹת (chokhmot)
“Wisdom.” Here associated with constructive, life-giving action.

v08: עָרוּם (arum)
“Prudent” or “shrewd.” Able to perceive circumstances and act with discernment.

v12: דֶּרֶךְ (derekh)
“Way” or “path.” A recurring wisdom image for the direction of a life.

v21: חָנַן (chanan)
“To show favor” or “be gracious.” To respond generously toward one in need.

v22: חֶסֶד (ḥesed)
“Steadfast love.” Faithful, enduring love expressed through concrete action.

v27: מְקוֹר חַיִּים (meqor chayyim)
“Fountain of life.” A continuing source of life and preservation.

v29: אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם (erekh appayim)
“Slow to anger.” Literally “long of nostrils,” an idiom for patience.

v34: צְדָקָה (tsedaqah)
“Righteousness.” Right conduct and right ordering within relationships and community.
