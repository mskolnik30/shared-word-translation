## The Righteous Are Bold

<p>
v01: The wicked flee
when no one pursues,
but the righteous
are confident like a lion.

v02: Because of a land’s transgression
its rulers become many,
but through a person
of understanding and knowledge
stability continues.

v03: A poor person
who oppresses the poor
is like sweeping rain
that leaves no food.

v04: Those who abandon instruction
praise the wicked,
but those who keep instruction
struggle against them.

v05: Evil people
do not understand justice,
but those who seek the LORD
understand it fully.
</p>

<p>
v06: Better is a poor person
who walks in integrity
than a rich person
whose ways are crooked.

v07: Whoever keeps instruction
is a discerning child,
but a companion of gluttons
brings shame to a parent.

v08: Whoever increases wealth
through interest and excessive profit
gathers it for one
who shows favor to the poor.

v09: Whoever turns away an ear
from hearing instruction,
even their prayer
is an abomination.

v10: Whoever leads the upright
into an evil way
will fall into their own pit,
but the blameless
will inherit good.
</p>

## Confession and Mercy

<p>
v11: A rich person
is wise in their own eyes,
but a discerning poor person
sees through them.

v12: When the righteous rejoice,
there is great glory,
but when the wicked rise,
people hide themselves.

v13: Whoever conceals transgressions
will not prosper,
but whoever confesses and abandons them
will receive mercy.

v14: Blessed is the one
who is always reverent,
but whoever hardens their heart
falls into disaster.

v15: Like a roaring lion
and a charging bear
is a wicked ruler
over a poor people.
</p>

## Faithfulness and Greed

<p>
v16: A ruler who lacks understanding
is a great oppressor,
but one who hates unjust gain
will prolong days.

v17: A person burdened
with the blood of another
will be a fugitive until death;
let no one support them.

v18: Whoever walks in integrity
will be saved,
but one crooked in two ways
will fall suddenly.

v19: Whoever works the land
will have plenty of bread,
but whoever pursues empty things
will have plenty of poverty.

v20: A faithful person
will abound with blessings,
but whoever hurries to become rich
will not go unpunished.
</p>

<p>
v21: Showing partiality
is not good,
yet for a piece of bread
a person may transgress.

v22: One with a stingy eye
hurries after wealth
and does not know
that poverty will come upon them.

v23: Whoever corrects a person
will afterward find more favor
than one who flatters
with the tongue.

v24: Whoever robs father or mother
and says, “It is no transgression,”
is companion
to a destroyer.
</p>

## Trust the LORD, Not Yourself

<p>
v25: A greedy person
stirs up conflict,
but whoever trusts in the LORD
will flourish.

v26: Whoever trusts
in their own heart
is a fool,
but whoever walks in wisdom
will be delivered.

v27: Whoever gives to the poor
will have no lack,
but whoever hides their eyes
will receive many curses.

v28: When the wicked rise,
people hide themselves,
but when they perish,
the righteous increase.
</p>
## Notes
v3: Poverty does not prevent someone from becoming an oppressor. The proverb rejects the assumption that suffering automatically produces justice.  
v8: Economic exploitation ultimately fails to secure permanent possession; unjustly accumulated wealth can pass into more generous hands.  
v9: Religious prayer cannot be detached from refusal to hear instruction.  
v13: Concealment and confession are contrasted. Mercy is connected not simply with admission but with abandoning the transgression.  
v17: Bloodguilt is pictured as a burden driving the guilty person toward judgment.  
v20: Faithful steadiness is contrasted with the impatient desire to become wealthy quickly.  
v26: Trust in oneself is not presented as maturity when the “heart” refuses external and divine wisdom.  
v27: Refusal to see poverty is itself a moral action; looking away does not remove responsibility.
