<p>
v01: Solomon ruled over all the kingdoms from the River to the land of the Philistines and as far as the border of Egypt, bringing tribute and serving Solomon all the days of his life.  
v02: Solomon’s provisions for one day were thirty cors of fine flour and sixty cors of flour,  
v03: ten fattened cattle, twenty pasture-fed cattle, and one hundred sheep, besides deer, gazelles, roebucks, and fattened birds.  
v04: For he ruled over all the region beyond the River, from Tiphsah to Gaza, over all the kings beyond the River. And he had peace on every side around him.  
v05: Judah and Israel lived in security, each under their vine and under their fig tree, from Dan to Beer-sheba, all the days of Solomon.  
v06: Solomon had forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen.  
</p>

<p>
v07: These officials provided for King Solomon and for all who came near King Solomon’s table, each in their month; they let nothing be lacking.  
v08: They brought the barley and the straw for the horses and the steeds to the place where they were, each according to their assignment.  
</p>

<p>
v09: God gave Solomon wisdom and very great understanding, and breadth of heart like the sand on the seashore.  
v10: Solomon’s wisdom exceeded the wisdom of all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt.  
v11: He was wiser than every other person—wiser than Ethan the Ezrahite, and Heman, Calcol, and Darda, the sons of Mahol. His name was known among all the nations around him.  
v12: He spoke three thousand proverbs, and his songs numbered one thousand and five.  
v13: He spoke about the trees, from the cedar that is in Lebanon to the hyssop that grows out of the wall. He spoke also about animals, birds, creeping things, and fish.  
v14: People came from all peoples to hear Solomon’s wisdom, from all the kings of the earth who had heard of his wisdom.  
</p>

<p>
v15: Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants to Solomon, because he had heard that they had anointed him king in place of his father; for Hiram had loved David all his days.  
v16: Solomon sent word to Hiram, saying,  
v17: “You know that David my father was not able to build a house for the name of the LORD his God because of the warfare that surrounded him, until the LORD put them under the soles of my feet.  
v18: But now the LORD my God has given me rest on every side. There is no adversary and no evil occurrence.  
v19: So I am planning to build a house for the name of the LORD my God, as the LORD spoke to David my father, saying, ‘Your son, whom I will set in your place on your throne—he will build the house for my name.’  
</p>

<p>
v20: “Now command that they cut cedars from Lebanon for me. My servants will be with your servants, and I will give you the wages of your servants according to all that you say. For you know that among us there is no one who knows how to cut timber like the Sidonians.”  
</p>

<p>
v21: When Hiram heard Solomon’s words, he rejoiced greatly and said, “Blessed be the LORD today, who has given David a wise son over this great people.”  
v22: Hiram sent word to Solomon, saying, “I have heard what you sent to me. I will do all that you desire concerning cedar timber and cypress timber.  
v23: My servants will bring them down from Lebanon to the sea, and I will make them into rafts on the sea as far as the place you direct me. I will break them apart there, and you will carry them away. You, in turn, will do what I desire by providing food for my household.”  
</p>

<p>
v24: So Hiram supplied Solomon with cedar timber and cypress timber, all that he desired.  
v25: And Solomon gave Hiram twenty thousand cors of wheat as food for his household, and twenty cors of beaten oil. Solomon gave this to Hiram year after year.  
v26: The LORD gave wisdom to Solomon, as he had spoken to him. There was peace between Hiram and Solomon, and the two of them made a covenant.  
</p>

<p>
v27: King Solomon conscripted forced labor from all Israel, and the forced labor force numbered thirty thousand men.  
v28: He sent them to Lebanon in shifts of ten thousand a month: they would be in Lebanon one month and at home two months. Adoniram was over the forced labor.  
v29: Solomon had seventy thousand burden-bearers and eighty thousand stonecutters in the hill country,  
v30: besides Solomon’s chief officers over the work: three thousand three hundred who ruled over the people doing the work.  
v31: At the king’s command they quarried large stones, costly stones, to lay the foundation of the house with dressed stones.  
v32: Solomon’s builders and Hiram’s builders, along with the Gebalites, shaped the timber and the stones to build the house.  
</p>

## Notes

- v2: A *cor* was a large dry measure. The text gives the quantities without converting them into a modern unit, emphasizing the scale of the royal household’s daily provisions.  
- v5: “Each under their vine and under their fig tree” is an image of settled security, household abundance, and freedom from threat. The chapter’s description of peace is thus both political and ordinary, extending into daily life.  
- v9: “Breadth of heart” describes expansive discernment and capacity for understanding. In biblical Hebrew, the heart is not merely the seat of feeling but of thought, judgment, and purpose.  
- v12: The Hebrew says literally, “his song was one thousand and five.” The singular can function collectively; the translation renders the sense as a count of songs.  
- v17: The written form reads “under the soles of his feet,” while the traditional reading gives “under the soles of my feet.” The latter fits Solomon’s speech and is followed here.  
- v18: “Rest” does not simply mean private quietness. It names freedom from surrounding conflict, creating the conditions in which the house for the LORD’s name may be built.  
- v26: The chapter attributes Solomon’s wisdom to the LORD, yet it also shows that wisdom working through diplomacy, trade, shared labor, and covenant-making.  
- v27: The same royal building project associated with wisdom and peace also depends on compulsory labor from Israel. The narrative preserves both the achievement and its human cost.  

## Vocabulary

- v09: **חָכְמָה** (*chokhmah*) — “wisdom.” Here wisdom is God-given capacity for governing, understanding, speaking, and ordering life; it is displayed in Solomon’s discernment and in the wide scope of his knowledge.  
- v09: **רֹחַב לֵב** (*rochav lev*) — “breadth of heart.” The phrase portrays an immeasurably expansive inner capacity, like the sand on the seashore, for perception and judgment.  
- v18: **נוּחַ** (*nuach*) — “give rest.” The LORD gives Solomon rest “on every side,” the political and military security that enables the temple project.  
- v19: **שֵׁם** (*shem*) — “name.” The house is built “for the name of the LORD,” expressing God’s covenantal presence and reputation without reducing God to the building itself.  
- v26: **בְּרִית** (*berit*) — “covenant.” Hiram and Solomon’s agreement is described with the covenant term, marking their arrangement as a binding relationship of mutual obligation.  
- v27: **מַס** (*mas*) — “forced labor.” The term identifies a levy of compulsory service rather than ordinary hired work, casting the temple preparations in a morally weighty light.
