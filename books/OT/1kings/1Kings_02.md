## Notes

- **2:2 — “the way of all the earth”:** David describes death as the path shared by all humanity. His charge to Solomon begins in the face of mortality, even as it concerns the enduring royal house.  

- **2:3–4 — “keep the charge”:** David’s language gathers several covenantal terms—statutes, commandments, judgments, and testimonies—and explicitly roots royal faithfulness in the law of Moses. The promised continuity of David’s line is expressed as conditional: “If your children keep their way.”  

- **2:5–9 — David’s final instructions:** The chapter does not soften the tension between David’s appeal to covenant faithfulness and his commands concerning Joab and Shimei. Joab’s killings are named as bloodshed “in peace,” while David leaves Solomon to exercise wisdom regarding both men.  

- **2:17, 21–25 — Abishag:** Though David had not had sexual relations with Abishag (1 Kings 1:4), Adonijah’s request to marry her can carry a claim upon the former king’s household and, by implication, the throne. Solomon interprets it as a renewed challenge to his rule.  

- **2:26–27 — Abiathar and Eli’s house:** Abiathar is spared death but removed from the priesthood. The narrator presents this action as fulfilling the LORD’s earlier word against Eli’s priestly house (1 Samuel 2:27–36).  

- **2:28–34 — the horns of the altar:** Joab takes hold of the altar’s horns, seeking sanctuary. Yet Solomon orders his execution there, grounding the decision in Joab’s shedding of innocent blood. The narrative joins sanctuary, royal judgment, and the unresolved burden of bloodguilt.  

- **2:33 — “peace”:** Solomon’s prayer contrasts blood returning upon Joab’s house with *shalom* for David’s house, offspring, and throne. Here peace includes wholeness, security, and the settled well-being that comes “from the LORD.”  

- **2:36–46 — Shimei’s confinement:** Shimei accepts the king’s oath-bound condition, but later crosses the boundary to recover his servants. Solomon connects this violation with Shimei’s earlier evil toward David, and the chapter closes by again declaring the kingdom established.  

## Vocabulary

- **2:3 — מִשְׁמֶרֶת (*mishmeret*), “charge”:** This term speaks of an entrusted duty or careful observance. Solomon is charged not merely with private morality but with guarding the LORD’s covenantal requirements.  

- **2:3 — תּוֹרָה (*torah*), “law”:** Literally instruction. David locates the king’s rule under the instruction of Moses rather than treating royal authority as independent of the LORD’s teaching.  

- **2:7 — חֶסֶד (*hesed*), “kindness”:** David calls Solomon to show loyal, active kindness to Barzillai’s family in response to their support during David’s flight.  

- **2:6, 9 — שְׁאוֹל (*Sheol*):** The realm of the dead. David’s words about Joab and Shimei use descent to Sheol as grave language for dying under judgment.  

- **2:33 — שָׁלוֹם (*shalom*), “peace”:** More than the absence of conflict, this word names well-being, security, and settled order. It is set against Joab’s bloodshed and is sought for David’s house from the LORD.  

- **2:45–46 — כּוּן (*kun*), “be established”:** This verb frames the chapter’s conclusion. Solomon’s kingdom is portrayed as made firm, while the narrative has shown that such stability comes through covenantal, political, and judicial conflict.
