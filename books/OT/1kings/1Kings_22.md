## Notes

**22:6 — “the Lord.”** The four hundred prophets use *Adonai* (“Lord”), while Jehoshaphat specifically asks for a prophet of the LORD. The narrative soon distinguishes their unanimous assurance from Micaiah’s word in the name of the LORD.  

**22:15 — “Go up and prosper.”** Micaiah repeats the court prophets’ words exactly. Ahab’s immediate demand for truth shows that he recognizes the reply as not Micaiah’s true message, perhaps because of its tone or its conspicuous conformity.  

**22:19–23 — “a lying spirit.”** Micaiah’s vision portrays the heavenly court and the deception of Ahab’s prophets without explaining every dimension of divine agency and creaturely responsibility. The vision makes clear both that the prophets’ message is false and that Ahab’s coming ruin stands under the LORD’s judgment.  

**22:27 — “bread of affliction and water of affliction.”** This likely refers to a meager prison ration, food and water given under harsh conditions.  

**22:34 — “at random.”** The phrase literally suggests simplicity or lack of intention. The archer does not appear to aim at Ahab specifically, yet the shot fulfills the word announced by Micaiah.  

**22:38 — “the prostitutes washed themselves.”** The Hebrew wording is brief and may refer to prostitutes washing at the pool, or possibly to the washing of equipment associated with them. In either reading, the scene concludes Ahab’s death with the fulfillment formula.  

**22:43–44 — “what was right … Only the high places.”** Jehoshaphat receives a generally favorable evaluation, but the account retains a significant qualification: worship continued at the high places.  

**22:47 — “male cult prostitutes.”** The term refers to men associated with prohibited cultic sexual practices. Their removal is presented as part of Jehoshaphat’s reform.  

## Vocabulary

**22:5 — דָּבָר (*davar*), “word.”** Jehoshaphat asks not merely for advice but for the word of the LORD. The chapter contrasts that sought word with the favorable speech of the assembled prophets.  

**22:17 — רֹעֶה (*ro'eh*), “shepherd.”** Israel scattered “like sheep that have no shepherd” signals the loss of royal leadership through Ahab’s death.  

**22:19 — צְבָא הַשָּׁמַיִם (*tseva hashamayim*), “host of heaven.”** The expression depicts the heavenly assembly standing before the LORD’s throne.  

**22:22 — שֶׁקֶר (*sheqer*), “falsehood, lie.”** The spirit becomes a “lying spirit” in the prophets’ mouths, exposing their united promise of victory as deceptive.  

**22:27 — לַחַץ (*lachats*), “affliction, oppression.”** The repeated phrase “bread of affliction and water of affliction” conveys Micaiah’s punitive confinement.  

**22:44 — בָּמָה (*bamah*), “high place.”** These local places of sacrifice remained active despite Jehoshaphat’s otherwise positive evaluation.
