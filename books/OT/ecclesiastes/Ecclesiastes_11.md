# Ecclesiastes 11

v01:
Send your bread upon the waters,  
for after many days you will find it.

v02:
Give a portion to seven, even to eight,  
for you do not know what harm may come upon the earth.

v03:
If clouds are full of rain,  
they pour it out on the earth;  
and if a tree falls to the south or to the north,  
in the place where the tree falls, there it will remain.

v04:
Whoever watches the wind will not sow,  
and whoever looks at the clouds will not reap.

v05:
Just as you do not know the way of the wind,  
or how bones form in the womb of a pregnant woman,  
so you do not know the work of God,  
who makes everything.

v06:
In the morning sow your seed,  
and in the evening do not let your hand rest;  
for you do not know which will prosper, this or that,  
or whether both alike will be good.

v07:
Sweet is the light,  
and good for the eyes to see the sun.

v08:
For if a person lives many years,  
let them rejoice in all of them;  
but let them remember the days of darkness,  
for they will be many.  
All that comes is vapor.

v09:
Rejoice, young man, in your youth,  
and let your heart make you glad in the days of your youth.  
Walk in the ways of your heart  
and by what your eyes see;  
but know that for all these things  
God will bring you into judgment.

v10:
Remove vexation from your heart,  
and put away harm from your flesh,  
for youth and the dawn of life are vapor.

## Notes

- v1: “Send your bread upon the waters” is deliberately vivid and open-ended. It may evoke generosity, commercial risk, or both; the saying commends action whose return cannot be immediately secured.

- v3: The rain and fallen tree images emphasize realities beyond human control. The tree’s final position contrasts with the uncertainty that surrounds human planning: some outcomes, once they arrive, cannot be altered.

- v5: **Wind** translates *ruaḥ*, a word that can also mean “breath” or “spirit.” The verse compares the hidden course of the wind and the hidden formation of life in the womb with the inaccessible work of God.

- v8: “Vapor” translates the recurring word *hevel*. It does not simply mean that life is meaningless; it names life’s fleeting, elusive, and insubstantial character, even when life contains joy.

- v9: The call to rejoice is not withdrawn by the reference to judgment. Joy, desire, and accountability are held together without explaining away the tension between them.

- v10: “The dawn of life” renders a term associated with early life or youthful vigor. The closing declaration returns to the chapter’s theme: youth, too, is fleeting.

## Vocabulary

- v5: **רוּחַ** (*ruaḥ*) — “wind.” In this passage, the word’s range of meaning—wind, breath, spirit—heightens the mystery of life and of God’s work.

- v8: **הֶבֶל** (*hevel*) — “vapor.” The word characterizes what is fleeting and impossible to grasp securely; here it includes “all that comes,” as well as youth in v10.

- v9: **מִשְׁפָּט** (*mishpat*) — “judgment.” God’s judgment gives moral weight to the invitation to enjoy youth; delight is neither denied nor detached from accountability.

- v10: **בָּשָׂר** (*basar*) — “flesh.” The term refers concretely to the body and its mortal life, making the instruction to put away harm bodily as well as inwardly directed.
