<p>
v01: Do not be hasty with your mouth, and do not let your heart be quick to bring forth a word before God. For God is in heaven and you are on earth; therefore let your words be few.
v02: For a dream comes through much activity, and a fool’s voice through many words.
v03: When you make a vow to God, do not delay in paying it, for he takes no pleasure in fools. What you vow, pay.
v04: Better that you do not vow than that you vow and do not pay.
v05: Do not let your mouth cause your flesh to sin, and do not say before the messenger, “It was an error.” Why should God be angry at your voice and destroy the work of your hands?
v06: For in an abundance of dreams, and vapors, and many words—fear God.
</p>

<p>
v07: If you see the oppression of the poor, and the robbery of justice and righteousness in a province, do not be astonished at the matter. For one high official watches over another high official, and higher ones are over them.
v08: Yet the advantage of a land is in everything: a king is served by a field.
</p>

<p>
v09: Whoever loves silver will not be satisfied with silver, nor whoever loves abundance with income. This too is vapor.
v10: When good things increase, those who consume them increase; and what advantage is there to their owners except to look on with their eyes?
v11: Sweet is the sleep of one who works, whether they eat little or much; but the abundance of the rich will not let them sleep.
v12: There is a grievous evil that I have seen under the sun: wealth kept by its owner to their harm.
v13: That wealth is lost through a bad venture, and they father a child, but there is nothing in their hand.
v14: As they came forth from their mother’s womb, naked they will return, going as they came; and from their labor they will carry nothing away in their hand.
v15: This also is a grievous evil: entirely as they came, so they go. What profit is there for them in laboring for the wind?
v16: Indeed, all their days they eat in darkness, with much vexation, sickness, and anger.
</p>

<p>
v17: Look: what I have seen to be good and fitting is to eat and drink and see good in all one’s labor under the sun, during the few days of one’s life that God has given; for this is one’s portion.
v18: Also, every person to whom God has given wealth and possessions, and whom he has enabled to eat from them, to carry their portion, and to rejoice in their labor—this is God’s gift.
v19: For they will not remember much the days of their life, because God answers with the joy of their heart.
</p>

## Notes

v05: **5.5 — “the messenger”:** The Hebrew *malʾakh* can mean an angelic messenger or a human envoy. In this setting of vows, it may refer to an official connected with worship, but the text does not specify.  

v06: **5.6 — “vapors”:** The word translated “vapors” is *hevel*, Ecclesiastes’ recurring image for what is fleeting, insubstantial, or elusive. Here it links excessive dreams and speech with the call to fear God.  

v07: **5.7 — “one high official watches over another”:** The saying describes a layered hierarchy of power. It can explain why injustice persists, while also leaving open whether the higher oversight is merely bureaucratic or ultimately divine.  

v08: **5.8 — “a king is served by a field”:** This compact and difficult line stresses the land’s basic importance. Even royal power depends upon cultivated ground and those who work it.  

v19: **5.19 — “God answers with the joy of their heart”:** The verb may suggest that God answers, occupies, or keeps a person engaged through joy. The line does not deny life’s brevity; it presents gladness as God’s response within it.  

v05: 5 — “the messenger”: The Hebrew *malʾakh* can mean an angelic messenger or a human envoy. In this setting of vows, it may refer to an official connected with worship, but the text does not specify.

## Vocabulary

v03: — נֶדֶר (*neder*), “vow”:: A vowed commitment to God is not treated as casual speech. The passage emphasizes that words spoken before God create a real obligation.  

v05: — מַלְאָךְ (*malʾakh*), “messenger”:: The term retains ambiguity here: it may name an angelic messenger or a human representative who receives or assesses a vow.  

v06: — הֶבֶל (*hevel*), “vapor”:: A central Ecclesiastes word for what slips away or cannot be grasped. It characterizes the unreliability of multiplying dreams and words.  

v07: — מִשְׁפָּט (*mishpat*), “justice,” and צֶדֶק (*tsedeq*), “righteousness”:: Together these terms name the public ordering of life that should protect the vulnerable but is here subject to robbery.  

v08: — יִתְרוֹן (*yitron*), “advantage”:: Ecclesiastes repeatedly asks what lasting gain remains from human activity. Here the term turns attention to the indispensable value of the cultivated land.  

v15: — רוּחַ (*ruach*), “wind”:: Laboring “for the wind” evokes effort spent on what cannot be held or retained, echoing the chapter’s concern with wealth that disappears.  

v17: — חֵלֶק (*cheleq*), “portion”:: One’s portion is not an accumulation secured against loss, but the received capacity to enjoy food, drink, and labor within the life God gives.
