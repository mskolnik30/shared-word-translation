v01:
A good name is better than good oil,
and the day of death than the day of one’s birth.

v02:
Better to go to a house of mourning
than to go to a house of feasting,
for this is the end of every human being,
and the living should take it to heart.

v03:
Sorrow is better than laughter,
for through a sad face the heart is made glad.

v04:
The heart of the wise is in a house of mourning,
but the heart of fools is in a house of joy.

v05:
Better to hear the rebuke of the wise
than for one to hear the song of fools.

v06:
For like the crackling of thorns under a pot
is the laughter of a fool;
this too is vapor.

v07:
For oppression drives the wise into madness,
and a bribe destroys the heart.

v08:
Better is the end of a matter than its beginning;
better is patience than pride.

v09:
Do not be quick in your spirit to become angry,
for anger rests in the lap of fools.

v10:
Do not say, “Why were the former days better than these?”
For it is not from wisdom that you ask this.

v11:
Wisdom is good with an inheritance,
and an advantage to those who see the sun.

v12:
For wisdom is a shelter, as money is a shelter;
but the advantage of knowledge is this:
wisdom preserves the life of its possessor.

v13:
Consider the work of God:
who can straighten
what he has made crooked?

v14:
In a day of prosperity, be in the good;
but in a day of adversity, consider:
God has made this alongside that,
so that no one can discover anything that will come after them.

v15:
I have seen everything in my days of vapor:
there is a righteous person perishing in their righteousness,
and there is a wicked person prolonging life in their wickedness.

v16:
Do not be excessively righteous,
and do not make yourself overly wise.
Why should you bring ruin on yourself?

v17:
Do not be excessively wicked,
and do not be foolish.
Why should you die before your time?

v18:
It is good that you hold fast to this
and do not let go of that;
for the one who fears God will come through them all.

v19:
Wisdom gives strength to the wise
more than ten rulers who are in a city.

v20:
Surely there is no righteous person on earth
who does good and does not sin.

v21:
Also, do not take to heart
all the things people say,
lest you hear your servant cursing you.

v22:
For your heart knows
that you yourself also
have often cursed others.

v23:
All this I tested by wisdom.
I said, “I will become wise,”
but it was far from me.

v24:
What has been is far off,
and deep—very deep.
Who can find it?

v25:
I turned, I and my heart,
to know and to search out and seek wisdom and an account,
and to know that wickedness is folly
and foolishness is madness.

v26:
And I find more bitter than death
the woman whose heart is snares and nets,
whose hands are chains.
The one who is good before God escapes her,
but the sinner is caught by her.

v27:
“See, this is what I found,” says Qoheleth,
“adding one thing to another
to find an account,

v28:
which my soul still seeks but has not found:
one man among a thousand I found,
but a woman among all these I did not find.

v29:
Only see this: I found
that God made humanity upright,
but they have sought out many schemes.”

## Notes

- **7:1–4:** The opening comparisons are not a denial of joy. They press the reader toward the hard clarity that mortality can bring: a house of mourning places “the end of every human being” before the living.
- **7:6:** The sound of burning thorns is sharp and brief; so is a fool’s laughter. The comparison culminates in *vapor*, the chapter’s recurring judgment on what is fleeting and insubstantial.
- **7:7:** “Oppression” may refer either to oppression suffered or to the practice of oppression. In either case, it distorts wisdom; the parallel warning about a bribe concerns the destruction of the heart, the inner center of judgment and desire.
- **7:11–12:** Wisdom and money both provide shelter, but they are not equal. Money can offer protection; wisdom’s distinctive advantage is that it preserves the life of the one who possesses it.
- **7:14:** The instruction does not explain adversity away. It places prosperity and adversity “alongside” one another within God’s work, while affirming that people cannot master what lies ahead.
- **7:16–18:** “Do not be excessively righteous” is not counsel to pursue less righteousness. The warning is directed against a self-made or excessive posture that claims mastery through righteousness or wisdom. The passage holds its warnings together rather than proposing a morally safe middle ground between righteousness and wickedness.
- **7:26–28:** The Teacher’s harsh portrayal of “the woman” belongs to this speaker’s limited and troubled search, not to a universal statement about women. The following verses continue to report what the Teacher “found” and did not find; the chapter’s concluding claim broadens the problem to humanity as a whole.
- **7:29:** “Humanity” renders a collective singular. The contrast is between God’s making people upright and their pursuit of “many schemes,” bringing the chapter’s reflections on wisdom, sin, and human limitation to a shared conclusion.

## Vocabulary

- **v6: הֶבֶל (*hevel*)** — “vapor.” The word names what is fleeting, elusive, and unable to bear the weight placed upon it. Here, the fool’s laughter is as brief and empty as the crackling of thorns.
- **v11: חׇכְמָה (*chokhmah*)** — “wisdom.” In this passage wisdom is practical discernment for life under God, yet it remains unable to uncover fully what has been or what will be.
- **v14: רָעָה (*ra‘ah*)** — “adversity.” The term stands opposite “prosperity” and names a harmful or difficult day without resolving why it comes.
- **v15: הֶבֶל (*hevel*)** — “vapor.” The Teacher’s “days of vapor” frame the troubling observation that righteousness does not always bring visible preservation and wickedness does not always bring swift ruin.
- **v18: יְרֵא אֱלֹהִים (*yere’ ’elohim*)** — “one who fears God.” This describes reverent orientation toward God, not mere fearfulness. Such reverence is presented as the way through the chapter’s difficult and competing warnings.
- **v23: חֶשְׁבּוֹן (*cheshbon*)** — “account.” The Teacher seeks an intelligible reckoning or pattern within reality, yet repeatedly meets what is beyond human finding.
- **v29: יָשָׁר (*yashar*)** — “upright.” Humanity’s original condition is described as straight or upright; the contrast with “many schemes” evokes a departure from that integrity.
