v01:
<p>
Again I saw all the oppressions  
that are done under the sun.

And look: the tears of the oppressed,  
and they have no comforter;  
power is in the hand of their oppressors,  
and they have no comforter.
</p>

v02:
<p>
So I praised the dead,  
who have already died,  
more than the living,  
who are still alive.
</p>

v03:
<p>
Better than both is the one who has not yet been—  
who has not seen the evil work  
that is done under the sun.
</p>

v04:
<p>
And I saw all toil  
and every skill in work:  
it is the envy of one person for another.

This too is vapor  
and a pursuit of wind.
</p>

v05:
<p>
The fool folds his hands  
and consumes his own flesh.
</p>

v06:
<p>
Better one handful with rest  
than two handfuls with toil  
and a pursuit of wind.
</p>

v07:
<p>
Again I saw vapor  
under the sun:
</p>

v08:
<p>
There is one person, and no second;  
they have neither son nor brother,  
yet there is no end to all their toil,  
and their eyes are never satisfied with wealth.

“For whom, then, am I toiling  
and depriving myself of good?”

This too is vapor  
and an evil task.
</p>

v09:
<p>
Two are better than one,  
because they have a good reward  
for their toil.
</p>

v10:
<p>
For if they fall,  
one will raise up the other.  
But woe to the one who falls  
and has no second to raise them up.
</p>

v11:
<p>
Again, if two lie down together, they are warm;  
but how can one be warm alone?
</p>

v12:
<p>
If someone overpowers one,  
two can stand against that person.  
And a threefold cord  
is not quickly torn apart.
</p>

v13:
<p>
Better a poor and wise youth  
than an old and foolish king  
who no longer knows how to take warning.
</p>

v14:
<p>
For from a prison house he came out to reign,  
though even in his kingdom he was born poor.
</p>

v15:
<p>
I saw all the living  
who walk under the sun  
with the second youth  
who stands in his place.
</p>

v16:
<p>
There was no end to all the people,  
to all who were before them.  
Yet those who come later  
will not rejoice in him.

Surely this too is vapor  
and a pursuit of wind.
</p>

v17:
<p>
Guard your steps when you go to the house of God.  
To draw near in order to listen is better  
than for fools to offer sacrifice,  
for they do not know that they are doing evil.
</p>

## Notes

- v1: The repeated declaration that the oppressed have “no comforter” gives the verse its force. The Teacher does not explain or resolve their suffering; he simply sets their tears and their oppressors’ power before the reader.
- v3: “The one who has not yet been” intensifies the bleak comparison. The statement arises from the sight of evil “under the sun,” not from a developed account of the value of life before God.
- v4: The phrase translated “envy of one person for another” identifies rivalry as a driving force behind much productive labor and skill. Work itself is not dismissed, but its competitive distortion is exposed.
- v5: “Consumes his own flesh” is a stark image for the self-destruction of idleness. It stands alongside the warning of v6, where relentless labor is also unsatisfying.
- v8: The speaker’s question may be the solitary worker’s own unasked question: wealth continues to grow, but no relationship or purpose answers why he denies himself good.
- v12: The “threefold cord” concludes the sayings about companionship. It emphasizes added resilience without identifying the third strand.
- v14–16: The shifting references to king, youth, people, and those who come later portray the instability of public acclaim and royal succession. The precise relationships among the figures remain open.
- v17: In many English Bibles this verse is numbered Ecclesiastes 5:1, following a different chapter division. The Hebrew chapter numbering places it here. “Guard your steps” calls for attentive reverence on entering God’s house, where listening is set above thoughtless sacrifice.

## Vocabulary

- v1: **מְנַחֵם** (*menaḥem*) — “comforter.” The word is repeated for the oppressed, underscoring both their need and the painful absence of anyone to stand with them.
- v4: **הֶבֶל** (*hevel*) — “vapor.” In this chapter it names what is fleeting, elusive, and unable to bear the weight placed upon it, including envy-driven achievement and public acclaim.
- v4: **קִנְאָה** (*qin’ah*) — “envy” or “jealousy.” Here it describes the competitive impulse that can animate a person’s successful work in relation to a neighbor.
- v6: **נַחַת** (*naḥat*) — “rest” or “quietness.” One handful joined with rest is preferable to abundance gained through exhausting toil.
- v8: **עִנְיָן** (*ʿinyan*) — “task” or “occupation.” The solitary pursuit of wealth is called an “evil task,” a burdensome activity that diminishes rather than gives life.
- v17: **שָׁמַר** (*shamar*) — “guard” or “keep.” The command to guard one’s steps frames worship as a matter of watchful, reverent approach.
