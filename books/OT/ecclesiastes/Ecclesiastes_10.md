<p>
v01: Dead flies make the perfumer’s oil stink—make it bubble;
so a little folly outweighs wisdom and honor.
</p>

<p>
v02: The heart of the wise inclines to the right;
the heart of the fool, to the left.
</p>

<p>
v03: Even when the fool walks along the road,
his heart is lacking,
and he tells everyone, “He is a fool.”
</p>

<p>
v04: If the ruler’s anger rises against you,
do not leave your place,
for calmness puts great offenses to rest.
</p>

<p>
v05: There is an evil I have seen under the sun,
like an error that goes out from the ruler:
</p>

<p>
v06: Folly is set in many high places,
while the rich sit in low places.
</p>

<p>
v07: I have seen servants on horses
and officials walking on the ground like servants.
</p>

<p>
v08: Whoever digs a pit will fall into it,
and whoever breaks through a wall will be bitten by a snake.
</p>

<p>
v09: Whoever moves stones will be hurt by them;
whoever splits logs will be endangered by them.
</p>

<p>
v10: If the iron is dull
and one does not sharpen its edge,
one must use greater strength;
but wisdom has the advantage of bringing success.
</p>

<p>
v11: If a snake bites before it is charmed,
there is no advantage for the master of the tongue.
</p>

<p>
v12: The words from the mouth of the wise are gracious,
but the lips of a fool consume him.
</p>

<p>
v13: The beginning of the words of his mouth is folly,
and the end of his mouth is wicked madness.
</p>

<p>
v14: The fool multiplies words.
No one knows what will be;
and what will be after him—
who can tell him?
</p>

<p>
v15: The toil of fools wearies them,
for they do not know how to go to a city.
</p>

<p>
v16: Woe to you, O land, when your king is a youth
and your officials feast in the morning!
</p>

<p>
v17: Happy are you, O land, when your king is a son of nobles
and your officials eat at the proper time—
for strength, and not for drinking.
</p>

<p>
v18: Through laziness the roof sinks in,
and through slack hands the house leaks.
</p>

<p>
v19: They make bread for laughter,
and wine gladdens life,
and money answers everything.
</p>

<p>
v20: Even in your thoughts do not curse a king,
and in your bedroom do not curse the rich;
for a bird of the air may carry the sound,
and a winged creature may tell the matter.
</p>

## Notes

v10: The Hebrew speaks literally of not sharpening the “face” of the iron—that is, its cutting edge.

v11: “Master of the tongue” may refer to a snake-charmer, whose skill is useless if the snake bites before being charmed. The phrase may also carry an echo of the danger and limits of skilled speech.

## Vocabulary

v01: סִכְלוּת (sikhlut)
“folly.” Here it is not mere lack of intelligence but conduct so ruinous that even a little of it can outweigh wisdom and honor.

v02: לֵב (lev)
“heart.” In this passage the heart is the center of judgment, intention, and practical orientation, not merely emotion.

v04: רוּחַ (ruach)
“spirit, wind, breath.” The ruler’s ruach can denote an aroused temper or disposition directed against another.

v10: יִתְרוֹן (yitron)
“advantage, gain.” A recurring term in Ecclesiastes, here describing wisdom’s practical benefit in making action effective.
