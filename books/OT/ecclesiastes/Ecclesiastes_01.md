# Ecclesiastes 1

v01:
<p>
The words of Qoheleth, son of David, king in Jerusalem.
</p>

v02:
Vapor of vapors, says Qoheleth,  
vapor of vapors—everything is vapor.

v03:
What gain does a person have from all their labor  
in which they labor under the sun?

v04:
A generation goes, and a generation comes,  
but the earth stands forever.

v05:
The sun rises, and the sun sets;  
it hurries to its place, and there it rises.

v06:
Going toward the south, and turning toward the north,  
turning, turning goes the wind;  
and upon its circuits the wind returns.

v07:
All the streams go to the sea,  
yet the sea is not full;  
to the place where the streams go,  
there they return to go again.

v08:
All things are weary;  
no one can speak of them.  
The eye is not satisfied with seeing,  
nor is the ear filled with hearing.

v09:
What has been is what will be,  
and what has been done is what will be done;  
there is nothing new under the sun.

v10:
Is there anything of which one says, “Look—this is new”?  
It has already been  
in ages before us.

v11:
There is no remembrance of the former ones,  
nor will there be remembrance of the later ones to come  
among those who come afterward.

v12:
<p>
I, Qoheleth, was king over Israel in Jerusalem.
</p>

v13:
<p>
I gave my heart to seek and to explore by wisdom all that is done under heaven. It is a grievous task that God has given to human beings to occupy them.
</p>

v14:
<p>
I have seen all the works that are done under the sun, and see: everything is vapor and a chasing after wind.
</p>

v15:
<p>
What is crooked cannot be made straight, and what is lacking cannot be counted.
</p>

v16:
<p>
I spoke with my heart, saying, “Look, I have grown great and increased in wisdom beyond all who were before me over Jerusalem, and my heart has seen much wisdom and knowledge.”
</p>

v17:
<p>
I gave my heart to know wisdom and to know madness and folly. I knew that this too is a pursuit of wind.
</p>

v18:
<p>
For with much wisdom comes much vexation, and one who increases knowledge increases pain.
</p>

## Notes

- **2 — “Vapor of vapors.”** The Hebrew *hevel* names a vapor or breath: visible for a moment, then gone. The repeated expression does not simply call creation worthless; it voices the fleeting, elusive, and resistant character of all that lies “under the sun.”

- **3 — “Gain.”** The word refers to what remains over, a surplus or lasting advantage. Qoheleth’s question asks what endures from human toil, not whether work can have immediate usefulness or goodness.

- **8 — “All things are weary.”** The Hebrew word can also mean “words” or “matters.” The line may speak both of the weariness of all things and of the inability of speech to exhaustively express them.

- **13 — “A grievous task.”** The phrase may describe an unhappy, burdensome, or troubling occupation. Qoheleth attributes this task to God without resolving the tension between divine gift and human distress.

- **14, 17 — “Chasing after wind” / “pursuit of wind.”** These related expressions portray effort directed toward what cannot be grasped or held. The imagery does not deny the reality of wisdom or work, but questions their ability to secure lasting gain.

- **15 — “What is lacking cannot be counted.”** The saying joins what is distorted with what is absent: some realities cannot be corrected, and some deficits cannot be made whole by calculation or human effort.

## Vocabulary

- 1 — **קֹהֶלֶת (*qohelet*)**: The speaker’s title, traditionally rendered “the Teacher” or “the Preacher.” Its form is grammatically feminine, while the surrounding description presents Qoheleth as a Davidic king. Retaining the title preserves its distinctive and somewhat enigmatic voice.

- 2 — **הֶבֶל (*hevel*)**: “Vapor,” “breath,” or mist. In this chapter it names the passing and ungraspable quality of everything pursued under the sun.

- 3 — **יִתְרוֹן (*yitron*)**: “Gain,” “advantage,” or what remains over. Qoheleth uses it to ask whether labor yields anything lasting.

- 13 — **עִנְיָן (*inyan*)**: A task, occupation, or matter demanding attention. Here it is the grievous human business God has given people to occupy them.

- 14 — **רְעוּת רוּחַ (*re'ut ruach*)**: Literally an expression involving “wind” or “spirit,” rendered “a chasing after wind.” It evokes an exhausting pursuit of what cannot be secured.
