## The LORD’s Appointed Times

<p>
v01: The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,
v02: “Speak to the Israelites and say to them: The LORD’s appointed times, which you shall proclaim as holy assemblies—these are my appointed times.
</p>

## The Sabbath

<p>
v03: Six days work may be done, but the seventh day is a Sabbath of complete rest, a holy assembly. You shall do no work. It is a Sabbath to the LORD in all your dwellings.
</p>

## Passover and Unleavened Bread

<p>
v04: These are the appointed times of the LORD, holy assemblies that you shall proclaim at their appointed times.
v05: In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month at twilight, is the LORD’s Passover.
v06: On the fifteenth day of the same month is the Festival of Unleavened Bread to the LORD. Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread.
v07: On the first day you shall have a holy assembly; you shall do no regular work.
v08: Seven days you shall bring an offering by fire to the LORD. On the seventh day there shall be a holy assembly; you shall do no regular work.”
</p>

## The First Sheaf

<p>
v09: The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,
v10: “Speak to the Israelites and say to them: When you come into the land that I am giving you and reap its harvest, you shall bring the first sheaf of your harvest to the priest.
v11: He shall elevate the sheaf before the LORD so that you may be accepted. On the day after the Sabbath the priest shall elevate it.
v12: On the day you elevate the sheaf, you shall bring a male lamb a year old without defect as a burnt offering to the LORD.
v13: Its grain offering shall be two-tenths of an ephah of fine flour mixed with oil, an offering by fire to the LORD, a pleasing aroma; and its drink offering shall be one-fourth of a hin of wine.
v14: You shall eat no bread, roasted grain, or fresh grain until that very day, until you have brought the offering of your God. It is a perpetual statute throughout your generations in all your dwellings.
</p>

## The Festival of Weeks

<p>
v15: You shall count from the day after the Sabbath, from the day you brought the sheaf of the elevation offering: seven complete Sabbaths.
v16: You shall count fifty days to the day after the seventh Sabbath, and then bring a new grain offering to the LORD.
v17: From your dwellings you shall bring two loaves as an elevation offering, made from two-tenths of an ephah of fine flour and baked with yeast, as first produce to the LORD.
v18: With the bread you shall bring seven male lambs a year old without defect, one young bull, and two rams. They shall be a burnt offering to the LORD with their grain offering and drink offerings, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.
v19: You shall also prepare one male goat as a sin offering and two male lambs a year old as a sacrifice of well-being.
v20: The priest shall elevate them with the bread of the first produce as an elevation offering before the LORD, together with the two lambs. They shall be holy to the LORD for the priest.
v21: On that same day you shall proclaim a holy assembly. You shall do no regular work. It is a perpetual statute in all your dwellings throughout your generations.
v22: When you reap the harvest of your land, you shall not reap your field all the way to its edge, and you shall not gather the gleanings of your harvest. You shall leave them for the poor and for the resident alien. I am the LORD your God.”
</p>

## The Memorial of Trumpet Blasts

<p>
v23: The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,
v24: “Speak to the Israelites, saying: In the seventh month, on the first day of the month, you shall have a complete rest, a memorial proclaimed with trumpet blasts, a holy assembly.
v25: You shall do no regular work, and you shall bring an offering by fire to the LORD.”
</p>

## The Day of Atonement

<p>
v26: The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,
v27: “Now on the tenth day of this seventh month is the Day of Atonement. It shall be a holy assembly for you; you shall humble yourselves and bring an offering by fire to the LORD.
v28: You shall do no work on that very day, for it is the Day of Atonement, to make atonement for you before the LORD your God.
v29: Anyone who does not humble themselves on that very day shall be cut off from the people.
v30: Anyone who does any work on that very day, I will destroy that person from among the people.
v31: You shall do no work. It is a perpetual statute throughout your generations in all your dwellings.
v32: It shall be a Sabbath of complete rest for you, and you shall humble yourselves. On the ninth day of the month at evening, from evening to evening, you shall keep your Sabbath.”
</p>

## The Festival of Booths

<p>
v33: The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,
v34: “Speak to the Israelites, saying: On the fifteenth day of this seventh month is the Festival of Booths for seven days to the LORD.
v35: On the first day there shall be a holy assembly; you shall do no regular work.
v36: Seven days you shall bring an offering by fire to the LORD. On the eighth day you shall have a holy assembly and bring an offering by fire to the LORD. It is a solemn assembly; you shall do no regular work.
</p>

<p>
v37: These are the LORD’s appointed times, which you shall proclaim as holy assemblies, to bring offerings by fire to the LORD—burnt offering, grain offering, sacrifice, and drink offerings, each on its appointed day—
v38: besides the LORD’s Sabbaths, besides your gifts, besides all your vow offerings, and besides all your freewill offerings that you give to the LORD.
</p>

<p>
v39: On the fifteenth day of the seventh month, when you have gathered in the produce of the land, you shall celebrate the LORD’s festival seven days. On the first day there shall be complete rest, and on the eighth day complete rest.
v40: On the first day you shall take fruit from majestic trees, palm branches, branches of leafy trees, and willows of the brook, and you shall rejoice before the LORD your God seven days.
v41: You shall celebrate it as a festival to the LORD seven days each year. It is a perpetual statute throughout your generations; you shall celebrate it in the seventh month.
v42: You shall live in booths seven days. Every native-born Israelite shall live in booths,
v43: so that your generations may know that I made the Israelites live in booths when I brought them out of the land of Egypt. I am the LORD your God.”
v44: So Moses declared to the Israelites the appointed times of the LORD.
</p>

##Notes
v02: “appointed times” — The Hebrew *mo'ed* refers to fixed times of meeting or observance, not merely festivals in a generic sense.
v11: “the day after the Sabbath” has generated different Jewish calendrical interpretations. TSW preserves the wording rather than resolving which Sabbath is intended.
v17: Unlike most grain offerings placed on the altar, the two festival loaves are baked with yeast and function as first produce.
v22: The harvest calendar pauses to repeat the gleaning command, joining worship and provision for the poor and resident alien.
v24: The Hebrew speaks of a “memorial of trumpet blast,” without specifying in this verse the later name by which the day became known.
v40: Several plant identifications are uncertain. The ritual action and rejoicing are clearer than the precise botanical species.

##Vocabulary
v02: מוֹעֵד (mo'ed)
“Appointed time.” A set time for sacred assembly or observance.
v05: פֶּסַח (pesach)
“Passover.” The appointed observance on the fourteenth day of the first month.
v10: עֹמֶר (omer)
“Sheaf.” A gathered measure or bundle of grain presented from the first harvest.
v24: תְּרוּעָה (teru'ah)
“Trumpet blast,” “shout,” or “signal.” A loud ritual sound marking the memorial day.
v34: סֻכּוֹת (sukkot)
“Booths.” Temporary shelters commemorating Israel’s wilderness dwelling.
