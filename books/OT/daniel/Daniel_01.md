## Jerusalem Falls under Babylonian Power

<p>
v01: In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and besieged it.

v02: The Lord gave Jehoiakim king of Judah into his hand, along with some of the vessels of the house of God. He brought them to the land of Shinar, to the house of his god, and he brought the vessels into the treasury of his god.
</p>

## Young Judeans Are Taken for Royal Service

<p>
v03: Then the king said to Ashpenaz, chief of his eunuchs, to bring some from the children of Israel, from the royal offspring and from the nobles,

v04: young men without any defect, good in appearance, discerning in all wisdom, knowing knowledge, understanding learning, and capable of standing in the king’s palace, and to teach them the literature and language of the Chaldeans.

v05: The king appointed for them a daily portion from the king’s delicacies and from the wine that he drank. They were to be educated for three years, and at the end of them they were to stand before the king.

v06: Among them were some from the children of Judah: Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah.

v07: The chief of the eunuchs gave them names: he gave Daniel the name Belteshazzar, Hananiah the name Shadrach, Mishael the name Meshach, and Azariah the name Abednego.
</p>

## Daniel Resolves Not to Defile Himself

<p>
v08: But Daniel set it upon his heart that he would not defile himself with the king’s delicacies or with the wine that he drank. So he sought permission from the chief of the eunuchs not to defile himself.

v09: God gave Daniel steadfast kindness and compassion before the chief of the eunuchs.

v10: But the chief of the eunuchs said to Daniel, “I fear my lord the king, who appointed your food and your drink. Why should he see your faces looking worse than the young men your own age? Then you would make my head guilty before the king.”

v11: Then Daniel said to the steward whom the chief of the eunuchs had appointed over Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah,

v12: “Please test your servants for ten days. Let vegetables be given to us to eat and water to drink.

v13: Then let our appearance and the appearance of the young men who eat the king’s delicacies be seen before you, and deal with your servants according to what you see.”

v14: So he listened to them in this matter and tested them for ten days.

v15: At the end of ten days their appearance was better and their flesh fuller than all the young men who ate the king’s delicacies.

v16: So the steward continued taking away their delicacies and the wine they were to drink and gave them vegetables.
</p>

## God Gives Knowledge and Wisdom

<p>
v17: As for these four young men, God gave them knowledge and skill in every kind of literature and wisdom, and Daniel had understanding in every kind of vision and dream.

v18: At the end of the days when the king had said they should be brought in, the chief of the eunuchs brought them in before Nebuchadnezzar.

v19: The king spoke with them, and among them all none was found like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. So they stood before the king.

v20: In every matter of wisdom and understanding about which the king questioned them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters who were in all his kingdom.

v21: And Daniel remained until the first year of King Cyrus.
</p>
## Notes
v01: The “third year of Jehoiakim” reflects Daniel’s chronological convention. Jeremiah 25:1 uses a different regnal reckoning for the corresponding period. TSW preserves Daniel’s dating rather than harmonizing it.
v02: The narrative interprets Jerusalem’s defeat theologically: “the Lord gave” Jehoiakim and temple vessels into Babylonian hands. Babylonian power is real but not ultimate.
v02: “Shinar” is an ancient biblical designation associated with Babylonia, evoking earlier biblical memories as well as Daniel’s present setting.
v03: “eunuchs” translates a term that can denote castrated court officials or high palace servants more broadly. The text does not state the physical status of every person under Ashpenaz.
v04: “literature and language of the Chaldeans” describes the education required for service in Babylon’s royal administration and learned culture.
v07: The four Judeans receive Babylonian court names while their Hebrew names continue to be used by the narrator. The dual naming embodies life within imperial institutions without erasing their earlier identity.
v08: The exact reason the royal food would “defile” Daniel is not stated. Proposed explanations include dietary law, food associated with idolatry, or dependence upon royal patronage. TSW does not choose among them.
v09: “steadfast kindness and compassion” describes divine favor operating through a Babylonian official rather than by removing Daniel from imperial structures.
v12: “vegetables” translates a word for foods grown from seed and can include grains or legumes as well as vegetables.
v17: Daniel’s gift concerning visions and dreams prepares for the revelations that dominate the rest of the book.
v21: Daniel’s survival until Cyrus marks the passage from Babylonian to Persian rule and frames his service across the great imperial transition.

##Vocabulary

v02: שִׁנְעָר (Shinar)
“Shinar.” An ancient biblical name for Babylonia.

v03: סָרִיס (saris)
“Eunuch” or “court official.”

v04: כַּשְׂדִּים (Kasdim)
“Chaldeans.” Here associated with Babylonian language, learning, and court culture.

v09: חֶסֶד (ḥesed)
“Steadfast kindness.” Loyal favor or covenant-like kindness.
