<p>
v01: Then the prophets Haggai and Zechariah, the son of Iddo, prophesied to the Jews who were in Judah and Jerusalem in the name of the God of Israel who was over them.
</p>
<p>
v02: Then Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel and Jeshua the son of Jozadak arose and began to build the house of God which is in Jerusalem. The prophets of God were with them, supporting them.
</p>
<p>
v03: At that time Tattenai, the governor beyond the River, and Shethar-bozenai and their associates came to them and said, "Who issued you a decree to build this house and to complete this structure?"
</p>
<p>
v04: They also asked, "What are the names of the men who are constructing this building?"
</p>
<p>
v05: But the eye of their God was upon the elders of the Jews, and they did not stop them until a report was sent to Darius, and then they returned an answer concerning this.
</p>
<p>
v06: This is a copy of the letter that Tattenai the governor beyond the River and Shethar-bozenai and his associates, the officials beyond the River, sent to King Darius.
</p>
<p>
v07: They sent a report to him, containing the following: "To Darius the king, all peace.
</p>
<p>
v08: Let it be known to the king that we went to the province of Judah, to the house of the great God. It is being built with large stones, and timber is laid in the walls. The work is being carried out diligently and is succeeding in their hands.
</p>
<p>
v09: Then we asked those elders, saying, 'Who issued you a decree to build this house and to complete this structure?'
</p>
<p>
v10: We also asked them their names in order to notify you, so that we could write down the names of the leaders among them.
</p>
<p>
v11: This was their reply to us, saying, 'We are the servants of the God of heaven and earth, and we are rebuilding the house that was built many years ago, which a great king of Israel built and completed.
</p>
<p>
v12: But because our ancestors provoked the God of heaven to wrath, he gave them into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, the Chaldean, who destroyed this house and carried the people away to Babylon.
</p>
<p>
v13: However, in the first year of Cyrus king of Babylon, King Cyrus issued a decree to rebuild this house of God.
</p>
<p>
v14: Moreover, the gold and silver articles of the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple in Jerusalem and brought to the temple of Babylon, these King Cyrus took from the temple of Babylon, and they were given to one named Sheshbazzar, whom he appointed governor.
</p>
<p>
v15: He said to him, 'Take these articles, go and place them in the temple in Jerusalem, and let the house of God be rebuilt on its site.'
</p>
<p>
v16: Then this Sheshbazzar came and laid the foundations of the house of God in Jerusalem. From that time until now it has been under construction and it is not yet completed.'
</p>
<p>
v17: Now, if it seems good to the king, let a search be made in the king's treasure archives there in Babylon to determine whether a decree was issued by King Cyrus to rebuild this house of God at Jerusalem. Then let the king send us his decision concerning this matter."
</p>

## Notes

v1: Haggai and Zechariah's prophetic activity helps initiate the resumption of temple construction, providing a divine endorsement expressed through their messages.
v5: "The eye of their God" serves as a figure of God's protective and providential oversight, underscoring that divine authority supersedes all earthly decrees.
v11: The phrase "God of heaven and earth" is a statement of monotheistic belief, recognizing the authority of God over all creation, a frequent formula in post-exilic literature.
v15: The verb "let be rebuilt" indicates a continuity with past divine commands to reestablish worship in Jerusalem, emphasizing the importance of the temple as a symbol of the covenant community’s identity.

## Vocabulary

v5: עַ֫יִן (a.yin) - "Eye" here implies divine surveillance and care, a common biblical metaphor reflecting God's omniscience and presence. It is pivotal to the narrative as it juxtaposes divine and political powers, indicating divine favor towards the temple's restoration.
