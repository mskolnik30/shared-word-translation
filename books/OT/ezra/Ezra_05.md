## Haggai and Zechariah Prophesy

<p>
v01: Now Haggai the prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo, the prophets, prophesied to the Judeans who were in Judah and Jerusalem in the name of the God of Israel who was over them.

v02: Then Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and Jeshua son of Jozadak arose and began to rebuild the house of God that is in Jerusalem, and the prophets of God were with them, supporting them.
</p>

## Tattenai Questions the Builders

<p>
v03: At that time Tattenai, governor of the province Beyond the River, and Shethar-Bozenai and their associates came to them and said this to them: “Who gave you a decree to build this house and complete this structure?”

v04: Then they also said to them, “What are the names of the men who are building this building?”

v05: But the eye of their God was upon the elders of the Judeans, and they did not make them stop until a report could go to Darius and then a written answer be returned concerning it.
</p>

## Tattenai’s Letter to Darius

<p>
v06: This is the copy of the letter that Tattenai, governor of the province Beyond the River, Shethar-Bozenai, and his associates, the officials who were in the province Beyond the River, sent to Darius the king.

v07: They sent a report to him, and this was written in it:

“To Darius the king, all peace.

v08: Let it be known to the king that we went to the province of Judah, to the house of the great God. It is being built with large stones, and timber is being laid in the walls. This work is being done diligently and is prospering in their hands.

v09: Then we asked those elders and said to them, ‘Who gave you a decree to build this house and complete this structure?’

v10: We also asked them their names in order to inform you, so that we might write the names of the men who are at their head.
</p>

## The Elders Answer

<p>
v11: “And this is the answer they returned to us:

‘We are servants of the God of the heavens and the earth, and we are rebuilding the house that was built many years ago, which a great king of Israel built and completed.

v12: But because our ancestors provoked the God of the heavens to anger, he gave them into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, the Chaldean, who destroyed this house and carried the people into exile to Babylon.

v13: Yet in the first year of Cyrus king of Babylon, King Cyrus issued a decree to rebuild this house of God.

v14: Also, the gold and silver vessels of the house of God that Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple in Jerusalem and brought into the temple of Babylon—King Cyrus took them from the temple of Babylon and gave them to one named Sheshbazzar, whom he had appointed governor.

v15: He said to him, “Take these vessels, go and deposit them in the temple in Jerusalem, and let the house of God be rebuilt on its site.”

v16: Then this Sheshbazzar came and laid the foundations of the house of God in Jerusalem. From then until now it has been under construction, but it is not yet completed.’
</p>

## Let the Royal Archives Be Searched

<p>
v17: “And now, if it seems good to the king, let a search be made in the royal treasury there in Babylon to see whether a decree was issued by King Cyrus to rebuild this house of God in Jerusalem. Then let the king send us his decision concerning this matter.”
</p>

##Notes

v01: Haggai and Zechariah are the same prophetic figures whose books preserve oracles connected with the rebuilding of the temple.

v02: The rebuilding resumes through the combined action of civic leaders and prophets. Prophecy is described not merely as speech but as active support for the work.

v03: “complete this structure” translates an Aramaic term whose precise architectural sense is debated; it can refer to the structure, walls, or finishing of the building.

v05: “the eye of their God was upon the elders” is an image of protective divine attention. Persian officials investigate, but the work is allowed to continue while the matter is appealed.

v06: The chapter remains in Aramaic, continuing the imperial-document section that began in Ezra 4:8.

v11–12: The Judean elders interpret exile theologically rather than simply politically: Babylon succeeded because Israel’s ancestors provoked the God of heaven.

v13: Cyrus is called “king of Babylon” here because Babylon was among the territories under his rule after its conquest. Elsewhere he is more commonly called king of Persia.

v14–16: Sheshbazzar is credited with receiving the temple vessels and laying the foundation. Ezra 3 emphasizes Zerubbabel and Jeshua in the rebuilding. TSW preserves both traditions without collapsing the figures into one person.

##Vocabulary

v01: נְבִיא (nevi)
“Prophet.” In the Aramaic section, one who speaks in the name of God.

v03: פֶּחָה (pechah)
“Governor.” A provincial administrator under Persian authority.

v05: עֵין אֱלָהֲהוֹן (ein elahon)
“The eye of their God.” An image of divine oversight and protection.

v08: אֶבֶן גְּלָל (even gelal)
“Large stone” or “rolled stone.” A term for substantial building stones.

v11: עֶבֶד (eved)
“Servant.” The Judean elders identify themselves as servants of the God of heaven and earth.

v17: גִּנְזַיָּא (ginzayya)
“Treasury” or “archive.” A royal repository in which records could be searched.
