## Opposition to Rebuilding

<p>
v01: When the adversaries of Judah and Benjamin heard that the children of the exile were building a temple to the LORD, the God of Israel,

v02: they approached Zerubbabel and the heads of ancestral houses and said to them, “Let us build with you, for we seek your God as you do, and we have been sacrificing to him since the days of Esar-Haddon king of Assyria, who brought us up here.”

v03: But Zerubbabel, Jeshua, and the rest of the heads of ancestral houses of Israel said to them, “You have no part with us in building a house for our God. We ourselves together will build for the LORD, the God of Israel, as King Cyrus, king of Persia, commanded us.”

v04: Then the people of the land weakened the hands of the people of Judah and frightened them from building.

v05: They hired counselors against them to frustrate their purpose all the days of Cyrus king of Persia, until the reign of Darius king of Persia.
</p>

## Accusations under Later Persian Kings

<p>
v06: In the reign of Ahasuerus, at the beginning of his reign, they wrote an accusation against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem.

v07: And in the days of Artaxerxes, Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel, and the rest of their associates wrote to Artaxerxes king of Persia. The text of the document was written in Aramaic and translated in Aramaic.

v08: Rehum the commander and Shimshai the scribe wrote a letter against Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes as follows.

v09: Then Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe, and the rest of their associates—the judges, officials, administrators, Persians, people of Erech, Babylonians, people of Susa, that is, Elamites,

v10: and the rest of the nations whom the great and noble Osnappar deported and settled in the city of Samaria and in the rest of the province Beyond the River—and so forth.
</p>

## The Letter to Artaxerxes

<p>
v11: This is a copy of the letter that they sent to him:

“To King Artaxerxes: Your servants, the people Beyond the River. And now:

v12: Let it be known to the king that the Judeans who came up from you have come to us at Jerusalem. They are rebuilding the rebellious and evil city. They are finishing the walls and repairing the foundations.

v13: Now let it be known to the king that if this city is rebuilt and the walls are finished, they will not pay tribute, tax, or toll, and in the end it will cause loss to the kings.

v14: Now because we eat the salt of the palace and it is not fitting for us to see the king dishonored, therefore we have sent and made this known to the king,

v15: so that a search may be made in the book of the records of your ancestors. You will find in the book of records and know that this city is a rebellious city, harmful to kings and provinces, and that rebellion has been stirred up within it from ancient days. For this reason this city was destroyed.

v16: We make known to the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are finished, you will have no possession in the province Beyond the River.”
</p>

## Artaxerxes Orders the Work Stopped

<p>
v17: The king sent an answer:

“To Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe, and the rest of their associates who live in Samaria and elsewhere in the province Beyond the River:

Peace. And now:

v18: The document that you sent to us has been read clearly before me.

v19: By my command a search was made, and it was found that this city has risen against kings from ancient days and that rebellion and revolt have been made in it.

v20: Powerful kings have ruled over Jerusalem and governed all the province Beyond the River, and tribute, tax, and toll were paid to them.

v21: Now issue a command to make these men stop, so that this city is not rebuilt until a command is issued by me.

v22: Be careful not to neglect this. Why should damage increase to the injury of the kings?”
</p>

## The Work on the Temple Stops

<p>
v23: Then, when the copy of King Artaxerxes’ document was read before Rehum, Shimshai the scribe, and their associates, they went quickly to Jerusalem to the Judeans and made them stop by force and power.

v24: Then the work on the house of God in Jerusalem stopped and remained stopped until the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.
</p>

## Notes

v01–05: The first opposition belongs to the early Persian period under Cyrus and Darius and concerns the temple project directly.

v02: The claim by neighboring peoples that they “seek your God” reflects the complex religious population created by Assyrian resettlement. The returned leaders reject joint control of the rebuilding project.

v06–23: The narrative moves forward to later reigns under Ahasuerus and Artaxerxes and then returns in verse 24 to the earlier temple chronology. Ezra groups opposition thematically rather than maintaining a simple chronological sequence.

v07: From this point through Ezra 6:18 the book shifts from Hebrew into Imperial Aramaic, the administrative language of the Persian Empire. TSW translates the Aramaic directly while retaining the public English verse numbering.

v07: The phrase commonly rendered “written in Aramaic and translated in Aramaic” likely signals both the script/language and the beginning of the Aramaic documentary section.

v09: Several ethnic and administrative names in the Aramaic list are difficult to identify precisely. TSW uses functional English where the terms appear to be offices and transliterates recognizable peoples and places.

v10: “Beyond the River” is the Persian administrative designation for the province west of the Euphrates River.

v14: “eat the salt of the palace” is an idiom of dependence or loyalty to the royal administration.

v15: The letter is politically strategic: Jerusalem’s earlier independence and rebellions are selected as evidence that rebuilding threatens Persian revenue and control.

v23: “force and power” makes clear that opposition escalates beyond administrative correspondence into coercive intervention.

## Vocabulary

v01: צָרֵי (tsarei)
“Adversaries.” Those opposing Judah and Benjamin.

v04: רָפָה יָדַיִם (raphah yadayim)
“To weaken the hands.” An idiom for discouraging or hindering work.

v05: סָכַר (sakhar)
“To hire.” Counselors are paid to frustrate the rebuilding purpose.

v07: אֲרָמִית (Aramit)
“Aramaic.” The language used in the imperial correspondence.

v08: בְּעֵל־טְעֵם (be'el-te'em)
“Commander” or high official. An Aramaic administrative title used of Rehum.

v10: עֲבַר־נַהֲרָה (Avar-Nahara)
“Beyond the River.” The Persian province west of the Euphrates.

v14: מְלַח הֵיכְלָא (melach heikhla)
“Salt of the palace.” An idiom expressing royal service, dependence, or loyalty.

v23: אֶדְרָע וְחַיִל (edra ve-chayil)
“Force and power.” Coercive means used to halt the work.
