## Ezra Goes Up from Babylon

<p>
v01: After these things, in the reign of Artaxerxes king of Persia, Ezra son of Seraiah, son of Azariah, son of Hilkiah,

v02: son of Shallum, son of Zadok, son of Ahitub,

v03: son of Amariah, son of Azariah, son of Meraioth,

v04: son of Zerahiah, son of Uzzi, son of Bukki,

v05: son of Abishua, son of Phinehas, son of Eleazar, son of Aaron the chief priest—

v06: this Ezra went up from Babylon. He was a skilled scribe in the instruction of Moses, which the LORD, the God of Israel, had given. The king granted him all his request, because the hand of the LORD his God was upon him.

v07: Some of the children of Israel, some of the priests, Levites, singers, gatekeepers, and temple servants also went up to Jerusalem in the seventh year of King Artaxerxes.

v08: Ezra came to Jerusalem in the fifth month, which was in the seventh year of the king.

v09: For on the first day of the first month he began the journey up from Babylon, and on the first day of the fifth month he came to Jerusalem, because the good hand of his God was upon him.

v10: For Ezra had set his heart to seek the instruction of the LORD, to do it, and to teach statute and judgment in Israel.
</p>

## Artaxerxes’ Letter to Ezra

<p>
v11: This is a copy of the document that King Artaxerxes gave to Ezra the priest and scribe, a scribe learned in the words of the commandments of the LORD and his statutes for Israel:

v12: “Artaxerxes, king of kings, to Ezra the priest, scribe of the instruction of the God of heaven, complete peace. And now:

v13: I issue a decree that anyone from the people of Israel, their priests, and the Levites in my kingdom who freely chooses to go to Jerusalem may go with you.

v14: For you are sent by the king and his seven counselors to inquire concerning Judah and Jerusalem according to the instruction of your God that is in your hand,

v15: and to carry the silver and gold that the king and his counselors freely offered to the God of Israel, whose dwelling is in Jerusalem,

v16: together with all the silver and gold that you find in the whole province of Babylon, along with the freewill offering of the people and priests, who freely offer for the house of their God in Jerusalem.
</p>

## Provision for the House of God

<p>
v17: “Therefore with this silver you shall diligently buy bulls, rams, lambs, their grain offerings, and their drink offerings, and offer them on the altar of the house of your God in Jerusalem.

v18: Whatever seems good to you and your brothers to do with the rest of the silver and gold, do according to the will of your God.

v19: The vessels that are given to you for the service of the house of your God, deliver in full before the God of Jerusalem.

v20: Whatever more is needed for the house of your God that falls to you to provide, provide it from the royal treasury.
</p>

## The Treasurers Beyond the River

<p>
v21: “I, King Artaxerxes, issue a decree to all the treasurers in the province Beyond the River: Whatever Ezra the priest, the scribe of the instruction of the God of heaven, requests from you shall be carried out diligently,

v22: up to one hundred talents of silver, one hundred cors of wheat, one hundred baths of wine, one hundred baths of oil, and salt without limit.

v23: Whatever is commanded by the God of heaven, let it be carried out exactly for the house of the God of heaven. Why should wrath come upon the kingdom of the king and his sons?

v24: We also make known to you that you have no authority to impose tribute, tax, or toll upon any of the priests, Levites, singers, gatekeepers, temple servants, or other servants of this house of God.
</p>

## Ezra Is Given Judicial Authority

<p>
v25: “And you, Ezra, according to the wisdom of your God that is in your hand, appoint magistrates and judges to judge all the people in the province Beyond the River who know the laws of your God; and teach anyone who does not know them.

v26: Whoever does not carry out the law of your God and the law of the king, let judgment be executed diligently upon him, whether for death, banishment, confiscation of property, or imprisonment.”
</p>

## Blessed Be the LORD

<p>
v27: Blessed be the LORD, the God of our ancestors, who put such a thing as this into the heart of the king, to beautify the house of the LORD in Jerusalem,

v28: and who extended steadfast love to me before the king, his counselors, and all the king’s powerful officials. I took courage because the hand of the LORD my God was upon me, and I gathered leaders from Israel to go up with me.
</p>

##Notes

v01–05: Ezra’s genealogy places him in the priestly line of Aaron through Eleazar and Phinehas. The genealogy is selective rather than an exhaustive father-to-son list across every generation.

v06: “skilled scribe” describes Ezra as an expert in the instruction of Moses, not merely a copyist. His royal access is interpreted through the hand of God upon him.

v09: The journey from Babylon to Jerusalem takes four months by the chapter’s dates. Its success is attributed to “the good hand” of God.

v10: Ezra’s vocation is ordered as seeking, doing, and teaching. Study is joined to embodied obedience before public instruction.

v11: Verse 11 is Hebrew. The royal letter beginning in verse 12 shifts again into Aramaic and remains Aramaic through verse 26. Hebrew resumes at verse 27.

v12: The Aramaic greeting includes a difficult term often understood as “perfect,” “complete,” or part of a formal salutation. TSW uses “complete peace” to preserve the fullness of the greeting without treating the adjective as a theological description of God.

v14: The “seven counselors” fit Persian court imagery of a privileged royal advisory group.

v22: Talent, cor, and bath are ancient units of weight and volume. TSW retains the units rather than converting them into modern measurements within the Scripture text.

v23: Persian royal support is again framed in concern that the God of heaven not bring wrath upon the king’s dynasty.

v25–26: Ezra receives both teaching and judicial authority under Persian authorization. The penalties listed belong to the imperial decree and range from death to imprisonment.

v27–28: Ezra interprets the king’s policy as the LORD’s work in the royal heart, bringing the narrative back from imperial Aramaic to Hebrew praise.

##Vocabulary

v06: סֹפֵר מָהִיר (sofer mahir)
“Skilled scribe.” An expert practitioner and interpreter of written instruction.

v06: תּוֹרַת מֹשֶׁה (torat Moshe)
“Instruction of Moses.” The body of teaching given by the LORD to Israel.

v09: יַד־יְהוָה (yad YHWH)
“Hand of the LORD.” An image of effective divine guidance and favor.

v10: דָּרַשׁ (darash)
“To seek” or “study.” Ezra sets his heart to seek the LORD’s instruction.

v13: נִתְנַדַּב (nitnaddav)
“To offer oneself freely.” Voluntary willingness to go to Jerusalem.

v22: כִּכָּר (kikkar)
“Talent.” A large unit of weight.

v22: כֹּר (kor)
“Cor.” A large dry measure.

v22: בַּת (bat)
“Bath.” A liquid measure.

v25: דַּיָּן (dayyan)
“Judge.” An official appointed to administer law.

v28: חֶסֶד (ḥesed)
“Steadfast love.” Ezra’s description of the favor God extended to him.
