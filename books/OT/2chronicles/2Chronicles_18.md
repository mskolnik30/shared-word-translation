## Jehoshaphat Allies with Ahab

<p>
v01: Jehoshaphat had riches and honor in abundance, and he allied himself by marriage with Ahab.  

v02: After some years he went down to Ahab at Samaria. Ahab slaughtered sheep and cattle in abundance for him and for the people who were with him and persuaded him to go up against Ramoth-Gilead.  

v03: Ahab king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat king of Judah, “Will you go with me to Ramoth-Gilead?” He said to him, “I am as you are, and my people as your people; we will be with you in the war.”  
</p>

## Jehoshaphat Asks for the Word of the LORD

<p>
v04: Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, “Please inquire today for the word of the LORD.”  

v05: So the king of Israel gathered the prophets, four hundred men, and said to them, “Shall we go against Ramoth-Gilead for battle, or shall I refrain?” They said, “Go up, for God will give it into the hand of the king.”  

v06: But Jehoshaphat said, “Is there not still a prophet of the LORD here, so that we may inquire through him?”  

v07: The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, “There is still one man through whom we may inquire of the LORD, but I hate him, for he never prophesies good concerning me, but always evil. He is Micaiah son of Imlah.” Jehoshaphat said, “Let not the king say so.”  
</p>

## Micaiah Is Summoned

<p>
v08: Then the king of Israel called one court official and said, “Bring quickly Micaiah son of Imlah.”  

v09: Now the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah were sitting each on his throne, clothed in their robes, sitting at the threshing floor at the entrance of the gate of Samaria, and all the prophets were prophesying before them.  

v10: Zedekiah son of Chenaanah made for himself horns of iron and said, “Thus says the LORD: With these you shall gore Aram until you have finished them.”  

v11: All the prophets prophesied likewise, saying, “Go up to Ramoth-Gilead and succeed, for the LORD will give it into the hand of the king.”  
</p>

## Micaiah Speaks the True Vision

<p>
v12: The messenger who had gone to call Micaiah spoke to him, saying, “Look, the words of the prophets are with one mouth good toward the king. Please let your word be like one of theirs and speak good.”  

v13: But Micaiah said, “As the LORD lives, whatever my God says, that I will speak.”  

v14: He came to the king, and the king said to him, “Micaiah, shall we go to Ramoth-Gilead for battle, or shall I refrain?” He said, “Go up and succeed, and they will be given into your hand.”  

v15: The king said to him, “How many times must I put you under oath that you speak to me nothing but truth in the name of the LORD?”  

v16: Then he said:  

“I saw all Israel  
scattered upon the mountains  
like sheep without a shepherd.  
And the LORD said,  
‘These have no master.  
Let each return to his house in peace.’”  

v17: The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, “Did I not tell you that he would not prophesy good concerning me, but evil?”  
</p>

## The Heavenly Council

<p>
v18: Micaiah said, “Therefore hear the word of the LORD: I saw the LORD sitting upon his throne, and all the host of the heavens standing on his right and on his left.  

v19: The LORD said, ‘Who will entice Ahab king of Israel so that he may go up and fall at Ramoth-Gilead?’ One said this, and another said that.  

v20: Then a spirit came forward and stood before the LORD and said, ‘I will entice him.’ The LORD said to him, ‘By what?’  

v21: He said, ‘I will go out and become a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.’ He said, ‘You will entice him and also prevail. Go out and do so.’  

v22: And now, look, the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouth of these prophets of yours, and the LORD has spoken disaster concerning you.”  
</p>

## Micaiah Is Imprisoned

<p>
v23: Then Zedekiah son of Chenaanah drew near and struck Micaiah on the cheek and said, “By what way did the Spirit of the LORD pass from me to speak with you?”  

v24: Micaiah said, “Look, you will see on that day when you go into an inner room to hide yourself.”  

v25: The king of Israel said, “Take Micaiah and return him to Amon governor of the city and to Joash the king’s son,  

v26: and say, ‘Thus says the king: Put this one in prison and feed him bread of affliction and water of affliction until I return in peace.’”  

v27: Micaiah said, “If you ever return in peace, the LORD has not spoken by me.” And he said, “Hear, all you peoples!”  
</p>

## Ahab Dies at Ramoth-Gilead

<p>
v28: So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth-Gilead.  

v29: The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, “I will disguise myself and enter the battle, but you wear your robes.” So the king of Israel disguised himself, and they entered the battle.  

v30: Now the king of Aram had commanded the commanders of his chariots, saying, “Fight with neither small nor great, but only with the king of Israel.”  

v31: And it happened when the commanders of the chariots saw Jehoshaphat that they said, “This is the king of Israel.” So they surrounded him to fight. But Jehoshaphat cried out, and the LORD helped him; God drew them away from him.  

v32: When the commanders of the chariots saw that he was not the king of Israel, they turned back from pursuing him.  

v33: But a man drew his bow in innocence and struck the king of Israel between the scale armor and the breastplate. He said to the chariot driver, “Turn your hand and take me out of the battle, for I am wounded.”  

v34: The battle increased that day, and the king of Israel was propped up in the chariot facing Aram until evening. He died at the time of sunset.  
</p>

## Notes

v1: Jehoshaphat’s marriage alliance with Ahab becomes the beginning of a dangerous entanglement with the northern dynasty.  

v14: Micaiah initially gives a sarcastic answer matching the court prophets, and Ahab recognizes it as insincere.  

v18–22: The heavenly-council vision portrays Ahab’s chosen deception becoming the means of judgment. The scene is intentionally difficult and holds divine sovereignty together with prophetic falsehood.  

v31: Chronicles adds explicit divine help for Jehoshaphat when he cries out.  

v33: “In innocence” means without deliberately targeting Ahab; the apparently random arrow fulfills the true prophecy.
