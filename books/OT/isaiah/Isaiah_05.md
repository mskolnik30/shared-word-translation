<p>
v01: Let me sing for my beloved my beloved’s song concerning his vineyard:  
&emsp;My beloved had a vineyard on a fertile hill.  
v02: He dug it and cleared it of stones,  
&emsp;and planted it with choice vines.  
He built a tower in the midst of it,  
&emsp;and also carved out a wine vat in it.  
He waited for it to yield grapes, but it produced wild grapes.  

</p>
<p>
v03: So now, inhabitants of Jerusalem  
&emsp;and people of Judah,  
&emsp;&emsp;judge between me and my vineyard.  
v04: What more was there to do for my vineyard  
&emsp;that I have not done in it?  
Why, when I waited for grapes,  
&emsp;did it yield wild grapes?  

</p>
<p>
v05: Now I will tell you  
&emsp;what I will do to my vineyard.  
I will remove its hedge, and it will become waste;  
&emsp;I will break down its wall, and it shall be trampled.  
v06: I will make it a desolation;  
&emsp;it will not be pruned nor hoed,  
&emsp;and briars and thorns shall grow;  
&emsp;I will also command the clouds  
&emsp;that they rain no rain upon it.  

</p>
<p>
v07: For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts  
&emsp;is the house of Israel,  
and the people of Judah  
&emsp;are his pleasant planting;  
he waited for justice,  
&emsp;but behold, bloodshed;  
for righteousness,  
&emsp;but behold, an outcry!  

</p>
<p>
v08: Woe to those who join house to house,  
&emsp;who add field to field,  
until there is no place left,  
&emsp;and you are left alone in the land.  
v09: In my hearing, the LORD of hosts said:  
&emsp;Surely many houses shall be desolate,  
&emsp;large and beautiful, without inhabitant.  
v10: For ten acres of vineyard shall produce but one bath,  
&emsp;and a homer of seed shall yield but an ephah.  

</p>
<p>
v11: Woe to those who rise early in the morning  
&emsp;that they may run after strong drink,  
who tarry late into the evening  
&emsp;as wine inflames them!  
v12: They have lyre and harp,  
&emsp;tambourine and flute, and wine at their feasts,  
but they do not regard  
&emsp;the deeds of the LORD,  
or see the work  
&emsp;of his hands.  

</p>
<p>
v13: Therefore my people go into exile  
&emsp;for lack of knowledge;  
their honored men go hungry,  
&emsp;and their multitude parched with thirst.  
v14: Therefore Sheol enlarges its appetite  
&emsp;and opens its mouth beyond measure;  
and down go her nobles,  
&emsp;and her masses, her revelers, and those who exult in her.  

</p>
<p>
v15: The people are bowed down,  
&emsp;everyone brought low;  
&emsp;&emsp;and the eyes of the haughty are humbled.  
v16: But the LORD of hosts is exalted in justice,  
&emsp;and the Holy God shows himself holy in righteousness.  
v17: Then shall the lambs graze  
&emsp;&emsp;as in their pasture,  
and nomads shall eat among  
&emsp;the ruins of the rich.  

</p>
<p>
v18: Woe to those who draw iniquity with cords of falsehood,  
&emsp;who draw sin as with cart ropes,  
v19: who say, “Let him make haste,  
&emsp;let him speed his work  
&emsp;that we may see it;  
let the purpose of the Holy One of Israel draw near  
&emsp;and come, that we may know it!”  

</p>
<p>
v20: Woe to those who call evil good  
&emsp;and good evil,  
who put darkness for light  
&emsp;and light for darkness,  
who put bitter for sweet  
&emsp;and sweet for bitter!  

</p>
<p>
v21: Woe to those who are wise  
&emsp;in their own eyes,  
&emsp;and shrewd in their own sight!  
v22: Woe to those who are heroes at drinking wine,  
&emsp;and valiant men in mixing strong drink,  
v23: who acquit the guilty for a bribe,  
&emsp;and deprive the innocent of his right!  

</p>
<p>
v24: Therefore, as the tongue of fire devours stubble,  
&emsp;&emsp;and as dry grass sinks down in flame,  
so their root will be as rottenness,  
&emsp;and their blossom go up like dust;  
for they have rejected the law of the LORD of hosts,  
&emsp;and have despised the word of the Holy One of Israel.  

</p>
<p>
v25: Therefore the anger of the LORD was kindled against his people,  
&emsp;and he stretched out his hand against them and struck them,  
and the mountains quaked;  
&emsp;and their corpses were as refuse in the streets.  
For all this his anger has not turned away,  
&emsp;and his hand is stretched out still.  

</p>
<p>
v26: He will raise a signal for nations far away,  
&emsp;and whistle for them from the end of the earth;  
And behold, they quickly come,  
v27: none is weary, none stumbles,  
&emsp;none slumbers or sleeps,  
not a belt is loose,  
&emsp;nor a sandal strap broken;  
v28: their arrows are sharp,  
&emsp;all their bows bent,  
Their horses' hooves seem like flint,  
&emsp;And their wheels like the whirlwind.  

</p>
<p>
v29: Their roaring is like a lioness;  
&emsp;like young lions they roar,  
they growl and seize their prey;  
&emsp;they carry it off, and none can rescue.  
v30: They will growl over it on that day,  
&emsp;like the roaring of the sea.  
And if one looks to the land—  
&emsp;behold, darkness and distress;  
&emsp;and the light is darkened by its clouds.  

</p>
## Notes

v1: **5:1**: "my beloved's song concerning his vineyard": This introduces a parabolic narrative where the vineyard symbolizes the house of Israel and God is the beloved caring for it.  
v4: **5:4**: "What more was there to do for my vineyard": A rhetorical question emphasizing the owner's (God's) exhaustive efforts and the vineyard's (Israel's) failure to produce.  
v7: **5:7**: "justice...bloodshed; righteousness...outcry": A wordplay in Hebrew between "mishpat" (justice) and "mishpach" (bloodshed), "tsedaqah" (righteousness) and "tse‘aqah" (outcry), emphasizing the people's moral failure.  
v20: **5:20**: "Woe to those who call evil good": This verse addresses the reversal of moral values, showing the people's deep corruption.  
v25: **5:25**: "For all this his anger has not turned away": A refrain in Isaiah highlighting ongoing divine displeasure despite previous punishments.  

## Vocabulary

v7: **5:7**: יְהוָה (YHWH, Yahweh) — This name of God refers to the eternal, self-existent one. It is personal and relational, as shown by God's expectation of justice and righteousness from Israel.  
v16: **5:16**: צְבָאוֹת (Tseva'oth, Hosts) — Often translated as "Almighty," this term depicts God as a military leader, commanding the armies of heaven.  
v19: **5:19**: קְדוֹשׁ (Kadosh, Holy One) — This title for God emphasizes his distinctness and moral purity, underscoring his rightful expectation of obedience and reverence.
