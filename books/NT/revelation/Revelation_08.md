## The Seventh Seal and the Golden Censer

<p>
v01: When the Lamb opened the seventh seal, there was silence in heaven for about half an hour.

v02: Then I saw the seven angels who stand before God, and seven trumpets were given to them.

v03: And another angel came and stood at the altar with a golden censer, and he was given much incense to offer with the prayers of all the saints on the golden altar before the throne,

v04: and the smoke of the incense, with the prayers of the saints, rose before God from the hand of the angel.

v05: Then the angel took the censer and filled it with fire from the altar and threw it on the earth, and there were peals of thunder, rumblings, flashes of lightning, and an earthquake.
</p>

## The Seven Trumpets

<p>
v06: Now the seven angels who had the seven trumpets prepared to sound them.

v07: The first angel sounded, and there followed hail and fire, mixed with blood, and these were thrown upon the earth. And a third of the earth was burned up, and a third of the trees were burned up, and all green grass was burned up.

v08: The second angel sounded, and something like a great mountain, burning with fire, was thrown into the sea, and a third of the sea became blood,

v09: and a third of the living creatures in the sea died, and a third of the ships were destroyed.

v10: The third angel sounded, and a great star fell from heaven, burning like a torch, and it fell on a third of the rivers and on the springs of water.

v11: The name of the star is called Wormwood. A third of the waters became wormwood, and many people died from the water, because it had been made bitter.

v12: The fourth angel sounded, and a third of the sun was struck, and a third of the moon, and a third of the stars, so that a third of their light might be darkened, and a third of the day might be kept from shining, and likewise a third of the night.

v13: Then I looked, and I heard an eagle crying with a loud voice as it flew in midheaven, “Woe, woe, woe to those who dwell on the earth, at the blasts of the remaining trumpets that the three angels are about to sound!”
</p>
## Notes
v01: Silence interrupts the sequence; response is suspended rather than continued.
v02: The angels are presented as standing before God, emphasizing position rather than origin.
v03–04: Prayers are joined with incense, linking petition and offering without distinction.
v05: The same source (altar fire) moves from offering to judgment.
v06: Preparation precedes action; the sounding is deliberate, not sudden.
v07–12: Each trumpet affects a “third,” indicating limitation rather than totality.
v07: Elements are combined (hail, fire, blood) without explanation of mechanism.
v08: The “mountain” is described comparatively, not identified.
v10–11: Naming (“Wormwood”) interprets effect (bitterness) rather than origin.
v12: Cosmic bodies are partially darkened, not extinguished.
v13: The eagle’s cry anticipates escalation; judgment intensifies rather than concludes.

## Vocabulary

v01: σιγή (sigē)

“Silence.” Absence of sound, here marking interruption or pause.

v02: σάλπιγξ (salpinx)

“Trumpet.” Instrument signaling announcement or action.

v03: λιβανωτός (libanōtos)

“Censer.” Vessel for burning incense.

v04: θυμίαμα (thymiama)

“Incense.” Fragrant offering, associated with prayer.

v05: πῦρ (pyr)

“Fire.” Element of offering and judgment.

v07: χάλαζα (chalaza)

“Hail.” Destructive precipitation, here intensified.

v08: θάλασσα (thalassa)

“Sea.” Domain of life, here partially undone.

v10: ἀστήρ (astēr)

“Star.” Celestial body, functioning as agent.

v11: ἄψινθος (apsinthos)

“Wormwood.” A bitter substance; symbolizes corruption.

v13: οὐρανόθεν (ouranothen)

“Midheaven.” The highest visible point, signaling universal proclamation.
