## From Death to Life

<p>
v01: And you were dead in your trespasses and sins,
v02: in which you once walked, following the course of this age, following the ruler of the authority of the air, the spirit now at work among the children of disobedience.
v03: Among them we too once lived, in the desires of our flesh, doing what the flesh and the mind willed; and we were by nature children of wrath, like the rest.
</p>

<p>
v04: But God, being rich in mercy, because of the great love with which he loved us,
v05: made us alive together with the Messiah—when we were dead in our trespasses—by grace you have been saved—
v06: and raised us up together with him and seated us together with him in the heavenly places, in the Messiah Jesus,
v07: so that in the ages to come he might display the surpassing riches of his grace, in his kindness toward us in the Messiah Jesus.
</p>

## Saved by Grace

<p>
v08: For by grace you have been saved, through faith. And this is not from yourselves; it is the gift of God—
v09: not from works, so that no one may boast.
v10: For we are his making, created in the Messiah Jesus for good works, which God prepared in advance, that we might walk in them.
</p>

## Once Far Off, Now Brought Near

<p>
v11: Therefore remember that once you, the Gentiles in the flesh—called "the uncircumcision" by what is called "the circumcision," the kind that is done in flesh by hands—
v12: that at that time you were apart from the Messiah, alienated from the citizenship of Israel, and strangers to the covenants of promise, having no hope and without God in the world.
v13: But now, in the Messiah Jesus, you who once were far off have been brought near in the blood of the Messiah.
</p>

## The One Who Is Our Peace

<p>
v14: For he himself is our peace, the one who has made the two one and has broken down the dividing wall of hostility,
v15: setting aside in his flesh the law of commandments in decrees, so that in himself he might create the two into one new humanity, making peace,
v16: and might reconcile both to God in one body through the cross, putting the hostility to death by it.
v17: And he came and proclaimed the good news of peace to you who were far off and peace to those who were near.
v18: For through him we both have access in one Spirit to the Father.
</p>

## A Household, a Temple

<p>
v19: So then you are no longer strangers and resident aliens, but you are fellow citizens with the saints and members of the household of God,
v20: built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with the Messiah Jesus himself as the cornerstone,
v21: in whom the whole building, fitted together, is rising into a holy temple in the Lord;
v22: in whom you also are being built together into a dwelling for God in the Spirit.
</p>
## Notes
v1–3: The chapter begins without a verb—"and you, dead." The Greek leaves the sentence dangling until verse 4, where God's "but" finally arrives. The grammar enacts the theology: there is no movement out of death except by what God does next.  
v2: "The course of this age" (αἰών) and "the ruler of the authority of the air" name two layers of formation: the spirit of the time, and a power working within it. The translation preserves both without resolving the relationship between them.  
v3: "Children of wrath" (τέκνα ὀργῆς) is a Hebraic idiom for those who belong to a category, not a metaphysical claim about origin. The phrase names the condition shared with "the rest"—a solidarity in disorder before any solidarity in mercy.  
v5: A parenthesis in the middle of the sentence—"by grace you have been saved"—interrupts the verb chain. Paul cannot finish his own thought without saying it.  
v6: Three verbs are compounded with "together with" (συν-): made alive together, raised together, seated together. The pattern names the believer's life as participation in Christ's, not imitation of it.  
v8: "And this is not from yourselves" — the antecedent of "this" (τοῦτο) has been debated for centuries. It is grammatically neuter while "faith" is feminine, suggesting "this" refers to the whole event of salvation rather than to faith alone. The translation preserves the openness of the Greek.  
v10: "Making" (ποίημα) is the word from which English "poem" comes. The works that follow are not the means of salvation but the form of the life that has been made.  
v11–12: The list of what the Gentiles were apart from Christ is graduated: apart from Messiah, alienated from a people, strangers to covenants, without hope, without God. The translation does not collapse the categories.  
v14: "The dividing wall" (μεσότοιχον) is unique to this verse in the New Testament. It evokes the barrier in the temple complex that kept Gentiles from the inner courts, but its meaning here is broader: every wall raised between the two is named.  
v15: "The law of commandments in decrees" is rendered carefully. The text does not say the law itself is set aside, but the law as a system of regulation that maintained the dividing wall. The translation resists either dismissing or rescuing the Torah; what is set aside is the wall.  
v17: "He came and proclaimed peace" recalls Isaiah 57:19. The risen Christ is the one who announces peace to both who were far and who were near.  
v19–22: The metaphor moves from politics (citizens) to family (household) to architecture (building, temple, dwelling). The community itself becomes the place where God dwells.  
v03: "Children of wrath" (τέκνα ὀργῆς) is a Hebraic idiom for those who belong to a category, not a metaphysical claim about origin. The phrase names the condition shared with "the rest"—a solidarity in disorder before any solidarity in mercy.

##Vocabulary

v01: παραπτώματα (paraptōmata)
“Trespasses." Literally a "falling beside"—deviations from a way, missteps that accumulate.

v03: φύσει (physei)
“By nature." Not by metaphysical essence but by the formed pattern of life apart from grace.

v04: ἔλεος (eleos)
“Mercy." Compassion that acts; the Greek term translates the Hebrew ḥesed in the Septuagint.

v05: συνεζωοποίησεν (synezōopoiēsen)
“Made alive together with." A compound verb stacking three elements: with, alive, made. Resurrection life is shared life from the start.
