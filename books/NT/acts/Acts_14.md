## Paul and Barnabas at Iconium

<p>
v01: Now in Iconium they entered together into the synagogue of the Jews and spoke in such a way that a great multitude of both Jews and Greeks believed.

v02: But the Jews who refused to believe stirred up and poisoned the minds of the Gentiles against the brothers.
</p>

<p>
v03: Therefore they remained for a long time, speaking boldly for the Lord, who was bearing witness to the word of his grace, granting signs and wonders to be done through their hands.

v04: But the multitude of the city was divided, and some were with the Jews and some with the apostles.
</p>

<p>
v05: And when an attempt was made by both Gentiles and Jews with their rulers to mistreat and to stone them,

v06: they became aware of it and fled to the cities of Lycaonia, Lystra and Derbe, and the surrounding region,

v07: and there they were proclaiming the good news.
</p>

## Paul and Barnabas at Lystra

<p>
v08: And in Lystra a certain man was sitting, without strength in his feet, lame from his mother’s womb, who had never walked.

v09: This man was listening to Paul speaking, who, looking intently at him and seeing that he had faith to be healed,

v10: said with a loud voice, “Stand upright on your feet.” And he leaped up and began to walk.
</p>

<p>
v11: And the crowds, seeing what Paul had done, lifted up their voice in the Lycaonian language, saying, “The gods have come down to us in human form!”

v12: And they were calling Barnabas Zeus, and Paul Hermes, because he was the chief speaker.
</p>

<p>
v13: And the priest of Zeus, whose temple was before the city, brought oxen and garlands to the gates and was wanting to offer sacrifice with the crowds.

v14: But when the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard of it, they tore their garments and rushed out into the crowd, crying out

v15: and saying, “Men, why are you doing these things? We also are men of like nature with you, proclaiming good news to you that you should turn from these worthless things to the living God, who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and all that is in them,
</p>

<p>
v16: who in past generations allowed all the nations to walk in their own ways.

v17: And yet he did not leave himself without witness, doing good, giving you rains from heaven and fruitful seasons, filling your hearts with food and gladness.”
</p>

<p>
v18: And saying these things, they scarcely restrained the crowds from offering sacrifice to them.
</p>

<p>
v19: But Jews came from Antioch and Iconium, and persuading the crowds, they stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, thinking that he was dead.

v20: But as the disciples stood around him, he rose up and entered into the city; and on the next day he went out with Barnabas to Derbe.
</p>

## Paul and Barnabas Return to Antioch

<p>
v21: And after they had proclaimed the good news to that city and had made many disciples, they returned to Lystra and to Iconium and to Antioch,

v22: strengthening the souls of the disciples, encouraging them to continue in the faith, and saying that through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdom of God.
</p>

<p>
v23: And appointing elders for them in every church, having prayed with fasting, they entrusted them to the Lord in whom they had believed.
</p>

<p>
v24: And passing through Pisidia, they came to Pamphylia,

v25: and speaking the word in Perga, they went down to Attalia,

v26: and from there they sailed to Antioch, from where they had been commended to the grace of God for the work which they had completed.
</p>

<p>
v27: And arriving and gathering the church together, they reported all that God had done with them and that he had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.

v28: And they stayed no little time with the disciples.
</p>

v03: παρρησιάζομαι (parrēsiazomai)
“To speak boldly.”

v05: ὑβρίζω (hybrizō)
“To mistreat.”

v07: εὐαγγελίζω (euangelizō)
“To proclaim good news.”
