## The Birth of Jesus
v01 In those days a decree went out from Caesar Augustus that all the inhabited world should be registered. v02 This was the first registration, taken while Quirinius was governing Syria. v03 All went to be registered, each to their own city.

v04 Joseph also went up from Galilee, from the city of Nazareth, to Judea, to the city of David called Bethlehem, because he was descended from the house and family of David. v05 He went to be registered with Mary, to whom he was pledged, and who was expecting a child.

v06 While they were there, the time came for her to give birth. v07 She gave birth to her firstborn child, wrapped him in bands of cloth, and laid him in a feeding trough, because there was no place for them in the guest room.

## Good News for All People
v08 In that region there were shepherds living in the fields, keeping watch over their flock by night.

v09 Then an angel of the Lord stood before them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them, and they were filled with great fear. v10 The angel said to them, “Do not be afraid; for see—I am bringing you good news of great joy for all the people.v11 To you is born this day, in the city of David, a Savior, who is the Messiah, the Lord. v12 This will be a sign for you: you will find a child wrapped in bands of cloth and lying in a feeding trough.”

v13 And suddenly there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly host, praising God and saying,

v14 “Glory to God in the highest heaven,
and on earth peace among those whom God favors.”

v15 When the angels had left them and gone into heaven, the shepherds said to one another, “Let us go now to Bethlehem and see this thing that has taken place, which the Lord has made known to us.” v16 So they went with haste and found Mary and Joseph, and the child lying in the feeding trough.

v17 When they saw this, they made known what had been told them about this child. v18 And all who heard it were amazed at what the shepherds told them. v19 But Mary treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart. v20 The shepherds returned, glorifying and praising God for all they had heard and seen, just as it had been told them.

## Offering Jesus to God
v21 When eight days were completed for his circumcision, he was named Jesus—the name given by the angel before he was conceived in the womb. v22 When the days of their purification according to the law of Moses were fulfilled, they brought him up to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord v23 (as it is written in the law of the Lord, “Every firstborn male shall be designated as holy to the Lord”), v24 and they offered a sacrifice according to what is stated in the law of the Lord, “a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons.”

## Notes
v01 Caesar Augustus — The reference situates the story within the reach of the Roman Empire. Luke deliberately names imperial power to contrast it with the quiet, unannounced birth that follows.

v01 all the inhabited world — This reflects the Roman understanding of the empire’s scope. It signals political reach rather than a literal claim about the entire globe.

v02 the first registration — Luke distinguishes this registration from later ones, anchoring the narrative in a specific historical moment.

v04 the city of David — Bethlehem is named not for sentiment but for lineage. Luke connects Jesus to David through geography and ancestry rather than explicit argument.

v05 pledged — The term indicates a formal legal commitment. Mary and Joseph are bound by covenant even before they live together.

v07 no place for them in the upper room — The emphasis is not rejection but lack of space. Luke does not describe hostility, only constraint.

v08 shepherds living in the fields — Shepherds occupied a marginal social position. Luke consistently places the first witnesses of God’s action among those outside centers of power.

v09 the glory of the Lord shone around them — The language echoes Israel’s worship tradition, where God’s presence is revealed as overwhelming light rather than verbal explanation.

v10 Do not be afraid — A standard opening in divine encounters. Fear is acknowledged, not dismissed, before good news is announced.

v11 Savior… Messiah… Lord — Three titles are named together. Luke allows their weight to accumulate without explanation, inviting recognition rather than argument.

v12 a sign for you — The sign is not miraculous power but vulnerability and specificity. The child is identified by circumstance rather than spectacle.

v14 peace among those whom God favors — The line emphasizes God’s initiative. Peace is described as gift, not achievement.

v15 which the Lord has made known to us — The shepherds interpret the event as revelation. Their response is trustful obedience, not analysis.

v19 Mary treasured… pondered — Luke portrays Mary as a reflective witness. She holds the meaning of events without resolving them.

v20 glorifying and praising God — The shepherds return to ordinary life changed by worship. Nothing about their social position is altered, but their orientation is.

## Vocabulary
v01 οἰκουμένη (oikoumenē) — the inhabited or civilized world; a term often used for the Roman Empire as an ordered whole.

v01 ἀπογράφεσθαι (apographesthai) — to be registered or enrolled; an administrative action tied to imperial governance.

v04 οἶκος (oikos) — house or household; here indicating lineage, ancestry, and belonging rather than a physical structure.

v05 ἐμνηστευμένη (emnēsteumenē) — pledged or betrothed; a binding legal relationship, stronger than modern engagement.

v07 πρωτότοκος (prōtotokos) — firstborn; a term with legal and covenantal significance, not merely a statement about birth order.

v07 κατάλυμα (katalyma) — guest room or upper room; a space within a home rather than a commercial inn.

v07 φάτνη (phatnē) — feeding trough or manger; a practical object, not a symbolic prop.

v09 δόξα (doxa) — glory; the visible manifestation of God’s presence and weightiness.

v10 εὐαγγελίζομαι (euangelizomai) — to announce good news; often used for royal or divine proclamations.

v11 σωτήρ (sōtēr) — savior or deliverer; a title used for rulers, now applied to a child born in obscurity.

v11 χριστός (christos) — anointed one; a title rooted in Israel’s hope rather than a personal name.

v11 κύριος (kyrios) — lord; a term used both for God and for imperial authority, carrying layered meaning in this context.

v12 σημεῖον (sēmeion) — sign; an identifying marker that points beyond itself rather than proving something by force.

v14 εἰρήνη (eirēnē) — peace; more than the absence of conflict, suggesting wholeness and restored relationship.

v19 συμβάλλω (symballō) — to ponder or bring together; indicating reflection that holds multiple meanings in tension.

v21: περιτομή (peritomē) — Circumcision. More than a physical act, it marks covenant belonging. Luke presents it without controversy, situating Jesus fully within Jewish religious life from the beginning.

v21: καλέω (kaleō) — “To name” or “to call.” In scripture, naming signifies identity and vocation. Jesus receives a name already spoken by God, underscoring divine initiative over human choice.

v22: καθαρισμός (katharismos) — Often translated “purification.” The term refers to ritual restoration, not moral cleansing. Luke avoids language of sin, framing the practice as communal participation rather than personal fault.

v22: παραστῆσαι (parastēsai) — “To present” or “to place before.” Used in sacrificial and dedicatory contexts. The verb emphasizes offering and availability rather than possession.

v23: πρωτότοκος (prōtotokos) — “Firstborn.” In Israel’s tradition, the firstborn represents both promise and obligation. Luke uses the term to frame Jesus within God’s claim before any human role emerges.

v24: θυσία (thysia) — “Offering” or “sacrifice.” Luke specifies the permitted substitute offering, highlighting accessibility within the law and the family’s modest means.

v24: τρυγόνων… περιστερῶν (trygonōn… peristerōn) — “Turtledoves… pigeons.” These birds were acceptable offerings for those unable to afford larger sacrifices. Luke includes this detail not to romanticize poverty, but to situate the incarnation within ordinary economic reality.
