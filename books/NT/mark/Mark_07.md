## Traditions and Defilement

<p>
v01: And the Pharisees gathered to him, with some of the scribes who had come from Jerusalem.

v02: And they saw that some of his disciples were eating bread with defiled hands, that is, unwashed.

v03: For the Pharisees and all the Jews do not eat unless they wash their hands carefully, holding to the tradition of the elders.

v04: And from the marketplace, unless they wash, they do not eat, and there are many other things which they have received to hold—washings of cups and pitchers and copper vessels.
</p>

<p>
v05: And the Pharisees and the scribes asked him, “Why do your disciples not walk according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with defiled hands?”

v06: And he said to them, “Well did Isaiah prophesy concerning you hypocrites, as it is written,

‘This people honors me with their lips,

but their heart is far from me;

v07: in vain do they worship me,

teaching as doctrines the commandments of men.’
</p>

<p>
v08: Leaving the commandment of God, you hold fast the tradition of men.”

v09: And he said to them, “You have a fine way of setting aside the commandment of God in order to establish your tradition.
</p>

<p>
v10: For Moses said, ‘Honor your father and your mother,’ and, ‘Whoever speaks evil of father or mother shall surely die.’

v11: But you say, ‘If a person says to father or mother, “Whatever benefit you might have received from me is Corban”—that is, a gift devoted to God—

v12: then you no longer allow him to do anything for his father or mother,

v13: nullifying the word of God by your tradition that you handed down. And many things like this you do.”
</p>

<p>
v14: And calling the crowd again, he said to them, “Hear me, all of you, and understand:

v15: there is nothing outside a person that going into him can defile him, but the things that come out of a person are what defile.”
</p>

## The Syrophoenician Woman

## What Defiles a Person

<p>
v17: And when he entered the house away from the crowd, his disciples asked him about the parable.

v18: He said to them, “Are you also without understanding? Do you not understand that nothing entering a person from outside can defile him,

v19: because it does not enter his heart but his stomach, and goes out into the latrine?” Thus he declared all foods clean.

v20: And he said, “What comes out of a person, that defiles the person.

v21: For from within, out of the heart of human beings, come evil thoughts, sexual immoralities, thefts, murders, adulteries,

v22: greed, wickedness, deceit, sensuality, an evil eye, slander, pride, and foolishness.

v23: All these evil things come from within and defile the person.”
</p>

<p>
v24: And rising from there, he went away to the region of Tyre. And entering a house, he wanted no one to know, yet he was not able to be hidden.

v25: But immediately a woman whose little daughter had an unclean spirit, hearing about him, came and fell at his feet.

v26: Now the woman was a Greek, Syrophoenician by birth, and she was asking him to cast the demon out of her daughter.

v27: And he said to her, “Let the children first be fed, for it is not right to take the children’s bread and throw it to the dogs.”

v28: But she answered and said to him, “Lord, even the dogs under the table eat from the children’s crumbs.”

v29: And he said to her, “Because of this word, go; the demon has gone out of your daughter.”

v30: And going to her house, she found the child lying on the bed, and the demon gone.
</p>

## Jesus Heals a Deaf Man

<p>
v31: Again, leaving the region of Tyre, he went through Sidon to the Sea of Galilee, through the midst of the region of the Decapolis.

v32: They brought to him one who was deaf and had difficulty speaking, and they begged him to lay his hand on him.

v33: Taking him away from the crowd by himself, he put his fingers into his ears, and after spitting, he touched his tongue.

v34: Looking up to heaven, he sighed and said to him, “Ephphatha,” that is, “Be opened.”

v35: Immediately his ears were opened, and the bond of his tongue was released, and he began speaking plainly.

v36: He ordered them that they should tell no one. But the more he ordered them, the more abundantly they proclaimed it.

v37: They were exceedingly astonished, saying, “He has done all things well. He even makes the deaf hear and the mute speak.”
</p>
## Notes
v08: Tension between commandment and tradition is not resolved abstractly but concretely.
v15: Defilement is reoriented from external contact to internal source.
v27–28: The exchange preserves tension; boundaries are tested but not erased.
v29: The woman’s “word” becomes the turning point.
v16: This verse is absent from the earliest and strongest Greek witnesses; later manuscripts add, “If anyone has ears to hear, let him hear.” Public verse numbering is retained.
