## The Genealogy of Jesus the Messiah

<p>
v01: The book of the genealogy of Jesus the Messiah, son of David, son of Abraham.

v02: Abraham fathered Isaac, and Isaac fathered Jacob, and Jacob fathered Judah and his brothers,

v03: and Judah fathered Perez and Zerah by Tamar, and Perez fathered Hezron, and Hezron fathered Ram,

v04: and Ram fathered Amminadab, and Amminadab fathered Nahshon, and Nahshon fathered Salmon,

v05: and Salmon fathered Boaz by Rahab, and Boaz fathered Obed by Ruth, and Obed fathered Jesse,

v06: and Jesse fathered David the king.

And David fathered Solomon by the wife of Uriah,

v07: and Solomon fathered Rehoboam, and Rehoboam fathered Abijah, and Abijah fathered Asa,

v08: and Asa fathered Jehoshaphat, and Jehoshaphat fathered Joram, and Joram fathered Uzziah,

v09: and Uzziah fathered Jotham, and Jotham fathered Ahaz, and Ahaz fathered Hezekiah,

v10: and Hezekiah fathered Manasseh, and Manasseh fathered Amos, and Amos fathered Josiah,

v11: and Josiah fathered Jechoniah and his brothers at the time of the deportation to Babylon.

v12: And after the deportation to Babylon: Jechoniah fathered Shealtiel, and Shealtiel fathered Zerubbabel,

v13: and Zerubbabel fathered Abiud, and Abiud fathered Eliakim, and Eliakim fathered Azor,

v14: and Azor fathered Zadok, and Zadok fathered Achim, and Achim fathered Eliud,

v15: and Eliud fathered Eleazar, and Eleazar fathered Matthan, and Matthan fathered Jacob,

v16: and Jacob fathered Joseph the husband of Mary, from whom Jesus was born, who is called the Messiah.
</p>

<p>
v17: So all the generations from Abraham to David were fourteen generations, and from David to the deportation to Babylon fourteen generations, and from the deportation to Babylon to the Messiah fourteen generations.
</p>

## The Birth of Jesus the Messiah

<p>
v18: Now the birth of Jesus the Messiah was in this way: when his mother Mary had been betrothed to Joseph, before they came together she was found to be with child from the Holy Spirit.

v19: And Joseph her husband, being righteous and not willing to expose her to public shame, resolved to divorce her quietly.

v20: But as he considered these things, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream, saying, “Joseph, son of David, do not fear to take Mary as your wife, for what is conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit.

v21: She will bear a son, and you shall call his name Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins.”
</p>

<p>
v22: All this took place to fulfill what the Lord had spoken by the prophet:

v23: “Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and they shall call his name Immanuel” (which means, God with us).
</p>

<p>
v24: And Joseph rose from sleep and did as the angel of the Lord commanded him: he took his wife,

v25: but he did not know her until she had borne a son. And he called his name Jesus.
</p>
## Notes
v01: “Book of the genealogy” signals both record and narrative opening; Matthew frames Jesus within covenant history rather than beginning with birth alone.  
v03: Inclusion of Tamar introduces irregular lineage; Matthew does not sanitize the line but foregrounds it.  
v05: Rahab and Ruth—Gentile women—anticipate the widening scope of the Messiah’s mission.  
v06: “David the king” marks the royal hinge of the genealogy; the structure centers on kingship.  
v11: Deportation to Babylon functions as theological rupture, not merely historical transition.  
v16: “From whom Jesus was born” shifts grammar—Joseph is not said to father Jesus; the line narrows to Mary.  
v17: The threefold fourteen pattern is schematic, emphasizing theological ordering over strict biological completeness.  
v18: “In this way” introduces not merely sequence but interpretive framing of the birth.  
v19: “Righteous” paired with restraint; justice is expressed as mercy.  
v21: “Jesus” (Yeshua) explicitly tied to saving action; name and mission are inseparable.  
v23: “Immanuel” interprets the birth as divine presence, not merely divine action.  
v25: “Did not know her” preserves both virginal conception and ordinary marital progression afterward.

## Vocabulary

v01: Χριστός (Christos)
“Messiah; Anointed One.” A royal title rooted in Israel’s expectation of an anointed ruler.

v01: γενέσεως (geneseōs)
“Genealogy; origin.” The word can denote lineage or origin and is related to the language of “genesis.”

v19: δίκαιος (dikaios)
“Righteous; just.” Describes Joseph as acting in accordance with what is right.

v23: Ἐμμανουήλ (Emmanouēl)
“Immanuel,” meaning “God with us.”

v11: μετοικεσία (metoikesia)
“Deportation; exile.” Refers to forced removal to Babylon.
