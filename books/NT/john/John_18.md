## Jesus Is Arrested

<p>
v01: After Jesus had spoken these words, he went out with his disciples across the Kidron Valley, where there was a garden, which he and his disciples entered.
v02: Now Judas, who betrayed him, also knew the place, because Jesus often met there with his disciples.
v03: So Judas brought a cohort of soldiers and some officers from the chief priests and the Pharisees, and they came there with lanterns and torches and weapons.
v04: Then Jesus, knowing all that would happen to him, came forward and said to them, “Whom are you looking for?”
v05: They answered him, “Jesus of Nazareth.” Jesus said to them, “I am he.” Judas, who betrayed him, was standing with them.
v06: When Jesus said to them, “I am he,” they drew back and fell to the ground.
v07: So he asked them again, “Whom are you looking for?” And they said, “Jesus of Nazareth.”
v08: Jesus answered, “I told you that I am he. So if you are looking for me, let these go.”
v09: This was to fulfill the word that he had spoken: “Of those you gave me I have lost not one.”
v10: Then Simon Peter, having a sword, drew it and struck the high priest’s slave and cut off his right ear. The slave’s name was Malchus.
v11: Jesus said to Peter, “Put your sword into its sheath. Shall I not drink the cup that the Father has given me?”
</p>

## Jesus Before the High Priest

<p>
v12: So the soldiers, their officer, and the Judean officers arrested Jesus and bound him.
v13: First they led him to Annas, for he was the father-in-law of Caiaphas, who was high priest that year.
v14: It was Caiaphas who had advised the Judeans that it was better to have one person die for the people.
</p>

## Peter Denies Jesus

<p>
v15: Simon Peter followed Jesus, and so did another disciple. Since that disciple was known to the high priest, he entered with Jesus into the courtyard of the high priest,
v16: but Peter stood outside at the door. So the other disciple, who was known to the high priest, went out and spoke to the woman who kept the door, and brought Peter in.
v17: The woman who kept the door said to Peter, “You are not also one of this man’s disciples, are you?” He said, “I am not.”
v18: Now the slaves and the officers were standing around a charcoal fire they had made, because it was cold, and they were warming themselves. Peter also was with them, standing and warming himself.
</p>

## Jesus Questioned

<p>
v19: The high priest questioned Jesus about his disciples and about his teaching.
v20: Jesus answered him, “I have spoken openly to the world. I always taught in synagogues and in the temple, where all the Judeans gather, and I have said nothing in secret.
v21: Why do you question me? Question those who have heard what I spoke to them. Look—they know what I said.”
v22: When he had said this, one of the officers standing by struck Jesus with his hand, saying, “Is that how you answer the high priest?”
v23: Jesus answered him, “If I have spoken wrongly, testify about the wrong; but if rightly, why do you strike me?”
v24: Annas then sent him bound to Caiaphas the high priest.
</p>

## Peter Denies Again

<p>
v25: Now Simon Peter was standing and warming himself. So they said to him, “You are not also one of his disciples, are you?” He denied it and said, “I am not.”
v26: One of the slaves of the high priest, a relative of the man whose ear Peter had cut off, said, “Did I not see you in the garden with him?”
v27: Peter again denied it; and at once a rooster crowed.
</p>

## Jesus Before Pilate

<p>
v28: Then they led Jesus from Caiaphas to the governor’s headquarters. It was early morning. They themselves did not enter the headquarters, so that they would not be defiled, but could eat the Passover.
v29: So Pilate went out to them and said, “What accusation do you bring against this man?”
v30: They answered him, “If this man were not doing evil, we would not have handed him over to you.”
v31: Pilate said to them, “Take him yourselves and judge him according to your law.” The Judeans said to him, “It is not lawful for us to put anyone to death.”
v32: This was to fulfill the word that Jesus had spoken to show by what kind of death he was going to die.
</p>

<p>
v33: So Pilate entered the headquarters again and called Jesus and said to him, “Are you the King of the Judeans?”
v34: Jesus answered, “Do you say this from yourself, or did others say it to you about me?”
v35: Pilate answered, “Am I a Judean? Your own nation and the chief priests have handed you over to me. What have you done?”
v36: Jesus answered, “My kingdom is not from this world. If my kingdom were from this world, my servants would be fighting so that I might not be handed over to the Judeans. But as it is, my kingdom is not from here.”
v37: Then Pilate said to him, “So you are a king?” Jesus answered, “You say that I am a king. For this I was born, and for this I have come into the world: to bear witness to the truth. Everyone who is of the truth listens to my voice.”
v38: Pilate said to him, “What is truth?” After saying this, he went out again to the Judeans and told them, “I find no case against him.
v39: But you have a custom that I release one prisoner for you at Passover. So do you want me to release for you the King of the Judeans?”
v40: They cried out again, “Not this one, but Barabbas!” Now Barabbas was a bandit.
</p>
## Notes
v01–03: garden / torches / weapons — The light-and-dark imagery begins early: armed power arrives at night to seize the Light of the world.
v04–06: “Whom are you looking for?… I am he… they fell” — Jesus is not captured by surprise; he steps forward in sovereign self-identification. The falling back signals the weight of his presence and authority.
v08–09: “let these go… lost not one” — Even in arrest, Jesus shepherds and protects his disciples; his self-giving is purposeful, not chaotic.
v10–11: Peter’s sword / “drink the cup” — Jesus rejects violent defense; his path is obedience to the Father, receiving suffering rather than inflicting it.
v12–14: Annas/Caiaphas — The narrative layers political-religious calculus: “better… one person die” exposes utilitarian logic dressed as public good.
v15–18: charcoal fire / Peter warming himself — John’s detail sets the stage for denial as fear-driven self-preservation; warmth is sought while loyalty cools.
v19–21: “I have spoken openly” — Jesus refuses the frame of secrecy; his teaching is public and accountable. The trial turns on power, not evidence.
v22–23: struck / “testify… if wrongly” — Jesus models non-retaliatory truth-telling: name the injustice, demand testimony, refuse humiliation as normal.
v25–27: three denials / rooster — Peter’s collapse is progressive and public; the rooster marks the bitter intersection of prediction and failure.
v28: “not enter… not be defiled… eat the Passover” — A sharp irony: ritual purity is guarded while injustice is pursued. John exposes piety that protects itself while violating the heart of covenant faithfulness.
v31–32: “not lawful… to put to death” — Responsibility is distributed through legal maneuvering; the machinery of execution is assembled while blame is displaced.
v33–37: “King… My kingdom is not from this world” — Jesus reframes kingship: not sourced in worldly power or enforced by violence. His reign is truth-bearing witness that summons allegiance.
v37: “Everyone who is of the truth listens” — Truth is personal and relational here—hearing and following Jesus’ voice, not merely holding correct propositions.
v38: “What is truth?” — Pilate’s cynicism (or evasiveness) embodies imperial pragmatism: power uninterested in truth unless it serves control.
v39–40: Barabbas released — The crowd chooses a violent insurgent (“bandit”) over the nonviolent king; the irony underscores how “freedom” is often imagined through force rather than truth-shaped peace.
