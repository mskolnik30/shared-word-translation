## Peace with God
v01 Therefore, having been set right through trust, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.
v02 Through him we have also been given access into this grace in which we stand, and we boast in the hope of the glory of God.
v03 And not only this—we also boast in sufferings, knowing that suffering produces endurance,
v04 and endurance produces tested character, and tested character produces hope.
v05 And hope does not put us to shame, because the love of God has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit that has been given to us.

v06 For while we were still weak, at the appointed time Christ died for the ungodly.
v07 Indeed, rarely will anyone die for a righteous person—though perhaps for a good person someone might even dare to die.
v08 But God demonstrates God’s love for us in this: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.
v09 Much more surely then, having now been set right by his blood, will we be saved through him from wrath.
v10 For if while we were enemies we were reconciled to God through the death of the Son, much more surely, having been reconciled, will we be saved by his life.
v11 And not only this, but we boast in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received reconciliation.

## Adam and Christ
v12 Therefore, just as sin entered the world through one human being, and death through sin, and so death spread to all, because all sinned—
v13 for sin was indeed in the world before the law, but sin is not reckoned where there is no law.
v14 Yet death exercised dominion from Adam to Moses, even over those whose sin was not like the transgression of Adam, who is a type of the one who was to come.

v15 But the gift is not like the trespass. For if the many died through the trespass of the one, much more surely have the grace of God and the gift in the grace of the one human being, Jesus Christ, overflowed for the many.
v16 And the gift is not like the effect of the one person’s sin. For the judgment following one trespass led to condemnation, but the gift following many trespasses leads to being set right.
v17 For if, by the trespass of the one, death exercised dominion through that one, much more surely will those who receive the abundance of grace and the gift of righteousness exercise dominion in life through the one—Jesus Christ.

## Notes
5:1 — “having been set right through trust”
The Greek participle (dikaiōthentes) summarizes the argument of Romans 3–4 rather than restating it. The phrase “through trust” is intentionally open, carrying forward the prior discussion of trust grounded in Christ’s faithful action without re-arguing the genitive question here.

5:2 — “we boast”
The verb often translated “rejoice” or “exult” (kauchōmetha) retains its stronger sense of confident boasting. The term is ethically charged in Paul: boasting is excluded when directed toward the self, but reclaimed when oriented toward God and God’s work.

5:3–5 — suffering → endurance → tested character → hope
This is not a general psychology of suffering. Paul describes a specifically eschatological sequence shaped by participation in God’s reconciling work. The logic assumes the prior reality of peace with God, not suffering as a means to earn it.

5:5 — “God’s love has been poured into our hearts”
The image evokes abundance and permanence, not a temporary infusion. The Spirit is named as the agent, but the love is explicitly God’s, preserving Trinitarian distinction without explanation.

5:6 — “at the appointed time”
The phrase signals divine timing rather than historical accident. Paul frames Christ’s death as purposive within God’s economy, not merely exemplary.

5:9 — “saved … from wrath”
“Wrath” functions here as an impersonal force within God’s righteous ordering of the world, not as an emotional outburst. The future orientation (“will be saved”) holds together present reconciliation and future hope without collapsing them.

5:12 — “because all sinned”
The clause is syntactically ambiguous in Greek and has generated extensive debate. This translation preserves the ambiguity rather than resolving it into inherited guilt or mere imitation. Paul’s focus is the spread of death, not a theory of transmission.

5:14 — “who is a type of the one who was to come”
“Type” (typos) signals correspondence without equivalence. Adam and Christ are linked structurally (each as a representative “one”), but the effects of their actions are deliberately unequal.

5:15–17 — “much more surely”
The repeated comparative intensifies Paul’s argument: grace does not merely counteract sin; it overwhelms it. The asymmetry is essential. Any reading that treats Adam and Christ as equal causal forces misses Paul’s emphasis.

5:17 — “exercise dominion in life”
The verb echoes the earlier claim that death “exercised dominion.” Dominion shifts from death to those who receive grace, anticipating Paul’s later language of new life without yet describing its ethical shape.

## Vocabulary
v01: δικαιόω (dikaioō)
“To set right.” Denotes being restored to right standing rather than merely declared acquitted. In Romans 5, the verb gathers the argument of chapters 3–4 and functions as a settled reality.

v01: πίστις (pistis)
“Trust.” A relational term of reliance and allegiance. Here it appears without an explicit object, allowing continuity with Christ’s faithful action while naming the human posture of trust.

v01: εἰρήνη (eirēnē)
“Peace.” Refers to restored relationship and wholeness, not inner calm. “Peace with God” signals the end of hostility.

v02: καυχάομαι (kauchaomai)
“To boast.” A morally charged verb reclaimed by Paul to describe confident orientation toward God’s work rather than self-achievement.

v05: ἀγάπη (agapē)
“Love.” Defined narratively rather than abstractly: God’s love is poured out and demonstrated in action, not sentiment.

v10: καταλλαγή (katallagē)
“Reconciliation.” A relational exchange involving the removal of enmity, initiated by God and received rather than negotiated.

v14: βασιλεύω (basileuō)
“To exercise dominion.” Used first of death’s reign, preparing for its reversal later in the chapter.

v17: βασιλεύω (basileuō)
“To exercise dominion.” The verb is transferred from death to those who receive grace, signaling a shift in authority and anticipating new life.
