# Focused bilingual authoring reread: Mark 4–8

Status: AUTHORING_REREAD_COMPLETED. This is the drafting assistant's own
source/English reread, not human review or independent scholarly review.
Editorial status remains REVIEW_PENDING; publication_allowed remains false.

All 214 exact pinned Greek verse payloads were read before authoring their
English drafts. Mark 4–6 was drafted before consulting prior Fluent; Mark 7–8
was likewise drafted from Greek before its prior wording was consulted by the
binding process. TSW was compared after drafting, not used as a source template.
The focused second pass displayed 75 selected Greek payloads beside current
Fluent and TSW. Exact rows are retained in focused-comparisons.json. Selection
includes every provisional F3 decision plus concrete names/numbers/variant
checks. This is not a full-book consistency review.

## Findings and retained choices

- 4:8, 20: the ascending thirty/sixty/hundred yields agree. In 4:11–12 the
  mystery is given, and the unsettling so-that/lest language is not changed to
  a causal explanation. Quotation lineation is retained in the chapter file.
- 4:22 keeps hiddenness directed toward revelation. 4:31 corrects the earlier
  'among the smallest' qualification to the source's unqualified superlative.
  The old apologetic note is replaced with a brief translation note.
- 5:7 retains the adjuration by God. 5:9–10 retains one voice saying 'my,'
  then 'we,' and pleading for 'them.' The man is not equated with a modern
  medical diagnosis. The approximate two thousand pigs and the real violence
  to the man and animals are not softened.
- 5:23, 28, 34 uses the healing sense of sōzō while recording its rescue/save
  range. 'Daughter' remains. 5:36 preserves overheard/disregarded uncertainty.
  The twelve-year illness and the girl's age of twelve remain distinct.
  5:41 retains the Aramaic and the narrative's own explanation; both immediacy
  markers in 5:42 are represented.
- 6:3 retains Mary and all four brothers' names. 6:5 retains could not, not
  would not. 6:8–9 permits staff and sandals. 6:11 does not import an extra
  Sodom saying. 6:14 follows plural 'they were saying,' and 6:20 follows
  'was puzzled.' 6:22 follows Herodias's own daughter, unlike the TSW comparator;
  no personal name is supplied for the girl. 6:30 actually contains apostles,
  unlike the absent clause corrected in the earlier 3:14 revision.
- 6:37–44 retains two hundred denarii, five loaves, two fish, groups of a hundred
  and fifty, twelve baskets, and five thousand men. Blessing is distinct from
  the thanksgiving in 8:6. 6:45 and 53 preserve Bethsaida as direction and
  Gennesaret as landing without a harmonizing route explanation.
- 6:48 keeps the fourth watch and the intention to pass by. 6:50 now uses
  natural self-identification, 'It is I,' with literal 'I am' in the note. The
  earlier verse-review policy's wording-specific decision is historical, not
  binding on this revision. The current ledger and chapter supersede that
  wording choice; independent review of the resonance remains pending.
  6:52 keeps hardened hearts; 6:56 notes the pronoun's possible referents.
- 7:3 does not pretend the washing gesture is certain. The note gives literal
  'with the fist' and alternatives. The narrator's broad ethnic description is
  retained without turning it into a timeless generalization. 7:4 includes
  dining couches from the actual pinned payload; variant markers are not
  instructions to remove those words. 7:16 remains absent.
- 7:10–13 preserves the severe quotation and the Corban sequence without a
  sermon. 7:19's masculine participle is read as a narrator's comment about
  Jesus, with the digestion reading noted. In 7:21–22 adultery belongs under
  verse 22 in this pinned source, unlike its placement in TSW. Every item in
  the list is retained. The evil-eye idiom is recorded beside 'envy.'
- 7:24 has Tyre alone; 7:31 also has Sidon and the Decapolis. 7:26's Greek /
  Gentile choice is explained. 7:27–29 preserves first, the dogs, the crumbs,
  and the woman's reply as the stated reason. No 'yes' is added at 7:28.
  7:33 does not invent where Jesus spat, and 7:35 does not import 'immediately.'
- 8:6–9 and 19–20 keep the two feeding accounts distinct: seven loaves, a few
  small fish, seven large baskets, and about four thousand in this account;
  five/twelve/five thousand in the recollection of the first. The larger-basket
  term remains distinct. No specifically male count is added at 8:9.
- 8:12 retains categorical refusal, not an imported Jonah exception. The
  warning has Herod, not another group. 8:23–25 preserves spitting, partial
  sight, the walking-tree comparison, and the second touch. 8:26 does not
  expand the short prohibition. 8:29 has Messiah with no imported confession
  clause. 8:31 keeps must and after three days. 8:33 preserves the rebuke of
  Peter in the disciples' presence. 8:34–38 keeps self-denial, the cross,
  save/lose life, reciprocal shame, and the severe description of the generation.
