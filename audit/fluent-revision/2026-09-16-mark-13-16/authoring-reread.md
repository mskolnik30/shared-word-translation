# Focused bilingual authoring reread: Mark 13–16

Status: completed by the drafting assistant. This is a source-based authoring reread, not human review or independent scholarly review. Editorial status remains `REVIEW_PENDING`; publication is not allowed.

## Method

I read every exact pinned SBLGNT record for Mark 13–16 before drafting its English verse and rationale. I wrote the draft and rationale before consulting the parent Fluent wording. I then compared every verse with the parent Fluent text and TSW, using both only as comparators. The build audit independently verifies each stored record hash, the full source-file SHA-256, and the Git blob bound to Faithlife/SBLGNT commit `c4d241a9c1c479a55b989ba35a4976c1d0b8052c`.

The focused reread covers all 175 extant source verses, including every F3 verse and every verse selected for names, numbers, speakers, movement, repetition, ambiguity, violence, or textual-variant sensitivity. Mark 15:28 is not an extant source verse; its public-label gap is preserved and documented.

## Findings retained in the draft

- Mark 13 keeps the temple and Mount of Olives setting, all four named disciples, wars that are not yet the end, worldwide proclamation, the abomination standing where it should not, the reader aside, the repeated chosen/chose language, the open referent in “it is near,” “this generation,” the Son’s not knowing, slaves with assigned work, four night watches, and repeated commands to stay alert.
- Mark 14 keeps Bethany, Simon the leper, the head-anointing, more than three hundred denarii, the difficult sentence about the poor, the Passover movements and speakers, body and “blood of the covenant” without adding “new,” two rooster crowings and three denials, Jesus’ anguish and cup prayer, difficult “Enough!”, Judas’s kiss, the unnamed attacker and high priest’s slave, the naked fleeing young man, made/not made by hands, “I am,” Power and the clouds, the first crow in verse 68, the source’s speech resemblance in verse 70, and the difficult final verb describing Peter’s weeping.
- Mark 15 keeps Barabbas’s revolt and murder, Pilate’s desire to satisfy the crowd, the whole cohort, Simon of Cyrene and Alexander and Rufus, the omitted label 28, the third/sixth/ninth hours, the Aramaic cry and translation, the centurion’s wording, the named women as followers and servants, and Pilate’s stark grant of the corpse.
- Mark 16 keeps the women’s names, first-day sunrise, the young man rather than importing “angel,” the empty-tomb announcement, and the continuous main text ending in verse 8 with trembling, astonishment, fear, and silence. The exact verse-8 payload’s bracketed shorter ending is translated only in a note as an alternative tradition. The bracketed longer ending is kept as verses 9–20 under a separate heading. Its belief, baptism, snake, poison, healing, exaltation, and proclamation statements are preserved without turning them into commands to test God.
