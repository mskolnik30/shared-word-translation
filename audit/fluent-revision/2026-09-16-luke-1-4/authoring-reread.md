# Luke 1–4: authoring reread

Editorial draft. This is the drafting assistant's self-reread; independent scholarly review and reader testing remain pending. Publication is not allowed.

The current canonical checkpoint was recovered after workspace maintenance removed the prior checkout. Its entire manifest was checked, the repository was recovered through its cumulative Git bundle, and the checkpoint head and exact parent history were preserved. No GitHub write was attempted. The recovered checkpoint included all Mark and 978 revised chapters. It did not yet include revised Luke chapters.

Every pinned Greek verse in Luke 1 was read before authoring chapter 1; chapter 2 was read before authoring chapter 2; chapters 3–4 were read before authoring those chapters. All 214 verses and individual rationales were drafted before the parent Fluent was read. The parent Fluent text and apparatus for all four chapters were then read. TSW was consulted afterward as a comparator. No English version supplied the drafting template; no word-change quota was used. Full source hashes match both the checkpoint's additional Gospel bindings and the earlier Luke source ledger's Git blob. Exact verse hashes retain whitespace and variant markers.

The 137 selected references in config.json were displayed with the Greek, the new English, and TSW and reread. They include all provisional F3 decisions, the entire genealogy, names and numbers, songs, speaker changes, and important source variants. The final records are bound in focused-comparisons.json. Structural assertions supplement rather than replace this reading.

The preface's opening causal construction was restored during review so its sentence does not become a comma splice. The formal tone remains distinct from the narrative. Zechariah and Elizabeth are both described as righteous; Elizabeth's disgrace is her description of her social situation. Father's hearts, Mary's slave self-designation and voiced assent, rulers/lowly and hungry/rich, the horn of salvation, and the dawn remain visible. Familiar concise biblical phrases are retained where changing them would add nothing. Words of promise, fulfillment, mercy, covenant, and release remain connected through the poetry.

The birth narrative retains first registration, Quirinius governing Syria, the marriage pledge, firstborn son, guest room with its wider lodging sense, and a manger explained as a feeding trough. No building layout, innkeeper, season, or chronology harmonization was added. The heavenly army speaks; the text does not say it sings. Their purification, his father and mother, Anna's seven married years and eighty-four with an alternative note, the child's age twelve, and the three-day search are retained. Dashes keep the sword saying within the larger opposition/revelation prophecy. Father's house has the alternative Father's affairs in a note.

All genealogy names were compared in sequence with the Greek and the comparator. Each repeated descent link is explicit. The pinned file's Iōbēl is represented as Jobel at 3:32 rather than the parent/comparator's Obed; this is a provisional source-form choice explicitly noted for independent onomastic review. Sala, Admin, Arni, Neri, Nathan, and both occurrences of Kainam/Cainan remain. Joshua at 3:29 and Cainan at 3:36–37 use established English name forms for the Greek, not alleged alternative Greek readings. Enosh follows the recognizable ancestral name. The list ends with Adam and God, without a new explanation of its relationship to Matthew's genealogy.

John addresses the crowds, and repentance retains clothing, food, tax limits, extortion, false accusations, and pay. The false-accusation prohibition was kept distinct from extortion during review. Prayer and bodily form remain explicit at Jesus' baptism. The approximate age is retained, and ‘began his work’ makes an elliptical expression readable.

The testing sequence is bread, kingdoms, Jerusalem. The short bread quotation, no supplied mountain, no added dismissal of Satan, no brokenhearted clause in 4:18, and Judea at 4:44 follow the pinned source. The devil's claim is marked as his speech. The Nazareth quotation keeps all its named recipients and images without importing a sermon. Elijah's three years and six months, the Sidonian widow, and Naaman the Syrian remain specific. Fever is rebuked without being called a demon; each sick person receives touch. A final wording adjustment uses natural singular ‘their’ in the generic prophet saying.

Affected entries in Luke's earlier book-wide ledger retain their original English hashes and classifications as historical data and are explicitly marked superseded with the new ledger location. The other twenty chapters' entries remain byte-for-byte equivalent as data. The old chapter reviews are preserved in the Git parent. Source provenance remains valid; old wording approval and Companion quotation bindings do not transfer. Whole-book reviews remain queued, including the previously queued review of Mark.
