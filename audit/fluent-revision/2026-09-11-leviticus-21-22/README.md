# Fluent Leviticus 21–22 revision

All 57 verses (24 + 33) received source-based drafting and verse-specific rationales. Cumulative coverage is 3,511 verses in 117 chapters: Genesis 1–50, Exodus 1–40, Leviticus 1–22, and James 1–5. Independent editorial review and reader testing remain pending. Structural QA does not authorize publication.

## Source and method

All selected Hebrew verses were read from the [pinned OSHB Leviticus source](https://github.com/openscriptures/morphhb/blob/6a5db284c715c18b239422e57bb89684e6a19f00/wlc/Lev.xml) before drafting. The file's SHA-256, Git blob and 859-record count were verified. Public and Hebrew numbering align in this batch. Each source hash covers the exact original XML substring, including the written and read forms at 21:5.

Selected NET translator notes for [chapter 21](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+21&version=NET) and [chapter 22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+22&version=NET) were consulted after Hebrew reading and before drafting, as lexical and grammatical comparators. All 57 TSW verses were read after the independent draft. The assembled English was reread, and the bodily-condition list and paired sanctuary prohibitions were clarified. This is an authoring self-check, not independent scholarly review.

## Editorial decisions

The ordinary priest's close-relative exceptions and the high priest's stricter restrictions remain distinct. All family members, marriage categories, mourning gestures and the fire penalty are preserved. The uncertain “husband” clause in 21:4 and the defiled/prostitute relationship are noted without adding a wife, in-law, age or consent condition not present in the wording.

The text retains the repeated food-of-God image and holiness language. Physical conditions are described without personal labels or confident modern diagnoses. The list's uncertain facial, limb, stature and skin terms are noted. Offering and access restrictions remain explicit; so does the permission to eat both holy and most holy food. The curtain prohibition is not silently changed into permission to approach it but not pass through.

Chapter 22 preserves uncleanness, washing and sunset, and distinguishes priestly eligibility from nationality. Purchased slavery, household birth, lodging, hired work, a daughter's marriage, and all conditions for her return remain visible. Restitution keeps the one-fifth addition. Ambiguous pronouns and the scope of priestly responsibility for others' eating are recorded rather than treated as settled.

The offering rules preserve male animals where specified, the herd/flock categories, bodily conditions, the freewill-versus-vow exception, four genital injuries and the prohibition concerning such animals obtained from foreigners. The text keeps seven days with the mother, eligibility from the eighth day onward, no slaughter of parent and young on one day, and same-day thanksgiving consumption. Holiness and the exodus identity close the chapter.
