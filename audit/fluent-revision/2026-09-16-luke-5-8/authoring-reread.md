# Bilingual authoring reread: Luke 5–8

Status: completed by the drafting assistant. This is a source-based authoring reread, not human review or independent scholarly review. Editorial status remains `REVIEW_PENDING`; publication is not allowed.

## Method

I read all 194 exact pinned SBLGNT verse records for Luke 5–8 before drafting their English and verse-specific rationales. I drafted from the Greek before viewing the parent Fluent wording or TSW. After binding the draft, I compared every verse with both English comparators, checked every quotation span and paragraph, and reread every source payload beside the proposed English. The automated audit separately verifies all verse hashes, the complete Luke source SHA-256, and the Git blob at Faithlife/SBLGNT commit `c4d241a9c1c479a55b989ba35a4976c1d0b8052c`.

The focused record includes all 194 verses, not only F3 decisions. That full selection supports the names, numbers, speakers, movement, repetition, harm, ambiguity, and source-variant checks below. Similarity with TSW remains triage information only; familiar short sayings were not changed merely to reduce overlap.

## Findings retained in the draft

- Luke 5 keeps Lake Gennesaret, two boats, singular and plural fishing commands, the nets beginning to tear, both boats beginning to sink, Simon Peter's confession, James and John as sons of Zebedee and Simon's partners, and the unusual promise of “catching people alive.” The cleansing scene retains touch, willingness, priest, Mosaic offering, and the open “testimony to them.” The paralytic scene keeps the carriers' plural faith, forgiveness, inner reasoning, Son of Man authority, the mat, and public praise. Levi remains a tax collector who leaves everything. “Their scribes,” bridegroom removal, the new garment, wineskins, and preference for old wine remain distinct.
- Luke 6 retains grain plucked, rubbed, and eaten on the Sabbath; consecrated bread; the man's right hand; Jesus' save/destroy question; the opponents' senseless rage; all-night prayer; the named Twelve and Judas becoming a traitor; the level place; Tyre and Sidon; and power going out from Jesus. Luke's blessings name the poor, hungry, and weeping, and the woes name the rich, full, laughing, and publicly praised without importing qualifications from Matthew. The enemy-love section keeps cheek, outer garment, tunic, possessions, reciprocal “credit,” lending, the difficult expectation clause, mercy, judgment, condemnation, forgiveness, and the overflowing lap measure. The blind guides, speck and beam, trees, heart's treasure, doubled “Lord,” deep foundation, flood, and great collapse retain their connected movement.
- Luke 7 keeps the centurion's household worker as a slave wherever Luke uses *doulos*, his Jewish elders and friends as separate messengers, authority above and below him, and Jesus' wonder. At Nain, the dead man is the widow's only son; the narrator calls Jesus “the Lord”; compassion precedes raising; and Jesus gives the son back to his mother. John's repeated question, Jesus' actions in that hour, six reported signs, and the possible stumbling remain. The discourse keeps the reed, royal luxury, John as more than a prophet, least/great comparison, the people's acknowledgment that God is just, the leaders' rejection of God's purpose for themselves, the children's two games, accusations against John and the Son of Man, and Wisdom's children. The anointing scene preserves the unnamed woman's social labeling, tears, hair, repeated kisses, perfume, Simon's inner judgment, debts of five hundred and fifty denarii, failures of hospitality, the compact relation of forgiveness and love, faith, salvation, and peace.
- Luke 8 names Mary Magdalene, Joanna, Chuza, Susanna, and many other women and retains their material support from their own resources. The sower keeps trampling, moisture, choking, hundredfold fruit, mysteries, the difficult “so that” quotation, the devil's removal of the word, temporary belief, testing, immature fruit, and endurance. Hearing and doing define Jesus' family. The storm retains real danger and the disciples' identity question. The pinned place name is Gerasenes; the man’s prolonged nakedness, tomb dwelling, restraint, isolation, “Legion,” abyss, pigs, restored clothing and mind, and community fear are not softened. God's work and Jesus' work are placed in deliberate correspondence. Jairus's only daughter and the bleeding woman are each linked with twelve years; the woman's whole livelihood, public testimony, Jesus' “Daughter,” salvation, the girl's death, returning spirit, food, and secrecy remain.
