# Fluent: Genesis source review

17 September 2026 · continuation of the Genesis 1–50 editorial block

Genesis 1–10 has now received a fresh verse-by-verse comparison with the bound Hebrew: 267 verses. Six Scripture verses were revised in four chapter files. Fifteen additional verses were examined for the previously identified questions in chapters 31, 35, 38 and 44, including a related expression in 24:50. Those targeted checks do not count as complete reviews of the additional chapters.

The complete Genesis block remains in progress. The next sequential source review begins at chapter 11. Full-book continuous reading, resolution of remaining language questions, independent review, reader testing and final approval remain open.

## Changes made

| Passage | Previous wording | Revised wording | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1:14 | “Let them mark signs” | “Let them serve as signs and mark appointed times, days, and years.” | The lights function as signs; they do not mark signs. |
| 1:26 | “to bear our likeness” | “in our likeness” | Preserve the likeness expression without introducing an explicit purpose construction. |
| 2:5 | Two sentences leave the causal connection implicit. | “...had sprouted, because the LORD God had not sent rain...” | Make the Hebrew's explicit explanatory connection clear in English. |
| 2:10 | “Beyond it” | “From there” | Preserve the source's location reference without specifying that the division occurs beyond the garden. |
| 5:32 | “After Noah reached 500 years of age...” | “Noah was 500 years old, and he became the father...” | Remove an added timing interpretation; retain the age and coordinated fatherhood statement. |
| 7:24 | “held their power over the earth” | “continued to overwhelm the earth” | Give the waters' continuing force natural English while retaining the 150 days. |

All six revisions have exact before/after wording, Hebrew source records, source hashes and rationales in the review evidence. No headings, paragraph breaks, notes or vocabulary entries changed during this source pass.

## What was compared

The review covered every verse in chapters 1–10, including the lists and repetitions. It considered actors, actions, negation, relationships, time, quantities, source imagery and consequential alternatives. The chapter 5 ages were checked individually against the Hebrew, including Enoch's different formula and the totals for the other named ancestors. The flood accounts retain their distinct periods, dates, entry statements and animal-count expressions. The chapter 10 names and recurring family/language/territory formulas were compared without harmonizing them to other accounts.

The first reading used aligned English and an unpointed Hebrew display. Source annotations and the written/read forms in 8:17 were then inspected separately. Exact complete source records, including notes and variants, are retained in the evidence; the simplified reading display is not the source of record.

This is author/model self-review. It does not claim that every lexical or textual problem has been settled, and it does not substitute for a qualified independent Hebrew review. `source-review.json` records the complete 267-verse coverage and the observations for each passage. Some passages remain explicitly open despite having been read.

## Decisions to retain wording

**Genesis 31:24 and 29:** Retain the existing “pronouncement” wording and the note that preserves a broader rendering. A separate Hebrew noun meaning pronouncement is absent, but that alone does not invalidate an idiomatic interpretation. The NET translators also favor a restriction on blessing or cursing while acknowledging uncertainty. The current choice therefore has a documented basis; its alternative must remain visible. This is an authorial decision pending independent review. [NET translators’ notes on Genesis 31](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+31&version=NET).

**Genesis 44:22:** Retain “his father will die.” The later appeal in 44:30–31 explicitly connects the father's death to the young man's absence. The existing note identifies the supplied referent and preserves the alternative. The NET translators similarly make the father explicit; their notes also explain the conditional relation between the final verbs. [NET translators’ notes on Genesis 44](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+44&version=NET).

These decisions do not require changing a passage merely because it resembles TSW or another translation. Fluent's independence depends on source-grounded choices and its sustained English voice, not on a quota of substituted words.

## Remaining questions

The plural verb with Elohim in **35:7** needs a broader review of grammar and reference. The existing note and singular English remain provisional.

The institutional meaning of qedeshah in **38:21–22** also remains open. The consulted NET note supports a cultic interpretation, but this does not settle the contrary analysis already acknowledged in Fluent's apparatus. Specialist evidence addressing that disagreement is still needed before changing or approving “shrine prostitute.” [NET note on Genesis 38:21](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+38%3A21-22&version=NET).

Within chapters 1–10, the updated register specifically retains four questions: a contemporary rendering of the interrupted preventive clause in **3:22**; the roof/light-opening and cubit language in **6:16**; the active wiping verb followed by passive repetition in **7:23**; and the compact farmer/begin/plant construction in **9:20**. Existing notes preserve the relevant uncertainties. Reading these verses is not the same as closing their editorial questions.
