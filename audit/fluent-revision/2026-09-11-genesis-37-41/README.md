# Fluent source-based revision: Genesis 37–41

September 11, 2026. This connected batch completes 169 verses in five chapters, from Joseph’s dreams and sale through the beginning of the famine. Genesis 1–41 and James 1–5 now have draft revision coverage: 1,361 verses in 46 chapters. Genesis 42–50 is next.

## Source and method

Every verse was read from the verified [Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible Genesis XML](https://github.com/openscriptures/morphhb/blob/6a5db284c715c18b239422e57bb89684e6a19f00/wlc/Gen.xml), commit `6a5db284c715c18b239422e57bb89684e6a19f00`, blob `dcc8be362134981d3054e9b64d3a465d08492a33`. The 1,533-record source file matched both its Git blob and SHA-256. The ledger hashes each exact original XML verse substring. Public and Hebrew verse numbering align throughout Genesis 37–41.

The [verse ledger](genesis-verse-review.json) records all 169 source-specific decisions, before/after wording, TSW comparators, source bindings, and editorial F0–F3 classifications. TSW was used as a comparator, not an English base. One decision retains exact existing wording, 35 are primarily lexical, 83 address syntax or idiom, and 50 flag consequential interpretive, textual, social, or literary choices. There is no minimum-change requirement, and surface difference is not treated as proof of fidelity or quality.

## Focused editorial pass

- Genesis 37 retains the dreams’ family and cosmic images, Joseph’s age, the brothers’ hatred, Reuben’s intervention, Judah’s economic proposal, the exact cargo and price, the robe-and-goat deception, Jacob’s reference to Sheol, and Potiphar’s offices. The rare robe expression, Joseph’s “mother” after Rachel’s reported death, the Ishmaelite/Midianite shifts, and the ambiguous actors in verse 28 are disclosed rather than harmonized.
- “Please recognize” remains audible in 37:32 and 38:25. The repeated demand links the brothers’ deception of Jacob with Tamar’s presentation of Judah’s own seal, cord, and staff.
- Genesis 38 does not name Judah’s wife “Shua”; she remains Shua’s daughter. Onan’s repeated sexual use of Tamar and deliberate denial of offspring are stated without euphemism or reduction to an unrelated general rule. Judah’s failure to give Tamar to Shelah, his demand that she be burned, Tamar’s evidence, and Judah’s acknowledgment that she is more in the right all remain explicit.
- The distinct term `qedeshah` in 38:21–22 is rendered “shrine prostitute” and noted as uncertain. Perez’s breach wordplay and the reversed twin birth order remain visible.
- Genesis 39 keeps Joseph’s enslaved status present alongside his success. Potiphar’s wife’s repeated pressure, physical seizure, false accusation, ethnic othering, and use of Joseph’s enslaved status are not reframed as mutual temptation. The object of Potiphar’s burning anger remains unspecified because the Hebrew does not name it.
- The LORD’s presence with Joseph, favor, steadfast love, and the repetition of household authority in prison remain the chapter’s frame. “Pit” continues to connect the cistern and prison.
- Genesis 40 preserves each dream’s details and the exact three-day outcomes. “Lift up your head” carries both restoration and execution; the baker’s death and exposure to birds are not softened. The cupbearer’s failure is repeated: he did not remember Joseph; he forgot him.
- Genesis 41 keeps Pharaoh’s retelling distinct from the first dream report, including the cows’ unchanged appearance after eating. Joseph denies that interpretation is in him and promises an “answer of peace,” with `shalom` explained rather than collapsed into “favorable.” The two dreams’ unity, seven-year sequence, one-fifth collection, and urgency remain exact.
- Pharaoh’s potentially ambiguous “spirit of God/gods,” the people’s response to Joseph’s command, the uncertain public cry `abrekh`, and the Egyptian name Zaphenath-paneah receive concise notes. Joseph’s age, Asenath’s family, his sons’ name associations, the stored grain’s sale, and the famine’s universal language are preserved.

The authoring model reread every source verse and the assembled English. This self-check caught and corrected two extra opening quotation marks in Pharaoh’s and Joseph’s continuing speeches. Focused assertions checked names, numbers, speakers, kinship, enslavement, sexual and lethal harm, repeated phrases, dream details, and difficult readings. This is not an independent scholarly or community review.

The NET translators’ notes for [Genesis 37](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+37&version=NET), [38](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+38&version=NET), [39](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+39&version=NET), [40](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+40&version=NET), and [41](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+41&version=NET) are recorded as comparison aids for difficult readings. They are not Fluent’s source and do not govern its wording.
