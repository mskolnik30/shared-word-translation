# Fluent: Genesis editorial progress

17 September 2026 · Genesis 1–50 · authoring review, independent review pending

The first editorial pass now covers every note and vocabulary entry in Genesis. It makes the apparatus more selective, removes production language, and records the remaining questions. A new Fluent editorial standard and matching reader guidance turn the proposed process into explicit criteria. This is progress toward full compliance; it is not certification that Genesis or the complete translation meets every criterion.

Most vocabulary revisions standardize verse-label padding; one clarifies the disputed scope of the expression in Genesis 31:24. Headings, paragraphs, and Scripture wording are unchanged throughout the book. Every removal and revision is recorded with its exact prior wording in `apparatus-decisions.json`. All removed notes remain recoverable there.

The apparatus now avoids describing itself as a draft, mentioning pinned files, or telling readers that a verse has been bound to a source record. Those details belong in the editorial evidence. Necessary reader information, such as the different Hebrew verse numbering at Genesis 31–32, remains.

Routine summaries were removed where the narrative already supplies the information. Notes about significant alternatives, wordplay, unusual grammar, textual differences, or difficult idioms were retained or condensed. The review also removes interpretive or application language that exceeds a selective translation apparatus. None of that material was inserted into the Companion.

## Specific corrections and unresolved questions

Genesis 39:21 provided a concrete apparatus error: the main text already read “steadfast love,” while its note still said “Covenant love renders hesed.” The note now describes loyal kindness or steadfast love and makes clear that the word does not itself assert a formal covenant. The bound Hebrew contains hesed; no Scripture change was needed.

Genesis 35:7 has a plural verb with Elohim. Its note previously used the surrounding narrative to assert the singular identification. The revised note states the grammatical fact and identifies the singular English choice. Whether the main rendering and note adequately represent the alternative remains open for the linguistic pass.

Genesis 36:39 now gives a precise comparison: the Masoretic Text reads Hadar here, whereas the parallel in 1 Chronicles 1:50 reads Hadad. Both bound source records were inspected. The previous note's assertion that Matred was Mehetabel's mother was removed; the two daughter relationships in the Hebrew do not establish that gender assignment by themselves.

Genesis 31:24 and 31:29 remain a priority. The source uses a verb for speaking and the expression “from good to bad.” The main text's “make any pronouncement” represents a narrower interpretation. The revised note and vocabulary entry acknowledge the uncertainty, but the main-text decision still needs contextual and lexical review. Inspecting the words is not enough to declare the idiom settled.

Genesis 38:8–10 was checked against the source to shorten a polemical note while preserving the local explanation: Onan prevents conception because the offspring would count as his brother's. The Scripture retains the narrated action and judgment. A separate unresolved question concerns “shrine prostitute” in 38:21–22, since the apparatus itself acknowledges uncertainty about the institutional connection.

Other outstanding work includes the pronoun interpretation in 44:22, difficult language in the chapter 49 poem, the named alternatives in 15:6; 16:13–14; 20:13,16; 24:55,63; and 25:18,23, and a full review of recurring terms and narrative voice. `open-issues.json` assigns explicit actions to these questions.

## Editorial standard and reader guidance

`FLUENT_EDITORIAL_STANDARD.md` defines twelve criteria and the evidence needed to close each one. It preserves the shared fidelity requirements while applying Fluent's established permission to restructure sentences into natural English. The original supplied criteria remain intact. An unresolved conflict is to be recorded, not silently dismissed.

`FLUENT_READER_GUIDANCE.md` supplies a Fluent-specific preface and reading guide. It explains natural English, meaningful difficulty, differing literary voices, notes, vocabulary, names, gender, and the relationship to TSW. It is itself an editorial draft and does not claim that the manuscript has already achieved every aim it describes.

`EDITORIAL_PROGRESS.json` covers all 66 books and separates source review, continuous reading, apparatus review, terminology and house style, independent review, reader testing, Companion reconciliation, and final signoff. Genesis's apparatus scope pass is recorded as self-review complete with open issues. The Genesis block as a whole remains in progress. The other books are not credited with a fresh pass merely because earlier drafting and automated checks exist.

## Verification and limits

The current verification checks all 145 revision ledgers, source bindings, and 1,189 chapter files. It checks the apparatus decision records against the prior checkpoint and revised chapters, verifies the exact targeted source records, and proves that Scripture wording is unchanged. Changed chapter hashes and their production-review bindings are updated. The prior source records, drafting rationales, TSW comparator text, and independent-review status are preserved.

Because Scripture wording is unchanged, this pass leaves the previous qualified TSW-overlap findings unchanged. It does not create distinctiveness by substituting synonyms or imposing a difference quota. Further distinctiveness review must consider source fidelity and Fluent's sustained voice.

The mechanical result belongs in `verification.json`. Passing it establishes binding and preservation integrity, not lexical accuracy, reader comprehension, independent approval, or complete editorial compliance. Surviving notes still require linguistic checking and reader-value testing. The full fresh source pass and continuous reading are the next work within Genesis 1–50, followed by subsequent 50-chapter blocks without routine approval requests.
