# Numbers: consistency self-check

The complete Numbers draft has received a structural scan of all 36 chapters and a focused authoring comparison of 153 distinct Hebrew/Fluent verse pairs. The book contains 1,288 public verses bound to all 1,289 original Hebrew records. Cumulative draft coverage remains 5,001 verses in 158 chapters; this review adds no new draft verses.

Two wording corrections improve accuracy and reading:

- Numbers 19:18: “all its containers” becomes “all the articles in it.” The Hebrew covers articles or implements broadly, rather than only containers.
- Numbers 35:30: the sentence now begins “A person who commits murder is to be put to death on the testimony of witnesses.” This makes the testimony phrase attach clearly to the death sentence. The following prohibition on a single witness remains intact.

Both affected draft ledgers and chapter records are rebound to the updated files. Their original base commits, before-texts, source bindings and TSW comparators remain intact. The immediate before/after changes and their individual F1/F2 rationales are in `review.json`; exact previous records remain in Git history. Previous batch verification reports describe historical snapshots; `verification.json` here verifies the current text.

The thematic comparisons cover census and service ages, shared leadership, spirit and divine-speech language, impurity and bloodguilt, foreigners and family obligations, inheritance and tribal settlement, and recurring images and sacrificial terms. They preserve meaningful differences, including the Levite entry ages, distinct census totals, the daughters’ differing name order, and the alternative Canaan allotment in Numbers 32:30. No attempt was made to harmonize such differences away.

The selected comparisons are listed in `focused-comparisons.json` with source references and exact original-verse hashes. `verification.json` records all forty cumulative ledger audits, complete Numbers structural coverage, all public/source numbering differences, focused assertions, the translation-family audit and `git diff --check`.
