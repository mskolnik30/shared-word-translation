# Fluent Companion visual scholarly red-team recovery

Date: 2026-09-07  
Scope: shared Biblical World visual program for the Fluent Companion  
Publication status: **blocked**

## Purpose

This record restores the scholarly controls identified during the visual
red-team review after the earlier large local image bundle became unavailable.
It does not approve any image or claim that the visual corpus is complete.

## Findings retained as binding controls

- Generated portraits are illustrations, not facial reconstructions.
- Ancient Judean, Israelite, Mesopotamian, Egyptian, Persian, Greek, Roman,
  North African, and Levantine people must not be defaulted to modern northern
  European appearance.
- Skin tone, hair, clothing, age, status markers, architecture, furnishings,
  weapons, tools, vegetation, and landscape must be evaluated independently;
  plausible ethnicity alone does not make an image historically reliable.
- Jesus must not be idealized as a beautiful European or ancient superhero.
  His exact face remains unknown. The Shroud of Turin is excluded as facial
  evidence because the published radiocarbon result dates its linen to the
  medieval period.
- A portrait of a named or traditional author must not imply certainty where
  authorship is anonymous, composite, pseudonymous, debated, or layered.
- Isaiah requires an authorship-aware presentation. A single invented face may
  not be used to settle the book's compositional history.
- Anonymous or composite books should normally use a nonportrait editorial
  treatment rather than one invented author headshot.
- Beauty, royal insignia, priestly garments, military rank, wealth, and court
  dress may be shown only when the biblical text or external evidence supports
  the specific claim.
- Maps must distinguish established, probable, possible, disputed, traditional,
  representative, and unknown locations or routes.
- Diagram wording must identify whether a claim belongs to the biblical text,
  a letter's narrated voice, a later interpretive tradition, or modern
  scholarship.
- Archaeological and place photographs must disclose their modern date and may
  not imply that surviving structures look exactly as they did in the biblical
  period.
- Interpretive art, symbolic art, reconstructions, and schematics may not be
  presented as documentary scenes.
- Rights verification, specialist review, accessibility review, visual review,
  and exact-release approval remain separate gates.

## Specific corrections required by the red-team review

1. Job: distinguish the heavenly prologue from a wager and state accurately
   that God rebukes the friends for not speaking rightly about God as Job did.
2. Jude: distinguish the Moses tradition later associated with the *Assumption
   of Moses* from the wording explicitly quoted from 1 Enoch.
3. Ephesians: state that 5:21 immediately precedes the household instructions;
   its grammatical relation and interpretive scope are debated.
4. 2 Thessalonians: attribute claims to "the letter's account," not
   automatically to the historical Paul.
5. 2 Timothy: attribute claims to "the letter," not automatically to the
   historical Paul.
6. Remove or quarantine pseudo-documentary scenes involving an invented low
   dining table in Martha's home, a Corinthian assembly, and a Bethlehem
   threshing floor unless a specialist-supported reconstruction brief exists.
