## The altar and the Sea

<p>
v01: He made a bronze altar twenty cubits long, twenty cubits wide, and ten cubits high.
v02: He made the Sea of cast metal, round in shape, ten cubits from rim to rim and five cubits high. A measuring line thirty cubits long encircled it.
v03: Figures of oxen encircled it underneath, ten to a cubit, all around the Sea. The oxen were in two rows, cast in one piece with it.
v04: It stood on twelve oxen: three facing north, three west, three south, and three east. The Sea rested above them, and all their hindquarters faced inward.
v05: It was a handbreadth thick. Its rim was shaped like the rim of a cup, like a lily blossom. It could hold three thousand baths.
</p>

## Basins, lamps, and courts

<p>
v06: He made ten basins, placing five on the right and five on the left, for washing. They rinsed the materials for the burnt offering in them, while the Sea was for the priests to wash in.
v07: He made ten gold lampstands according to their prescribed design and put them in the main hall, five on the right and five on the left.
v08: He made ten tables and put them in the main hall, five on the right and five on the left. He also made a hundred gold sprinkling bowls.
v09: He made the priests’ courtyard, the great courtyard, and doors for the courtyard, and overlaid the doors with bronze.
v10: He placed the Sea on the right side, toward the southeast.
</p>

## The finished furnishings

<p>
v11: Huram made the pots, shovels, and sprinkling bowls. So Huram finished the work he did for King Solomon in God’s house:
v12: the two pillars, the two bowl-shaped capitals on top of the pillars, and the two networks covering the two bowl-shaped capitals;
v13: the four hundred pomegranates for the two networks, two rows of pomegranates for each network covering the two bowl-shaped capitals on top of the pillars;
v14: the stands and the basins he made on the stands;
v15: the one Sea and the twelve oxen beneath it;
v16: and the pots, shovels, forks, and all their utensils. Huram-abi made these of polished bronze for King Solomon for the LORD’s house.
</p>

<p>
v17: The king had them cast in the clay ground of the Jordan plain, between Succoth and Zeredah.
v18: Solomon made all these utensils in such great quantities that the weight of the bronze was never determined.
</p>

<p>
v19: Solomon also made all the furnishings for God’s house: the gold altar, the tables on which the bread of the Presence was placed,
v20: the lampstands and their lamps of pure gold, to burn as prescribed before the inner sanctuary,
v21: the flowers, lamps, and tongs of gold—the purest gold—
v22: and the wick trimmers, sprinkling bowls, dishes, and firepans of pure gold. The entrances of the house—the inner doors to the Most Holy Place and the doors to the main hall—were gold.
</p>

## Notes

v01: The Hebrew altar is ten cubits high. The TSW comparator has twenty; the source-based draft retains ten.

v03: Chronicles describes ox figures here; 1 Kings 7:24 describes gourds.

v05: Three thousand baths is Chronicles’ stated capacity; 1 Kings 7:26 gives two thousand.

v16: The Hebrew form is Huram-abiv, “Huram his father,” an honorific related to Huram-abi in 2:13. The draft uses Huram-abi consistently for the craftsman.

## Vocabulary

v02: Sea
The large cast-bronze basin used for priestly washing.

v19: Bread of the Presence
Bread placed before God in the sanctuary; related passages call it arranged bread.
