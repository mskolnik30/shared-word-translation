## Jesus Before Pilate and Herod

<p>
v01: Then their whole assembly rose and led Jesus to Pilate.
v02: They began accusing him: “We found this man misleading our nation, forbidding us to pay taxes to Caesar, and saying that he himself is Messiah, a king.”
v03: Pilate asked him, “Are you the king of the Jews?” Jesus answered, “You say so.”
v04: Then Pilate told the chief priests and the crowds, “I find no basis for a charge against this man.”
v05: But they kept insisting, “He stirs up the people by teaching throughout all Judea, beginning from Galilee even to this place.”
v06: When Pilate heard this, he asked whether the man was a Galilean.
v07: Learning that Jesus was under Herod’s jurisdiction, he sent him to Herod, who was also in Jerusalem during those days.
v08: Herod was very glad to see Jesus, because for a long time he had wanted to see him after hearing about him. He hoped to see Jesus perform some sign.
v09: He questioned Jesus at considerable length, but Jesus gave him no answer.
v10: The chief priests and scribes stood there, vehemently accusing him.
v11: Then Herod and his soldiers treated Jesus with contempt and mocked him. Dressing him in splendid clothing, Herod sent him back to Pilate.
v12: That very day Herod and Pilate became friends with each other; before this they had been enemies.
</p>

## Pilate Hands Jesus Over

<p>
v13: Pilate called together the chief priests, the rulers, and the people
v14: and said to them, “You brought me this man as one who was turning the people away. Look, after examining him in your presence, I found no basis in this man for the charges you bring against him.
v15: Neither did Herod, because he sent him back to us. Clearly, he has done nothing deserving death.
v16: Therefore, I will have him flogged and release him.”
v18: But they all shouted together, “Away with this man! Release Barabbas to us!”
v19: Barabbas had been thrown into prison for an uprising in the city and for murder.
v20: Wanting to release Jesus, Pilate addressed them again.
v21: But they kept shouting, “Crucify him! Crucify him!”
v22: A third time he said to them, “Why? What evil has this man done? I have found in him nothing deserving death. Therefore, I will have him flogged and release him.”
v23: But they pressed him with loud shouts, demanding that Jesus be crucified, and their voices prevailed.
v24: So Pilate decided that their demand should be carried out.
v25: He released the man they asked for, the one imprisoned for uprising and murder, but handed Jesus over to their will.
</p>

## The Way to the Cross

<p>
v26: As they led Jesus away, they seized Simon of Cyrene, who was coming in from the country, and laid the cross on him to carry behind Jesus.
v27: A great multitude of people followed him, including women who were beating their breasts and lamenting for him.
v28: Jesus turned to them and said, “Daughters of Jerusalem, do not weep for me; instead, weep for yourselves and for your children.
v29: For look, days are coming when people will say, ‘Blessed are the women who are barren, the wombs that never gave birth, and the breasts that never nursed!’
v30: Then they will begin to say to the mountains, ‘Fall on us!’ and to the hills, ‘Cover us!’
v31: For if they do these things when the wood is green, what will happen when it is dry?”
v32: Two others, both criminals, were also led away with him to be executed.
</p>

## Jesus Is Crucified

<p>
v33: When they came to the place called the Skull, they crucified Jesus there with the criminals, one on his right and one on his left.
v34: [Jesus said, “Father, forgive them, because they do not know what they are doing.”] Then they cast lots to divide his clothes.
v35: The people stood watching, and the rulers also ridiculed him, saying, “He saved others; let him save himself if he is God’s Messiah, the Chosen One!”
v36: The soldiers also mocked him, coming up and offering him sour wine
v37: and saying, “If you are the king of the Jews, save yourself!”
v38: There was also an inscription above him: “This is the King of the Jews.”
v39: One of the criminals hanging there blasphemed him: “Are you not the Messiah? Save yourself and us!”
v40: But the other rebuked him: “Do you not even fear God, since you are under the same sentence?
v41: We are punished justly, because we are receiving what our deeds deserve. But this man has done nothing wrong.”
v42: Then he said, “Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom.”
v43: Jesus told him, “Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in paradise.”
</p>

## Jesus Dies

<p>
v44: It was now about noon, and darkness came over the whole land until three in the afternoon,
v45: because the sun’s light failed. The curtain of the sanctuary was torn down the middle.
v46: Then Jesus cried out with a loud voice, “Father, into your hands I entrust my spirit.” After saying this, he breathed his last.
v47: When the centurion saw what happened, he glorified God and said, “This man truly was righteous.”
v48: When all the crowds who had gathered for this spectacle saw what happened, they returned home beating their breasts.
v49: But all who knew Jesus, including the women who had followed him from Galilee, stood at a distance watching these things.
</p>

## Jesus Is Buried

<p>
v50: Now there was a man named Joseph, a council member who was good and righteous.
v51: He had not agreed with the council’s decision and action. He was from the Jewish town of Arimathea and was waiting for the kingdom of God.
v52: Joseph went to Pilate and asked for Jesus’ body.
v53: He took it down, wrapped it in linen, and placed Jesus in a rock-hewn tomb where no one had ever been laid.
v54: It was the Preparation Day, and the Sabbath was about to begin.
v55: The women who had come with Jesus from Galilee followed Joseph and saw the tomb and how his body was laid there.
v56: Then they returned and prepared spices and perfumed oils. On the Sabbath they rested according to the commandment.
</p>

## Notes

v17: Public verse 17 is absent from the pinned Greek text, so the numbering moves from 16 to 18.

v28–31: Jesus addresses women who are already mourning him and names the future vulnerability of them and their children. Wombs, breasts, mountains, hills, and green and dry wood remain concrete.

v34: Jesus’ prayer for forgiveness is bracketed in the pinned source because of significant textual variation.

v43: Ancient manuscripts had no comma after ‘today.’ The natural Greek construction supports the main-text punctuation, ‘Today you will be with me in paradise,’ while the wording itself remains the evidence.

v47: The centurion calls Jesus ‘righteous’ or ‘innocent.’ ‘Righteous’ preserves the term’s wider moral and theological range while fitting Pilate’s repeated innocence declarations.

## Vocabulary

v26: **σταυρός (stauros)**: Cross, a Roman instrument of execution and public shame.

v35: **ἐκλεκτός (eklektos)**: Chosen or selected one.

v43: **παράδεισος (paradeisos)**: Paradise, a garden-like place of blessed life with God.

v51: **βασιλεία τοῦ θεοῦ (basileia tou theou)**: The kingdom or reign of God.
