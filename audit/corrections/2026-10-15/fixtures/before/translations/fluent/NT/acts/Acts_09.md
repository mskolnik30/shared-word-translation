## Saul Encounters Jesus

<p>
v01: Meanwhile Saul was still breathing threats and murder against the Lord’s disciples. He went to the high priest
v02: and asked him for letters to the synagogues in Damascus, so that if he found any who belonged to the Way, men or women, he could bring them bound to Jerusalem.
v03: As he traveled and approached Damascus, suddenly a light from heaven flashed around him.
v04: He fell to the ground and heard a voice say to him, “Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?”
v05: He asked, “Who are you, Lord?” The voice replied, “I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.
v06: But get up and go into the city, and you will be told what you must do.”
v07: The men traveling with Saul stood speechless. They heard the voice but saw no one.
v08: Saul got up from the ground, but when he opened his eyes he could see nothing. So they led him by the hand into Damascus.
v09: For three days he could not see, and he neither ate nor drank.
</p>

## Ananias Is Sent to Saul

<p>
v10: In Damascus there was a disciple named Ananias. The Lord said to him in a vision, “Ananias!” He replied, “Here I am, Lord.”
v11: The Lord told him, “Get up and go to the street called Straight. At the house of Judas, ask for a man from Tarsus named Saul, for he is praying.
v12: In a vision he has seen a man named Ananias come in and lay hands on him so that he may see again.”
v13: But Ananias answered, “Lord, I have heard from many people about this man and how much harm he has done to your holy people in Jerusalem.
v14: And here he has authority from the chief priests to bind everyone who calls on your name.”
v15: But the Lord told him, “Go, because this man is my chosen instrument to carry my name before Gentiles, kings, and the people of Israel.
v16: I will show him how much he must suffer for my name.”
v17: So Ananias went and entered the house. He laid his hands on Saul and said, “Brother Saul, the Lord Jesus, who appeared to you on the road as you came here, has sent me so that you may see again and be filled with the Holy Spirit.”
v18: Immediately something like scales fell from Saul’s eyes, and he could see again. He got up and was baptized,
v19: and after taking food he regained his strength. Saul stayed with the disciples in Damascus for several days.
</p>

## Saul Proclaims Jesus

<p>
For several days Saul stayed with the disciples in Damascus.
v20: He immediately began proclaiming Jesus in the synagogues: “He is the Son of God.”
v21: Everyone who heard him was astonished and asked, “Isn’t this the man who devastated those in Jerusalem who call on this name? Didn’t he come here to take them bound to the chief priests?”
v22: But Saul grew more and more powerful and confounded the Jewish people living in Damascus by proving that Jesus is the Messiah.
v23: After many days had passed, some Jewish leaders plotted to kill him,
v24: but Saul learned of their plot. They watched the city gates day and night in order to kill him.
v25: But his disciples took him by night and lowered him through an opening in the wall in a basket.
</p>

## Barnabas Brings Saul to the Apostles

<p>
v26: When Saul came to Jerusalem, he tried to join the disciples, but they were all afraid of him because they did not believe he was a disciple.
v27: But Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles. He told them how Saul had seen the Lord on the road and that the Lord had spoken to him, and how Saul had spoken boldly in Jesus’ name at Damascus.
v28: So Saul moved freely with them in Jerusalem, speaking boldly in the name of the Lord.
v29: He spoke and debated with the Greek-speaking Jews, but they tried to kill him.
v30: When the brothers and sisters learned of this, they took him down to Caesarea and sent him off to Tarsus.
v31: Then the church throughout Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and was strengthened. Living in the fear of the Lord and the encouragement of the Holy Spirit, it continued to grow.
</p>

## Aeneas Is Healed

<p>
v32: As Peter traveled throughout the region, he also went down to the holy people living in Lydda.
v33: There he found a man named Aeneas who had been confined to bed for eight years because he was paralyzed.
v34: Peter said to him, “Aeneas, Jesus Christ heals you. Get up and make your bed.” He got up immediately.
v35: Everyone living in Lydda and the Plain of Sharon saw him, and they turned to the Lord.
</p>

## Tabitha Is Restored to Life

<p>
v36: In Joppa there was a disciple named Tabitha, which in Greek is Dorcas. She was always doing good works and helping the poor.
v37: At that time she became sick and died. After washing her body, they placed it in an upstairs room.
v38: Because Lydda was near Joppa, the disciples heard that Peter was there and sent two men to urge him, “Please come to us without delay.”
v39: Peter got up and went with them. When he arrived, they took him to the upstairs room. All the widows stood beside him, weeping and showing him the tunics and other clothing Dorcas had made while she was with them.
v40: Peter sent everyone out, knelt down, and prayed. Turning toward the body, he said, “Tabitha, get up.” She opened her eyes, saw Peter, and sat up.
v41: Peter gave her his hand and helped her stand. Then he called in the holy people and the widows and presented her alive.
v42: This became known throughout Joppa, and many believed in the Lord.
v43: Peter stayed in Joppa for many days with a tanner named Simon.
</p>

## Notes

v02: ‘The Way’ is an early name for the Jesus-following movement. Both men and women are explicit targets of Saul’s arrests.

v07: The companions hear the voice but see no one. Later retellings emphasize different aspects of what they perceived; this verse is translated on its own terms.

v15–16: Saul is chosen to carry Jesus’ name to Gentiles, kings, and Israel, and he is also told that this mission will involve suffering.

v36: Tabitha is Aramaic and Dorcas Greek; both names mean gazelle. She is explicitly called a female disciple.

v39: The widows’ tunics and garments make Dorcas’s care materially visible rather than merely describing her as kind.

## Vocabulary

v02: **ὁδός (hodos)**: Way, road, or manner of life; here a name for the early movement.

v15: **σκεῦος ἐκλογῆς (skeuos eklogēs)**: A chosen instrument or vessel.

v27: **παρρησιάζομαι (parrēsiazomai)**: Speak openly, freely, or boldly.
