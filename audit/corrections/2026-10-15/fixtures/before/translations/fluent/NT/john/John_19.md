## Jesus Is Mocked as King

<p>
v01: Then Pilate took Jesus and had him flogged.
v02: The soldiers wove a crown from thorns, put it on his head, and dressed him in a purple robe.
v03: They kept coming up to him and saying, “Hail, king of the Jews!” And they slapped him.
v04: Pilate went outside again and told them, “Look, I am bringing him out to you so that you may know I find no basis for a charge against him.”
v05: Jesus came out wearing the crown of thorns and the purple robe. Pilate told them, “Here is the man!”
v06: When the chief priests and officers saw him, they shouted, “Crucify him! Crucify him!” Pilate told them, “Take him and crucify him yourselves, because I find no basis for a charge against him.”
v07: The Judean leaders answered him, “We have a law, and according to that law he ought to die because he made himself the Son of God.”
v08: When Pilate heard this statement, he became even more afraid.
v09: He went back into the headquarters and asked Jesus, “Where are you from?” But Jesus gave him no answer.
v10: So Pilate asked him, “You will not speak to me? Don’t you know that I have authority to release you and authority to crucify you?”
v11: Jesus answered him, “You would have no authority over me at all unless it had been given to you from above. Therefore, the one who handed me over to you has the greater sin.”
v12: From then on Pilate kept trying to release him, but the Judean leaders shouted, “If you release this man, you are no friend of Caesar. Everyone who makes himself a king opposes Caesar.”
v13: When Pilate heard these words, he brought Jesus outside and sat down on the judgment seat at a place called the Stone Pavement, or in Hebrew, Gabbatha.
v14: It was the day of preparation for Passover, about noon. Pilate told the Judean leaders, “Here is your king!”
v15: They shouted, “Away with him! Away with him! Crucify him!” Pilate asked them, “Shall I crucify your king?” The chief priests answered, “We have no king but Caesar.”
v16: Then Pilate handed Jesus over to them to be crucified. So they took Jesus.
</p>

## Jesus Is Crucified

<p>
They took Jesus,
v17: Carrying the cross himself, he went out to the place called Place of the Skull, which in Hebrew is called Golgotha.
v18: There they crucified him, along with two others, one on each side and Jesus in the middle.
v19: Pilate also wrote a notice and placed it on the cross. It read: Jesus the Nazarene, the King of the Jews.
v20: Many Jewish people read this notice because the place where Jesus was crucified was near the city. It was written in Hebrew, Latin, and Greek.
v21: So the Jewish chief priests told Pilate, “Do not write, ‘The King of the Jews,’ but, ‘This man said, I am king of the Jews.’”
v22: Pilate answered, “What I have written, I have written.”
</p>

## The Soldiers Divide Jesus’ Clothing

<p>
v23: When the soldiers crucified Jesus, they took his garments and divided them into four parts, one for each soldier. They also took his tunic, which was seamless, woven in one piece from top to bottom.
v24: So they said to one another, “Let’s not tear it, but cast lots to see whose it will be.” This fulfilled the Scripture: “They divided my garments among themselves, and for my clothing they cast lots.” So the soldiers did these things.
</p>

## Jesus’ Mother and the Beloved Disciple

<p>
v25: Standing near Jesus’ cross were his mother, his mother’s sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene.
v26: When Jesus saw his mother and the disciple he loved standing nearby, he told his mother, “Woman, here is your son.”
v27: Then he told the disciple, “Here is your mother.” From that hour the disciple took her into his own home.
</p>

## Jesus Dies

<p>
v28: After this, knowing that everything was now completed, Jesus said, “I am thirsty,” so that Scripture would be fulfilled.
v29: A jar full of sour wine was there. So they put a sponge full of sour wine on a hyssop branch and held it to his mouth.
v30: After receiving the sour wine, Jesus said, “It is completed.” Then he bowed his head and gave up his spirit.
</p>

## Jesus’ Side Is Pierced

<p>
v31: Because it was the day of preparation, the Judean leaders did not want the bodies to remain on the crosses during the Sabbath—especially since that Sabbath was a great day. So they asked Pilate to have the men’s legs broken and the bodies removed.
v32: The soldiers came and broke the legs of the first man and then of the other man who had been crucified with Jesus.
v33: But when they came to Jesus and saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs.
v34: Instead, one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and immediately blood and water came out.
v35: The one who saw this has testified, and his testimony is true. He knows that he tells the truth, so that you also may believe.
v36: For these things happened so that the Scripture would be fulfilled: “Not one of his bones will be broken.”
v37: And another Scripture says, “They will look on the one they pierced.”
</p>

## Jesus Is Buried

<p>
v38: After these things, Joseph from Arimathea, who was secretly a disciple of Jesus because he feared the Judean leaders, asked Pilate for permission to take away Jesus’ body. Pilate gave permission, so Joseph came and took his body.
v39: Nicodemus, who had first come to Jesus at night, also came, bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes weighing about a hundred Roman pounds.
v40: They took Jesus’ body and wrapped it in linen cloths with the spices, according to the Jewish burial custom.
v41: At the place where Jesus was crucified there was a garden, and in the garden was a new tomb in which no one had yet been laid.
v42: Because it was the Jewish day of preparation and the tomb was nearby, they laid Jesus there.
</p>

## Notes

v01–03: The flogging, thorn crown, robe, mockery, and blows are concrete state and military violence. The translation does not soften the harm.

v11: ‘From above’ can point to God as the source of Pilate’s derivative authority and also echoes John’s recurring above-below language.

v14: The sixth hour is approximately noon. John’s Passover chronology is preserved rather than harmonized with another Gospel.

v20: ‘Hebrew’ here refers to the local Jewish language, commonly understood as Aramaic in this setting. The other languages are Latin and Greek.

v25: The grammar may name four women or allow Jesus’ mother’s sister to be Mary of Clopas. The main text does not force a count.

v30: The verb means ‘it has been completed’ or ‘accomplished,’ not merely that suffering has stopped.

v31–34: The soldiers break the legs of the two crucified men and pierce Jesus’ side. These acts of bodily violence remain explicit.

v39: A Roman pound was smaller than a modern pound, but the total remains an exceptionally large quantity. The ancient measure is retained with an approximate modern description.

## Vocabulary

v13: **βῆμα (bēma)**: A raised judgment seat or tribunal.

v17: **Γολγοθᾶ (Golgotha)**: The Hebrew or Aramaic name for the Place of the Skull.

v30: **τετέλεσται (tetelestai)**: It has been completed or accomplished.
