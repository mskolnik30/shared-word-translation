# 2 Corinthians 11

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Paul's defense becomes deliberately uncomfortable. He speaks of jealousy, deception, financial support, and the community's tolerance of domination. Then he adopts the very language of boasting that he has questioned. Follow what he chooses to boast about before assuming this is a straightforward list of achievements.

### Chapter Path

- **11:1–6:** Paul fears that the community is being led away from its devotion to Christ.
- **11:7–12:** He explains his refusal to burden the Corinthians financially.
- **11:13–15:** He accuses rival apostles of disguise and deception.
- **11:16–21:** His ironic appeal exposes what the community has tolerated.
- **11:22–33:** His boast names suffering, concern for others, weakness, and escape.

### Watch For

Attend to the difference between Paul's accusations, his ironic speech, and his descriptions of what happened. Especially read the words about being “too weak” alongside the acts of domination listed immediately before them (11:20–21).

## Read the Chapter

Read the entire chapter in Fluent. Let the final escape in a basket remain part of the argument rather than stopping at the more impressive experiences.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What picture of leadership emerges when Paul's account of support, danger, and weakness is read together?

### The Chapter in Brief

Paul fears deception and challenges the standing of the “super-apostles.” He explains that other churches supplied his needs while he refused to burden the Corinthians. He condemns deception and marvels that the community tolerates people who enslave, exploit, exalt themselves, or strike them. His ensuing boast recounts labor, imprisonment, beatings, shipwrecks, danger, deprivation, and concern for the churches. He concludes by saying that he will boast in weakness and recalls being lowered through a window in a basket to escape Damascus (11:1–33).

### Follow the Movement

The financial argument is specific. Paul describes receiving support from elsewhere, not a universal rule that faithful ministry can never receive support. His language about “robbing” other churches is deliberately provocative in this explanation (11:7–12).

The irony in verses 19–21 is likewise pointed. Being too “weak” to act like the domineering figures is not an endorsement of their behavior. The list in verse 20 exposes the community's willingness to endure mistreatment from people claiming authority.

Paul's catalog includes bodily harm and fear, not only perseverance. The daily concern for the churches places other people inside his account of suffering. Ending with escape rather than triumph unsettles a simple equation of authority with invulnerability (11:23–33).

### Threads Through Scripture

**2 Corinthians 10:17–18** questions self-commendation and locates boasting in the Lord. **2 Corinthians 12:7–10** continues the account of weakness and grace. Reading those passages with chapter 11 keeps its rhetoric from becoming a competition over who has suffered most.

### Questions for Conversation

What conduct have the Corinthians tolerated? How does the basket story change the tone of the boast? When can a leader's account of sacrifice become a demand that others accept mistreatment?

### Prayer and Practice

“God of grace, free us from confusing dominance with faithfulness.” Consider one way a leader's work can be evaluated without rewarding self-exaltation or requiring unlimited personal sacrifice. This chapter should not be used to send someone back into abuse or to make endurance of danger the price of belonging.

## Go Deeper

### Reading Carefully

The correction at 11:21 removes an unnumbered duplicate of the transition into boasting. The transition remains in the verse once, together with the ironic reference to weakness. Earlier Companion paraphrases of translation notes have been replaced here with observations tied directly to the corrected passage. Paul's use of jealousy and accusation is not a general authorization for leaders to control people or declare every critic deceptive.
