# Revelation 20

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

John sees restraint, reign, renewed deception, and judgment. The chapter's repeated thousand years and its accounts of resurrection have prompted competing readings, but the first task here is to follow what the vision actually names. A supplied chronological system should not replace the words on the page.

### Chapter Path

- **20:1–6:** Satan is bound, witnesses reign with Christ, and a blessing names the first resurrection.
- **20:7–10:** Satan is released, deceives the nations, and is finally cast into the lake of fire.
- **20:11–15:** The dead stand before the throne; judgment is narrated; Death and Hades are cast into the lake of fire.

### Watch For

Notice the distinct places and figures: abyss, thrones, beloved city, sea, Death, Hades, lake of fire, books, and book of life. Do not collapse them into a single word such as “hell.”

## Read the Chapter

Read Revelation 20 in Fluent before comparing a system of interpretation. Distinguish the sequence of scenes John reports from conclusions about how every scene relates to the rest of Revelation.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

Who is restrained, who receives authority, who is deceived, and who is judged?

### The Chapter in Brief

An angel binds Satan for a thousand years to prevent his deception of the nations. John sees thrones and witnesses who come to life and reign with Christ. A blessing identifies participation in the first resurrection and freedom from the authority of the second death. After release, Satan again deceives the nations and gathers them against the holy ones. The attack ends with fire from heaven and Satan's final judgment. John then sees the dead, great and small, before the throne. Books and the book of life are opened. The dead are judged according to their works, and Death and Hades are thrown into the lake of fire (20:1–15).

### Follow the Movement

The witnesses' refusal of the beast is named alongside the violence done to them. Their vindication should not become a demand that present readers seek suffering or prove devotion through danger (20:4–6).

The release and renewed deception resist a simple account of steadily improving conditions. At the same time, the vision does not leave the deceiver's power unbounded. The chapter narrates an end to it rather than giving readers a command to identify and attack contemporary enemies (20:7–10).

The judgment scene includes both great and small. The sea, Death, and Hades give up their dead; none is presented as a refuge from the scene. The distinction between the books and the book of life remains visible without being converted here into a complete account of salvation (20:11–15).

### Threads Through Scripture

**Revelation 19:19–21** describes the preceding defeat of the beast and false prophet. **Revelation 21:1–4** follows the judgment with a new heaven and earth, God's dwelling with people, and the end of death and mourning. Read the movement across these boundaries rather than ending the book with this chapter's severest image.

### Questions for Conversation

What distinction among the images had you previously overlooked? What does the inclusion of great and small contribute? How can hope for the defeat of evil avoid becoming certainty that one's own group stands beyond judgment?

### Prayer and Practice

“God of life, hold those who have suffered and teach us to resist deception without dehumanizing others.” You may identify one claim that needs careful verification before repeating it. This practice does not require assigning a living person or nation a role in the vision.

### Reading Carefully

The corrected note at 20:4 leaves open whether the syntax identifies two distinct groups. It retains “came to life” and the thousand years without adding a chronological system. Gog and Magog remain the vision's names rather than labels replaced by modern countries. At 20:14, Death and Hades are distinct from the lake of fire into which they are thrown.
