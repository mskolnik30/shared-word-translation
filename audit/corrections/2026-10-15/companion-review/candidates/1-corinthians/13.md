# 1 Corinthians 13

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Paul has described many gifts within one body. Before explaining how those gifts should function in a gathering, he asks what becomes of extraordinary ability without love. Read this chapter between 12:27–31 and 14:1–5. Its concern includes the shared life of a gifted but divided community, not only the affection of a couple.

### Chapter Path

- **13:1–3:** Speech, knowledge, faith, generosity, and bodily surrender cannot substitute for love.
- **13:4–7:** Love takes recognizable form in conduct toward others.
- **13:8–13:** Partial knowledge and temporary gifts are contrasted with what endures.

### Watch For

Notice the repeated conditions in verses 1–3 and the repeated “all things” in verse 7. Then notice the change from “I” to “we” in the description of partial knowing. The limitations Paul names include himself.

## Read the Chapter

Read the complete chapter in Fluent, including the note at 13:3. Do not let an especially familiar line replace the movement of the argument.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

How does Paul move from impressive abilities to ordinary conduct and then to the limits of knowing?

### The Chapter in Brief

Even extraordinary speech, understanding, faith, generosity, and bodily surrender are nothing without love. Love is patient and kind rather than envious, boastful, or self-seeking. It rejoices with truth, not injustice. Prophecy, tongues, and knowledge will come to an end, while love does not fail. Paul compares partial knowing with childhood and with seeing dimly in a mirror. He closes with faith, hope, and love, naming love as the greatest (13:1–13).

### Follow the Movement

The opening does not dismiss gifts; it refuses to let them become substitutes for love. Chapter 14 will still urge the pursuit of spiritual gifts. The question is what those abilities do within a community and how their exercise treats other people (13:1–3; 14:1–5).

The account of love is not permission to overlook injustice. Verse 6 belongs beside the bearing, believing, hoping, and enduring of verse 7. A community should not turn this description into a demand that a harmed person conceal what happened, remain exposed to danger, or stop seeking accountability.

The closing comparison also limits claims to mastery. “We know in part” is a confession within the argument, not merely a criticism of someone else's understanding. The chapter does not identify “completeness” more precisely than its own contrast with the partial (13:9–12).

### Threads Through Scripture

**1 Corinthians 12:27–31** places diverse gifts within the body. **1 Corinthians 14:1–5** resumes the discussion with love and the strengthening of others together. These immediate connections explain why chapter 13 belongs within the letter's argument.

### Questions for Conversation

What ability is most easily mistaken for maturity in your community? Which descriptions of love concern how power is used? How could acknowledging partial knowledge change a disagreement without requiring anyone to pretend that truth does not matter?

### Prayer and Practice

“God of love, help us receive our gifts without making them measures of another person's worth.” Choose one act of patient attention or truthful kindness. Where harm is involved, kindness may include a clear boundary and an accountable next step rather than renewed exposure.

## Go Deeper

### Reading Carefully

The corrected translation note at 13:3 distinguishes two readings. The bound SBLGNT text prints the burning reading; Fluent provisionally retains the alternative boasting reading. This Companion does not claim that boasting is the reading printed in that source or rank the manuscripts without a documented argument. The textual decision remains open. In either reading, the sentence ends with the same challenge: the act described brings no benefit without love.
