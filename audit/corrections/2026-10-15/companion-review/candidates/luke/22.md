# Luke 22

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

The approach to Jesus' death includes a meal, arguments about greatness, a warning to Simon, anguished prayer, arrest, denial, and interrogation. Betrayal and failure are not located only outside the circle of disciples. Attend also to what Jesus gives, promises, and does within that circle.

### Chapter Path

- **22:1–13:** Judas negotiates betrayal while Peter and John prepare the Passover.
- **22:14–30:** Jesus shares the meal, speaks of betrayal, and redefines greatness through service.
- **22:31–38:** Simon receives warning and promise; the disciples hear difficult words about provision and swords.
- **22:39–53:** Jesus prays, is betrayed, and heals the injured servant during his arrest.
- **22:54–71:** Peter denies Jesus; Jesus is mocked and questioned.

### Watch For

Notice how confidence, fear, grief, and violence appear among those around Jesus. Keep his prayer for Simon beside the later denials, and his words about swords beside his healing of the injured man.

## Read the Chapter

Read Luke 22 in Fluent. In the supper scene, distinguish the narrator's report of taking the cup from the words Jesus speaks.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What happens between the disciples' meal with Jesus and the morning questioning? How does Jesus respond to the failures and dangers within that movement?

### The Chapter in Brief

Judas arranges to hand Jesus over away from the crowd. Peter and John prepare the Passover. At the meal, Jesus speaks of his suffering, gives bread and cup, and names betrayal. An argument about greatness prompts his statement that he is among them as one who serves. He warns Simon but also prays for him and gives him a responsibility after turning back. On the Mount of Olives Jesus prays while the disciples sleep from sorrow. During the arrest a disciple injures a servant, and Jesus heals him. Peter denies knowing Jesus, remembers the warning, and weeps. Jesus is beaten, mocked, and brought before the council (22:1–71).

### Follow the Movement

The meal and the argument belong together. Jesus' service is not a reward for a group that has already understood him. His instruction confronts rivalry while his actions and promises precede the disciples' coming failures (22:14–34).

Jesus does not hide his desire for the cup to be taken away. The prayer gives words to anguish and obedience without making anguish evidence of failed faith. The disciples' sleep is explicitly linked to sorrow (22:39–46).

The sword exchange is difficult, but the subsequent injury and healing must remain audible. Do not use “That is enough” to erase its ambiguity or ignore what Jesus does when violence occurs (22:35–38,49–51).

### Threads Through Scripture

**Luke 9:46–48** records an earlier dispute about greatness. **Luke 23:1–25** continues from the council to the hearings before Pilate and Herod. These connections locate service and public power within Luke's narrative rather than in a detached leadership slogan.

### Questions for Conversation

How does Jesus answer rivalry? What comes before Simon's failure, and what responsibility is named beyond it? What changes when we hear the disciples' sorrow alongside their sleep? How can service be practiced without making a vulnerable person carry everyone else's burden?

### Prayer and Practice

“Christ who serves, meet us in fear and teach us to strengthen one another.” Choose one act of service that does not depend on status. This prayer is not a demand that someone accept violence, hide fear, or return to an unsafe relationship.

## Go Deeper

### Reading Carefully

The corrected notes distinguish two kinds of source marking. The bound SBLGNT has square doubt-brackets around part of 22:19–20. It prints 22:43–44 without those brackets, although its apparatus records variation. Fluent now reflects that distinction. The supper punctuation separates narration from Jesus' speech; the English bracket groups do not make the narrator's report into words spoken by Jesus. The sweat description remains a comparison, not a medical diagnosis supplied by this Companion.
