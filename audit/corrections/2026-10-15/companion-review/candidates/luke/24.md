# Luke 24

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

The women return to the tomb after the rest described at the end of chapter 23. The narrative moves through astonishment, dismissed testimony, a journey with an unrecognized companion, shared food, opened understanding, and a commission. Recognition is not immediate for everyone.

### Chapter Path

- **24:1–12:** The women find the tomb empty, remember Jesus' words, and report what happened.
- **24:13–35:** Two disciples speak of disappointed hope and recognize Jesus through the journey and meal.
- **24:36–43:** Jesus meets the gathered disciples amid fear, doubt, joy, and amazement.
- **24:44–53:** Scripture is opened, witnesses are commissioned to await power, and Jesus blesses them.

### Watch For

Follow the verbs of remembering, telling, hearing, seeing, recognizing, and understanding. Notice who is believed and who is dismissed. The range of responses within the chapter resists a simple division between people who understand immediately and people who do not belong.

## Read the Chapter

Read Luke 24 in Fluent. Let the conversations and the meals have their full place, rather than moving immediately from an empty tomb to a summary of resurrection belief.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

How does the chapter move from the women's discovery to the community's joy and continuing worship?

### The Chapter in Brief

The women find the stone moved and the body absent. They hear that Jesus has risen, remember his words, and tell the apostles, who dismiss their account. Peter visits the tomb. Two disciples walking to Emmaus recount their disappointed hope to Jesus without recognizing him. He interprets Scripture, stays with them, and is recognized in the breaking of bread. They return to Jerusalem. Jesus appears among the gathered disciples, invites attention to his body, and eats before them. He opens their understanding, names their witness, promises power, and blesses them before departing (24:1–53).

### Follow the Movement

The dismissal of the women's report is part of the story, not a model for hearing testimony. Luke names the women and records their persistence; the apostles' initial judgment does not have the last word (24:9–12).

On the road, disappointed hope is spoken at length. Jesus' presence precedes recognition, and the invitation to stay precedes the meal in which the disciples' eyes are opened. The sequence gives more room to encounter than a demand to manufacture certainty (24:13–35).

The commission also includes waiting. The disciples are witnesses, but they are told to remain until they receive the promised power. Their calling is not described as self-sufficiency. The final actions are blessing, return, joy, and praise (24:44–53).

### Threads Through Scripture

**Luke 23:50–56** supplies the burial and the women's preparation. **Acts 1:1–11** returns to Jesus' instruction, the promise of the Spirit, and witness. Compare the two narratives without forcing all their time markers into a single sequence in this Companion.

### Questions for Conversation

Whose testimony is initially dismissed? Where is disappointed hope allowed to be spoken? What does the command to wait add to the commission? How can a community welcome uncertainty without making it a permanent condition for belonging?

### Prayer and Practice

“Risen Christ, meet us in the words we struggle to speak and the testimony we struggle to hear.” Make room for one person's account without immediately correcting it or supplying your own story. Listening does not require instant certainty, and grief does not have to meet a timetable.

## Go Deeper

### Reading Carefully

The corrected source notes distinguish ordinary apparatus variation from square doubt-brackets. Verses 24:6 and 24:12 are printed in the bound SBLGNT without square doubt-brackets, and Fluent now follows that presentation. The source does use such brackets at 24:40 and in the clauses identified at 24:51–52; those remain marked. This is a specific account of one source edition, not a claim that every textual question has been settled.
