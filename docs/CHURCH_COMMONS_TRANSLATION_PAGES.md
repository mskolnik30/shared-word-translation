## `/bible/translations/` — Choose a translation

### Page title

Two translations. Two ways of paying attention.

### Introductory copy

The Shared Word Project offers two complete English translations of Scripture. Both are translated from verified Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek sources. Each is designed for a different reading posture.

You do not have to choose one forever. Read Fluent to follow the movement of a passage in natural contemporary English. Turn to TSW to slow down, notice its structure and tensions, and read closely with others.

### Translation cards

#### TSW · Close Reading

**For close reading, preaching, study, and shared discernment.**

The Shared Word Translation preserves literary shape, repetition, tension, and meaningful ambiguity when English can bear them. It is especially suited to communities that want to listen carefully, discuss honestly, and resist resolving the text too quickly.

Suggested buttons: **Read TSW** · **About TSW**

#### Fluent · Natural Reading

**For daily reading, devotion, teaching, and growing biblical fluency.**

Fluent carries the sense and movement of Scripture in direct, natural contemporary English. It is especially suited to sustained reading, prayer, formation, teaching, and accessible group engagement.

Suggested buttons: **Read Fluent** · **About Fluent**

### Comparison

| If you want to… | Begin with… |
| --- | --- |
| Follow a whole chapter or book in a natural reading voice | Fluent |
| Prepare to preach or teach a passage closely | TSW |
| Read Scripture as a daily practice | Fluent |
| Notice repetition, rhetorical movement, or ambiguity | TSW |
| Introduce a group to a less familiar biblical book | Fluent |
| Remain with a difficult interpretive question together | TSW |

These are starting points, not restrictions. Both translations can be proclaimed, prayed, studied, and read in community.

### Why use both?

Every translation helps readers hear some features of a passage more readily than others. Reading two translations can slow down familiarity and open better questions. The difference between TSW and Fluent is intentional: one leans toward close attention to textual form and tension; the other leans toward clear, sustained movement in contemporary English.

Neither is the “expert” Bible or the “easy” Bible. They are two invitations to attend to the same Scriptures.

### Provisional status

Both translations are complete across all 66 books and remain provisional. They will continue to be tested in worship, teaching, personal reading, and communal study. Corrections and substantial revisions will be documented publicly.

## `/bible/tsw/` — The Shared Word Translation

### Page title

The Shared Word Translation

### Deck

**TSW · Close Reading** — for close reading, preaching, study, and shared discernment.

### Body copy

The Shared Word Translation is a contemporary English translation created for communities that want to listen slowly and faithfully. It seeks clarity without controlling the passage and depth without unnecessary technical language.

TSW preserves ambiguity where the source text sustains it. It attends to repetition, cadence, rhetorical movement, poetry, and the shape of an argument. Notes and vocabulary remain selective, helping readers notice consequential features without becoming a second commentary.

TSW is especially useful when preparing to preach or teach, reading across theological difference, asking how a passage works, or holding an interpretive tension together rather than forcing a quick answer.

It is not only for scholars. Its purpose is communal: to support readers who listen carefully, speak humbly, and live faithfully.

Suggested buttons: **Read TSW** · **Read the full translation philosophy** · **Compare translations**

## `/bible/fluent/` — Fluent Translation

### Page title

Fluent Translation

### Deck

**Fluent · Natural Reading** — for daily reading, devotion, teaching, and growing biblical fluency.

### Body copy

Fluent is a contemporary English translation made for natural, sustained reading. It helps readers follow the movement of a passage, hear its voices, recognize recurring language, and become more at home in the biblical story.

Fluent uses direct, natural English while retaining the meaning, relationships, literary character, and consequential ambiguity of the source text. Narrative moves as narrative; poetry remains poetry; letters retain their argument and pastoral force.

Fluent is especially useful for reading whole chapters or books, establishing a daily Scripture practice, introducing a group to an unfamiliar passage, teaching, worship, and formation.

It is not a paraphrase, a simplified edition of TSW, or a devotional commentary. Fluent and TSW are sibling translations, each made from verified biblical-language sources.

## Shared FAQ copy

### Which translation is more accurate?

Accuracy is not a single setting. A translation may foreground syntax, literary form, ambiguity, natural English, or the movement of a whole passage. TSW and Fluent make different responsible judgments about how to carry those features into English. Both should remain accountable to the source texts.

### Is Fluent based on TSW?

No. Fluent and TSW are translated independently from verified biblical-language sources. TSW may be consulted during review, just as other translations may be, but Fluent is not a sentence-by-sentence rewrite of TSW.

### Is TSW only for group study?

No. TSW can be used for personal reading, prayer, worship, and teaching. “Close Reading” names its characteristic posture, not a restriction.

### Is Fluent only for private devotion?

No. Fluent is suitable for worship, teaching, formation, and group reading as well as personal devotion. “Natural Reading” names its characteristic posture, not a restriction.

### Why are some verse numbers absent in one translation?

Ancient textual traditions and modern source editions sometimes differ in whether or where a sentence appears. When a public verse number is not present in the source edition used by a translation, the reader should say so rather than silently renumber or substitute text. Notes record the consequential cases.
