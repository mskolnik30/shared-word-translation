# One in Christ, Differences Still Present

## First Look

Paul describes the law as a guardian until Christ, then addresses the baptized as God's children who have put on Christ. Jew and Greek, enslaved and free, male and female no longer determine access to belonging in Christ.

## Why This Matters

This passage bears directly on The *paidagogos* was a household guardian, not a modern classroom teacher. 'There is no' concerns status in the baptized community; the text does not claim embodied differences or unequal conditions have magically disappeared.. Translation and interpretation therefore affect not only ideas but the treatment of real people and communities. A responsible reading should identify what the text says, what readers infer, and where later institutions have made the passage do additional work.

## Look Closely

The promise to Abraham frames the paragraph. Paul argues about Gentile inclusion, not the worthlessness of Judaism; translating the law only as a prison can feed a supersessionist story the larger letter does not require.

Read the passage within its whole literary unit before isolating a single phrase. TSW's visible repetitions and tensions are evidence for study rather than problems to harmonize prematurely.

## More Than One Reading

Some interpret verse 28 as baptismal equality with direct social consequences. Others limit it to salvation while retaining differentiated roles. Liberation, feminist, queer, and Black interpreters test whether communities embody the unity they confess.

These positions should be compared by their linguistic evidence, historical assumptions, canonical relationships, and practical consequences. Naming several readings does not imply that every reading is equally persuasive or safe.

## Across Christian History

Patristic and Reformation readers emphasized faith and adoption, though Christian reception often opposed grace to a caricatured Judaism. Modern ecumenical interpretation increasingly rejects that contrast and attends to Paul's Jewish identity.

Reception history shows what communities noticed and what they overlooked. It is evidence about interpretation, not an automatic verdict that the oldest, newest, or most powerful reading is correct.

## Power and Consequences

Unity language can hide inequality when dominant groups declare difference irrelevant. The verse calls communities to dismantle status barriers, not to silence testimony about racism, slavery's legacies, sexism, or exclusion.

Interpretation should be evaluated in the presence of people who bear its consequences. Safeguarding, truthful history, and accountable power are theological concerns rather than optional additions.
