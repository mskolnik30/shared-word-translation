# Truth, Hospitality, and a Dangerous Teacher

## First Look

The elder warns the chosen lady and her children about deceivers who deny Jesus Christ coming in flesh. They are not to receive such a teacher into the house or offer a greeting that shares in harmful work.

## Why This Matters

This passage bears directly on In house-based assemblies, receiving a traveling teacher could mean material support, lodging, and public sponsorship, not merely private politeness. The Greek greeting can carry endorsement in that setting.. Translation and interpretation therefore affect not only ideas but the treatment of real people and communities. A responsible reading should identify what the text says, what readers infer, and where later institutions have made the passage do additional work.

## Look Closely

The warning targets teachers and their work, not every person holding a different belief. It follows the command to walk in love, creating a boundary question rather than canceling love.

Read the passage within its whole literary unit before isolating a single phrase. TSW's visible repetitions and tensions are evidence for study rather than problems to harmonize prematurely.

## More Than One Reading

Some traditions apply the prohibition broadly to social contact; others limit it to authorization of itinerant missionaries. Historical readings connect it to conflict over Christ's embodied coming.

These positions should be compared by their linguistic evidence, historical assumptions, canonical relationships, and practical consequences. Naming several readings does not imply that every reading is equally persuasive or safe.

## Across Christian History

Churches used boundary texts to police heresy and, at times, to shun families or deny ordinary care. Ecumenical practice distinguishes doctrinal conviction from dehumanization.

Reception history shows what communities noticed and what they overlooked. It is evidence about interpretation, not an automatic verdict that the oldest, newest, or most powerful reading is correct.

## Power and Consequences

Boundaries can protect communities from exploitation, but shunning can become coercive control. Refusing sponsorship does not require withholding emergency aid, family affection, civil rights, or truthful engagement.

Interpretation should be evaluated in the presence of people who bear its consequences. Safeguarding, truthful history, and accountable power are theological concerns rather than optional additions.
