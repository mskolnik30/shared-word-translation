# Hospitality and Competing Authority

## First Look

The elder praises Gaius for supporting traveling coworkers, condemns Diotrephes for loving first place and expelling hospitable members, and commends Demetrius.

## Why This Matters

This passage bears directly on The verb for Diotrephes's conduct depicts seeking preeminence. The letter's authority claims are relational and contested: letters, personal presence, testimony, hospitality, and expulsion all shape who is recognized.. Translation and interpretation therefore affect not only ideas but the treatment of real people and communities. A responsible reading should identify what the text says, what readers infer, and where later institutions have made the passage do additional work.

## Look Closely

The missionaries accept no support from 'the Gentiles,' making intra-community hospitality essential. Economic dependence and doctrinal authority therefore intersect.

Read the passage within its whole literary unit before isolating a single phrase. TSW's visible repetitions and tensions are evidence for study rather than problems to harmonize prematurely.

## More Than One Reading

Some see Diotrephes as a local authoritarian resisting wider mission; others caution that only his opponent's letter survives. The text still explicitly condemns malicious speech and punitive exclusion.

These positions should be compared by their linguistic evidence, historical assumptions, canonical relationships, and practical consequences. Naming several readings does not imply that every reading is equally persuasive or safe.

## Across Christian History

Later tradition cast Diotrephes as an archetypal bad bishop. That can illuminate clerical ambition, but it can also let every faction label its opponent without examining evidence.

Reception history shows what communities noticed and what they overlooked. It is evidence about interpretation, not an automatic verdict that the oldest, newest, or most powerful reading is correct.

## Power and Consequences

Leaders must not expel people for showing lawful hospitality or use access, membership, and reputation to silence questions. Fair process requires hearing more than one side while protecting targets of retaliation.

Interpretation should be evaluated in the presence of people who bear its consequences. Safeguarding, truthful history, and accountable power are theological concerns rather than optional additions.
