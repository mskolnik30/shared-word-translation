# Vice Lists, Translation, and Belonging

## First Look

Paul warns that injustice is inconsistent with God's kingdom, gives a vice list, and reminds the Corinthians that they were washed, made holy, and set right in Jesus and the Spirit.

## Why This Matters

This passage bears directly on The rare terms *malakoi* and *arsenokoitai* are disputed. *Malakos* literally means soft and carried gendered moral associations; *arsenokoitēs* may echo Greek Leviticus but its exact social referent is not established by etymology alone.. Translation and interpretation therefore affect not only ideas but the treatment of real people and communities. A responsible reading should identify what the text says, what readers infer, and where later institutions have made the passage do additional work.

## Look Closely

English Bibles have moved from terms such as effeminate and abusers of themselves to homosexuals or men who have sex with men. Those choices import different modern categories into an ancient list.

Read the passage within its whole literary unit before isolating a single phrase. TSW's visible repetitions and tensions are evidence for study rather than problems to harmonize prematurely.

## More Than One Reading

Some interpreters see a broad condemnation of male same-sex intercourse; others limit the terms to exploitative, status-marked, or commercial practices. All should distinguish ancient acts and social roles from modern sexual orientation and identity.

These positions should be compared by their linguistic evidence, historical assumptions, canonical relationships, and practical consequences. Naming several readings does not imply that every reading is equally persuasive or safe.

## Across Christian History

Christian tradition long read the list through gender hierarchy and sexual vice. Twentieth-century translation changes made it a major anti-LGBTQ proof text; affirming and traditional churches now dispute both lexicon and moral scope.

Reception history shows what communities noticed and what they overlooked. It is evidence about interpretation, not an automatic verdict that the oldest, newest, or most powerful reading is correct.

## Power and Consequences

The passage has been used to expel, shame, criminalize, and endanger LGBTQ+ people. Study must preserve human dignity and safety, reject slurs, and disclose lexical uncertainty instead of claiming that one modern label simply reproduces Paul's word.

Interpretation should be evaluated in the presence of people who bear its consequences. Safeguarding, truthful history, and accountable power are theological concerns rather than optional additions.
