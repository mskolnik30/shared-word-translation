# New Creation and Reconciliation

## First Look

Paul declares that anyone in Christ participates in new creation. God reconciles the world through Christ, entrusts a ministry of reconciliation, and makes the apostles ambassadors.

## Why This Matters

This passage bears directly on The compact phrase 'made him sin who knew no sin' can mean made a sin offering, identified Christ with the sphere and consequences of sin, or appointed him to bear sin. The Greek does not require the claim that Christ became morally sinful.. Translation and interpretation therefore affect not only ideas but the treatment of real people and communities. A responsible reading should identify what the text says, what readers infer, and where later institutions have made the passage do additional work.

## Look Closely

God is the grammatical initiator of reconciliation. The appeal 'be reconciled to God' answers that prior action; ambassadors do not own the peace they announce.

Read the passage within its whole literary unit before isolating a single phrase. TSW's visible repetitions and tensions are evidence for study rather than problems to harmonize prematurely.

## More Than One Reading

Substitutionary readings emphasize Christ acting for sinners; participatory readings emphasize humanity entering Christ's death and new creation; Christus Victor and representative readings stress liberation and corporate identity.

These positions should be compared by their linguistic evidence, historical assumptions, canonical relationships, and practical consequences. Naming several readings does not imply that every reading is equally persuasive or safe.

## Across Christian History

Eastern and Western traditions share the language of exchange while construing it through healing, satisfaction, victory, or justification. Reformation readings made verse 21 central to debates over imputed righteousness.

Reception history shows what communities noticed and what they overlooked. It is evidence about interpretation, not an automatic verdict that the oldest, newest, or most powerful reading is correct.

## Power and Consequences

Reconciliation with God cannot be collapsed into forced reunion with an abuser or unjust institution. Christian ambassadors lose credibility when they demand cheap peace without truth, repair, and freedom.

Interpretation should be evaluated in the presence of people who bear its consequences. Safeguarding, truthful history, and accountable power are theological concerns rather than optional additions.
