# When Worship Becomes Noise

## First Look

God rejects festivals, assemblies, offerings, and songs, then demands that justice roll like waters and righteousness like an enduring stream.

## Why This Matters

This passage bears directly on The first-person verbs are deliberately forceful: hate, reject, refuse to smell, refuse to look, refuse to hear. *Mishpat* and *tsedaqah* concern right public order and faithful relationship, not private kindness alone.. Translation and interpretation therefore affect not only ideas but the treatment of real people and communities. A responsible reading should identify what the text says, what readers infer, and where later institutions have made the passage do additional work.

## Look Closely

The oracle does not oppose worship as such; it opposes worship practiced alongside the exploitation named across Amos. The water image demands a stable social reality, not a brief charitable event.

Read the passage within its whole literary unit before isolating a single phrase. TSW's visible repetitions and tensions are evidence for study rather than problems to harmonize prematurely.

## More Than One Reading

Prophetic readings call worship and justice inseparable. Some liturgical traditions hear a warning against ritualism, but that label can caricature Jewish practice unless the critique is directed first at one's own community.

These positions should be compared by their linguistic evidence, historical assumptions, canonical relationships, and practical consequences. Naming several readings does not imply that every reading is equally persuasive or safe.

## Across Christian History

The passage shaped abolitionist preaching and the modern civil-rights movement, especially Martin Luther King Jr.'s public rhetoric. Its familiarity can domesticate the economic indictment that gives it force.

Reception history shows what communities noticed and what they overlooked. It is evidence about interpretation, not an automatic verdict that the oldest, newest, or most powerful reading is correct.

## Power and Consequences

Quoting justice while maintaining discriminatory wages, policing, access, or leadership repeats the contradiction Amos names. Institutions should connect confession to measurable repair and accountability.

Interpretation should be evaluated in the presence of people who bear its consequences. Safeguarding, truthful history, and accountable power are theological concerns rather than optional additions.
