# Titus book guide

## Begin Here

Titus connects truth with godliness and grace with public good. Titus is to appoint credible local leaders, silence destructive teaching, form households across generations, and remind believers that salvation is God's mercy rather than their achievement. Grace trains a people eager for good works without making those works the source of salvation.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Pastoral letter with leadership criteria, household exhortation, doctrinal summary, and practical instruction.
- **Primary setting:** Developing churches in Crete.
- **Central movement:** Healthy teaching orders community life so grace becomes visible in trustworthy conduct and shared good.
- **People to know:** Paul, Titus, elders/overseers, false teachers, older and younger believers, enslaved people, Artemas, Tychicus, Zenas, and Apollos.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 15 minutes.

## What Is Going On?

The letter quotes a hostile Cretan stereotype in a polemical context. It should not be generalized as ethnic truth. Instructions to enslaved believers address survival and witness inside slavery; they do not make owning people good.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapter 1:** Appoint faithful elders and confront teaching that denies God through works.
2. **Chapter 2:** Teach conduct fitting healthy doctrine across age, gender, and social status because grace trains all.
3. **Chapter 3:** Remember mercy, practice public good, avoid fruitless conflict, and support coworkers.

## Watch For

- Truth measured by godliness.
- Leaders called elders and overseers in the same passage.
- Grace as teacher before good works as response.
- Public reputation joined to internal integrity.

## A Thread to Carry

Titus places good works after God's kindness appears. Communities serve neighbors not to earn rescue but because mercy is forming a different public life.
