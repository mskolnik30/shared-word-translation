# Proverbs book guide

## Begin Here

Proverbs trains people to live skillfully in God's world. It joins reverence for the LORD with attention to speech, money, sex, friendship, work, conflict, parenting, leadership, and care for vulnerable people. A proverb names a reliable pattern, not an exceptionless guarantee.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Wisdom instruction, short sayings, poems, comparisons, and collected royal counsel.
- **Primary setting:** Household, street, market, court, field, and royal administration.
- **Central movement:** Extended invitations to wisdom lead into collections of compact sayings and a final portrait of embodied strength.
- **People to know:** Parents and children, teachers and learners, Woman Wisdom, the foolish woman, kings, workers, neighbors, and the woman of strength.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 3½ hours.

## What Is Going On?

Wisdom is practical moral skill rooted in “fear of the LORD”—reverent trust and accountable life before God. Contrasting proverbs may address different situations; wise use requires discernment. The book describes how choices usually grow into consequences while Job and Ecclesiastes remind readers that life cannot be mastered by a formula.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapters 1–9:** A parent urges a young person to choose Wisdom over violence, exploitation, and destructive desire.
2. **Chapters 10–29:** Several collections apply wisdom to ordinary and royal life.
3. **Chapter 30:** Agur's sayings emphasize humility, wonder, and limits.
4. **Chapter 31:** A mother's counsel to a king and an acrostic woman of strength close the book.

## Watch For

- Speech as a power that heals or destroys.
- Wealth judged by its effects on neighbors.
- Diligence without worship of productivity.
- Wisdom learned through observation, correction, and community.

## A Thread to Carry

Proverbs forms attention. Wisdom grows as people notice where a path leads, receive correction, govern speech, resist shortcuts, and practice justice before crisis arrives.
