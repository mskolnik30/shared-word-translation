# Zephaniah book guide

## Begin Here

Zephaniah announces the day of the LORD to Judah during Josiah's reign. Divided worship, violence, deceptive wealth, and settled complacency bring creation-reversing judgment. Yet the book moves toward a humble remnant, restored speech, gathered outcasts, and God rejoicing over the people.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Judgment oracle, prophetic litany, summons, and restoration song.
- **Primary setting:** Judah and Jerusalem, with surrounding nations drawn into view.
- **Central movement:** Sweeping judgment narrows toward a humble people and widens into worldwide restoration.
- **People to know:** Zephaniah, Josiah, Jerusalem's officials, traders, priests, nations, and the remnant.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 20 minutes.

## What Is Going On?

The prophet attacks neither open idolatry alone nor religious absence alone. Some swear by the LORD and another god, mixing allegiances. Others assume God will do neither good nor harm. The day of the LORD exposes both divided loyalty and practical indifference.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapter 1:** Judgment sweeps through creation and searches Jerusalem.
2. **Chapter 2:** The people are urged to seek humility; surrounding nations face judgment.
3. **Chapter 3:** Jerusalem is accused, then purified peoples and gathered outcasts share joy.

## Watch For

- Creation language moving from undoing toward renewal.
- Wealth failing as refuge.
- Humility as dependence rather than self-contempt.
- God's final presence described through song and delight.

## A Thread to Carry

Zephaniah refuses complacent religion, but judgment is not the book's final sound. The closing voice is God singing over a restored and gathered people.
