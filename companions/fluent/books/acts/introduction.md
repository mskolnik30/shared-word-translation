# Acts book guide

## Begin Here

Acts continues Luke's account after Jesus' resurrection. The Spirit empowers witness from Jerusalem through Judea, Samaria, and toward Rome. The community shares resources, crosses entrenched boundaries, argues about belonging, suffers opposition, and repeatedly discovers that God's mission is already moving beyond its expectations.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Theological history, travel narrative, speeches, trials, conversion accounts, and communal scenes.
- **Primary setting:** Jerusalem, the eastern Mediterranean, and Rome.
- **Central movement:** Spirit-empowered witness crosses geographic, ethnic, social, and religious boundaries.
- **People to know:** Peter, John, Stephen, Philip, Barnabas, Paul, James, Lydia, Priscilla and Aquila, and many local communities.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 3 hours.

## What Is Going On?

Acts describes a movement before later institutional forms were settled. Repeated summaries show generosity and growth, while conflicts reveal real failures and discernment. Speeches address particular audiences and should be read as testimony within the story, not detached formulas.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapters 1–7:** The Spirit comes; the Jerusalem community witnesses, shares, conflicts, and loses Stephen.
2. **Chapters 8–12:** Witness reaches Samaria and Gentiles; Saul is called; Peter's boundaries are transformed.
3. **Chapters 13–20:** Mission communities form; the Jerusalem council discerns belonging; Paul travels widely.
4. **Chapters 21–28:** Paul is arrested, tried, shipwrecked, and carried to Rome, where the story remains open.

## Watch For

- The Spirit interrupting human plans.
- Hospitality and households enabling mission.
- Money revealing truth about community.
- Disagreement handled through testimony, Scripture, and shared discernment.

## A Thread to Carry

Acts portrays mission as God's movement before it is the church's possession. Witness follows the Spirit across boundaries and learns new faithfulness on the way.
