# 3 John book guide

## Begin Here

Third John offers a window into early Christian hospitality, mission, and conflict over authority. The elder writes to Gaius, praising his welcome of traveling believers. He contrasts that faithfulness with Diotrephes, who loves to be first, rejects the elder's authority, attacks others with words, refuses hospitality, and expels those who offer it.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** A personal pastoral letter.
- **Primary setting:** Connected house churches that depend on hosts to support traveling workers.
- **Central movement:** Truth becomes shared work through hospitality; self-promoting leadership blocks fellowship and is confronted.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 3 minutes.

## What Is Going On?

Supporting travelers meant food, lodging, funds, and public welcome. Gaius's hospitality makes him a “fellow worker for the truth.” Diotrephes controls access and belonging in the opposite direction.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Verses 1–4:** Greeting and joy that Gaius walks in truth.
2. **Verses 5–8:** Hospitality makes believers fellow workers.
3. **Verses 9–12:** Diotrephes is confronted; Demetrius receives a good testimony.
4. **Verses 13–15:** Written words yield to hoped-for face-to-face presence.

## Watch For

- Truth expressed as conduct.
- The material work of sending travelers well.
- Leadership that serves versus leadership that needs to be first.
- Testimony confirmed by multiple witnesses.

## A Thread to Carry

Third John measures authority by what it makes possible for others. Faithful leaders welcome, equip, and tell the truth; controlling leaders hoard status, weaponize speech, and expel people who resist them.
