# 1 Kings book guide

## Begin Here

First Kings begins with Solomon receiving David's throne and building the temple. It ends with the kingdom divided and royal houses repeatedly evaluated by covenant faithfulness. Splendor and wisdom sit beside forced labor, accumulated wealth, idolatry, and political violence. Prophets enter royal space to announce that kings are accountable to God.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Royal history, prophetic narrative, temple account, prayer, wisdom story, and political chronicle.
- **Primary setting:** Jerusalem, the northern kingdom of Israel, Judah, and neighboring lands.
- **Central movement:** A united kingdom reaches grandeur under Solomon, fractures, and enters a long struggle over worship and power.
- **People to know:** Solomon, the queen of Sheba, Rehoboam, Jeroboam, Ahab, Jezebel, Elijah, and several unnamed prophets.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 2½–3 hours.

## What Is Going On?

The book measures rulers less by territorial success than by whether they direct the people toward the LORD and practice covenant justice. Solomon asks for a listening heart and builds the temple, yet his marriages, wealth, military expansion, and labor policies expose the danger anticipated in Deuteronomy's law for kings.

After the kingdom divides, Jeroboam creates rival worship centers to secure political control. Later, Ahab and Jezebel sponsor Baal worship and seize Naboth's land. Elijah's ministry confronts both idolatry and royal violence. Some prophetic conflicts include ridicule, execution, and warfare; the text's ancient judgments must not become permission for modern religious coercion.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapters 1–2 — Succession:** Solomon receives the throne through a tense and violent transfer of power.
2. **Chapters 3–11 — Solomon's reign:** Wisdom, temple, prayer, wealth, labor, and compromise shape a brilliant but divided legacy.
3. **Chapters 12–16 — A kingdom split:** Rehoboam's harshness fractures Israel; rival dynasties and worship systems emerge.
4. **Chapters 17–22 — Elijah and Ahab:** Drought, provision, Mount Carmel, Naboth's vineyard, and prophetic judgment expose the royal house.

## Watch For

- Listening wisdom and the gradual loss of it.
- Temple worship alongside economic and political practice.
- Prophets speaking truth where kings control the consequences.
- Vulnerable people—widows, foreigners, farmers—at the center of God's concern.
- Repeated formulas evaluating each reign.

## A Thread to Carry

First Kings asks whether success can conceal unfaithfulness. Buildings, wealth, and victories cannot compensate for divided loyalty, exploited labor, or power that takes a neighbor's inheritance.
