# 2 Corinthians book guide

## Begin Here

Second Corinthians opens a difficult relationship between Paul and the Corinthian church. Painful visits, letters, rival teachers, financial collection, reconciliation, and contested authority shape the letter. Paul interprets ministry through God's comfort, fragile bodies, Christ's self-giving, new creation, generous sharing, and power made complete in weakness.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Pastoral letter with autobiography, defense, appeal, Scripture interpretation, and collection instructions.
- **Primary setting:** Paul in Macedonia writing to Corinth amid strained partnership.
- **Central movement:** Affliction and reconciliation redefine credible ministry before a final confrontation with rival claims.
- **People to know:** Paul, Timothy, Titus, the Corinthians, the offending person, Macedonian churches, Jerusalem's saints, and rival apostles.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 75 minutes.

## What Is Going On?

The letter's shifts in tone are sharp, and interpreters debate whether it preserves parts of several letters. Whatever its compositional history, the received form refuses a polished image of ministry. Paul can defend authority, confess weakness, plead for open hearts, and organize material aid.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapters 1–2:** Comfort, changed plans, pain, forgiveness, and restored relationship.
2. **Chapters 3–7:** New-covenant ministry appears in fragile vessels and moves toward reconciliation.
3. **Chapters 8–9:** Grace becomes equitable and willing generosity for Jerusalem.
4. **Chapters 10–13:** Paul contests boastful authority and boasts paradoxically in weakness.

## Watch For

- Comfort received becoming comfort shared.
- Transparency rather than image management.
- Reconciliation initiated by God and embodied between people.
- Giving measured by willingness and fairness, not pressure.

## A Thread to Carry

Second Corinthians does not hide pain inside partnership. Christ's love makes room for truth, repair, generosity, and an authority that serves rather than performs strength.
