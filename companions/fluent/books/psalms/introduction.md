# Psalms book guide

## Begin Here

Psalms is Scripture's prayer book and songbook. Its 150 poems give language for praise, grief, anger, confession, wonder, protest, gratitude, fear, trust, and hope. They teach communities not that every feeling is right, but that the whole truth of life can be brought before God.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** A collection of poetic prayers and songs arranged in five books.
- **Primary setting:** Israel's worship and the changing lives of individual and communal speakers.
- **Central movement:** Honest speech before God is gathered into worship and finally into the repeated call to praise.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 5–6 hours.

## What Is Going On?

Many psalms include headings naming a person, event, musical direction, or collection. These headings are ancient and important, though their exact historical relationship to each poem is sometimes uncertain. Parallel lines, images, repetition, and shifts of voice carry meaning that prose summary cannot replace.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Psalms 1–2:** Wisdom and God's anointed king introduce the collection.
2. **Books I–III:** Prayer moves through Davidic hope, crisis, exile, and the collapse of royal confidence.
3. **Book IV:** God's eternal kingship answers the fragility of human rulers.
4. **Book V:** Thanksgiving, pilgrimage, Torah, restoration, and widening praise.
5. **Psalms 146–150:** Every living thing is summoned to praise the LORD.

## Watch For

- Who speaks, who is addressed, and when the voice changes.
- Movement between complaint, request, trust, and praise.
- Bodily, natural, royal, temple, and warfare imagery.
- Tensions that the poem leaves unresolved.

## A Thread to Carry

The Psalms form people through practiced honesty. They make room for pain without allowing pain to become the only word, and they offer words to people who cannot yet supply their own.
