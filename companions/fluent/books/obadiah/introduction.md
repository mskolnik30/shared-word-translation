# Obadiah book guide

## Begin Here

Obadiah is the shortest book in the Old Testament. Its single vision addresses Edom after violence against Jerusalem. Edom and Israel are portrayed as brother peoples through the ancestral relationship of Esau and Jacob, making Edom's actions a family betrayal as well as an international wrong.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** A prophetic judgment oracle in poetry.
- **Primary setting:** Edom, Jerusalem, and the aftermath of Jerusalem's disaster.
- **Central movement:** Edom's proud security is exposed; complicity in Judah's destruction is judged; Zion and displaced people are promised restoration.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 5–7 minutes.

## What Is Going On?

Edom did more than watch Jerusalem fall. The poem accuses it of rejoicing, plundering, intercepting fugitives, and handing over survivors. Obadiah answers this violence with the prophetic principle that deeds return upon the doer. The book's own violent imagery must be read honestly rather than turned into permission for revenge.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Verses 1–9:** Edom's pride, alliances, wisdom, and warriors fail.
2. **Verses 10–14:** The charge: violence and betrayal during Jerusalem's disaster.
3. **Verses 15–18:** The day of the LORD brings corresponding judgment and deliverance.
4. **Verses 19–21:** Exiles receive a future, and kingship belongs to the LORD.

## Watch For

- The language of height, security, descent, and reversal.
- “Brother Jacob” as the moral center of the accusation.
- The widening horizon from Edom to all nations.
- The difference between human deliverers and the LORD's final rule.

## A Thread to Carry

Obadiah treats detached observation as complicity when observers benefit from violence and help trap survivors. It also insists that proud power is not permanent and that displaced people have not disappeared from God's concern.
