# 1 Corinthians book guide

## Begin Here

First Corinthians addresses a gifted but divided church learning how the gospel reshapes status, bodies, sex, marriage, money, worship, leadership, freedom, and hope. Paul responds to reports and questions, repeatedly returning to the crucified Messiah, love, the building up of neighbors, and resurrection.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Pastoral letter with argument, exhortation, Scripture interpretation, case response, and resurrection teaching.
- **Primary setting:** A diverse house-church network in Roman Corinth.
- **Central movement:** Cross-shaped wisdom confronts rivalry and self-assertion, forming one body whose gifts serve love and resurrection hope.
- **People to know:** Paul, Sosthenes, Chloe's people, Apollos, Cephas, Stephanas's household, women and men who pray and prophesy.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 90 minutes.

## What Is Going On?

Corinth valued patronage, rhetoric, status, and public honor. Those patterns entered church gatherings. Paul does not reject knowledge, gifts, freedom, or leadership; he relocates each inside responsibility for people with less power.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapters 1–4:** Rivalries are judged by the cross.
2. **Chapters 5–7:** Bodies, sexual ethics, lawsuits, marriage, and calling receive pastoral response.
3. **Chapters 8–11:** Freedom, food, gendered practice, and the Lord's Supper are ordered toward others.
4. **Chapters 12–14:** One body receives diverse gifts governed by love and intelligible edification.
5. **Chapters 15–16:** Bodily resurrection grounds steadfast work and generous partnership.

## Watch For

- “Build up” as the measure of gathered practice.
- Freedom limited by love.
- The body honoring members treated as less important.
- Resurrection protecting embodied life from meaninglessness.

## A Thread to Carry

First Corinthians moves partnership from shared identity to shared practices: a community belongs to Christ by refusing status competition and making every gift answerable to love.
