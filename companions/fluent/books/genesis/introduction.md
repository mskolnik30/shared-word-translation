# Genesis book guide

## Begin Here

Genesis opens the Bible's story with creation and follows the beginnings of humanity, violence, nations, covenant, and the family of Abraham and Sarah. The book moves from the whole world to one family through whom God promises blessing for all families of the earth.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Foundational narrative containing poetry, genealogy, blessing, covenant, and family story.
- **Primary settings:** The garden, Mesopotamia, Canaan, and Egypt.
- **Central movement:** God creates and blesses; humans mistrust, grasp, and wound; God preserves life and begins a covenant family whose faith is real but deeply imperfect.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 3½–4 hours.

## What Is Going On?

Genesis 1–11 gives a wide-angle account of creation and humanity. Genesis 12–50 follows Abraham and Sarah, Isaac and Rebekah, Jacob and his family, and Joseph and his brothers. Ancient customs appear throughout, but the book often narrates them without approving every action.

## The Book's Movement

1. **1–11:** Creation, human rebellion, flood, covenant with all flesh, and Babel.
2. **12–25:** Abraham and Sarah receive promises of land, descendants, and worldwide blessing.
3. **25–36:** Isaac's household and Jacob's long transformation amid rivalry and reconciliation.
4. **37–50:** Joseph, Judah, family betrayal, famine, forgiveness, and migration to Egypt.

## Watch For

- Blessing threatened and renewed.
- Younger siblings, outsiders, and women moving the story unexpectedly.
- Human schemes beside God's patient faithfulness.
- Repeated family wounds that do not prevent the promise from continuing.

## A Thread to Carry

Genesis does not present its ancestors as flawless heroes. It shows God working through compromised families while continually protecting life and widening the horizon of blessing.
