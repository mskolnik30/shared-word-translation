# Malachi book guide

## Begin Here

Malachi speaks through a series of disputes after temple worship has resumed but communal faithfulness has grown thin. Priests offer contemptuous worship and distort instruction; households practice betrayal; people question justice; giving and labor become places of mistrust. The book promises a messenger who will prepare for God's purifying arrival.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Prophetic disputation, accusation, warning, and promise.
- **Primary setting:** Persian-period Judah and its rebuilt temple.
- **Central movement:** Cynical questions are answered by covenant accountability and hope for refining presence.
- **People to know:** Priests, worshipers, spouses, workers, the covenant messenger, Elijah, and those who fear the LORD.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 25 minutes.

## What Is Going On?

Each section often begins with God's claim and the people's skeptical reply: “How?” The questions reveal habituated contempt rather than open inquiry. Malachi links worship quality to teaching, marriage, wages, justice, and shared trust.

## The Book's Movement

1. **1:1–5:** God's love is questioned and defended.
2. **1:6–2:9:** Priests dishonor God through offerings and corrupt teaching.
3. **2:10–16:** Covenant betrayal enters marriage and communal life.
4. **2:17–3:12:** The coming Lord will refine, judge exploitation, and call the people to return.
5. **3:13–4:6:** Cynicism is answered by remembered faithfulness and promised Elijah.

## Watch For

- “Covenant” joining worship, teaching, and marriage.
- Leaders whose distorted instruction causes others to stumble.
- God as witness for workers and vulnerable spouses.
- Refining fire as accountability before restoration.

## A Thread to Carry

Malachi asks whether ordinary practice tells the truth about professed worship. God is not honored by religious gifts joined to betrayal at home, exploitation at work, or careless teaching.
