# Amos book guide

## Begin Here

Amos, a herdsman and orchard worker from Judah, prophesies to prosperous northern Israel. Worship is active and wealth visible, but courts are corrupt, poor people are exploited, and luxury depends on their loss. Amos announces that covenant privilege increases accountability rather than guaranteeing protection.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Judgment oracle, lament, vision, disputation, and brief narrative.
- **Primary setting:** Eighth-century BCE Israel, especially Bethel and Samaria.
- **Central movement:** Judgment moves from surrounding nations toward Israel, exposing worship without justice before a final promise of restoration.
- **People to know:** Amos, Jeroboam II, Amaziah the priest, wealthy elites, poor people, merchants, and neighboring nations.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 45 minutes.

## What Is Going On?

Israel is politically successful, so Amos's warnings sound implausible. He names concrete practices: debt slavery, sexual exploitation, dishonest scales, bribed courts, seized grain, luxurious indifference, and silenced truth. The coming “day of the LORD” will therefore be darkness for Israel, not automatic national victory.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapters 1–2:** Oracles circle the nations and land on Israel.
2. **Chapters 3–6:** Privilege, false worship, injustice, luxury, and false security face judgment.
3. **Chapters 7–9:** Visions announce the end; Amaziah resists Amos; a closing promise rebuilds ruins.

## Watch For

- Economic details inside spiritual indictment.
- Worship rejected because justice is absent.
- Creation imagery serving judgment and hope.
- The prophet's outsider status before a royal sanctuary.

## A Thread to Carry

Amos will not allow worship to become shelter from the neighbor's claim. Seeking God and seeking good belong together wherever courts, markets, and religious gatherings shape common life.
