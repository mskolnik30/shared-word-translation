# Ezekiel book guide

## Begin Here

Ezekiel is a priest-prophet among Judean exiles in Babylon. He sees God's mobile glory, performs demanding sign-acts, exposes Jerusalem's violence and idolatry, and announces the city's fall. After destruction, the book turns toward responsible shepherds, a new heart and spirit, revived bones, reunited people, and restored divine presence.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Prophetic vision, sign-act, allegory, lawsuit, lament, oracle, and temple vision.
- **Primary setting:** Babylonian exile, with visions concerning Jerusalem and nations.
- **Central movement:** Glory departs a corrupted city and ultimately returns to a renewed dwelling.
- **People to know:** Ezekiel, exiles, Jerusalem, false shepherds, surrounding nations, and visionary figures.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 5 hours.

## What Is Going On?

Ezekiel's symbolic language is intentionally intense and sometimes sexually violent. Metaphors comparing cities to unfaithful women can expose political betrayal while also harming readers when repeated carelessly. They should never be used to shame women, sex workers, or survivors.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapters 1–24:** Call, signs, temple corruption, glory's departure, and Jerusalem's coming fall.
2. **Chapters 25–32:** Nations and imperial powers face judgment.
3. **Chapters 33–39:** Responsibility, new shepherding, restored hearts, dry bones, and reunification.
4. **Chapters 40–48:** A vast temple-and-land vision pictures ordered life with God present.

## Watch For

- “They will know that I am the LORD.”
- Individual and communal responsibility.
- False shepherds versus God's care for the flock.
- Resurrection-like imagery after national death.

## A Thread to Carry

Ezekiel insists that God's presence is not trapped in a ruined building. The God who departs injustice can find exiles, expose false leaders, give new hearts, and imagine life beyond devastation.
