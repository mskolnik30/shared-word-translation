# Hosea book guide

## Begin Here

Hosea addresses the northern kingdom of Israel during political instability before Assyria's conquest. The book portrays covenant unfaithfulness through Hosea's marriage and children's names, then through poetry of betrayal, judgment, parental love, healing, and return. Its sexualized accusations must not be used to shame women or excuse coercive relationships.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Prophetic sign-story, lawsuit, poetry, lament, accusation, and promise.
- **Primary setting:** Eighth-century BCE Israel under Assyrian pressure.
- **Central movement:** Broken covenant brings judgment, yet divine compassion repeatedly interrupts abandonment.
- **People to know:** Hosea, Gomer, their children, Israel/Ephraim, Judah, Assyria, priests, and rulers.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 70–80 minutes.

## What Is Going On?

Israel seeks security through other gods and shifting political alliances. Hosea describes this as marital betrayal, while also using parent-child and healing images. The metaphors reveal God's wounded covenant love but do not turn controlling or violent human behavior into a model for marriage.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapters 1–3:** Hosea's household becomes a sign of rupture and promised restoration.
2. **Chapters 4–10:** Priests, rulers, worship, economics, and alliances are charged with unfaithfulness.
3. **Chapters 11–14:** Divine compassion, judgment, return, healing, and fruitful life contend toward the close.

## Watch For

- Names shifting from rejection toward belonging.
- Knowledge of God expressed through loyal practice.
- Political alliances treated as false salvation.
- God speaking as spouse, parent, healer, dew, and tree.

## A Thread to Carry

Hosea refuses to make betrayal unreal, yet it also refuses to make rejection final. God's holy compassion opens a return no human bargaining can manufacture.
