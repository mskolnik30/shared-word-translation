# Micah book guide

## Begin Here

Micah speaks from Judah's countryside while Samaria and Jerusalem concentrate wealth and power. He condemns land seizure, violent leadership, paid prophecy, dishonest commerce, and confidence in sacred institutions. Judgment alternates with visions of gathered survivors, peaceful nations, a ruler from Bethlehem, and God delighting in steadfast love.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Prophetic lawsuit, lament, judgment oracle, salvation promise, and disputation.
- **Primary setting:** Eighth-century BCE Judah and Israel under Assyrian threat.
- **Central movement:** Corrupt centers face ruin, yet a remnant is gathered and covenant mercy receives the last word.
- **People to know:** Micah, rulers, prophets, priests, landowners, Jerusalem/Zion, Samaria, and the remnant.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 40 minutes.

## What Is Going On?

Micah links theology and material life. Leaders who “know justice” instead consume the people; landowners devise seizure at night; prophets adjust messages to payment. Zion cannot say “the LORD is among us” while building with blood.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapters 1–2:** Samaria and Judah face judgment for idolatry and land theft; a remnant will be gathered.
2. **Chapters 3–5:** Leaders are indicted; Zion falls and is renewed; a ruler comes from Bethlehem.
3. **Chapters 6–7:** God's covenant case calls for justice, faithful kindness, humility, confession, and hope.

## Watch For

- Rural families harmed by urban power.
- False security in temple and national identity.
- Shepherding as an alternative to devouring leadership.
- Justice and mercy held in covenant relationship.

## A Thread to Carry

Micah condenses covenant life without making it easy: do justice, love faithful kindness, and walk humbly with God. Each practice corrects the others.
