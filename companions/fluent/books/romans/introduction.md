# Romans book guide

## Begin Here

Romans is Paul's sustained account of God's faithfulness revealed in the good news of Jesus Christ. He writes to assemblies in Rome he did not establish, seeking partnership for future mission and addressing a community learning to live together across Jewish and Gentile difference.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** A theological and pastoral letter using argument, Scripture, dialogue, exhortation, and travel plans.
- **Primary setting:** House churches in Rome within the first-century Roman Empire.
- **Central movement:** God is faithful to judge sin, justify through Christ, give the Spirit, keep promises, form one merciful people, and send that people into faithful public life.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 1½ hours.

## What Is Going On?

Romans should be read as a whole letter, not merely a chain of isolated doctrinal verses. Paul addresses Jews and Gentiles together, dismantles boasting, grieves conflict, and insists that neither group may despise the other. Chapters 1–11 lead into a shared life of mercy in chapters 12–15.

## The Book's Movement

1. **1–4:** The good news reveals God's righteousness; all stand under sin; Abraham becomes father of a family marked by trust.
2. **5–8:** Peace, grace, new humanity in Christ, freedom from sin, struggle, life in the Spirit, and inseparable love.
3. **9–11:** Paul wrestles with Israel, God's promises, Gentile inclusion, and mercy for all.
4. **12–15:** Worship becomes communal practice: gifts, enemy-love, public responsibility, and welcome across disputed practices.
5. **16:** A network of named coworkers and house churches receives Paul's greetings.

## Watch For

- God's faithfulness and human boasting.
- Jew and Gentile addressed in changing combinations.
- “Therefore” as Paul turns divine mercy toward embodied practice.
- The many women and men whose partnership carries the mission.

## A Thread to Carry

Romans does not end with an abstract system. It ends with people learning to welcome one another because the God who justifies enemies has created one body through mercy.
