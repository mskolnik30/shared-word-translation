# Luke book guide

## Begin Here

Luke offers an orderly account of Jesus' life within Israel's story and the wider Roman world. Songs announce reversals before Jesus speaks: the lowly are lifted, hungry people filled, and mercy remembered. The Spirit anoints good news for poor people, release, sight, and freedom. Meals, prayer, wealth, women, outsiders, and the road to Jerusalem shape discipleship.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Gospel narrative with songs, teaching, parable, healing, travel account, passion, and resurrection.
- **Primary setting:** Galilee, the long journey to Jerusalem, and Jerusalem itself.
- **Central movement:** Spirit-filled promise becomes a boundary-crossing ministry, a cross, resurrection, and preparation for worldwide witness.
- **People to know:** Jesus, Mary, Zechariah and Elizabeth, John, the Twelve, women disciples, Samaritans, tax collectors, poor and wealthy people.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 2½–3 hours.

## What Is Going On?

Luke is the first volume of Luke–Acts. The Gospel ends in Jerusalem; Acts begins there and moves outward. Luke foregrounds people marginalized by status, gender, illness, reputation, or ethnicity without romanticizing poverty itself.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapters 1–4:** Births, songs, preparation, testing, and Jesus' Nazareth mission statement.
2. **Chapters 5–9:** Calling, healing, teaching, meals, conflict, and recognition.
3. **Chapters 9–19:** The journey to Jerusalem gathers distinctive parables and discipleship teaching.
4. **Chapters 19–24:** Jerusalem ministry, supper, cross, resurrection, and opened understanding.

## Watch For

- Prayer before decisive moments.
- The Holy Spirit and joy.
- Tables revealing inclusion and economic change.
- Questions reversed so the questioner must act.

## A Thread to Carry

Luke shows salvation becoming visible in restored bodies, shared meals, released possessions, welcomed outsiders, forgiven sinners, and communities made capable of witness.
