# Matthew book guide

## Begin Here

Matthew presents Jesus as Israel's Messiah, God's Son, authoritative teacher, healer, crucified king, and risen Lord. The story begins with Abraham and David, repeatedly says events fulfill Scripture, and gathers Jesus' teaching into large blocks. Disciples learn the kingdom through mercy, justice, forgiveness, costly following, and life together.

## At a Glance

- **Kind of writing:** Gospel narrative with genealogy, teaching, parable, miracle, conflict, passion, and resurrection.
- **Primary setting:** Galilee and Judea under Roman rule, culminating in Jerusalem.
- **Central movement:** Jesus announces and embodies heaven's kingdom, forms disciples, gives his life, rises, and commissions worldwide teaching.
- **People to know:** Jesus, Mary and Joseph, John the Baptist, the Twelve, women disciples, crowds, opponents, rulers, and outsiders who recognize faith.
- **Time to read aloud:** About 2½–3 hours.

## What Is Going On?

Matthew is deeply Jewish and often stages family arguments about Torah, authority, and Israel's hope. Polemical references to leaders must not become hostility toward Jewish people. Jesus intensifies righteousness toward reconciled relationships and repeatedly centers the “weightier matters” of justice, mercy, and faithfulness.

## The Book's Movement

1. **Chapters 1–4:** Origins, testing, kingdom announcement, and first disciples.
2. **Chapters 5–10:** Mountain teaching, healings, and mission.
3. **Chapters 11–18:** Responses, parables, Peter's confession, cross-shaped discipleship, and community life.
4. **Chapters 19–25:** Journey to Jerusalem, confrontation, and teaching about readiness.
5. **Chapters 26–28:** Supper, arrest, crucifixion, resurrection, and commission.

## Watch For

- Jesus as “God with us” from beginning to end.
- Fulfillment as continuity, transformation, and completion.
- Kingdom greatness redefined around children and service.
- Forgiveness joined to accountable communal practice.

## A Thread to Carry

Matthew's risen Jesus sends disciples to teach a practiced way of life. Authority serves presence and formation rather than domination.
