# Genesis 1

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Genesis opens with God, a dark and unformed world, deep waters, and God's Spirit moving over them. The chapter does not begin by answering every modern question about matter and time. It announces who creates and portrays creation becoming ordered, inhabited, blessed, and very good.

### Chapter Path

- **1:1–5:** Light and darkness; day and night.
- **1:6–13:** Sky, seas, dry land, and vegetation.
- **1:14–23:** Lights for times and seasons; creatures of sea and sky.
- **1:24–31:** Land creatures and humanity in God's image; blessing, vocation, provision, and “very good.”

### Watch For

Listen for repetition: God says, creation responds, God sees goodness, and evening and morning mark the days. Notice the pattern of forming realms and then filling them.

## Read the Chapter

Read Genesis 1 in the Fluent Translation. Reading aloud helps the patterned language become visible to the ear.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What conditions are present at the beginning? What does God separate, name, bless, and call good? What task is given to humanity?

### The Chapter in Brief

God speaks light into darkness and orders creation through a sequence of days. Sky, sea, land, vegetation, lights, and living creatures receive their places and purposes. God creates humankind, male and female, in the divine image and blesses them with responsibility within the living world. God provides food and sees the whole as very good.

### Follow the Story

Creation emerges through God's purposeful speech. The sun and moon are not named as deities; they are lights serving times and seasons. Human beings are distinguished by bearing God's image, but they remain creatures created on the same day as land animals and dependent on God's provision.

“Rule” and “subdue” grant real authority. Because the rulers bear the Creator's image and the creation is God's good work, this vocation should reflect God's life-giving order rather than permission to destroy.

### Threads Through Scripture

Later Scripture returns to creation when speaking of worship, wisdom, human dignity, Sabbath, new creation, and God's care for all creatures. The image of God grounds the worth of every human life, while Revelation's renewed world brings creation's story toward healing and communion.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which repeated phrases organize the chapter?
- **Understand:** What does God's naming and blessing communicate about creation?
- **Connect:** How does shared divine image challenge rankings of human worth?
- **Respond:** What practice would exercise human authority as care rather than consumption?

### Prayer and Practice

Spend time attentively with one part of creation without using it. Give thanks for its goodness and choose one action that protects the conditions in which living things flourish.

## Go Deeper

### How to Read This

Christians hold several views about how Genesis 1 relates to scientific accounts and the length of the days. The chapter's theological claims do not depend on pretending those questions are simple: the one God creates, creation is ordered and good, humans bear God's image, and authority carries responsibility.

### Wrestle with This

“Male and female” names both as God's image-bearers; it does not make one a lesser image. Readers differ on how this verse relates to later questions of sex and gender. At minimum, no human hierarchy can claim that men alone represent God.

### Further Study

Read Psalm 104 and Revelation 21:1–5. Compare how creation, divine presence, provision, and renewal are described.
