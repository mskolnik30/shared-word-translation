### Follow the Movement

The order of Leviticus 20 matters. Its movement through Molech and Forbidden Practices, Be Holy, Penalties for Forbidden Sexual Relations, and finally You Will Be Set Apart controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 6 observes: Turning to mediums is described with the same prostitution metaphor used for idolatrous worship elsewhere in Leviticus. The translation note at verse 9–21 observes: The chapter moves from prohibitions stated in chapter 18 to specified penalties. The translation preserves the legal severity without attempting to harmonize these penalties with later legal or theological systems. One vocabulary entry at verse 3 identifies חִלֵּל (chillel) “To profane.” To treat what is holy as common or defiled.

For Leviticus 20: Keep the chapter within Israel's narrated covenant life, allowing its legal, historical, and theological tensions to remain visible. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
