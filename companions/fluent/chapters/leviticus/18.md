# Leviticus 18

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Leviticus 17–26 is often called the Holiness Code because it repeatedly connects Israel's conduct with the holiness of the LORD. Chapter 18 addresses sexual and family boundaries in an ancient kinship society. Some verses have become points of modern conflict. Read the whole chapter, attend to its world, and do not use isolated lines to target or demean people.

### Chapter Path

- **18:1–5:** Israel must not simply copy Egypt or Canaan but live by God's instruction.
- **18:6–18:** A series of prohibitions protects boundaries within extended households and kinship relations.
- **18:19–23:** Additional prohibited relations include adultery, giving offspring to Molech, male same-sex intercourse, and sex with animals.
- **18:24–30:** The chapter warns that such practices defile people and land.

### Watch For

Notice that the commands address a community, not merely isolated private choices. “Uncover nakedness” is an ancient idiom for sexual relations. The household relationships named reveal how sex, authority, inheritance, and family stability were intertwined.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

How does the chapter frame Israel's distinct calling? What relationships and boundaries does it seek to order?

### The Chapter in Brief

God calls Israel to a way of life distinct from the societies surrounding them. A long sequence forbids sexual relations within close kinship structures and other practices presented as violations of covenant holiness. The conclusion says these acts defile both people and land and warns Israel not to repeat the practices attributed to the land's former inhabitants.

### Follow the Story

The chapter begins and ends with identity: “I am the LORD your God.” Israel's ethic is grounded in belonging to the God who delivered them. Verse 5 says the person who does these statutes will “live by them,” a phrase later biblical writers revisit. Here, obedience is presented as a life-giving order for the covenant community.

Most of the chapter protects kinship boundaries in crowded extended households, where unequal power could be disguised as family access. The text assumes an ancient patriarchal structure and does not answer every modern ethical question. Its commands should not be flattened into a complete contemporary sexual ethic without the wider witness of Scripture, careful interpretation, and attention to those affected.

Verse 22 has played a major role in debates about same-sex relationships. Christians disagree about how this ancient prohibition relates to sexual orientation, covenantal same-sex unions, and the church today. Whatever conclusion a community reaches, the chapter gives no permission for contempt, bullying, coercion, or violence against LGBTQ people. Interpretation must remain joined to truthfulness, humility, and love of neighbor.

### Threads Through Scripture

The land “vomiting out” its inhabitants personifies the moral consequences of communal corruption. Later prophets similarly join worship, sexual wrongdoing, exploitation, and violence in their indictments. Jesus places love of God and neighbor at the center of faithful obedience; the apostles repeatedly call communities to holiness expressed through bodies and mutual honor.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** How often does the chapter name relationships rather than abstract acts?
- **Understand:** Why would household power matter when reading these boundaries?
- **Connect:** What helps a community discuss contested texts without turning people into arguments?
- **Respond:** How can you practice conviction and humility together?

### Prayer and Practice

Ask God to make your relationships truthful, non-exploitative, and honoring. If this chapter touches personal harm or rejection, choose a safe person or qualified caregiver rather than carrying it alone.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

The identity of the practice associated with Molech is debated, but the verse clearly prohibits handing offspring over in a way that profanes God's name. The final warnings use the language of ritual and moral defilement together.

### Wrestle with This

How can readers take an ancient holiness command seriously without pretending that cultural distance does not exist—or weaponizing the text against selected neighbors?

### Further Study

Read Leviticus 19 next. Its commands about worship, labor, disability, courts, immigrants, enemies, and love of neighbor show the breadth of holiness.
