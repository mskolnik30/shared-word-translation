# Malachi 3

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

The people have asked where the God of justice is. Malachi answers with a coming messenger and a divine arrival that purifies worshipers and confronts exploitation. The chapter then disputes withheld tithes and the claim that serving God has no value.

### Chapter Path

- **3:1–5:** A messenger prepares the way; the Lord comes to refine worship and testify against personal, economic, and social wrongdoing.
- **3:6–12:** God's unchanged covenant commitment preserves Jacob; the people are called to return through full contributions supporting God's house.
- **3:13–18:** Cynics call arrogance profitable, while those who fear God speak together and are remembered as a treasured possession.

### Watch For

The refining image begins with religious leadership and leads directly to justice for workers, widows, orphans, and aliens. Purified worship is measured in part by how vulnerable neighbors are treated.

Read Malachi 3 in the Fluent Translation. Notice who is purified, who is protected, and which different kinds of speech God hears.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What makes the coming difficult to endure, what forms of injustice receive judgment, and why is a book of remembrance written?

### The Chapter in Brief

God sends a messenger to prepare the way before a sudden coming to the temple. Like fire refining metal and soap cleaning cloth, the Lord purifies the sons of Levi so offerings may again be righteous. God also becomes a swift witness against sorcery, adultery, false oaths, withheld wages, and the exclusion of widows, orphans, and aliens. Because the LORD does not change, Jacob is not consumed and may return. The people have withheld tithes needed for God's house; they are invited to bring them fully and see whether agricultural blessing follows. Another dispute concerns arrogant people who prosper. Those who fear God speak together, are heard, and are recorded as God's treasured possession. A future day will reveal what presently seems hidden.

### Follow the Story

Refining is demanding but purposeful: removing corruption so worship can serve its covenant purpose. The image should not be used to romanticize abuse, illness, or trauma as a divinely assigned method of personal improvement.

The judgment list moves beyond private spirituality. Fraudulent speech, wage theft, and pushing aside people without social protection all count as failure to fear God. The alien is the resident outsider who can easily be denied standing.

The tithe promise belongs to Judah's covenant economy and temple storehouse. It should not become a prosperity formula by which religious leaders pressure poor people to give money in exchange for guaranteed wealth. The stated purpose includes food in God's house and the flourishing of the land as a community.

The book of remembrance answers people who think faithful service disappears unnoticed. God listens not only to accusations but also to quiet conversation among those seeking faithfulness together.

### Threads Through Scripture

The messenger language is taken up in the Gospels' descriptions of John the Baptist. Torah repeatedly joins worship with fair wages and protection for widows, orphans, and resident aliens. James condemns wages withheld from laborers.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which injustices appear beside corrupt worship?
- **Understand:** Why does God's unchanging character make return possible?
- **Connect:** How can giving appeals become coercive or exploit people with little money?
- **Respond:** Whose labor or belonging needs concrete protection in your community?

### Prayer and Practice

Check one payment, contract, benevolence policy, or giving appeal for fairness. Correct pressure, delay, or exclusion where you have responsibility.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

The temple storehouse supported worship and communal obligations within ancient Judah. The chapter's agricultural promise is not a universal guarantee that individual donors will become wealthy.

### Wrestle with This

What would it mean for worship to be refined until economic justice and welcome become visible within it?
