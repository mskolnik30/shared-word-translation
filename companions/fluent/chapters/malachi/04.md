# Malachi 4

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Malachi closes with the coming day of the LORD. Fire for arrogant evildoers is contrasted with sunrise, healing, freedom, and joy for those who fear God's name. The final words look backward to Moses and forward to Elijah's work of turning generations toward one another.

### Chapter Path

- **4:1–3:** The coming day burns like a furnace, yet the sun of righteousness rises with healing for those who fear God's name.
- **4:4:** Israel is commanded to remember the Torah given through Moses at Horeb.
- **4:5–6:** Elijah is promised before the fearful day to turn parents and children toward one another and avert destruction.

### Watch For

The chapter ends not with a timetable but with remembered instruction and repaired relationships. Turning hearts cannot mean forcing unsafe contact with an abusive relative.

## Read the Chapter

Read Malachi 4 in the Fluent Translation. Hold together its contrasting images of furnace and sunrise, stubble and healing, judgment and reconciliation.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What different experiences does the coming day bring, and what two forms of preparation close the book?

### The Chapter in Brief

A day is coming when arrogant people and evildoers will be consumed like dry stubble, leaving neither root nor branch. For those who fear God's name, however, the sun of righteousness rises with healing in its wings, and they leap with the freedom of calves released from confinement. The wicked become ashes beneath their feet. Israel is told to remember the instruction given through Moses at Horeb. God will send Elijah before the great and fearful day to turn parents' hearts toward children and children's hearts toward parents, so the land will not be struck with a decree of destruction.

### Follow the Movement

Fire and sunrise both describe the same approaching divine day from different positions. The text refuses the idea that entrenched arrogance and wrongdoing continue forever, while promising healing and open movement to people who honor God.

The image of treading ashes is severe victory language. It should not be used to authorize humiliation, vigilantism, or delight in another person's suffering. God acts in the chapter; readers are not commissioned to decide whom they may burn or trample.

Remembering Torah means more than retaining information. Malachi has already connected teaching with impartial leadership, faithful relationships, fair wages, care for vulnerable people, and truthful worship. Memory becomes embodied covenant practice.

Elijah's work is relational turning across generations. Reconciliation may include truth, repentance, protection, boundaries, and long processes. No survivor should be told that honoring this verse requires returning to danger or granting access to someone who remains abusive.

### Threads Through Scripture

The Gospels connect Elijah's promised work with John the Baptist while also preserving questions about Elijah's coming. Luke echoes the turning of generations in its opening chapter. Moses and Elijah later appear together in the transfiguration narratives.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** How do the furnace and sunrise images contrast?
- **Understand:** Why do Moses and Elijah stand at the book's close?
- **Connect:** What is the difference between reconciliation and forced access?
- **Respond:** What truthful, safe step could help one generational relationship turn toward understanding?

### Prayer and Practice

Pray for healing between generations. Take one safe step—listening, apologizing, establishing a boundary, or seeking qualified help—without pressuring another person to reconcile on your timetable.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

Horeb is another name for Sinai. “Healing in its wings” uses the sun's spreading rays as an image. Christian ordering places Malachi last in the Old Testament, while the Hebrew Bible has a different book order; the chapter's canonical position therefore functions differently across traditions.

### Wrestle with This

How can hope for restored relationships remain accountable to truth, changed behavior, and the safety of those harmed?
