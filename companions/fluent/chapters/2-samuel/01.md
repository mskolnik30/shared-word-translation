### Follow the Movement

The order of 2 Samuel 1 matters. Its movement through David Learns of Saul and Jonathan’s Death, David Mourns Saul and Jonathan, The Amalekite Is Put to Death, and finally David’s Lament over Saul and Jonathan controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 9 observes: “agony” translates a rare Hebrew term whose exact sense is uncertain; it may describe dizziness, anguish, or convulsion. The translation note at verse 12 observes: David’s mourning includes Saul, Jonathan, the people of the LORD, and the whole house of Israel. Personal rivalry does not erase Saul’s covenantal place in David’s grief. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies עָפָר (afar) “Dirt” or “dust.” Placed on the head as a sign of mourning.

For 2 Samuel 1: Keep the chapter within Israel's narrated covenant life, allowing its legal, historical, and theological tensions to remain visible. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
