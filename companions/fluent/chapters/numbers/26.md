### Follow the Movement

The order of Numbers 26 matters. Its movement through The Second Census, Reuben, Simeon, Gad, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun, Joseph: Manasseh and Ephraim, Benjamin, Dan, Asher, Naphtali, The Land to Be Divided, The Levites Counted, and finally A New Generation controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 10–11 observes: Korah’s rebellion becomes a “sign,” yet the explicit notice that Korah’s sons did not die prevents the judgment from being read as total erasure of his line. The translation note at verse 33 observes: Zelophehad’s daughters are named within the census before their inheritance case is heard in chapter 27, integrating them into the land-allotment narrative. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies פָּקַד (paqad) “To count,” “appoint,” or “attend to.” Here used for the census.

For Numbers 26: Keep the chapter within Israel's narrated covenant life, allowing its legal, historical, and theological tensions to remain visible. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
