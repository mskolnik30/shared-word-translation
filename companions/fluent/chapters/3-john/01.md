# 3 John 1

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

The elder writes to Gaius within a network of churches and travelers. Hospitality is not background courtesy; it sustains people who have gone out for the sake of the Name. A leader named Diotrephes is trying to control that network.

### Chapter Path

- **1:1–4:** Greeting, prayer for well-being, and joy in Gaius's truthful life.
- **1:5–8:** Gaius faithfully supports strangers and becomes a fellow worker for truth.
- **1:9–12:** Diotrephes rejects accountability and hospitality; Demetrius receives good testimony.
- **1:13–15:** The elder hopes to speak face to face and sends personal greetings.

### Watch For

Compare Gaius and Diotrephes by what happens to other people around them. One equips; the other blocks, slanders, and expels.

## Read the Chapter

Read 3 John in the Fluent Translation. Mark each reference to testimony, truth, welcome, and imitation.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

Why does the elder praise Gaius? What does Diotrephes do? How is Demetrius commended?

### The Chapter in Brief

The elder greets Gaius and celebrates that he walks in truth. Gaius acts faithfully by caring for traveling brothers and sisters, even strangers, and sending them onward in a manner worthy of God. Such support makes him a fellow worker for truth. Diotrephes, who loves to be first, rejects the elder, spreads malicious talk, refuses travelers, prevents others from welcoming them, and removes dissenters from the church. Gaius should imitate good rather than evil, and Demetrius comes with strong testimony. The elder hopes for face-to-face conversation and ends with peace and greetings by name.

### Follow the Story

The letter makes partnership tangible. Gaius does not travel with the messengers, yet his resources make their work possible. Support is participation, not passive agreement.

Diotrephes's failure is a pattern of leadership abuse: desire for preeminence, rejection of accountability, damaging speech, control of resources, and exclusion of people who disagree. The elder plans to call attention to the behavior publicly rather than treat it as a personality difference.

Demetrius is not commended by a single powerful sponsor. Everyone, the truth itself, and the elder's group provide converging testimony.

### Threads Through Scripture

Biblical hospitality welcomes strangers and equips servants of God. Jesus teaches that greatness is found in service rather than status. The prophets and wisdom writings also insist that speech, treatment of outsiders, and use of authority reveal character.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which actions make Gaius and the travelers fellow workers?
- **Understand:** Why is the desire “to be first” dangerous when joined to control over belonging?
- **Connect:** What signs distinguish accountability from a struggle for status?
- **Respond:** Who can you equip without needing to control their work or receive credit?

### Prayer and Practice

Offer concrete support to a trustworthy person doing good work: funds, food, lodging, introductions, or encouragement. If a leader uses retaliation or expulsion to silence concerns, document patterns and seek safe, independent counsel.

## Go Deeper

### Wrestle with This

Not every leadership disagreement is abuse, but the letter names a cluster of observable actions. Responsible evaluation focuses on conduct and impact rather than diagnosing hidden motives beyond the text.

### What Needs Context

Traveling Christian workers often depended on households and congregations. Sending them “in a manner worthy of God” likely included provisions for the next stage of travel.

### Further Study

Read Mark 10:42–45 and 1 Peter 5:1–5. Compare their models of authority with Diotrephes's conduct.
