# James 4

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

James has contrasted wisdom from above with envy and selfish ambition. Chapter 4 traces community conflict back to disordered desires and then calls readers toward humility, repentance, careful judgment, and plans that acknowledge life's limits.

### Chapter Path

- **4:1–5:** Desires at war produce conflict and distorted prayer.
- **4:6–10:** God gives greater grace; submit, resist, draw near, and humble yourselves.
- **4:11–12:** Do not speak against or judge a sibling.
- **4:13–17:** Merchants make confident plans without acknowledging their fragile lives or responsibility to do good.

### Watch For

Notice how “greater grace” stands at the center of severe language. James does not call for humility because God is unwilling to receive people; he calls for return because God gives grace to the humble.

## Read the Chapter

Read James 4 in the Fluent Translation. Trace the movement from conflict to grace and from arrogant plans to humble action.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

Where does James locate the source of quarrels? What promises surround the call to repentance? What is wrong with the merchants' speech?

### The Chapter in Brief

James says conflict grows from cravings that demand satisfaction. Some prayers are distorted by the same desire to consume. Friendship with the world's status-seeking ways competes with loyalty to God, but God gives greater grace. Readers should draw near, become honest about divided hearts, and humble themselves. They must not claim God's place by condemning one another. They also should not boast as if business plans and tomorrow were under their control; life is a mist, and knowing the good creates responsibility to do it.

### Follow the Story

James' diagnosis is searching, but it should not be used to tell every harmed person that conflict is equally their fault. Elsewhere he names exploiters directly. Here he addresses communities whose envy and ambition create rivalry.

Humility means truthful dependence, not self-hatred. “Draw near to God” is met by “God will draw near to you.” The call to lament refuses shallow cheerfulness about wrongdoing, while the promise of exaltation belongs to God rather than self-promotion.

### Threads Through Scripture

Israel's prophets often described idolatry and unjust alliances as unfaithfulness in a covenant relationship. Proverbs warns against boasting about tomorrow. Jesus likewise teaches dependence on God for daily bread and warns against anxious or self-secure accumulation.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which commands describe turning toward God, and which describe turning from harm?
- **Understand:** How does greater grace change the tone of the chapter's call to repentance?
- **Connect:** What desires most often distort conflict in groups—recognition, control, security, or being proved right?
- **Respond:** What good do you already know to do but keep postponing?

### Prayer and Practice

Hold your plans with open hands. Name what you intend, what you cannot control, and one good action that should not wait. If conflict involves abuse or danger, humility may require telling the truth and seeking protection rather than quiet submission to harm.

## Go Deeper

### How to Read This

“Friendship with the world” does not mean hatred of creation or withdrawal from neighbors. In James, “world” here names an order driven by envy, self-indulgence, rivalry, and pride.

### Wrestle with This

The warning against judging does not forbid moral discernment, accountability, or legal protection. James himself evaluates conduct throughout the letter. It forbids speaking from a posture that seizes God's authority and reduces a neighbor to condemnation.

### Further Study

Compare James 4:13–17 with Proverbs 27:1 and Jesus' parable in Luke 12:13–21. What kinds of control do the speakers imagine they possess?
