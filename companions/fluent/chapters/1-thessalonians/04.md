# 1 Thessalonians 4

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Timothy has returned with encouraging news about the young church. Paul now turns from thanksgiving and relationship to instructions about holy bodies, mutual love, quiet work, grief, and Christ's coming.

### Chapter Path

- **4:1–8:** God's will is holiness; sexual conduct must practice self-control and refuse exploitation.
- **4:9–12:** God-taught love should keep increasing through quiet life, ordinary work, and responsible relationships.
- **4:13–18:** Grieving believers receive resurrection hope: the dead in Christ will not be left behind when the Lord comes.

### Watch For

Paul does not forbid grief. He distinguishes grief shaped by hope from grief without resurrection hope. The climax is relational: “we will always be with the Lord.”

## Read the Chapter

Read 1 Thessalonians 4 in the Fluent Translation. Notice holiness, love, work, grief, and hope as one shared life.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

How does holiness protect another person, and what fear does Paul's teaching about the dead address?

### The Chapter in Brief

Paul urges believers to keep pleasing God. Holiness includes abstaining from sexual immorality, governing one's body honorably, and refusing to exploit another member. Mutual love has been taught by God and should abound more through quiet, responsible work. Paul then addresses concern for believers who have died. Because Jesus died and rose, God will gather the dead with him. At the Lord's commanding arrival, the dead rise first and the living join them to meet the Lord. Therefore the community should comfort one another.

### Follow the Story

Sexual ethics are communal, not merely private rule-keeping. Verse 6 names transgression as taking advantage of a sibling. Desire becomes unholy when another person is treated as available for use.

“Make it your ambition to live quietly” redirects status-seeking toward ordinary faithfulness. Work supports responsible public witness but should not become contempt for people unable to work or a denial of later communal sharing.

The meeting imagery may evoke people going out to welcome an arriving ruler and accompanying him. Paul does not specify a detailed travel itinerary. His purpose is pastoral: dead believers are not excluded, and the whole community will be with Christ.

### Threads Through Scripture

Jesus' resurrection grounds Paul's hope. Daniel speaks of sleepers awakening; the Gospels picture the Son of Man gathering people. Paul develops bodily resurrection more fully in 1 Corinthians 15.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** How does each section concern other people?
- **Understand:** What false fear does “the dead rise first” correct?
- **Connect:** How can hope accompany grief without silencing it?
- **Respond:** Who needs comfort that makes room for tears?

### Prayer and Practice

Remember a person who has died by naming their life rather than rushing to explanation. Offer steady presence to someone grieving.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

The word translated “body” literally means vessel and may also have been understood as wife; the surrounding language favors self-governance. “Meet” often describes welcoming an arriving dignitary.

### Wrestle with This

How does resurrection hope change grief without asking people to grieve less?

### Further Study

Read Daniel 12:1–3, 1 Corinthians 15:20–28, and 1 Thessalonians 5:1–11.
