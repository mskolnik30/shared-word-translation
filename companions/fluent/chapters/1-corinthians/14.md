# 1 Corinthians 14

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Paul has described one body with many Spirit-given gifts and placed love at the center. Chapter 14 applies that ordering to gathered worship, especially tongues and prophecy. The repeated measure is whether other people are built up through intelligible participation.

### Chapter Path

- **14:1–5:** Pursue love and desire gifts; prophecy is preferred in public because people understand it.
- **14:6–19:** Instruments and languages illustrate why clarity matters; Paul wants spirit and mind engaged together.
- **14:20–25:** Tongues and prophecy affect outsiders differently; intelligible disclosure can lead to worship.
- **14:26–33:** Each participant may bring something, but speech is limited, interpreted, and discerned for peace.
- **14:34–40:** A disputed restriction on women's speech, apostolic authority, and the closing command for order.

### Watch For

Earlier Paul assumes women pray and prophesy. Interpret verses 34–35 in conversation with 11:5 and the chapter's setting rather than erasing women's speech throughout the letter.

## Read the Chapter

Read 1 Corinthians 14 in the Fluent Translation. Count how often Paul names understanding, interpretation, edification, learning, and peace.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

Why does Paul prefer prophecy in the gathering, and what limits does he place on every speaker?

### The Chapter in Brief

Paul urges the church to pursue love and seek gifts, especially prophecy, because understandable speech strengthens, encourages, and consoles others. Tongues address God unless interpreted; in public, Paul would rather speak a few intelligible words. Prophecy may expose a visitor's heart and lead to worship. Gatherings include contributions from many people, but only a limited number speak in tongues or prophecy, interpretation is required, others discern, and speakers remain responsible for self-control. Everything should be done decently and in order.

### Follow the Story

Paul does not oppose ecstatic prayer. He prays in tongues and gives thanks for the gift. His question is communal: who can receive it? “Build up” turns giftedness away from self-display and toward shared formation.

Order is participatory, not silent centralization. “Each one” may bring a song, teaching, revelation, tongue, or interpretation. Limits keep strong voices from filling all available space and make room for discernment.

Verses 34–35 have major interpretive difficulties. They may address disruptive questioning, evaluation of prophecies, a local practice, or a quoted Corinthian position; some manuscripts place them elsewhere. Christian communities disagree about their scope. What is clear is that Paul has already recognized women praying and prophesying, so the lines cannot responsibly mean women have no vocal role whatsoever.

### Threads Through Scripture

Pentecost presents Spirit speech understood across languages. Joel's sons and daughters prophesy. The chapter's concern for peace reflects God's character and Paul's wider call for the body to honor every member.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which speakers are limited or evaluated?
- **Understand:** How does love make intelligibility a spiritual concern?
- **Connect:** Whose gifts disappear when a few people occupy the gathering?
- **Respond:** What could make your community's worship more understandable and participatory?

### Prayer and Practice

Ask one newer participant what felt clear or confusing in worship. Make one change that helps people join rather than merely observe.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

Tongues may involve human or Spirit-given languages; Paul focuses on their public intelligibility rather than defining the phenomenon. Prophecy is spoken and weighed by others, not treated as unquestionable private authority.

### Wrestle with This

How can worship welcome surprising gifts while protecting shared understanding, peace, and accountable discernment?

### Further Study

Read Joel 2:28–29, Acts 2, 1 Corinthians 11:2–16, and chapters 12–13.
