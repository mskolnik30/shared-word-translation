# 1 Corinthians 1

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

This opening chapter begins 1 Corinthians’ 16-chapter movement. The chapter is organized around Greeting, Thanksgiving, Divisions in the Church, Christ the Power and Wisdom of God, and finally God’s Choice of the Lowly. Read it as one movement before separating individual lines for closer study.

- **1:1–3:** Greeting.
- **1:4–9:** Thanksgiving.
- **1:10–17:** Divisions in the Church.
- **1:18–25:** Christ the Power and Wisdom of God.

### The Chapter in Brief

1 Corinthians 1 moves through Greeting; then Thanksgiving; then Divisions in the Church; then Christ the Power and Wisdom of God; and the remaining movement leads to God’s Choice of the Lowly. These headings provide the chapter’s visible architecture. The details within them show how the chapter develops its claims rather than presenting a collection of detached sayings. The ending at “God’s Choice of the Lowly” should therefore be heard in relation to the opening at “Greeting.”

### Follow the Movement

The order of 1 Corinthians 1 matters. Its movement through Greeting, Thanksgiving, Divisions in the Church, Christ the Power and Wisdom of God, and finally God’s Choice of the Lowly controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 2 observes: The church is addressed both as already “made holy” and as “called” into holiness. Paul holds status and vocation together without collapsing one into the other. The translation note at verse 3 observes: Grace precedes peace. The order reflects Paul’s conviction that reconciliation and wholeness flow from divine gift, not human effort. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies κλητός (klētos) “Called.” The term denotes divine initiative rather than human qualification. Paul’s identity begins in response, not achievement.

For 1 Corinthians 1: Follow the argument across the whole letter, remembering that a sentence addresses a particular community and should not be isolated from its surrounding appeal. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
