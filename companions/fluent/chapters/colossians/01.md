# Colossians 1

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

This opening chapter begins Colossians’ 4-chapter movement. The chapter is organized around Greeting and Thanksgiving, A Prayer for Knowledge and Fruitful Living, The Son before All Things, Reconciled through Christ’s Death, and finally Christ in You, the Hope of Glory. Read it as one movement before separating individual lines for closer study.

- **1:1–8:** Greeting and Thanksgiving.
- **1:9–14:** A Prayer for Knowledge and Fruitful Living.
- **1:15–20:** The Son before All Things.
- **1:21–23:** Reconciled through Christ’s Death.
- **1:24–29:** Christ in You, the Hope of Glory.

### The Chapter in Brief

Colossians 1 moves through Greeting and Thanksgiving; then A Prayer for Knowledge and Fruitful Living; then The Son before All Things; then Reconciled through Christ’s Death; and the remaining movement leads to Christ in You, the Hope of Glory. These headings provide the chapter’s visible architecture. The details within them show how the chapter develops its claims rather than presenting a collection of detached sayings. The ending at “Christ in You, the Hope of Glory” should therefore be heard in relation to the opening at “Greeting and Thanksgiving.”

### Follow the Movement

The order of Colossians 1 matters. Its movement through Greeting and Thanksgiving, A Prayer for Knowledge and Fruitful Living, The Son before All Things, Reconciled through Christ’s Death, and finally Christ in You, the Hope of Glory controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 9–10 observes: Knowledge of God’s will is ordered toward a worthy walk and good fruit, not speculation detached from practice. The translation note at verse 13 observes: Rescue from darkness and transfer into the Son’s kingdom use liberation and political-realm imagery together. One vocabulary entry at verse 5 identifies ἐλπίς (elpis) “Hope.”
