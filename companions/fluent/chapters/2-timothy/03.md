# 2 Timothy 3

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Timothy has been urged to entrust teaching to faithful people, endure hardship, avoid useless quarrels, and correct opponents gently. Chapter 3 warns about religious appearance without transforming power and tells Timothy to continue in learned Scripture and embodied example.

### Chapter Path

- **3:1–5:** Difficult last-days patterns culminate in the appearance of godliness without its power.
- **3:6–9:** Exploitative teachers target burdened women; like Jannes and Jambres, their folly will be exposed.
- **3:10–13:** Timothy has followed Paul's teaching, purpose, conduct, love, endurance, and suffering.
- **3:14–17:** Childhood Scriptures make wise for salvation through trust in Christ; God-breathed Scripture equips every good work.

### Watch For

The vice list targets corruption within professing religion, not simply “bad people out there.” The women described are exploited by teachers; the passage must not become a claim that women are naturally gullible.

## Read the Chapter

Read 2 Timothy 3 in the Fluent Translation. Notice how doctrine, conduct, relationships, suffering, Scripture, and good work remain connected.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What kind of danger does religious appearance conceal, and what has Timothy actually learned to continue?

### The Chapter in Brief

The last days include self-love, money-love, boastfulness, brutality, betrayal, and pleasure-love covered by godly appearance. Exploitative teachers enter households and manipulate vulnerable learners but will be exposed. Timothy has followed not only Paul's teaching but his purpose, trust, patience, love, endurance, persecution, and rescue. He must continue in what he learned from trustworthy people and sacred writings. Every God-breathed Scripture is useful for teaching, correction, and training so God's person is equipped for every good work.

### Follow the Story

“Last days” can describe the era already underway, not only a final short countdown. The warning therefore forms enduring discernment rather than headline panic.

Jannes and Jambres are traditional names for Pharaoh's magicians, not names found in Exodus. The letter freely draws on a known interpretive tradition while distinguishing it from the sacred writings Timothy knew from childhood—the Jewish Scriptures.

“God-breathed” names Scripture's divine source but does not by itself supply a complete technical theory of inspiration. The chapter emphasizes purpose: Scripture makes wise toward salvation and equips practiced good. Interpretation that produces arrogance or exploitation contradicts that aim.

### Threads Through Scripture

The magicians recall Exodus 7–9. Psalm 119 joins God's word to practiced paths, and Jesus judges teaching by fruit. Timothy's faith came through Lois and Eunice, showing Scripture learned through relationship.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** What sits beneath the appearance of godliness?
- **Understand:** What kinds of evidence make teaching trustworthy here?
- **Connect:** How can Scripture knowledge be separated from Scripture's intended good work?
- **Respond:** Which learned truth needs embodied practice?

### Prayer and Practice

Choose one passage you know well and name the good work it should equip this week. Ask who taught it to you through their life.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

“All Scripture” can grammatically mean every Scripture or Scripture as a whole; the traditional collective reading fits the practical emphasis. Persecution is expected broadly, not promised in identical form to every believer.

### Wrestle with This

What practices protect a community from teachers whose religious appearance hides exploitation?
