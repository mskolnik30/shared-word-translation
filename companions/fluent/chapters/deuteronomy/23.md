### Follow the Movement

The order of Deuteronomy 23 matters. Its movement through Those Excluded from the Assembly, Edomites and Egyptians, Holiness in the Camp, Do Not Return an Escaped Servant, No Cult Prostitution, and finally Interest, Vows, and Neighbor’s Produce controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 2 observes: The Hebrew term mamzer is difficult and may refer to a person born from a prohibited union or another socially excluded lineage. The translation uses the broader “forbidden union.” The translation note at verse 3–6 observes: The exclusion of Ammon and Moab is grounded in remembered hostility during Israel’s wilderness journey, especially the Balaam episode. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies קָהָל יְהוָה (qahal YHWH) “Assembly of the LORD.” The covenantal public community.

For Deuteronomy 23: Keep the chapter within Israel's narrated covenant life, allowing its legal, historical, and theological tensions to remain visible. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
