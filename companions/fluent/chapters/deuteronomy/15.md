### Follow the Movement

The order of Deuteronomy 15 matters. Its movement through The Year of Release, There Should Be No Poor among You, Open Your Hand to the Poor, Release Hebrew Servants in the Seventh Year, and finally Consecrate the Firstborn Males controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 7 observes: Hard heart and closed hand are paired. Economic refusal is treated as a spiritual condition. The translation note at verse 9 observes: The approaching release year must not become an excuse to stop lending to the poor. The law protects access to credit precisely when repayment seems uncertain. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies שְׁמִטָּה (shemittah) “Release.” The seventh-year remission of claims.

For Deuteronomy 15: Keep the chapter within Israel's narrated covenant life, allowing its legal, historical, and theological tensions to remain visible. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
