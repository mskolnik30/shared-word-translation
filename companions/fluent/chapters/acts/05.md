# Acts 5

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

The Spirit-filled Jerusalem community shares resources so that needs are met. Barnabas has sold land openly. Acts 5 begins with a deceptive gift and immediate deaths, then moves to healing, arrest, civil disobedience, restraint from Gamaliel, beating, and continued witness.

### Chapter Path

- **5:1–11:** Ananias and Sapphira misrepresent a land gift and die after separate confrontations.
- **5:12–16:** Signs, healings, attraction, and reverent distance accompany growth.
- **5:17–32:** Leaders imprison the apostles; an angel releases them; they return to teach and declare obedience to God.
- **5:33–42:** Gamaliel urges restraint; the apostles are beaten, rejoice in bearing dishonor, and continue teaching.

### Watch For

Peter explicitly says the property and proceeds were under the couple's authority. The offense is deception, not refusal to surrender compulsory communal property.

## Read the Chapter

Read Acts 5 in the Fluent Translation. Keep generosity, truth, authority, judgment, and coercion distinct.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What did Ananias and Sapphira represent falsely, and how do the apostles respond when human and divine commands conflict?

### The Chapter in Brief

Ananias and Sapphira keep part of a land sale while claiming a fuller gift. Peter identifies their lie as directed to the Holy Spirit and God; each dies, and great fear comes on the church. Apostolic signs draw crowds. Authorities imprison the apostles, but an angel frees them and sends them back to public teaching. Arrested again, they say they must obey God rather than people and witness to Jesus' exaltation. Gamaliel cautions the council against fighting God. The apostles are beaten and ordered to stop, yet they rejoice and continue proclaiming Jesus.

### Follow the Story

The couple seeks the honor of complete generosity without its cost. Acts distinguishes shared possessions from coerced confiscation: the land remained theirs, and they retained authority over the proceeds. Community depends on truthful participation.

The deaths are narrated as immediate judgment, but the text does not say Peter kills either person. The scene should not be used to threaten giving, demand financial disclosure beyond accountable need, or claim church leaders possess lethal authority.

“We must obey God rather than people” concerns a direct command to stop bearing witness after God has commanded speech. It is not a license to ignore every law or label personal preference divine. The apostles accept consequences and do not respond with violence.

### Threads Through Scripture

The deception echoes Achan, whose hidden taking endangered a covenant community. Jesus warns against performing generosity for honor. Gamaliel's counsel illustrates restraint, though Acts does not endorse every detail of his historical analogy.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** What is voluntary, and what is falsely represented?
- **Understand:** What limits the claim to obey God over people?
- **Connect:** How can communities create pressure to perform generosity?
- **Respond:** Where can you replace image management with honest participation?

### Prayer and Practice

Make one promise smaller and truer rather than impressive and misleading. Protect voluntary generosity from manipulation.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

The word *ekklēsia*, assembly or church, first appears for this community here. Public signs produce both attraction and distance, showing growth is not socially simple.

### Wrestle with This

How can holy seriousness be preserved without leaders using fear to control money or speech?
