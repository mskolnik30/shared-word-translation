### Follow the Movement

The order of Acts 12 matters. Its movement through James Is Killed and Peter Is Imprisoned, Peter Is Rescued from Prison, Peter Comes to the Praying Community, Herod Dies, and finally God’s Word Continues to Grow controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 3 observes: “Some of the Jewish people” reflects the constituency whose approval Herod notices. The passage must not be generalized into collective Jewish responsibility. The translation note at verse 5 observes: The church’s earnest prayer stands beside Peter’s heavily guarded imprisonment, but Acts does not claim prayer guarantees every imprisoned believer the rescue Peter receives. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies κακόω (kakoō) “To harm.”

For Acts 12: Distinguish what the narrative reports from what it commands, and notice how the Spirit leads real communities through conflict and change. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
