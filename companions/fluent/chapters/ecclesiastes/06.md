The translation note at verse 3–5 observes: The comparison with a stillborn child is deliberately stark. The Teacher’s point is not to diminish the tragedy of such a death, but to expose the misery of a long life marked by insatiability, abandonment, and the loss of honor in death. The translation note at verse 7 observes: “Appetite” translates nephesh, a word that can refer to the living self and its desires. Human labor may provide food for the mouth, yet it does not necessarily satisfy the deeper self. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies נֶפֶשׁ (nephesh) — “appetite” here is the desiring self. Wealth may satisfy outward lack while leaving the person unable to receive or enjoy what they possess.
