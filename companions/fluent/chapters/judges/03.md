### Follow the Movement

The order of Judges 3 matters. Its movement through Nations Left to Test Israel, Othniel Delivers Israel, Ehud and Eglon, Moab Is Defeated, and finally Shamgar controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 7 observes: “Asheroth” — The Hebrew plural can refer to cultic objects associated with the goddess Asherah or to manifestations of the goddess herself. The translation note at verse 10 observes: “Spirit of the LORD” — The Spirit equips Othniel for judgment and deliverance. Judges repeatedly connects the Spirit with particular acts of empowered leadership. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies נָסָה (nasah) “To test.” To expose or prove faithfulness through trial.

For Judges 3: Keep the chapter within Israel's narrated covenant life, allowing its legal, historical, and theological tensions to remain visible.
