### Follow the Movement

The order of Judges 12 matters. Its movement through Ephraim Confronts Jephthah, Shibboleth, and finally Ibzan, Elon, and Abdon controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 3 observes: “put my life in my hand” — The Hebrew idiom means risking one’s life. Jephthah presents himself as acting under mortal danger when Ephraim did not help. The translation note at verse 4 observes: The insult against Gilead appears to question their tribal legitimacy or portray them as displaced Ephraimites. The exact force of the taunt is difficult. One vocabulary entry at verse 3 identifies שִׂים נֶפֶשׁ בְּכַף (sim nefesh be-khaf) “To put one’s life in one’s hand.” An idiom for risking one’s life.
