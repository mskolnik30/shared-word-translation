### Follow the Movement

The order of Judges 8 matters. Its movement through Ephraim Confronts Gideon, Succoth and Penuel Refuse Bread, Zebah and Zalmunna Captured, Gideon Punishes Succoth and Penuel, Gideon Kills the Kings of Midian, Gideon Refuses Kingship, and finally Gideon’s Final Years controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 17 observes: Penuel’s refusal of support results in the destruction of its tower and the killing of its men, marking a severe turn in Gideon’s leadership toward fellow Israelites. The translation note at verse 19 observes: Gideon’s killing of Zebah and Zalmunna is tied to blood vengeance for his brothers, adding personal retaliation to the public war against Midian. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies עֹלֵלוֹת (olelot) “Gleanings.” Grapes left after the main harvest, used metaphorically for Ephraim’s contribution.
