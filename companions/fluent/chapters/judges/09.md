### Follow the Movement

The order of Judges 9 matters. Its 8 named sections move from “Abimelech Seizes Power” through “Zebul Warns Abimelech” to “Abimelech Dies at Thebez.” Read that complete arc before using one detail to characterize the chapter as a whole.

The translation note at verse 8–15 observes: Jotham’s fable contrasts fruitful trees that refuse kingship with the bramble, which offers useless shade and threatens fire. The fable exposes Abimelech’s kingship as destructive rather than fruitful. The translation note at verse 23 observes: “evil spirit” — The phrase describes a destructive spirit sent by God to produce division. The text presents the resulting conflict as judgment upon violence already committed. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies עֶצֶם וּבָשָׂר (etsem u-basar) “Bone and flesh.” An idiom of close kinship.
