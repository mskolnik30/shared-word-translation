# Judges 21

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

This final chapter completes Judges’ 21-chapter movement. The chapter is organized around Israel Grieves for Benjamin, Jabesh-Gilead Is Destroyed, Peace with Benjamin, The Women of Shiloh, and finally Everyone Did What Was Right in Their Own Eyes. Read it as one movement before separating individual lines for closer study.

### The Chapter in Brief

Judges 21 moves through Israel Grieves for Benjamin; then Jabesh-Gilead Is Destroyed; then Peace with Benjamin; then The Women of Shiloh; and the remaining movement leads to Everyone Did What Was Right in Their Own Eyes. These headings provide the chapter’s visible architecture. The details within them show how the chapter develops its claims rather than presenting a collection of detached sayings. The ending at “Everyone Did What Was Right in Their Own Eyes” should therefore be heard in relation to the opening at “Israel Grieves for Benjamin.”

### Follow the Movement

The order of Judges 21 matters. Its movement through Israel Grieves for Benjamin, Jabesh-Gilead Is Destroyed, Peace with Benjamin, The Women of Shiloh, and finally Everyone Did What Was Right in Their Own Eyes controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 3 observes: Israel laments the possible loss of Benjamin after having nearly destroyed the tribe. Grief follows the consequences of their own collective action. The translation note at verse 11 observes: The sexual wording is deliberately explicit in the Hebrew because virginity becomes the criterion by which women are spared and reassigned. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies שָׁבַע (shava) “To swear.” To bind oneself by an oath.

For Judges 21: Keep the chapter within Israel's narrated covenant life, allowing its legal, historical, and theological tensions to remain visible. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
