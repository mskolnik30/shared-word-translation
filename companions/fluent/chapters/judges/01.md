# Judges 1

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

This opening chapter begins Judges’ 21-chapter movement. The chapter is organized around Judah Goes Up First, Jerusalem and Hebron, Judah and Simeon, The House of Joseph Takes Bethel, and finally Israel Does Not Drive Them Out. Read it as one movement before separating individual lines for closer study.

### Chapter Path

- **1:1–7:** Judah Goes Up First.
- **1:8–15:** Jerusalem and Hebron.
- **1:16–21:** Judah and Simeon.
- **1:22–26:** The House of Joseph Takes Bethel.
- **1:27–36:** Israel Does Not Drive Them Out.

### Watch For

In Judges 1, watch how the movement from “Judah Goes Up First” toward “Israel Does Not Drive Them Out” changes the chapter's emphasis. Do not let one verse erase the sequence created by the whole chapter. The translation notes especially mark verse 1 for careful attention.

### The Chapter in Brief

Judges 1 moves through Judah Goes Up First; then Jerusalem and Hebron; then Judah and Simeon; then The House of Joseph Takes Bethel; and the remaining movement leads to Israel Does Not Drive Them Out. These headings provide the chapter’s visible architecture. The details within them show how the chapter develops its claims rather than presenting a collection of detached sayings. The ending at “Israel Does Not Drive Them Out” should therefore be heard in relation to the opening at “Judah Goes Up First.”

### Follow the Movement

The order of Judges 1 matters. Its movement through Judah Goes Up First, Jerusalem and Hebron, Judah and Simeon, The House of Joseph Takes Bethel, and finally Israel Does Not Drive Them Out controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 6–7 observes: Adoni-Bezek interprets his mutilation as recompense corresponding to what he had inflicted on others. The narrative reports his interpretation without explicitly presenting the act as a general legal principle. The translation note at verse 16 observes: “Kenite” — The Kenites are associated with Moses’ father-in-law and later appear in close relationship with Israel while retaining a distinct identity. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies יָרַשׁ (yarash) “To possess,” “inherit,” or “dispossess.” A central term for taking possession of the land.

For Judges 1: Keep the chapter within Israel's narrated covenant life, allowing its legal, historical, and theological tensions to remain visible. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
