### Follow the Movement

The order of Judges 11 matters. Its movement through Jephthah the Gileadite, The Elders Ask Jephthah to Lead, Jephthah Sends Messengers to Ammon, Jephthah’s Vow, The LORD Gives Ammon into His Hand, and finally Jephthah’s Daughter controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 12–27 observes: Jephthah attempts diplomacy before battle. His argument retells Israel’s journey and distinguishes Ammonite territory from the Amorite land Israel actually seized. The translation note at verse 24 observes: Jephthah refers to Chemosh as Ammon’s god, although elsewhere Milcom is associated with Ammon and Chemosh with Moab. The speech may reflect rhetorical accommodation, overlapping traditions, or textual complexity; the translation does not correct the text. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies גִּבּוֹר חַיִל (gibbor chayil) “Mighty warrior.” A person of strength or military ability.

For Judges 11: Keep the chapter within Israel's narrated covenant life, allowing its legal, historical, and theological tensions to remain visible. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
