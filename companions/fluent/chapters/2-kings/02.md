# 2 Kings 2

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Elijah has confronted kings and called Israel back to the LORD. Elisha was summoned from his fields to follow him. The opening verse tells readers that Elijah is about to be taken away, so the chapter focuses on succession: will Elisha remain, and will Elijah's prophetic work continue? The final scene, in which bears maul forty-two youths, is severe and should not be reduced to a joke about children insulting a bald man.

### Chapter Path

- **2:1–6:** Elijah repeatedly asks Elisha to remain behind; Elisha refuses to leave him.
- **2:7–14:** Elijah divides the Jordan, is taken in a whirlwind, and leaves his cloak; Elisha crosses back.
- **2:15–18:** Prophets recognize Elisha's authority but unsuccessfully search for Elijah.
- **2:19–22:** Elisha heals Jericho's bad water by the LORD's word.
- **2:23–25:** A group of youths mocks Elisha near Bethel; he curses them, and bears maul forty-two.

### Watch For

Notice repeated places and repeated requests to stay. The journey retraces Israel's entry into the land and leads across the Jordan, linking prophetic succession with Moses and Joshua.

## Read the Chapter

Read 2 Kings 2 in the Fluent Translation. Keep the gifts of succession and healing in view without allowing them to make the violent ending feel simple.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

How does Elisha remain with Elijah, what does he request, and what signs show that the prophetic calling continues?

### The Chapter in Brief

Elijah travels from Gilgal to Bethel, Jericho, and the Jordan, while Elisha repeatedly vows not to leave him. Elijah divides the river with his cloak. Elisha asks for a firstborn's share of his spirit. A fiery chariot separates them, and Elijah is taken in a whirlwind. Elisha receives the cloak and divides the Jordan. The prophetic community recognizes his succession. He then heals Jericho's water. Near Bethel, youths mock him; he curses them, and two bears maul forty-two.

### Follow the Story

Elisha's request for a “double portion” is inheritance language: the firstborn son's share. He is not merely asking to be twice as powerful but to be recognized as Elijah's true successor. Elijah says the gift is not his to control; Elisha's seeing the departure will confirm it.

The fiery chariot does not carry Elijah upward. It separates Elijah and Elisha, while the whirlwind takes Elijah. Elisha's cry, “My father, my father! The chariots of Israel and its horsemen,” recognizes prophetic presence as a deeper defense than military force.

Elisha returns by the route they came. The divided Jordan echoes Moses at the sea and Joshua entering the land. The same cloak now acts in Elisha's hands, but the prophets' search reminds readers that Elijah cannot simply be retrieved. Succession continues a calling; it does not erase grief.

Jericho's healed water shows prophetic authority serving communal life. Salt is a sign, not a magic ingredient; Elisha attributes the healing to the LORD.

The final story uses a word that can describe youths rather than only small children. Their number suggests a threatening group, and Bethel has become a center of rival worship. These details correct the childish caricature, but they do not make the mauling easy. The text says forty-two were mauled, not explicitly that all died. Readers should name the disproportion they feel and avoid using the passage to frighten children or defend violent retaliation for disrespect.

### Threads Through Scripture

Elijah and Elisha's crossing recalls Moses and Joshua, while Elijah's departure later shapes expectations about prophetic return. The New Testament places Elijah with Moses at Jesus' transfiguration. Jesus' authority, however, is repeatedly shown through healing and refusal of personal vengeance.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** What repeats in the journey, and what changes after the whirlwind?
- **Understand:** Why is “double portion” about inheritance and succession?
- **Connect:** How can a community honor a departing leader without trying to reproduce that person exactly?
- **Respond:** What calling or responsibility needs to be carried forward in your community?

### Prayer and Practice

Give thanks for someone whose faithful work prepared you. Take one concrete step to carry that work forward in your own form. Bring your discomfort with the closing judgment honestly to God rather than turning it into humor.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

Bethel was significant in Israel's worship history and later housed a royal sanctuary condemned by prophets. Baldness may have been part of the taunt, but “go up” may also mock Elijah's departure and challenge Elisha's authority.

### Wrestle with This

What practices help readers remain truthful when a biblical judgment feels severe—neither explaining away the text nor using it to authorize harm?

### Further Study

Compare the Jordan crossing with Joshua 3–4, prophetic succession with Numbers 27:12–23, and Elisha's later acts of provision in 2 Kings 4.
