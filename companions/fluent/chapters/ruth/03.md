# Ruth 3

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Ruth has gleaned through the harvests under Boaz's protection. Naomi now seeks lasting “rest” or security for Ruth. Her plan sends Ruth to the threshing floor at night, creating a scene filled with vulnerability, social risk, and carefully chosen words.

### Chapter Path

- **3:1–5:** Naomi proposes a plan; Ruth agrees.
- **3:6–9:** Ruth approaches Boaz and asks him to spread his garment over her because he is a redeemer.
- **3:10–13:** Boaz praises her loyalty and promises to act, while naming a nearer redeemer.
- **3:14–18:** Ruth leaves safely with barley; Naomi tells her to wait for the matter to be settled.

### Watch For

Read slowly. The language contains suggestive possibilities, but the narrator emphasizes Ruth's request for covenant responsibility and Boaz's concern for honor and procedure. Avoid claiming more than the text states.

## Read the Chapter

Read Ruth 3 in the Fluent Translation. Notice where Ruth follows Naomi's instruction and where she speaks with her own initiative.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What does Naomi hope for? What does Ruth ask Boaz to do? Why does Boaz not settle the matter immediately?

### The Chapter in Brief

Naomi directs Ruth to approach Boaz after the work at the threshing floor. Ruth uncovers his feet and lies down. When he wakes, she identifies herself and asks him to spread his garment over her as redeemer. Boaz receives the request honorably and promises to act, but explains that another relative has the first claim. He protects Ruth's reputation, gives her barley, and sends her back to Naomi.

### Follow the Story

Ruth is not merely passive. She turns Naomi's plan into a direct appeal: “You are a redeemer.” The image of a spread garment can suggest both marriage and protection, echoing Boaz's earlier blessing that Ruth had sought refuge under God's wings. She now asks Boaz to become part of the answer to his own blessing.

Boaz's honorable response matters because the setting is risky and the power difference is real. He does not exploit secrecy. He names the nearer relative, promises public action, and takes steps to protect Ruth as dawn approaches.

### Threads Through Scripture

Biblical refuge under God's wings often expresses safety and covenant care. Here divine refuge takes social form through a person asked to assume responsibility. The chapter also belongs to Scripture's wider concern that family, property, and law serve life rather than abandon the vulnerable.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** What exactly does Ruth ask? What does Boaz promise?
- **Understand:** Why are both courage and proper public process necessary here?
- **Connect:** When can a request make someone responsible for acting on values they already speak about?
- **Respond:** Is there a promise, role, or position you need to embody more concretely?

### Prayer and Practice

Pray for people who must take risks to ask for protection, work, housing, or justice. Consider one way you can make a process clearer and safer for someone who depends on it.

## Go Deeper

### More Than One Faithful Reading

The threshing-floor scene uses language that readers interpret differently. Some hear sexual overtones; others emphasize a symbolic marriage proposal and the narrator's portrayal of integrity. A careful reading can acknowledge ambiguity without accusing Ruth or Boaz of actions the story does not name.

### What Needs Context

Ancient redemption customs involved obligations and rights within an extended family. Ruth combines the questions of family continuity and marriage in a way that does not map neatly onto a single modern legal category.

### Wrestle with This

Naomi's plan should not become a universal model for romance or advice to enter unsafe situations. The narrative describes a culturally specific, high-risk strategy. Modern application should emphasize agency, consent, accountability, and protection.

### Further Study

Compare Boaz's blessing in Ruth 2:12 with Ruth's request in 3:9. Ask how prayer and human responsibility meet.
