### Follow the Movement

Even without internal headings, the order of Ezekiel 31 matters. Trace its shifts in speaker, image, action, and tone before deciding what any single line means.

The translation note at verse 8–9 observes: The “garden of God” and “trees of Eden” draw on Eden imagery without fully identifying Assyria with Eden itself. The language heightens the cedar’s splendor while preparing for its descent. The translation note at verse 10 observes: The cedar’s downfall is explicitly connected to its lifted-up heart. Height itself is not condemned; pride in height is. One vocabulary entry at verse 4 identifies תְּהוֹם (tehom) — “the Deep.” Here the Deep is the source of the waters that make the cedar flourish. In verse 15, the same Deep is restrained and made to mourn, showing that the abundance sustaining imperial life is under God’s command.
