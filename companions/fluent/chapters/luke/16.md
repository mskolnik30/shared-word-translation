### Follow the Movement

The order of Luke 16 matters. Its movement through The Shrewd Steward, God Knows the Heart, and finally The Rich Man and Lazarus controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 9 observes: “Unrighteous wealth” is Luke’s deliberately suspicious language for money in a world marked by unjust systems. The command is to deploy it toward relationships and eternal ends. The translation note at verse 15 observes: The Pharisees “justify themselves”; God’s knowledge of the heart exposes the limits of public moral status. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies οἰκονόμος (oikonomos) “Steward, household manager.”
