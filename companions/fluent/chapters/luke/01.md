The chapter is organized around Preface, The Birth of John Foretold, The Birth of Jesus Foretold, Mary Visits Elizabeth, Mary’s Song, The Birth of John, and finally Zechariah’s Prophecy. ### The Chapter in Brief

Luke 1 moves through Preface; then The Birth of John Foretold; then The Birth of Jesus Foretold; then Mary Visits Elizabeth; and the remaining movement leads to Zechariah’s Prophecy. These headings provide the chapter’s visible architecture. The details within them show how the chapter develops its claims rather than presenting a collection of detached sayings. The ending at “Zechariah’s Prophecy” should therefore be heard in relation to the opening at “Preface.”

### Follow the Movement

The order of Luke 1 matters. Its movement through Preface, The Birth of John Foretold, The Birth of Jesus Foretold, Mary Visits Elizabeth, Mary’s Song, The Birth of John, and finally Zechariah’s Prophecy controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 20 observes: Zechariah’s silence is explicitly connected with his failure to trust Gabriel’s words; the same words are nevertheless said to be fulfilled at their appointed time. The translation note at verse 28 observes: “Favored one” renders a perfect passive form related to charis, favor or grace. The verse does not use Mary’s later ecclesial titles. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies αὐτόπτης (autoptēs) “Eyewitness.”

For Luke 1: Follow the narrative sequence and the responses of different characters before combining this Gospel's presentation with parallel accounts. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
