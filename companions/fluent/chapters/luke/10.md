# Luke 10

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Jesus has turned toward Jerusalem and taught that following him outranks security and social obligation. Chapter 10 sends a larger circle of disciples, celebrates God's revelation to the lowly, redefines the neighbor through a Samaritan, and welcomes Mary as a disciple at Jesus' feet.

### Chapter Path

- **10:1–16:** Seventy-two are sent in pairs with vulnerability, peace, healing, and the announcement of God's near kingdom.
- **10:17–24:** They return joyful; Jesus redirects joy from power toward belonging and rejoices in the Spirit.
- **10:25–37:** A legal expert tests Jesus; a Samaritan becomes neighbor to a wounded man.
- **10:38–42:** Martha welcomes Jesus; Mary sits as a learner; Jesus addresses anxious distraction.

### Watch For

The lawyer asks which person qualifies as his neighbor. Jesus asks which person became a neighbor. The movement is from defining the worthy recipient to practicing mercy.

## Read the Chapter

Read Luke 10 in the Fluent Translation. Notice households, roads, tables, and feet as places where discipleship occurs.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What are the sent disciples asked to depend on? How do the Samaritan and Mary cross expectations?

### The Chapter in Brief

Jesus sends seventy-two disciples in pairs, telling them to travel lightly, offer peace, receive hospitality, heal, and announce the kingdom whether accepted or rejected. They return joyful that harmful powers submit, but Jesus tells them to rejoice that their names are known by God. A legal expert asks about eternal life and then seeks to define the neighbor. Jesus tells of a wounded traveler ignored by religious figures and tended generously by a Samaritan. In a home, Mary learns at Jesus' feet while Martha is distracted by much service; Jesus says one thing is necessary.

### Follow the Story

Mission depends on receiving as well as giving. The messengers cannot control food, lodging, or response. Vulnerability makes partnership necessary and prevents mission from looking like self-sufficient expansion.

The Samaritan is not praised for holding correct social status but for compassion that becomes touch, transport, money, time, and continuing responsibility. “Go and do likewise” does not ask whom we may exclude; it sends readers to become neighbors.

Mary's place at Jesus' feet is a disciple's posture. Jesus does not condemn service or reduce Martha to a stereotype; he names her anxious distraction and protects Mary's learning. Formation and service need not compete when both receive Jesus' presence.

### Threads Through Scripture

The two love commands come from Deuteronomy and Leviticus. Samaritan hostility appears nearby in Luke, making mercy across the boundary sharper. Acts will show Samaritans receiving the gospel and Spirit.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** What does the Samaritan spend or risk?
- **Understand:** Why does Jesus change the lawyer's question?
- **Connect:** Which people are treated as unlikely moral agents?
- **Respond:** How can you become a neighbor in a concrete way?

### Prayer and Practice

Do one act of mercy that includes a next step, not only a feeling. Make room for someone commonly excluded from learning or leadership.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

Manuscripts vary between seventy and seventy-two; the pinned text reads seventy-two. Samaritans shared Israelite roots but lived amid centuries of religious and political hostility with Judeans.

### Wrestle with This

How can service become anxious control, and how can attentive presence restore it?

### Further Study

Read Leviticus 19:17–18, Deuteronomy 6:4–9, Luke 9:51–56, and Acts 8:4–25.
