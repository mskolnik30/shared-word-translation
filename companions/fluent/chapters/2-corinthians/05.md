# 2 Corinthians 5

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Paul has described ministry as treasure carried in fragile clay jars: suffering reveals mortality, yet Jesus' life is also at work. Chapter 5 develops bodily hope, accountable living, Christ's love, new creation, and God's ministry of reconciliation.

### Chapter Path

- **5:1–5:** The earthly tent is fragile; Paul longs not for naked disembodiment but to be clothed and swallowed by life.
- **5:6–10:** Present absence and confidence coexist; believers walk by faith, seek to please Christ, and face judgment.
- **5:11–15:** Reverence motivates persuasion; Christ's love holds the community because his death reorients life.
- **5:16–21:** Old ways of regarding people give way to new creation; God reconciles the world in Christ and entrusts the message to ambassadors.

### Watch For

Reconciliation begins with God. The community represents and extends it; it does not originate it or control the other person's response.

## Read the Chapter

Read 2 Corinthians 5 in the Fluent Translation. Follow the movement from personal mortality toward a shared vocation in the world.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What does Paul hope for beyond mortality, and how does Christ's death change perception, purpose, and relationship?

### The Chapter in Brief

Paul compares mortal life to a tent and God's future to a lasting dwelling. He groans not to escape embodiment but for mortality to be swallowed by life; the Spirit is the pledge. Until then, believers walk by faith and aim to please Christ, knowing all appear before his judgment seat. Christ's love controls Paul's ministry because one died for all so the living no longer live only for themselves. In Christ there is new creation. God reconciles the world, does not count trespasses, and makes the community ambassadors of divine appeal.

### Follow the Story

Tent and building are metaphors, not a technical map of the afterlife. Verse 4 corrects the idea that Paul desires a bodiless state. Christian hope is transformation—life swallowing mortality.

Walking by faith does not mean ignoring evidence or facts. It describes trustful orientation toward a reality not yet fully visible. Judgment keeps confidence accountable; grace does not make embodied choices meaningless.

“According to the flesh” names superficial human measures shaped by status and appearance. New creation changes how people are regarded. Reconciliation is therefore not merely private peace with God; God entrusts a public word and representative vocation to a community.

Reconciliation must not be used to force immediate contact with an unsafe person. God's reconciling initiative includes truth and transformed life; human ambassadors do not control timing, erase accountability, or demand access from those harmed.

### Threads Through Scripture

Creation language reaches back to Genesis and forward to renewed creation. Isaiah speaks of new heavens and earth, while Jesus blesses peacemakers and teaches truthful repair. Paul's collection for Jerusalem makes reconciliation materially communal.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which present tensions does Paul refuse to eliminate?
- **Understand:** Why is “swallowed by life” different from escape from the body?
- **Connect:** What superficial measures distort how communities see people?
- **Respond:** What truthful, safe step could represent God's reconciling initiative?

### Prayer and Practice

Ask where you need a new way of seeing someone. Take one step toward truth and peace that respects boundaries and does not bypass harm.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

The Spirit as “pledge” is a down payment guaranteeing future completion. Ambassador language assumes delegated representation: the message and authority belong to the sender.

### Wrestle with This

How can reconciliation remain God's grace without becoming cheap peace that leaves injustice untouched?
