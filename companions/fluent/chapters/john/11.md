### Follow the Movement

The order of John 11 matters. Its movement through Lazarus Dies, I Am the Resurrection and the Life, Jesus Weeps, Lazarus Is Raised, and finally The Plot to Kill Jesus controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 11–14 observes: Jesus uses sleep as a death metaphor; the disciples misunderstand it literally, continuing John’s pattern of misunderstanding language with deeper meaning. The translation note at verse 25 observes: “I am the resurrection and the life” makes resurrection not only a future event but a reality centered in Jesus. One vocabulary entry at verse 4 identifies δόξα (doxa) “Glory.”

For John 11: Follow the narrative sequence and the responses of different characters before combining this Gospel's presentation with parallel accounts. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
