### Follow the Movement

The order of John 20 matters. Its movement through Mary Magdalene Finds the Tomb Open, Jesus Appears to Mary Magdalene, Jesus Appears to the Disciples, Thomas Sees and Trusts, and finally Why This Gospel Was Written controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 2 observes: Mary says “we do not know,” implying others may be associated with her even though John’s scene names her alone at this point. The translation note at verse 8–9 observes: The beloved disciple “saw and trusted,” yet the narrator says the disciples still did not understand the Scripture about resurrection. Trust and full understanding are not identical. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies μνημεῖον (mnēmeion) “Tomb.”

For John 20: Follow the narrative sequence and the responses of different characters before combining this Gospel's presentation with parallel accounts. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
