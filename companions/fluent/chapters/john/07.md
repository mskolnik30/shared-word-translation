# John 7

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

After the bread discourse, many disciples have withdrawn and authorities seek Jesus' death. At the Festival of Booths, Jesus' timing, origin, teaching, identity, and promised Spirit become public disputes. Fear fragments the crowd's speech.

### Chapter Path

- **7:1–13:** Jesus' brothers urge public display; he goes to the festival privately amid whispered debate.
- **7:14–24:** Jesus teaches and calls for judgment beyond appearance.
- **7:25–36:** Crowds and authorities dispute Jesus' origin and destination; arrest attempts fail because his hour has not come.
- **7:37–39:** On the festival's last day, Jesus invites the thirsty and promises rivers of living water, interpreted as the Spirit.
- **7:40–52:** The crowd divides; guards refuse arrest; Nicodemus asks whether law judges without hearing.
- **7:53:** A separately marked ancient textual tradition begins.

### Watch For

Context distinguishes Judean authorities seeking Jesus' death from Jewish crowds and disciples. Do not turn an internal Jewish conflict into contempt for Jewish people.

## Read the Chapter

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What divisions emerge around Jesus, and how do timing, origin, law, water, and Spirit shape them?

### The Chapter in Brief

Jesus declines his brothers' demand for public action, then attends the festival privately. Midway through, he teaches that his message comes from the One who sent him and tells the crowd to judge rightly rather than by appearance. People debate whether his known hometown disqualifies messianic claims. Authorities attempt arrest, but guards are arrested by his words instead. Jesus invites thirsty people to come and trust, promising living water associated with the Spirit. Division deepens, and Nicodemus exposes leaders who judge without hearing. The chapter closes where a textually distinct story begins.

### Follow the Story

Jesus' statement “I am not going up” stands in tension with his later action; some manuscripts read “not yet.” The Fluent translation follows the pinned main text rather than silently harmonizing it. Such openness lets readers see textual history without panic.

Festival water ceremonies provide a rich setting for Jesus' invitation. The punctuation of “from within him” is debated, but the common reading sees living water flowing from the person who trusts. The Spirit is promised as abundance received and shared.

The guards' refusal and Nicodemus's procedural question show cracks inside official resistance. Righteous judgment requires hearing before condemnation—a standard the leaders claim to know but fail to practice.

### Threads Through Scripture

Living water recalls wilderness provision, prophetic rivers, and John's conversation with the Samaritan woman. John 7:53–8:11 is ancient and beloved but absent from the earliest strong witnesses; transparent labeling honors both reception and evidence.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Who speaks openly, secretly, fearfully, or procedurally?
- **Understand:** Why does festival water deepen Jesus' invitation?
- **Connect:** Where do institutions judge before genuinely hearing?
- **Respond:** Whom do you need to hear before forming a conclusion?

### Prayer and Practice

Practice one conversation governed by Jesus' command not to judge by appearance. Ask a clarifying question before defending your conclusion.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

The Festival of Booths remembered wilderness life and celebrated harvest with water and light imagery. “Dispersion among the Greeks” refers to Jewish communities outside Judea.

### Wrestle with This

How can transparent acknowledgment of manuscript variation strengthen rather than weaken trust?
