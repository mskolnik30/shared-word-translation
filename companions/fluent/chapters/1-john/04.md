# 1 John 4

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

The community is navigating competing spiritual claims and painful division. The writer tells believers neither to accept every claim nor to live in panic. Teaching must be tested by its confession of Jesus Christ come in the flesh. The chapter then turns to its fullest statement about God and love.

### Chapter Path

- **4:1–6:** Test the spirits; confess Jesus Christ come in the flesh; discern truth and error.
- **4:7–12:** Love one another because love is from God; God showed love by sending the Son.
- **4:13–16:** The Spirit, apostolic testimony, confession, and remaining in God's love.
- **4:17–21:** Mature love gives confidence and drives out fear; love of God cannot coexist with hatred of a sibling.

### Watch For

Notice that love begins with God's action, not ours. Also notice that testing teaching and loving people are not competing tasks. Truth about the embodied Christ protects a community from a spirituality detached from bodily life and responsibility.

## Read the Chapter

Read 1 John 4 in the Fluent Translation. Mark every statement describing what God has done before the commands describing what believers should do.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

How are spiritual claims tested? How has God defined love? What does mature love do to fear?

### The Chapter in Brief

Believers should test spiritual claims because not every spirit is from God. Confession that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh marks God's Spirit in the community's conflict. Those born of God should love because God is love. God's love is revealed in sending the Son so that people might live. Love begins not with human initiative but with God. As believers remain in this love, the Spirit gives assurance, and love matures toward confidence rather than fear of punishment. Anyone claiming love for God while hating a sibling is living a contradiction.

### Follow the Story

“God is love” does not mean that every desire or pleasant feeling is divine. The chapter defines love through God's initiative: God gives, comes near in the Son, creates life, and forms a people who love.

The claim that perfect love drives out fear concerns fear of punishment in relation to confidence before God. It should not be used to shame trauma responses, anxiety disorders, or prudent fear of danger. Growth in love can be real while the body still carries fear and needs care.

### Threads Through Scripture

The embodied confession continues the Gospel's announcement that the Word became flesh. God's prior love echoes Israel's story, where divine election and deliverance precede the command to love God and neighbor. Jesus also joins visible love among disciples to the credibility of their witness.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** What tests are theological, relational, and ethical?
- **Understand:** Why does the flesh-and-blood reality of Jesus matter for love?
- **Connect:** How can a community practice discernment without becoming suspicious of everyone?
- **Respond:** What action could make God's prior love more believable to someone in need?

### Prayer and Practice

Begin with reception: “We love because you first loved us.” Then choose one relationship in which love needs truth, patience, protection, or repair. Seek appropriate help if fear is rooted in trauma or present danger.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

“Test the spirits” addresses prophets and teachings claiming spiritual authority. The test is not a technique for labeling unfamiliar people as evil; it asks whether teaching confesses the apostolic Jesus and produces a life consistent with God's love.

### Wrestle with This

Love does not eliminate boundaries or accountability. Refusing hatred means refusing to deny another person's humanity. It can coexist with naming wrongdoing, protecting others, and allowing consequences.

### Further Study

Read John 1:14–18 and 13:34–35 beside 1 John 4. How do incarnation, witness, and community love support one another?
