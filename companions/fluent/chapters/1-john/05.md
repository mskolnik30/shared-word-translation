# 1 John 5

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

The final chapter gathers the letter's themes: trust that Jesus is the Christ, birth from God, love expressed in obedience, victory over the world, testimony about the Son, eternal life, confident prayer, care for a sinning sibling, and protection from idols.

### Chapter Path

- **5:1–5:** Trust, new birth, love, obedience, and victory through faith.
- **5:6–12:** Water, blood, and Spirit testify that life is in God's Son.
- **5:13–17:** Assurance of eternal life, confidence in prayer, and prayer for a sibling who sins.
- **5:18–21:** Three concluding assurances and a final command to guard against idols.

### Watch For

Listen for the repeated word “testimony” and the repeated phrase “we know.” The chapter seeks assurance grounded in God's action and promise, not confidence manufactured by the reader.

## Read the Chapter

Read 1 John 5 in the Fluent Translation. Note where the writer is clear and where he leaves a difficult distinction unexplained.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What overcomes the world? What testimony has God given? What does the writer want believers to know?

### The Chapter in Brief

Those who trust that Jesus is the Christ are born from God, love God's children, and discover that God's commands are not crushing. Faith overcomes the world's opposed order. Jesus came by water and blood, and the Spirit testifies; eternal life is located in the Son. The writer addresses believers so they may know they have life and pray with confidence according to God's will. They should pray for a sibling caught in sin, though the writer distinguishes a sin leading to death without fully explaining it. The message closes with assurance of God's protection, the Son's coming, and the command to guard against idols.

### Follow the Story

The witness to “water and blood” resists any account of Jesus detached from his real earthly life and death. Interpretations differ on the precise reference—Jesus' baptism and death are a common reading—but the chapter insists that saving life comes through the embodied Son to whom God bears witness.

Confidence in prayer is relational, not mechanical. Prayer is heard “according to God's will,” so the promise does not turn desire into control. The community prays for a struggling sibling as part of refusing to abandon one another.

### Threads Through Scripture

Multiple witnesses recall Israel's legal concern for reliable testimony. The confession that life is in the Son continues the Gospel of John's signs and purpose. The final warning about idols reaches back through Israel's story: even a community speaking truly about God must guard what receives its trust, desire, and obedience.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** What sources of assurance does the writer name?
- **Understand:** How are trust, love, and obedience related without becoming a way to earn new birth?
- **Connect:** What rival loyalties function like idols in a community?
- **Respond:** For whom can you pray in a way that seeks restoration rather than control?

### Prayer and Practice

Name one assurance from the chapter and one allegiance that competes with it. Pray for a person who is struggling, keeping their dignity and privacy. Offer help directly when appropriate rather than using prayer as a substitute for care.

## Go Deeper

### More Than One Faithful Reading

Christians have proposed several meanings for water and blood, including Jesus' baptism and crucifixion, the blood and water at his death, or sacramental associations. The passage's stable center is God's testimony to the incarnate Son who truly lived and died.

### Wrestle with This

The “sin leading to death” is not defined. Faithful interpreters disagree about whether it refers to settled rejection of Christ, apostasy, a particular grave sin, or physical judgment. The text does not authorize readers to diagnose another person's final standing with confidence.

### What Needs Context

The final command about idols may appear abrupt, but it names the letter's deepest contrast. The true God gives life in the Son; idols promise life while displacing trust and love.

### Further Study

Read John 20:30–31 beside 1 John 5:11–13. What does each writer want readers to trust and know?
