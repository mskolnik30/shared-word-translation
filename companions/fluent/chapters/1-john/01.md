# 1 John 1

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

The work opens with testimony rather than a conventional greeting. The writer speaks of what “we” have heard, seen, looked at, and touched concerning the word of life. This embodied testimony becomes an invitation into fellowship with God and with one another.

### Chapter Path

- **1:1–4:** The life made visible is proclaimed so that fellowship and joy may be complete.
- **1:5–7:** God is light; walking in light brings fellowship and cleansing.
- **1:8–10:** Claims of sinlessness deceive, while confession meets God's faithful forgiveness.

### Watch For

Notice the sensory language and the repeated “we.” Christian proclamation is not presented as escape from embodied life. Watch also how walking in light is connected to fellowship, not isolated moral perfection.

## Read the Chapter

Read 1 John 1 in the Fluent Translation. Listen to the repeated claims and responses.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What has been made visible? What is the purpose of proclamation? What happens when sin is denied or confessed?

### The Chapter in Brief

The writer announces the life revealed in Jesus, grounded in heard, seen, and touched testimony. The purpose is shared fellowship with the Father, the Son, and the community. The message is that God is light without darkness. Claims of fellowship are contradicted by walking in darkness, while walking in light joins believers to one another and to Jesus' cleansing. Denying sin produces deception; confessing sin meets God's reliable forgiveness and cleansing.

### Follow the Story

The chapter begins with God's self-disclosure, not human achievement. Life has appeared and is announced. Fellowship is received and shared.

Walking in light does not mean pretending never to fail. The final verses make such a claim impossible. Light is the place where truth can be spoken, sin confessed, cleansing received, and relationships repaired. Darkness depends on hiding and denial.

### Threads Through Scripture

Light is associated throughout Scripture with God's presence, creation, guidance, and truth. The Gospel of John similarly speaks of the Word becoming flesh and life becoming humanity's light. Biblical confession tells the truth about sin while trusting God's faithful character.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which verbs emphasize physical testimony?
- **Understand:** How can walking in light include confession rather than perfectionism?
- **Connect:** What makes a community safe enough for truthful acknowledgment of failure?
- **Respond:** Is there a wrong you can name and begin to repair without excuse or self-destruction?

### Prayer and Practice

Use a simple prayer: “God of light, show me what is true; forgive what is wrong; lead me toward repair.” If confession could expose you to retaliation or abuse, seek wise and safe support before disclosing.

## Go Deeper

### Wrestle with This

Confession is not the same as carrying false blame. People who have been harmed may be pressured to confess merely for having boundaries, anger, or a truthful memory. God's light exposes both personal wrongdoing and wrongdoing done to us.

### A Theme to Follow

“Fellowship” is more than friendliness. It is shared participation in the life proclaimed in Christ, and it is tested by whether people walk truthfully with God and one another.

### Further Study

Compare 1 John 1:1–5 with John 1:1–18. List the shared themes of beginning, life, witness, light, and embodied revelation.
