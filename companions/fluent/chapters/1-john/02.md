# 1 John 2

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

The writer has rejected denial of sin and promised cleansing through Jesus. Chapter 2 holds prevention and restoration together: believers are called not to sin, yet Jesus is named as advocate when they do. The chapter then tests claims to know God by obedience, love, and remaining in what was heard from the beginning.

### Chapter Path

- **2:1–6:** Jesus the advocate and atoning sacrifice; knowing God shown through obedience.
- **2:7–14:** The old and new command to love; reassurance to children, fathers, and young people.
- **2:15–17:** Do not love the world's cravings and pride; the world is passing.
- **2:18–27:** Separation and false teaching; the community's anointing and the call to remain.
- **2:28–29:** Remain in Christ as children who practice righteousness.

### Watch For

Notice how pastoral reassurance interrupts warning. The repeated address to children, fathers, and young people reminds the community what it has already received before the writer names danger.

## Read the Chapter

Read 1 John 2 in the Fluent Translation. Mark every occurrence of “know,” “remain,” and “love.”

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What does Jesus do for sinners? How is knowing God tested? What are believers told to remain in?

### The Chapter in Brief

Jesus is advocate and atoning sacrifice, so the call away from sin is grounded in grace. A claim to know God is tested by keeping God's command, especially the command to love a sibling. Hatred belongs to darkness. The writer reassures the community that it is forgiven, knows God, and has overcome the evil one. It must not give allegiance to the world's disordered desires and pride. Some former members now oppose the confession of Jesus as Christ, but the community has received an anointing and should remain in the message heard from the beginning.

### Follow the Story

The “new command” is also old because the community has heard it from the beginning. It is new because its truth has been made visible in Jesus and is becoming visible among his people.

The label “antichrist” refers first to people and teaching that oppose the truth about Christ within this conflict. The chapter does not invite speculation about every disliked public figure. Its concern is faithful confession and a community that remains rooted rather than manipulated by fear.

### Threads Through Scripture

Jesus gives his disciples a command to love one another as he has loved them. The warnings about desires and pride echo wisdom teaching about what rules the heart. The language of anointing recalls God's gift and calling, here serving assurance that believers have received what they need to discern and remain.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which assurances are given before and during the warnings?
- **Understand:** Why are obedience and sibling-love evidence of knowing God?
- **Connect:** What kinds of novelty or fear can pull a community away from its center?
- **Respond:** What would remaining in Christ look like in one strained relationship?

### Prayer and Practice

Receive the chapter's reassurance before choosing a response: forgiven, known by God, and addressed as beloved. Then take one action that refuses contempt toward another believer while still speaking truthfully about disagreement.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

“World” here does not mean the created earth or every human culture. It names an order organized around grasping desire, possessive sight, and pride in status. Elsewhere in the Johannine writings, God loves the world and sends the Son for its life.

### More Than One Faithful Reading

Christians explain the scope and mechanism of “atoning sacrifice” in several ways. The chapter's central assurance is that Jesus, the righteous one, stands for sinners and addresses the sin of the whole world.

### Further Study

Read John 13:34–35 and 15:1–12. Compare the commands to love and remain with their use in 1 John 2.
