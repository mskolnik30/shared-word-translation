### Follow the Movement

The order of Matthew 11 matters. Its movement through John Asks About Jesus, Jesus Speaks About John, A Generation That Refuses to Respond, Woe to Unrepentant Towns, and finally Come to Me and Rest controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 5 observes: Jesus answers John through a cluster of scriptural restoration signs rather than with a bare yes/no claim. The translation note at verse 12 observes: This is one of Matthew's most difficult sayings. The verb and noun can be heard positively or negatively—forcefully advancing, suffering violence, or being seized. Fluent uses restrained wording and leaves the debate open in apparatus. One vocabulary entry at verse 5 identifies εὐαγγελίζω (euangelizō) “To announce good news.”
