### Follow the Movement

The order of Matthew 23 matters. Its movement through The Scribes and Pharisees, Woes Against Hypocrisy, and finally Judgment on Jerusalem controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 8–10 observes: The sayings about titles expose status hierarchy and rival claims to ultimate authority. They should not be mechanically converted into a ban on every ordinary use of “father” or “teacher” in unrelated contexts. The translation note at verse 14 observes: Public verse 14 is absent from SBLGNT's main text. Later manuscripts add a woe about devouring widows' houses and making long prayers. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies καθέδρα (kathedra) “Seat, chair,” here teaching authority.
