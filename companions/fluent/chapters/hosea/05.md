### Follow the Movement

The order of Hosea 5 matters. Its movement through Judgment on Priests, Israel, and the Royal House, Their Deeds Do Not Allow Them to Return, Blow the Horn, and finally Ephraim Goes to Assyria controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 4 observes: “Their deeds do not allow them to return” portrays accumulated behavior as shaping moral incapacity. The people are not merely making isolated mistakes. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies מוֹקֵשׁ (moqesh) “Snare.” A trap, here describing corrupt leadership.

For Hosea 5: Keep the prophecy's first historical horizon visible before tracing later echoes, and do not turn judgment or restoration imagery into a timetable for current events. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
