### Follow the Movement

The order of Jeremiah 31 matters. Its movement through The LORD Will Restore Israel, Return from Exile, Rachel Weeps; Hope Is Promised, Judah Also Will Be Restored, The LORD Will Rebuild and Replant, The New Covenant, The Covenant Secured by Creation, and finally Jerusalem Rebuilt controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 2–3 observes: “grace in the wilderness… everlasting love… steadfast love” — Exile is re-narrated as a wilderness where God’s love persists. The ground of return is not Israel’s merit but God’s enduring ḥesed. The translation note at verse 4–6 observes: tambourines / vineyards / watchmen — Restoration is embodied and economic: worship, agriculture, and communal vigilance return to normal life and joy. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies חֵן (ḥen) — grace; favor.
