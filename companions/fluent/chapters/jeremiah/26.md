### Follow the Movement

The order of Jeremiah 26 matters. Its movement through Jeremiah Speaks in the Temple, Priests and Prophets Seize Jeremiah, Jeremiah Is Put on Trial, The Officials Defend Jeremiah, and finally Uriah Is Killed controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 6 observes: Shiloh was an earlier sanctuary site associated with destruction. Invoking it challenges any assumption that the Jerusalem temple is automatically immune from judgment. The translation note at verse 11 observes: Priests and prophets, rather than foreign rulers, seek the death sentence. The conflict concerns whether a word against temple and city can truly come from the LORD. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies גָּרַע (gara) “To diminish” or “hold back.” Jeremiah is forbidden to omit any commanded word.

For Jeremiah 26: Keep the prophecy's first historical horizon visible before tracing later echoes, and do not turn judgment or restoration imagery into a timetable for current events. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
