# Daniel 7

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Daniel 7 moves backward to Belshazzar's first year and shifts the book from court stories toward apocalyptic visions. Symbols reveal the moral character and limit of empires. They are not meant to turn readers into confident date-setters.

### Chapter Path

- **7:1–8:** Four beasts rise from a chaotic sea; the fourth produces an arrogant little horn.
- **7:9–14:** The Ancient of Days convenes court; beastly dominion ends; a human-like figure receives everlasting rule.
- **7:15–22:** Daniel asks for meaning; beasts are kings, yet holy ones receive the kingdom.
- **7:23–28:** The fourth kingdom persecutes for a limited time before court removes its rule.

### Watch For

Contrast beastlike empires with “one like a son of man”—literally a human-like figure. The kingdom is given both to this figure and to the holy ones, without fully explaining their relation.

## Read the Chapter

Read Daniel 7 in the Fluent Translation. Notice that the vision meant to encourage the oppressed still leaves Daniel alarmed.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What comes from the sea, what happens in the heavenly court, and who finally receives dominion?

### The Chapter in Brief

Four winds stir the sea and four different beasts emerge. The terrifying fourth beast has horns, including one that speaks arrogantly. The Ancient of Days sits amid fire and opened books. The fourth beast is destroyed, and a human-like figure comes with clouds to receive everlasting rule over all peoples. An interpreter says the beasts are kings or kingdoms and that the holy ones will receive the kingdom. A final arrogant ruler persecutes them for a symbolic limited period before judgment. Daniel keeps the matter in his heart, deeply troubled.

### Follow the Story

Empires portray themselves as magnificent and civilizing; the vision sees predatory animals rising from chaos. Arrogant power becomes almost personal through the horn's eyes and mouth. Apocalyptic imagery unveils rather than photographs political reality.

The vision itself does not name the four empires, and traditions differ. Responsible reading should not present a later scheme as though Daniel supplied the labels. The central contrast is clearer than the chronology: beastly rule is temporary, God's court sits, and truly human dominion is received rather than seized.

“A time, times, and half a time” limits persecution without defining an exact modern schedule. The vision forms endurance by placing oppression inside a boundary known to God.

### Threads Through Scripture

Creation emerges from subdued waters in Genesis, while empires repeatedly resemble beasts in prophetic poetry. Jesus adopts “Son of Man” language, and Revelation reworks Daniel's beasts and heavenly court under Roman power.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** What human traits appear in the beasts, and what beastly traits appear in empire?
- **Understand:** Why might symbolic time comfort without giving a date?
- **Connect:** What makes political power dehumanizing?
- **Respond:** Which practice helps a community remain human under coercive systems?

### Prayer and Practice

Pray for people living under regimes that punish faithful speech. Practice one non-dehumanizing act of truth, solidarity, or refusal.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

Daniel 2–7 forms an Aramaic section; chapter 7 closes it. The Ancient of Days emphasizes God's priority over temporary rulers. The books opened picture accountable judgment.

### Wrestle with This

How can apocalyptic hope resist empire without making readers obsessed with decoding every symbol?

### Further Study

Compare Daniel 2, Mark 14:61–62, and Revelation 13–14.
