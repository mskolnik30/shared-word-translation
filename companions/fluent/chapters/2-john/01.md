# 2 John 1

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

The elder writes to a chosen lady and her children amid a conflict familiar from 1 John. The community must keep walking in love while resisting teachers who deny Jesus Christ coming in the flesh.

### Chapter Path

- **1:1–3:** Greeting in truth and love.
- **1:4–6:** Joy in people walking in truth; the command to love one another.
- **1:7–11:** Deceivers, remaining in Christ's teaching, and limits on material welcome.
- **1:12–13:** A desire to speak face to face.

### Watch For

Notice that truth and love appear together from the first sentence. The letter does not permit either to cancel the other.

## Read the Chapter

Read 2 John in the Fluent Translation. Ask what “walking” and “remaining” imply about sustained practice.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What brings the elder joy? What teaching marks the deceivers? What kind of participation must the community refuse?

### The Chapter in Brief

The elder greets a chosen lady and her children in truth and love. He rejoices that some are walking in truth and repeats the command heard from the beginning: love one another by walking according to God's commands. Many deceivers deny Jesus Christ coming in flesh, so the community must remain in Christ's teaching. It should not receive or greet a traveling teacher in a way that shares in harmful work. The elder closes by hoping to replace paper and ink with face-to-face conversation.

### Follow the Story

The warning arises inside a network of hospitality and mission. A house could function as lodging, meeting place, and platform. Receiving a teacher was not necessarily a private act of politeness; it could equip and authorize the person's work.

The same letter that sets a boundary also commands love. Discernment is not permission for contempt. It asks the community to consider what its resources and reputation make possible.

The title “antichrist” is applied here to deceiving opposition to the embodied Christ. It should not become a sensational label for modern political enemies.

### Threads Through Scripture

The command to love comes from Jesus and remains central in 1 John. The concern for embodied confession continues John's announcement that the Word became flesh. Scripture also repeatedly distinguishes hospitality to vulnerable strangers from participation in destructive conduct.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** How often are truth and love joined?
- **Understand:** Why could lodging and greeting a traveling teacher amount to partnership?
- **Connect:** What is the difference between treating a person with dignity and endorsing their work?
- **Respond:** Which support, platform, or resource are you responsible to use with greater discernment?

### Prayer and Practice

Review one cause, teacher, or organization you materially support. Ask whether its actual conduct aligns with the truth and love it claims. Change your support if careful evidence requires it.

## Go Deeper

### More Than One Faithful Reading

The chosen lady may be an individual woman or a figurative way of addressing a church. The final greeting from a chosen sister's children makes the church reading plausible, but the letter does not settle the question.

### Wrestle with This

Do not use verses 10–11 to justify hostility toward non-Christians, isolation from questions, or refusal of emergency care. The setting is material and public support for teachers actively spreading a specific denial.

### Further Study

Compare 2 John 4–11 with 1 John 2:18–27 and 4:1–3. Identify the shared tests and the shared call to remain.
