# Exodus 3

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Israel is enslaved in Egypt. Moses, once raised in Pharaoh's household, fled after killing an Egyptian who was beating a Hebrew. He now tends his father-in-law's flock in Midian. At the end of chapter 2, God hears Israel's groaning, remembers the covenant, sees the people, and knows.

### Chapter Path

- **3:1–6:** A bush burns without being consumed; God calls Moses by name on holy ground.
- **3:7–10:** God says that Israel's suffering has been seen and sends Moses to Pharaoh.
- **3:11–15:** Moses asks who he is and who God is; God's presence and name answer him.
- **3:16–22:** Moses receives a message for Israel's elders and a warning that Pharaoh will resist.

### Watch For

Notice the verbs of attention: God sees, hears, knows, comes down, and sends. Also notice that Moses' questions are not dismissed. The call develops through conversation.

## Read the Chapter

Read Exodus 3 in the Fluent Translation. Pause at the movement from “I have seen” to “I am sending you.”

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What draws Moses toward the bush? What does God reveal about Israel's suffering, Moses' task, and God's own identity?

### The Chapter in Brief

While tending sheep, Moses sees a bush aflame yet unconsumed. God calls from the fire, names the ground holy, and identifies himself with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. God has seen Israel's oppression and will deliver them, sending Moses to Pharaoh. Moses questions his fitness and asks what name he should give Israel. God promises to be with him and reveals the divine name. Moses is to gather the elders, confront Egypt's king, and prepare for resistance and eventual departure.

### Follow the Story

The fire signals presence without consuming what it fills. Moses is told both to come no closer and to remove his sandals. Holiness draws him and establishes a boundary; God's nearness is gift, not something he controls.

God's response begins with the enslaved people's pain, not Moses' potential. Deliverance is God's purpose before it is Moses' project. Yet God works through a human agent who feels inadequate. When Moses asks, “Who am I?” the answer is not a list of his strengths but a promise: God will be with him.

The divine name is related to the verb “to be.” Its wording can suggest “I am,” “I will be,” or an active freedom that cannot be reduced to a label. The chapter gives Israel a name to remember while resisting the idea that God can be mastered by naming.

### Threads Through Scripture

The “mountain of God” anticipates Sinai, where the delivered people will enter covenant. God's promise of presence will recur at the sea, in the wilderness, in the tabernacle, and in later call stories. The pattern—God sees suffering, calls a reluctant person, and promises presence—echoes through the prophets.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which details show both God's holiness and God's attention to suffering?
- **Understand:** Why might presence be a deeper answer to Moses than reassurance about his ability?
- **Connect:** Where do people confuse noticing suffering with having already acted against it?
- **Respond:** What is one faithful next step you can take without pretending you can complete the whole work?

### Prayer and Practice

Name before God one suffering you are tempted to avoid. Ask for the courage to look, listen, and take one accountable step alongside others already doing the work.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

Horeb and Sinai are names associated with the same mountain region in the biblical story. The promised sign—that Israel will worship at this mountain—comes after Moses obeys, not before. The departure with goods reverses some of slavery's theft; it is not a general license to take from others.

### Wrestle with This

God announces that Pharaoh will resist. The call therefore includes neither instant success nor freedom from conflict. What changes when faithfulness is measured by trusting God's presence rather than controlling the outcome?

### Further Study

Compare Moses' call with Isaiah 6, Jeremiah 1, and Luke 5:1–11. Notice fear, objection, commission, and promised presence.
