### Follow the Movement

The order of Exodus 15 matters. Its movement through The Song of Moses and Israel, The Peoples Hear and Tremble, Miriam Leads the Women, and finally Bitter Water at Marah controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 8 observes: “breath of your nostrils” uses embodied poetic language for the wind by which the waters are driven back. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies גָּאָה (ga'ah) “To rise in triumph” or “be exalted.” The LORD’s victory is celebrated in song.
