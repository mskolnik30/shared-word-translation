### Follow the Movement

The order of Exodus 21 matters. Its 8 named sections move from “Laws concerning Hebrew Servants” through “Injury to a Pregnant Woman” to “Laws concerning Pits and Goring Oxen.” Read that complete arc before using one detail to characterize the chapter as a whole.

The translation note at verse 2 observes: “servant” translates Hebrew eved, which can denote a range of dependent labor statuses. These laws regulate Israelite debt-servitude in an ancient economy and should not be collapsed into the later race-based chattel slavery familiar from modern history. The translation note at verse 4 observes: The woman and children remaining with the master reflects the legal household structure assumed by the law. The text regulates this institution rather than resolving its moral distance from modern expectations. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies מִשְׁפָּטִים (mishpatim) “Judgments” or “case laws.” Legal decisions applied to particular situations.
