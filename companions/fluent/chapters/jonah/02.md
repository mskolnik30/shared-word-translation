# Jonah 2

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Jonah has fled God's call and been thrown into the sea. A great fish has received him. From inside the fish, Jonah prays in the language of Israel's psalms, remembering danger, descent, rescue, and worship.

### Chapter Path

- **2:1–2:** Jonah calls from distress and says God answered.
- **2:3–6:** He remembers sinking beneath waves, waters, and mountains.
- **2:7–9:** He remembers the LORD, contrasts empty loyalties with faithful love, and promises thanksgiving.
- **2:10:** God commands the fish, and Jonah returns to dry land.

### Watch For

Listen for spatial movement: depths, sea, temple, pit, and dry land. Notice also what Jonah says clearly—salvation belongs to the LORD—and what he does not yet say about Nineveh or his refusal.

## Read the Chapter

Read Jonah 2 in the Fluent Translation. Reading the prayer aloud can make its poetic movement easier to hear.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What danger does Jonah remember? What does he believe God has done? What commitments does he make?

### The Chapter in Brief

Jonah prays from the fish. He describes himself sinking toward death and remembers calling to God. He speaks of looking again toward God's holy temple, giving thanks, and keeping his vows. His prayer reaches its center in the confession that salvation belongs to the LORD. God commands the fish to release him onto dry land.

### Follow the Story

The prayer is a grateful testimony of rescue. Jonah can name God's action even before he is back on land. Yet the story will show that rescue and inner transformation are not identical. A person may speak true words about grace while still resisting where that grace leads.

The chapter therefore deserves both sympathy and patience. Jonah has nearly died. His prayer should not be mocked. But it should be read within the whole book, where God's saving freedom will soon extend beyond Jonah.

### Threads Through Scripture

Jonah's prayer echoes many psalms of distress and thanksgiving: a worshiper cries from danger, remembers God, and promises praise. His descent toward the pit recalls biblical language for approaching death. His return to dry land also resonates with scriptural stories in which God brings life through waters that humans cannot master.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which parts of the prayer describe Jonah's action, and which describe God's action?
- **Understand:** What does “salvation belongs to the LORD” say about who controls mercy?
- **Connect:** Can gratitude be genuine even when a person's understanding is still incomplete?
- **Respond:** What rescue or sustaining grace can you name without pretending every struggle is resolved?

### Prayer and Practice

Write a short prayer with three movements: “I was…,” “You were…,” and “Lead me….” Keep it honest about both gratitude and unfinished change.

## Go Deeper

### How to Read This

The prayer uses poetic language rather than giving a technical account of Jonah's time in the fish. Read its images for the experience they communicate: engulfing danger, nearness to death, remembered worship, and rescue.

### Wrestle with This

Jonah speaks critically of those who cling to empty idols, but the sailors in chapter 1 have just turned toward the LORD. The statement may be true while also revealing how easily religious language can place other people at a distance.

### Further Study

Read Psalms 18, 42, and 116 and list phrases or movements that resemble Jonah's prayer. Ask what Jonah has learned from Israel's worship.
