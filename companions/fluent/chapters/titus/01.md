# Titus 1

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Paul greets Titus as a partner in shared trust and assigns him to put unfinished work in order across Cretan towns. Leadership criteria are followed immediately by a description of disruptive teachers. The chapter includes a harsh ethnic quotation that must not be generalized about Cretans or any people.

### Chapter Path

- **1:1–4:** Truth is tied to godliness and eternal life rests on the God who does not lie.
- **1:5–9:** Titus should appoint elders/overseers whose household, character, hospitality, self-control, and teaching are credible.
- **1:10–16:** Empty talkers upset households for gain; a Cretan stereotype is quoted; false purity and works-denying profession are exposed.

### Watch For

The same leaders are called elders and overseers, suggesting overlapping language here. Leadership begins with recognizable character rather than charisma alone.

## Read the Chapter

Read Titus 1 in the Fluent Translation. Distinguish the letter's polemical quotation from its own universal description of every Cretan.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What qualities make a leader trustworthy, and what fruit reveals harmful teaching?

### The Chapter in Brief

Paul serves God's chosen people by advancing truth that produces godliness and rests on God's truthful promise. Titus is to appoint faithful local elders. An overseer must be above reproach, hospitable, self-controlled, just, holy, disciplined, and able both to encourage and correct through sound teaching. Many rebellious talkers, especially from a circumcision group, disrupt households for shameful gain. Paul quotes a Cretan critic and urges sharp correction. People who claim to know God may deny that profession through corrupt works.

### Follow the Story

“Faithful to one spouse” renders a “one-woman man” idiom as relational fidelity rather than reducing suitability to one mechanical marital history. Household credibility matters because leaders will steward God's household.

Encouragement and correction both depend on holding the trustworthy word. Correction is not license for humiliating dissent; its purpose is restored health in faith.

The quoted line calling Cretans liars and lazy gluttons comes from a Cretan source and functions inside a sharp controversy. The text preserves ethnic rhetoric that modern readers must not treat as objective description of an entire people. Likewise, “especially those of the circumcision” identifies a disputed group, not all Jewish believers.

### Threads Through Scripture

Jesus warns that trees are known by fruit. Acts and Paul's letters show elders serving local communities, while 1 Peter explicitly forbids shepherds to dominate.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which criteria concern power, hospitality, and self-control?
- **Understand:** How do works reveal whether professed knowledge is sound?
- **Connect:** How can polemical stereotypes corrupt a community's discernment?
- **Respond:** What leadership criterion deserves greater practical weight?

### Prayer and Practice

Evaluate a leader by sustained character and effects on households, not public force. Refuse ethnic or cultural stereotypes in correction.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

Ancient households were broad economic communities, and hospitality was essential to traveling believers. “Jewish myths” identifies contested teachings, not Jewish Scripture or identity.

### Wrestle with This

How can strong correction protect a community without adopting contemptuous generalization?
