### Follow the Movement

The order of Psalm 139 matters. Its movement through You Have Searched Me, Where Can I Go, You Knit Me, and finally Search Me controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 5 observes: “Hem me in” is a verb of siege. Whether it is felt as protection or constraint is left open; both senses are present in the word. The translation note at verse 7–12 observes: The second stanza moves through cosmic locations—the heavens, Sheol, the wings of the morning (the east at sunrise), the limits of the sea (the west). The poem covers vertical and horizontal extremities. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies חָקַר (ḥāqar) — To search out, to examine deeply.

For Psalm 139: Attend to image, repetition, parallel lines, changes of speaker, and emotional movement; poetic language should not be reduced to a flat proposition.
