### Follow the Movement

The order of Psalm 46 matters. Its movement through God Is Our Refuge, A River for the City of God, and finally Come and See controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 2–3 observes: The first stanza imagines the unmaking of the world: earth giving way, mountains falling into the sea, waters in chaos. These are the elements that the ancient Near Eastern imagination feared most. The psalm names them and refuses to fear them. The translation note at verse 4 observes: A river appears in the midst of an inland city. Jerusalem has no river. The river is theological geography: a reference to Eden, to Ezekiel's vision, to the streams that the LORD's presence makes flow. Where God dwells, water comes. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies מַחֲסֶה (maḥseh) — Refuge. A place to which one runs and is received. The word recurs across the Psalter as an image of the LORD himself.

For Psalm 46: Attend to image, repetition, parallel lines, changes of speaker, and emotional movement; poetic language should not be reduced to a flat proposition. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
