A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

At verse 02–03, the source notes preserve this detail: The first stanza imagines the unmaking of the world: earth giving way, mountains falling into the sea, waters in chaos. These are the elements that the ancient Near Eastern imagination feared most. The psalm names them and refuses to fear them. At verse 04, the source notes preserve this detail: A river appears in the midst of an inland city. Jerusalem has no river. The river is theological geography: a reference to Eden, to Ezekiel's vision, to the streams that the LORD's presence makes flow. Where God dwells, water comes. One vocabulary entry at verse 01 identifies מַחֲסֶה (maḥseh) — Refuge. A place to which one runs and is received. The word recurs across the Psalter as an image of the LORD himself..
