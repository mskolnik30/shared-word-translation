### Follow the Movement

The order of Ephesians 6 matters. Its movement through Children, Parents, Slaves, and Masters, The Armor of God, and finally Final Greetings controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 5–9 observes: The household code speaks inside the first-century institution of slavery rather than directly abolishing it. Yet both enslaved people and masters are placed under the same heavenly Lord, and masters are forbidden threats and reminded that God shows no partiality. Fluent does not sanitize the word doulos into an employee relationship. The translation note at verse 5 observes: “Fear and trembling” is conventional language of serious responsibility; the object is not necessarily terror before abusive masters. One vocabulary entry at verse 4 identifies παιδεία (paideia) “Discipline, formation.”
