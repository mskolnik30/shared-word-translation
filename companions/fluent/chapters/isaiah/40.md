### Follow the Movement

The order of Isaiah 40 matters. Its movement through Comfort, Comfort My People, A Voice in the Wilderness, Look Your God, To Whom Will You Liken God, and finally They Who Wait controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 2 observes: “Double for all her sins” (kiplayim) has been read both as exact compensation and as full measure. The Hebrew is ambiguous; the translation preserves the doubled image without quantifying it. The translation note at verse 3 observes: The verse is quoted in all four Gospels, applied to John the Baptist. In its original setting, the highway is for the LORD’s return to a people in exile. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies נָחַם (nāḥam) — To comfort, to console. Also used of repentance and relenting; the word names a turn of feeling.

For Isaiah 40: Keep the prophecy's first historical horizon visible before tracing later echoes, and do not turn judgment or restoration imagery into a timetable for current events.
