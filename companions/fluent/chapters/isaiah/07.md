# Isaiah 7

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Judah's King Ahaz faces invasion by Aram and the northern kingdom of Israel, which want to replace him with another ruler. Assyria appears to offer rescue. Isaiah confronts the frightened king with a different choice: quiet trust in the LORD rather than a powerful alliance that will later become its own threat.

### Chapter Path

- **7:1–9:** Ahaz and Jerusalem shake with fear; Isaiah says the invasion plan will not stand and calls Ahaz to stand firm in trust.
- **7:10–17:** God offers any sign; Ahaz refuses with pious language; Isaiah announces the child Immanuel.
- **7:18–25:** Assyria, the power Ahaz trusts, will flood the land and reduce cultivated abundance to survival.

### Watch For

The child-sign has an immediate horizon in Ahaz's crisis: before the child matures, the two threatening kings will be gone. Matthew later reads the Immanuel promise through Jesus; begin by hearing its force for frightened Judah.

## Read the Chapter

Read Isaiah 7 in the Fluent Translation. Listen for forms of “stand”: trust is presented as the ground of stability.

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What frightens Ahaz, what does Isaiah ask of him, and how does the promised solution of Assyria become a deeper danger?

### The Chapter in Brief

Aram and Israel attack Jerusalem but cannot conquer it. When their plan to replace Ahaz is reported, the royal house shakes like trees in wind. Isaiah meets Ahaz with his son Shear-jashub and tells him not to fear: the plot will fail, but Ahaz must stand in trust. God offers a sign; Ahaz refuses, claiming he will not test God. Isaiah gives the sign of a young woman bearing a son named Immanuel, “God is with us.” Before the child matures, the immediate threat will pass, but Assyria will bring devastation.

### Follow the Story

Isaiah does not deny military danger. “Be quiet” calls Ahaz away from fear-driven reaction toward trust. God even accommodates weakness by offering a sign. Ahaz's refusal sounds humble but conceals a settled strategy; he has chosen Assyrian power and does not want confirmation that would challenge it.

The Hebrew word describes a young woman of marriageable age. The sign's timing requires a near birth known within the crisis, though the text does not name the woman with certainty. “Immanuel” claims that God remains with David's house when the promise looks fragile.

Trust does not mean all danger disappears. The kings Ahaz fears will pass, but the empire he recruits will sweep into Judah. The “solution” becomes humiliation and dependence. Fear narrows vision until the strongest available ally looks like the only real savior.

### Threads Through Scripture

Isaiah's child's name, “A remnant shall return,” holds judgment and future together. Matthew 1 reads Jesus' birth as the fullest expression of God-with-us. Across Scripture, divine presence grounds courage while worldly empires promise protection at a cost.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which images describe fear and Assyria's later pressure?
- **Understand:** Why is Ahaz's refusal of a sign not genuine trust?
- **Connect:** When does an urgent solution create deeper dependence?
- **Respond:** What decision needs quiet attention rather than fear-driven speed?

### Prayer and Practice

Before a pressured decision, name what you fear, whom the choice may harm, and what allegiance it creates. Ask for counsel from people not invested in panic.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

Aram and Israel's attack is often called the Syro-Ephraimite crisis. “Curds and honey” can sound luxurious but here may describe food available after agriculture has collapsed into sparse pastoral survival.

### Wrestle with This

How can “God with us” become courage for faithful action rather than a slogan claiming God automatically endorses our strategy?

### Further Study

Read 2 Kings 16 for Ahaz's alliance, Isaiah 8 for the sign's continuation, and Matthew 1:18–25 for the Gospel's rereading.
