### Follow the Movement

The order of Job 15 matters. Its movement through Eliphaz: Does a Wise Man Answer with Wind, Were You the First Human Born, What Is a Human That He Should Be Pure, I Will Tell You What I Have Seen, The Wicked Writhes All His Days, and finally He Will Not Become Rich controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 4 observes: Eliphaz now charges Job’s speech itself with undermining reverence and prayer before God, intensifying his criticism from earlier speeches. The translation note at verse 8 observes: “council of God” — The phrase evokes privileged access to divine deliberation. Ironically, the reader has seen a heavenly council in chapters 1–2, but Job and Eliphaz have not. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies רוּחַ (ruach) “Wind,” “spirit,” or “breath.” Eliphaz compares Job’s knowledge to empty wind.

For Job 15: Attend to image, repetition, parallel lines, changes of speaker, and emotional movement; poetic language should not be reduced to a flat proposition. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
