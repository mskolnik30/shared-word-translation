### Follow the Movement

The order of Job 39 matters. Its movement through Do You Know When the Mountain Goats Give Birth, Who Set the Wild Donkey Free, Will the Wild Ox Serve You, The Wings of the Ostrich Flutter, Do You Give the Horse Its Might, and finally Does the Hawk Fly by Your Wisdom controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 5–12 observes: Wild donkey and wild ox are valuable precisely because they are not domesticated. The speech celebrates creaturely freedom that does not serve human economic purposes. The translation note at verse 9 observes: The “wild ox” is Hebrew re'em, probably the extinct aurochs rather than the mythical unicorn found in some older translations. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies יָעֵל (ya'el) “Mountain goat” or “ibex.” A wild goat living in rocky terrain.

For Job 39: Attend to image, repetition, parallel lines, changes of speaker, and emotional movement; poetic language should not be reduced to a flat proposition. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
