### Follow the Movement

The order of Job 22 matters. Its movement through Eliphaz: Can a Human Be of Use to God, You Have Taken Pledges from Your Brothers, Does God Not See, Will You Keep the Ancient Path, and finally Return to the Almighty controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 13–14 observes: Eliphaz puts a theology of divine remoteness into Job’s mouth, although Job’s complaint has usually been the opposite: God is painfully present and attentive. The translation note at verse 15–20 observes: The “ancient path” may allude to a remembered catastrophic judgment, perhaps the flood, but the reference is not explicit. One vocabulary entry at verse 4 identifies מִשְׁפָּט (mishpat) “Judgment” or “legal case.” Eliphaz assumes Job’s suffering is divine litigation for guilt.

For Job 22: Attend to image, repetition, parallel lines, changes of speaker, and emotional movement; poetic language should not be reduced to a flat proposition. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.

None of these charges has been supported by the narrative.
