### Follow the Movement

The order of Job 4 matters. Its movement through Eliphaz Begins to Speak, Who Ever Perished Being Innocent, A Word Came Secretly to Me, and finally Can a Human Be More Righteous Than God controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 3–5 observes: Eliphaz appeals to Job’s former role as a comforter and implies that Job should now apply to himself the counsel he gave others. The translation note at verse 7 observes: The rhetorical questions reveal Eliphaz’s theology of retribution: the innocent do not perish and the upright are not cut off. Job’s experience immediately puts the claim under pressure. One vocabulary entry at verse 6 identifies תִּקְוָה (tiqvah) “Hope” or “expectation.” Eliphaz identifies Job’s integrity as the supposed basis of hope.

For Job 4: Attend to image, repetition, parallel lines, changes of speaker, and emotional movement; poetic language should not be reduced to a flat proposition. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
