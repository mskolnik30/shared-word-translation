### Follow the Movement

The order of Job 27 matters. Its movement through I Will Not Put Away My Integrity, Let My Enemy Be Like the Wicked, I Will Teach You About the Hand of God, and finally This Is the Portion of a Wicked Man controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 5 observes: “declare you righteous” — Job refuses to vindicate the friends by admitting guilt merely to make their theology work. The translation note at verse 6 observes: Job’s claim is not that he has never sinned in any sense, but that his conscience does not accuse him of the hidden wickedness alleged by the friends. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies מִשְׁפָּט (mishpat) “Justice” or “case.” Job says God has taken away his justice.

For Job 27: Attend to image, repetition, parallel lines, changes of speaker, and emotional movement; poetic language should not be reduced to a flat proposition.
