### Follow the Movement

The order of Job 3 matters. Its movement through Job Curses the Day of His Birth, Why Did I Not Die at Birth, and finally Why Is Light Given to the Suffering controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 3–10 observes: Job imagines undoing his birth by reversing creation imagery: light disappears, darkness returns, and the day is removed from ordered time. The translation note at verse 5 observes: “death-shadow” — Hebrew tsalmavet can mean deep darkness or the shadow of death. The compound image suits Job’s desire for the day to disappear into deathlike darkness. One vocabulary entry at verse 1 identifies קָלַל (qalal) “To curse” or “treat as accursed.” Job directs the curse toward his birth day.
