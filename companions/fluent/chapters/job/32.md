### Follow the Movement

The order of Job 32 matters. Its movement through Elihu Becomes Angry, I Am Young, and You Are Aged, I Waited for Your Words, and finally I Am Full of Words controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 2 observes: “righteous rather than God” — The Hebrew can mean Job justified himself more than God or at God’s expense. Elihu frames the dispute as involving the character of God as well as Job. The translation note at verse 8 observes: Elihu qualifies the wisdom of age by appealing to the divine spirit and breath that give understanding. Wisdom is not simply accumulated years. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies חָרָה אַף (charah af) “Anger burned.” The repeated idiom describing Elihu’s reaction.

For Job 32: Attend to image, repetition, parallel lines, changes of speaker, and emotional movement; poetic language should not be reduced to a flat proposition. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
