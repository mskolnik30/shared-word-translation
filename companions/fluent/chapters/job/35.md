### Follow the Movement

The order of Job 35 matters. Its movement through Elihu: What Do You Gain by Being Righteous, Look at the Heavens, People Cry Out Under Oppression, and finally Your Case Is Before Him controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 5–8 observes: Elihu emphasizes divine transcendence: human behavior does not enrich or diminish God as it affects fellow humans. The point does not mean God is indifferent to morality. The translation note at verse 9–13 observes: Elihu acknowledges widespread oppression and unanswered cries but attributes failure of response to pride and empty appeal. The explanation risks sounding similar to the friends’ assumptions about sufferers. One vocabulary entry at verse 2 identifies מִשְׁפָּט (mishpat) “Justice” or “judgment.” Elihu asks whether Job’s claim is just.
