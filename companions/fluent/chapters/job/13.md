### The Chapter in Brief

Job 13 moves through I Would Speak to the Almighty; then Will You Speak Unjustly for God; then Though He Kill Me, I Will Hope in Him; then Withdraw Your Hand from Me; and the remaining movement leads to How Many Are My Sins. ### Follow the Movement

The order of Job 13 matters. Its movement through I Would Speak to the Almighty, Will You Speak Unjustly for God, Though He Kill Me, I Will Hope in Him, Withdraw Your Hand from Me, and finally How Many Are My Sins controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 4 observes: “worthless physicians” — The friends claim to diagnose Job’s condition but their treatment is based on what Job calls lies. The translation note at verse 7–10 observes: Job argues that defending God through falsehood is itself an offense against God. Divine honor does not require dishonest advocacy. One vocabulary entry at verse 3 identifies יָכַח (yakhach) “To argue,” “prove,” or “contend.” Job desires a reasoned case with God.

For Job 13: Attend to image, repetition, parallel lines, changes of speaker, and emotional movement; poetic language should not be reduced to a flat proposition. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
