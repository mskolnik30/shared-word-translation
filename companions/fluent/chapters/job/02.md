### Follow the Movement

The order of Job 2 matters. Its movement through The Accuser Returns, Job Is Struck with Painful Sores, and finally Job’s Three Friends Come controls how isolated details should be heard. A reading that begins with the conclusion but ignores the earlier tension may make the chapter say more—or less—than its complete shape allows.

The translation note at verse 4 observes: “Skin for skin” — The proverb’s precise origin is uncertain. In context the accuser claims that loss of possessions and even family has not reached the deepest point of self-interest: one’s own bodily life. The translation note at verse 7 observes: The Hebrew shechin ra describes severe inflamed sores or boils. The text does not identify a modern diagnosis. One vocabulary entry at verse 3 identifies תֻּמָּה (tummah) “Integrity” or “wholeness.” Job continues to hold fast to his moral integrity.

For Job 2: Attend to image, repetition, parallel lines, changes of speaker, and emotional movement; poetic language should not be reduced to a flat proposition. The goal is attentive understanding, not premature resolution. Where the translation notes identify uncertainty, readers should keep that uncertainty visible.
