# Galatians 3

## Before You Read

### Where You Are

Paul has confronted Peter for withdrawing from Gentile tables and defended right standing through Jesus Christ's faithfulness rather than works marking Torah identity. Chapter 3 appeals to the Galatians' Spirit experience and Abraham's story.

### Chapter Path

- **3:1–5:** The Spirit came through hearing with trust, not Torah-status performance.
- **3:6–14:** Abraham trusted God; nations share his blessing; Christ bears the law's curse so the Spirit reaches Gentiles.
- **3:15–22:** Promise precedes the law and is centered on Abraham's singular “offspring.”
- **3:23–29:** The law served as guardian until Christ; baptism clothes believers in Christ and creates equal standing.

### Watch For

Paul does not call the law evil. He asks what role it plays after the promise and Christ, especially when teachers make it the condition of Gentile belonging.

## Read the Chapter

Read Galatians 3 in the Fluent Translation. Follow Spirit, trust, promise, law, offspring, and “one.”

## After You Read

### Tell It in Your Own Words

What evidence does Paul use, and how does baptism change the meaning of social distinctions?

### The Chapter in Brief

Paul reminds the Galatians that they received the Spirit through trusting hearing. Abraham's trust was counted as righteousness, and Scripture announced beforehand that nations would be blessed in him. Christ redeems from the curse by bearing it on the cross so Abraham's blessing and the Spirit reach Gentiles. Promise came before the law and cannot be canceled by it. The law served as guardian until Christ. Through trust and baptism all are God's children, clothed in Christ; Jew and Greek, slave and free, male and female no longer determine unequal standing in him.

### Follow the Story

The Spirit is not a prize for completing ethnic boundary requirements. The community's own received life testifies that grace came first. Paul links this to Abraham before Sinai.

His curse argument concerns universal failure and Christ's redemptive bearing, not Torah being wicked. The statements about law's purpose and mediation are compressed and debated; the translation wisely avoids expanding them into a theory of defective revelation.

“No Jew or Greek, slave or free, male and female” does not pretend social realities vanished. It denies that they assign different covenant worth. The rest of Paul's letters shows that unequal systems still require embodied change.

### Threads Through Scripture

Genesis promises blessing for all families through Abraham. Deuteronomy supplies the tree-curse language; Habakkuk joins righteous life with faithfulness. Paul's argument carries these texts into the cross and multiethnic baptism.

### Questions for Conversation

- **Notice:** Which came first—promise, law, Christ, or the Galatians' Spirit experience?
- **Understand:** What does a household guardian do temporarily?
- **Connect:** Which distinctions still become rankings inside churches?
- **Respond:** What shared practice can make equal belonging visible?

### Prayer and Practice

Notice whose presence at a table, meeting, or ministry remains conditional. Remove one unnecessary status barrier.

## Go Deeper

### What Needs Context

The Greek “faithfulness of Jesus Christ” can also be read “faith in Jesus Christ”; the Fluent rendering distinguishes Christ's faithfulness from the trust of recipients. *Paidagōgos* was a household guardian, not mainly a classroom teacher.

### Wrestle with This

How can a community honor Scripture's differences without using them to create unequal standing?
